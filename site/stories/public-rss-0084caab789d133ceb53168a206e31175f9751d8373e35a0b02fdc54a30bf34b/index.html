    迈凯伦如何缓解拉斯维加斯直道速度劣势

    在拉斯维加斯大奖赛周五排位赛中，迈凯伦车手诺里斯和皮亚斯特里因直道速度不足而处于劣势，诺里斯最终位列第五，落后杆位得主安东内利近0.8秒。

    迈凯伦如何缓解拉斯维加斯F1直道速度劣势

    由于兰多·诺里斯和奥斯卡·皮亚斯特里的最高速度不足，这对迈凯伦车手不得不充分利用拉斯维加斯的赛道位置博弈

    奥斯卡·皮亚斯特里，迈凯伦，兰多·诺里斯，迈凯伦

    尽管在这条放大了MCL40缺陷而不奖励其优点的赛道上取得了还算体面的成绩，兰多·诺里斯对拉斯维加斯大奖赛周末周五的表现并不满意。

    “我们在直道上简直太糟糕了，”诺里斯在排位赛获得第五名后不久说道，他落后杆位得主基米·安东内利近0.8秒，后者因引擎罚单将退后五位，使诺里斯在发车格上升至第四。

    长直道和低下压力需求意味着拉斯维加斯大道赛道从来不会成为这辆赛车最强的赛道，正如迈凯伦在蒙扎和巴库这两个最接近的对比赛道上表现平淡所预示的那样。

    但迈凯伦在拉斯维加斯的差距仍然大得令人惊讶，至少对安东内利来说是这样，诺里斯和奥斯卡·皮亚斯特里在拉斯维加斯大道的长直道以及通往终点的最后一段直道上通常都慢了20公里/小时。

    如此巨大的差异很少仅仅归因于单一因素，周五的情况也证明了这一点。尽管有定期升级，MCL40仍然相对阻力较大且空气动力学效率不高，而车队在季前选择采用更短的传动比，使其在拥有极长直道的赛道上损失了最高速的爆发力。

    与所有车队一样，迈凯伦本可以选择在赛季中期对齿轮比进行一次调整，但最终决定不这么做，因为其较短的齿比在其他赛道上仍然能带来优势。然而在拉斯维加斯，车队不得不为此付出代价。

    当被 Motorsport.com 问及车队的劣势是否比预期更严重时，车队领队安德烈亚·斯特拉回答说：“我们确实有些担心这条赛道的要求对我们的赛车有多适合。这不仅仅是最高速度的问题，还涉及最高速度与大家都在努力利用的尾流效应的结合。因此，你会进入发动机在某些工况下的运转状态，而用我们的齿比来说并不理想。我们知道从空气动力学角度来看，阻力并不是我们的强项。我们看到了与蒙扎、巴库相似的特征。而在所有条件下，我们都不是特别有竞争力。”

    由于尾流效应如此关键——这一点从主导第三节排位赛并让伊萨克·哈贾尔和乔治·拉塞尔等人付出代价的位置战术中可见一斑——迈凯伦试图通过更积极地追逐尾流来弥补最高速度的不足。

    斯特拉补充说：“我们在这里做得更好的是通过非常好的位置安排来缓解这一问题。在排位赛中，赛车几乎处于我们想要的位置。这确实有帮助。因为我必须说，在这些规则下，当你跟随一辆甚至多辆赛车时，阻力减少似乎比过去更多。因此，处于正确位置的激励甚至更高。”

    诺里斯详细解释道：“我必须在距离前车1.5秒的地方开始我的飞驰圈，这意味着在每个弯道我至少会损失半成到一成的时间，因为我损失的了下压力太多了。显然，最终拥有一个好的尾流会带来更大的收益，但我们不得不弥补直线速度的严重损失——因此我们必须牺牲我们的过弯速度。”

    再加上外部因素，安东内利在小红牛和阿尔派车队的一群赛车末尾获得了“极端”的尾流，迈凯伦的真实差距可能被夸大了。两位车手在直道上损失的时间远不及安东内利的队友乔治·拉塞尔，也不及马克斯·维斯塔潘——后者将在周六因安东内利五位的发车位罚退而继承杆位。

    尽管如此，皮亚斯特里认为迈凯伦最高速度劣势的幅度比预期更大，不过他也承认自己第七快的成绩是一圈混乱的飞驰圈所致。“说实话，这让我们感到意外，”这位澳大利亚车手说。“我认为我们没料到这会是个这么大的问题。在蒙扎我们还行，对我们来说奇怪的是，这主要是与其他梅赛德斯动力赛车相比。”

    “我认为有几件事我们可能会复盘，看看能否做得稍微好一点。但我觉得我的圈速也有点混乱。”

    斯特拉对迈凯伦修改后的底板持乐观态度，该底板由诺里斯在维加斯试用，并帮助提升了低速性能，但这位意大利人透露了一个不寻常的原因，说明为何该升级的性能收益在很大程度上被抵消了。

    “在兰多这边，一个塑料袋卡在了底板里，我们在那里损失了相当多的下压力。所以，考虑到十分之一秒就会带来很大差别，那个塑料袋上肯定有十分之一秒的损失，这有点可惜。

    “但现在我们需要研究如何在正赛中保持赛车的可比赛性。在蒙扎我们看到，由于这种尾流效应，你实际上可以保持纠缠、与其他赛车相连。所以我们肯定期待明天，并且排位赛一直很有竞争力这一事实令我们感到鼓舞。”

    How McLaren tried to mitigate its Las Vegas F1 top speed deficit

    With Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri down on top speed, the McLaren pair had to make the most of Las Vegas's track position games

    Oscar Piastri, McLaren, Lando Norris, McLaren

    Despite what was a respectable result on a circuit that accentuated the MCL40's flaws without rewarding its virtues, Lando Norris was not best pleased with how Friday at the Las Vegas Grand Prix weekend.

    “We're just shocking on the straights,” said Norris shortly after qualifying fifth, just under eight tenths behind polesitter Kimi Antonelli, who is set to drop five places for an engine penalty, elevating Norris to fourth on the grid.

    The long straights and low downforce demands meant the Las Vegas Strip circuit was never going to be the strongest track for this car, as foreshadowed by McLaren's muted form on the circuit's nearest comparisons in Monza and Baku.

    But McLaren's deficit in Vegas was still surprisingly vast, at least to Antonelli, with Norris and Oscar Piastri routinely 20km/h down on the circuit's long Las Vegas Boulevard as well as the final straight towards the finish.

    Differences so massive are rarely just down to one single factor, and that also proved to be the case on Friday. Despite regular upgrades, the MCL40 remains relatively draggy and aerodynamically inefficient, which is compounded by the team's pre-season choice to go for a shorter gearing, costing it top-end grunt on circuits with extremely long straights.

    Like all teams, McLaren had the option to make one mid-season change to its gear ratios, but decided against it because its shorter gearing would still stand it in good stead on other circuits. In Vegas, though, the team had to pay the price.

    "Definitely we had some concern in terms of how suitable the demands of this circuit would be for our car," team principal Andrea Stella replied when asked by Motorsport.com whether the squad's deficit was more severe than anticipated. "This is not only the top speed, it is also the top speed that is combined with the towing that everyone is trying to do. So, you go in operating regimes of the engine that with our ratios is not ideal. We know that the drag from an aerodynamic point of view is not a point of strength. We have seen traits that are similar to Monza, to Baku. And in all conditions we were not particularly competitive."

    With slipstreaming so crucial, as evidenced by the positional tactics that dominated Q3 and cost the likes of Isack Hadjar and George Russell, McLaren tried to compensate for its lack of top speed by more aggressively chasing the tow.

    "What we have done better here is to mitigate this by having really good positioning," Stella added. "The car was pretty much where we wanted it to be in qualifying. And this definitely helped. Because I have to say that it seems like with these regulations the drag reduction when you follow one car or even multiple cars is more than in the past. And therefore, there is an even higher incentive to be positioned in the right place."

    Norris elaborated: “I have to start my lap 1.5 seconds off the car ahead, which means in every corner I'm losing at least half a tenth to a tenth, because of how much downforce I'm losing. There's obviously a bigger gain at the end of the day in having a good slipstream, but we're having to make up for a really terrible loss of straight-line speed - and therefore we have to harm our cornering speed."

    Add in the external factor of Antonelli receiving an "extreme" tow at the end of a gaggle of cars from Racing Bulls and Alpine, and McLaren's real deficit has probably been exaggerated. Neither driver lost nearly as much time on the straights to Antonelli's team-mate George Russell or indeed Max Verstapppen, who will inherit pole on Saturday due to Antonelli's five-place grid penalty.

    Still, Piastri felt the size of McLaren's top speed disadvantage was bigger than expected, though he also admitted his seventh-fastest time was the result of a messy lap. "t caught us by surprise, to be honest," the Australian said. "I don't think we expected it to be such a big problem. At Monza we were okay and the strange part for us is it's mostly compared to the other Mercedes-powered cars.

    "I think there were a few things that we probably will review and see if we can do a slightly better job. But I think also my laps were a little bit messy."

    Stella was upbeat about McLaren's revised floor which was trialled by Norris in Vegas and helped low-speed performance, but the Italian revealed an unusual reason why that upgrade's performance benefit was largely cancelled out.

    "On Lando's side, a plastic bag got stuck in the floor and we lost quite a lot of downforce there. So, considering that one tenth of a second would make a big difference, certainly there was one tenth of a second on a plastic bag, which is a bit of a shame.

    "But now we need to look at how we keep the car raceable in the race. At Monza we saw that because of this towing effect you actually can stay tangled, linked to the other cars. So we are definitely looking forward to tomorrow and encouraged by the fact that qualifying has been competitive."
