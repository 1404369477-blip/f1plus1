    What time the 2026 F1 Qatar Grand Prix starts in the UK, plus the full schedule and how to watch live on Sky Sports F1 and NOW

    The Qatar Grand Prix starts at 4pm UK time on Sunday 29 November. Formula 1 returns to the Lusail International Circuit for the penultimate race of the 2026 season, with every session live on Sky Sports F1. The full UK schedule and viewing information are below.

    Qatar Grand Prix 2026 schedule in UK time

    Qatar is three hours ahead of the UK. This is not a sprint weekend.

    What time does the Qatar Grand Prix start in the UK?

    The Qatar Grand Prix starts at 4pm UK time on Sunday 29 November. The race runs over 57 laps of the 5.419km (3.367-mile) Lusail International Circuit, covering 308.611km (191.8 miles).

    Without interruptions, the race should finish at around 5:30pm UK time. If it runs beyond two hours, the chequered flag will fall before all 57 laps are completed.

    What time is qualifying for the Qatar Grand Prix?

    Qualifying starts at 6pm UK time on Saturday 28 November and lasts about an hour. The slowest five drivers are eliminated in Q1 and another five in Q2, leaving 10 to fight for pole position in Q3.

    Lusail is dominated by medium and high-speed corners, with long, flowing sections putting significant load through the tyres. The long start-finish straight provides the circuit's clearest overtaking opportunity.

    How to watch the Qatar Grand Prix in the UK

    Sky Sports F1 shows every session live. Without a Sky TV subscription, the same coverage is available through NOW. Channel 4 will show free highlights of qualifying at 9:30pm on Saturday and the race at 9pm on Sunday.

    Kimi Antonelli has already secured the 2026 drivers' championship and Mercedes has won the constructors' title, but the fight for second place remains open with two races remaining.

    Max Verstappen leads that battle on 304 points after winning in Las Vegas. Lewis Hamilton is second among the chasing group on 267, with George Russell and Lando Norris tied on 264. With 50 points still available across Qatar and Abu Dhabi, all four can still finish runner-up.

    Ferrari is second in the constructors' championship on 517 points, ahead of Red Bull on 440 and McLaren on 431. Red Bull and McLaren are separated by just nine points in the fight for third.

    The 5.419km Lusail International Circuit is a fast, flowing permanent circuit dominated by medium- and high-speed corners. Its long main straight leads into the primary overtaking opportunity at Turn 1, while the sequence of sweeping corners places sustained loads on the tyres.

    Max Verstappen won the 2025 Qatar Grand Prix ahead of Oscar Piastri and Carlos Sainz. Oscar Piastri started from pole for McLaren.

    Verstappen has won each of the last three Qatar Grands Prix, taking victory in 2023, 2024 and 2025.
