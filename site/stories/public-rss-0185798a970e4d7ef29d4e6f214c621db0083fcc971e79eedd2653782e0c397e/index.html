    维斯塔潘坦言卡塔尔与阿布扎比之行宁愿待在家

    维斯塔潘在卡塔尔与阿布扎比两站前接受荷兰《电讯报》采访时表示，自己对这一行程感到不适，宁愿待在家里。

    维斯塔潘坦言，在卡塔尔与阿布扎比两站前，他“宁愿待在家里”。

    维斯塔潘在接受荷兰《电讯报》采访时，毫不掩饰自己对F1中东双赛的不适。

    致红牛车迷。关注红牛车队，获取来自米尔顿凯恩斯的F1新闻、车手动态、技术解析与比赛分析。

    F1围场抵达多哈，迎来2026赛季倒数第二站，但维斯塔潘并不掩饰这趟行程让他感到不轻松。

    这位四届世界冠军在接受荷兰媒体《电讯报》采访时，对在地区敌对行动持续期间完成卡塔尔和阿布扎比23站赛历一事给出了直率的评价。

    “我对此感到安心吗？不，我宁愿待在家里，”维斯塔潘说。“但我们得到了某些保证，因此我们必须假定这些保证是真的。明知周边国家仍可能有火箭飞过头顶还要去那里，这并不令人愉快。但不管怎样，我们还是要去的。”

    由于中东冲突，F1直接取消了4月的沙特阿拉伯大奖赛，并将巴林站改到10月的马来西亚雪邦国际赛道举行——维斯塔潘随后在那里从杆位夺冠。数月来，官员一直让意大利伊莫拉赛道待命，作为收官战的低温备选方案，直到11月10日全部11支车队都支持在海湾地区完成赛历后，才撤回了这些备用计划。

    同一天，国际汽联主席穆罕默德·本·苏拉耶姆在Instagram上发文，宣布该管理机构已为这两个场地做好准备。

    “我们已做好充分准备，并承诺呈现两个精彩的大奖赛周末以及F1锦标赛的盛大收官，”本·苏拉耶姆写道，并补充说官员们“正与合作伙伴密切协作，确保为车手、车队和车迷做好一切安排”。

    在围场内，车手们过去一个月里的语气明显不同。在10月的美国大奖赛之前，大奖赛车手协会理事卡洛斯·塞恩斯公开质疑锦标赛的确定性，对记者表示：“他们似乎非常确信比赛会继续进行。就我个人而言，我并不这么认为，”同时敦促所有人“保持警惕，并对任何情况做出反应”。

    相比之下，同为大奖赛车手协会理事的乔治·拉塞尔在拉斯维加斯对英国媒体表示，尽管有官方旅行警告，他“毫不犹豫”前往卡塔尔和阿联酋，而迈凯伦的兰多·诺里斯则表示，他相信F1高层不会让锦标赛约2000名随行工作人员置身险境。

    一旦赛车在周五驶上卢赛尔国际赛道进行练习（11月27日至29日），随后赛季在阿布扎比收官（12月4日至6日），维斯塔潘仍有大量需要捍卫的东西。

    尽管20岁的基米·安东内利在墨西哥城锁定了车手总冠军，梅赛德斯也在圣保罗拿下了第九个车队总冠军，但红牛赛季末的强势反弹让维斯塔潘连续九场比赛登上领奖台——包括最近六场比赛中的四场胜利。

    刚刚在拉斯维加斯那场出现352次超车的惊险大战中获胜后，维斯塔潘以304分位居积分榜第二，在剩余50个车手积分的情况下，领先刘易斯·汉密尔顿（267分）37分，领先拉塞尔和诺里斯（各264分）40分。关键的是，红牛在车队积分榜上以440比431领先迈凯伦、位居第三——这关系到数百万美元的奖金分成——在还剩86个车队积分的情况下，这一优势依然极其微弱。

    卡塔尔站本身在2026年取消了冲刺赛赛制，但倍耐力和国际汽联周三又增加了一个新的策略变数：在周日57圈的大奖赛中，每套轮胎最多使用27圈——这保证了比赛至少需要两次进站。维斯塔潘或许更愿意在11月底待在别处，但红牛的车队排名仍未确定，他几乎没有时间东张西望，只能专注于前方的道路。

    Max Verstappen admits he would “rather stay at home” ahead of Qatar and Abu Dhabi

    Speaking to Dutch newspaper De Telegraaf, Max Verstappen made no secret of his discomfort ahead of Formula 1’s Middle East double-header

    For Red Bull fans. Keeping watch on Red Bull Racing, with F1 news, driver updates, technical insight and race analysis from Milton Keynes.

    Formula 1’s paddock is touching down in Doha for the penultimate round of the 2026 championship, yet Max Verstappen is not pretending the trip sits easily with him.

    Speaking to Dutch outlet De Telegraaf, the four-time world champion delivered a blunt assessment of finishing the 23-race calendar in Qatar and Abu Dhabi amid ongoing regional hostilities.

    "Do I feel comfortable with it? No, I'd rather stay at home," Verstappen said. "But we've been given certain guarantees, and therefore we have to assume they're true. It's not exactly pleasant to go somewhere knowing that rockets can still fly over your head in the surrounding countries. But, anyway, we're going."

    F1 cancelled April’s Saudi Arabian Grand Prix outright and relocated the Bahrain round to Malaysia’s Sepang International Circuit in October - a race Verstappen went on to win from pole - due to the conflict in the Middle East. For months, officials kept Italy’s Imola circuit on standby as a cold-weather contingency for the finale, only to stand down those backup plans on 10 November once all 11 teams backed completing the calendar in the Gulf.

    Writing on Instagram the same day, FIA President Mohammed Ben Sulayem declared the governing body ready for both venues.

    "We are fully prepared and committed to delivering two outstanding grand prix weekends and a spectacular conclusion to the [F1 championship]," Ben Sulayem wrote, adding that officials are "working closely with our partners to ensure everything is in place for our drivers, teams and fans".

    Inside the paddock, drivers have struck noticeably different tones over the past month. Ahead of October’s United States Grand Prix, Grand Prix Drivers’ Association director Carlos Sainz openly questioned the championship’s certainty, telling reporters: "They seem very convinced that it's going to go ahead. On my personal side, I'm not," while urging everyone to "stay diligent, and reactive to whatever happens".

    By contrast, fellow GPDA director George Russell told British media in Las Vegas that he has "no hesitation" in travelling to Qatar and the United Arab Emirates despite official travel warnings, while McLaren’s Lando Norris said he trusts F1 bosses would not place the championship’s roughly 2,000 travelling staff in harm’s way.

    Once cars roll out for Friday practice at the Losail International Circuit (27-29 November) before the season closes in Abu Dhabi (4-6 December), Verstappen still has plenty to protect.

    Although 20-year-old Kimi Antonelli wrapped up the drivers’ title in Mexico City and Mercedes sealed its ninth constructors’ crown in Sao Paulo, Red Bull’s late-season turnaround has put Verstappen on a nine-race podium streak - including four victories in his last six outings.

    Fresh off winning Las Vegas’s 352-overtake thriller, Verstappen sits second in the standings on 304 points, holding a 37-point advantage over Lewis Hamilton (267) and 40 over Russell and Norris (264 apiece) with 50 driver points remaining. Crucially, Red Bull’s 440–431 edge over McLaren for third in the constructors’ standings - worth millions in prize payouts - remains razor-thin with 86 team points still on the board.

    Qatar itself drops its sprint format for 2026, yet Pirelli and the FIA added a fresh strategic wrinkle on Wednesday by mandating a 27-lap maximum stint length per tyre set across Sunday’s 57-lap Grand Prix - guaranteeing a minimum two-stop contest. Verstappen may have preferred spending late November elsewhere, but with Red Bull’s constructors’ finish still on the line, he will have little time to look anywhere except the road ahead.
