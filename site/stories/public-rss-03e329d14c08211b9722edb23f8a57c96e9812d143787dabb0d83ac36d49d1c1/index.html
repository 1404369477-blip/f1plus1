    劳森获小红牛2027年续约

    劳森与林德布拉德将在2027年继续为小红牛车队出战，红牛选择不让F2争冠车手措洛夫升入正式车手席位。

    利亚姆·劳森与阿尔维德·林德布拉德将在2027年继续为小红牛车队出战，红牛选择不做那笔本会堪称F1车手大清洗的决定。

    以下是我们团队对留下劳森和林德布拉德、而不将F2争冠车手尼古拉·措洛夫提升为正式车手的看法。

    一个关乎存亡的问题得到了回答。

    很少有车手决定像劳森从小红牛获得新合同这样让人觉得理所当然。

    他今年表现非常出色。是的，还有一些粗糙之处需要打磨，但他整年都很快，始终强劲，提供了工程师们喜爱的技术反馈，并且出色地屏蔽了所有关于他未来的质疑噪音。

    在艾伦·珀曼的带领下，这支车队能否独立自主、做出自己的决定，并规划一条只专注于让自己变得更好的道路？

    还是说它只是姊妹车队红牛的傀儡，是未来车手的测试场和培训学校，即使这意味着让新秀匆忙上位会带来一些短期阵痛？

    在劳森经历了这样的赛季之后，小红牛放弃他并抛弃已经积累的所有良好势头是毫无道理的。所以暂时留住他给了我们关于小红牛定位的答案。

    措洛夫在F1有着光明的未来，但时间站在他这边，让他能够迈出那一步。

    很确定这是对小红牛最好的决定，也是未来对红牛最好的决定。

    劳森和林德布拉德——别忘了，后者还是个新秀——都已经证明，当比赛周末一切按计划进行时，他们能够带回成绩。

    两人都有能力在伊萨克·哈贾尔或马克斯·维斯塔潘中任何一人踩到香蕉皮滑倒时成为替补。红牛清楚劳森在需要时能带来什么，所以他是衡量林德布拉德潜在贡献的一个很好的参照。

    让措洛夫在幕后待命并参加几节第一次自由练习赛，将为他2028年正式登场做好准备。

    就我而言，这对所有相关方都是正确的决定。

    如果小红牛想被真正视为一支独立的F1车队，拥有自己的雄心、梦想等等，而不是纯粹的红牛人才农场，那么是的，劳森基本上必须留下。

    措洛夫未来可能会更快。据我们所知，他可能已经更快了。但劳森今年提供的那种高效得分能力，新秀是无法匹敌的——我认为这是F1的一条规律。

    因此，用措洛夫替换劳森，将赤裸裸地牺牲小红牛的现在来换取红牛主队的未来，而每一个这样的决定都会让红牛在F1拥有两支车队变得越来越难以接受。

    只有小红牛拥有自己的抱负，它才可能被允许继续存在，而给劳森一份新合同在我看来能换来一些可信度。

    马尔科早就该放弃劳森了

    在他权力鼎盛时期，赫尔穆特·马尔科早就把劳森抛弃了。

    劳森曾有机会进入顶级车队，但没能成功，而且很可能不会再得到那样的机会了。

    现在马尔科不再掌权，这是迄今为止最明显的迹象，表明小红牛不是红牛二队；培养年轻车手不再是这支车队的唯一目标，甚至不是主要目标。

    你不能怪红牛。小红牛如今是一个价值数十亿美元的实体，而不是像红牛二队那样一支运营起来既费钱又亏钱的车队。在如今的F1商业环境里，制造商锦标赛的最终排名已不再是衡量价值的唯一标准。

    在一支以接受亏损为前提建立起来的车队身上冒险是一回事。在一支价值数十亿美元的车队身上冒险则是另一回事。

    这一切并非批评其所作决定，因为劳森是一位配得上F1的车手。这只是为了指出更多证据，表明小红牛正像一支独立车队那样运作和管理。

    劳森明智的话应寻找退路

    劳森现在处境极佳，是本赛季的明星车手之一，但他应该已经开始考虑2028年的退路了。

    措洛夫明年将在旁等待机会，小红牛没有理由不会在2028年面临完全相同的“三选二”难题。

    这一次劳森是压倒性的合理人选，下一次他可能就不是了，而且鉴于红牛会把那些决定拖到赛季后期才做，他不想突然在2028年落得没有席位。

    所以他明智的做法是尝试效仿皮埃尔·加斯利和卡洛斯·塞恩斯的先例，在红牛认定你已无用之前另寻出路。

    我很欣赏这段职业生涯救赎的弧线。

    在我看来，劳森效力红牛一队始终显得格格不入，从官宣那一刻起就是如此。我确实没看到任何迹象表明他能解决车队的二号车手难题。

    红牛随后把事情搞砸了，这也没帮上忙，但那段不幸的两场比赛经历本可能成为劳森F1生涯走向终结的开端。

    然而，接下来发生的一切充分揭示了他的心理韧性——因为在过去20个月里他不得不消化许多事情，却始终镇定自若。

    为顶级车队驾驶是每位车手都渴望的，但这并非必须成为全部和终极目标；如果你没能做到，也并不会自动让你的职业生涯就此崩塌。

    劳森今年已经有力地证明了自己配得上F1的一席之地。因此，这次续约是唯一可以接受的结果。

    英文原文

    Liam Lawson and Arvid Lindblad will continue to race for Racing Bulls in 2027, with Red Bull electing against what would have been a huge Formula 1 driver sacking.

    Here's what our team makes of the move to keep Lawson and Lindblad and not promote Formula 2 title contender Nikola Tsolov into a race seat.

    An existential question has been answered

    Very few driver decisions have felt as right as Lawson getting a fresh contract with Racing Bulls.

    He has been superb this year. Yes, there are still some rough edges to smooth out, but he has been fast throughout, consistently strong, delivering technical input that his engineers have loved, and brilliantly able to shut out all the noise regarding the doubts over his future.

    Is it an outfit that, under Alan Permane, can stand on its own two feet, take its own decisions and plot a path that is only focused on making itself better?

    Or is it simply a puppet for sister squad Red Bull, a testing ground and training school for its future drivers, even if that means some short-term pain in rushing rookies through?

    After the season Lawson has had, it would have made no sense for Racing Bulls to drop him and throw away all the good momentum it has built up. So keeping him for now gives us the answer about what Racing Bulls is about.

    Tsolov has a bright future in F1, but time is on his side to make that step up.

    Pretty sure this is the best decision for Racing Bulls and in the future for Red Bull.

    Both Lawson and Lindblad - a rookie, let's not forget - have shown that they can bring home the results when everything goes as planned over a race weekend.

    Both are capable of being a replacement for Isack Hadjar or Max Verstappen if either of them slip on a banana skin. Red Bull knows what Lawson can bring to them if required, so he is a good measure of what Lindblad could potentially bring too.

    Having Tsolov in the background and doing a few FP1 sessions will prepare him for an introduction in 2028.

    As far as I'm concerned, it's the correct decision for all involved.

    If Racing Bulls is to be at all seen as an F1 team in its own right, with its own ambitions and dreams and what have you, rather than a pure Red Bull talent farm, then yeah, Lawson pretty much had to be kept on.

    Tsolov may end up faster down the line. For all we know, he may be faster already. But the kind of efficient scoring that Lawson has provided this year a rookie cannot match - I would say that's a rule in F1.

    So Tsolov-for-Lawson would be nakedly sacrificing Racing Bulls's present for the senior Red Bull team's future, and every decision like that makes it less and less palatable for Red Bull to own two teams in F1.

    Only if Racing Bulls has its own aspirations can it be allowed to continue, and a new deal for Lawson buys some credibility in my eyes.

    Marko would have dropped Lawson long ago

    At the height of his powers, Helmut Marko would have ditched Lawson ages ago.

    Lawson had his shot at the top team, didn't make it, and will likely not be given that chance again.

    Now Marko's no longer in charge, and this is the biggest indicator yet that Racing Bulls is not Toro Rosso; developing young drivers is no longer the only, or primary, end goal for this team.

    You can't blame Red Bull. Instead of a team that cost and lost money to run like Toro Rosso, Racing Bulls is now a multi-billion dollar entity. Constructors' championship finishing position is not the only definition of value in an F1 business anymore.

    Taking risks with a team built with the acceptance of losing money is one thing. Taking risks with a team worth billions is another.

    None of this is to criticise the decision it has made, as Lawson is a worthy F1 driver. It's just to point out yet more evidence that Racing Bulls is acting and being run like its own team.

    Lawson would be wise to find an exit route

    Lawson is in a fantastic position now and has been one of the star drivers of the season, but he should already be thinking about exit routes for 2028.

    Tsolov is going to be waiting in the wings next year, and there's no reason why Racing Bulls might be faced with the exact same 'three into two' dilemma for 2028.

    This time Lawson was the overwhelmingly logical pick, next time he might not be and, given Red Bull leaves those decisions until late in the season, he doesn't want to suddenly be left without a seat for 2028.

    So he'd be wise to try to follow the Pierre Gasly and Carlos Sainz playbook of finding another driver before Red Bull decides it's done with you.

    I've got a lot of time for this career redemption arc.

    Lawson at the Red Bull senior team always felt like an uncomfortable fit to me, right from when it was announced. I certainly hadn't seen anything to suggest he would solve the team's second-driver problem.

    Red Bull's bungling of what followed didn't help, but that ill-fated, two-race spell could've marked the beginning of the end of Lawson's F1 career.

    Instead, what's followed has revealed plenty about his mental resolve - because he's had to process a lot these past 20 months yet has been absolutely unflappable.

    Driving for the top teams is what every driver craves, but it doesn't have to be the be-all and end-all and, if you don't make it, it doesn't automatically consign you to career collapse.

    Lawson's proved emphatically this year that he deserves a place in F1. This contract extension was therefore the only acceptable outcome.
