    安东内利解释拉斯维加斯电量失误

    安东内利在2026年拉斯维加斯大奖赛中从第六位发车，本有望在最后一圈争夺胜利，但一次瞬间的踏板调整耗尽电池电量，最终无缘领奖台。

    安东内利解释拉斯维加斯失误：他被“两辆迈凯伦甩开”

    安东内利解释比赛后段一次电量回收失误如何让他错失拉斯维加斯大奖赛夺冠机会，并最终位列第四

    致梅赛德斯车迷。来自银箭车队的最新消息，包括F1新闻、车手动态、赛车研发以及来自布拉克利和布里克斯沃思的赛事分析。

    安东内利在2026年拉斯维加斯大奖赛中从第六位发车，本有望以最后一圈的戏剧性胜利为冲刺收官。然而，一次瞬间的踏板调整耗尽了他的电池电量——让马克斯·维斯塔潘获得喘息空间，也使这位梅赛德斯车手彻底跌出领奖台。

    周五排位赛后，安东内利因动力单元罚退五位而降至第六位发车，随后他采用中性胎换软胎的替代策略，重新杀回竞争行列。

    比赛后期，队友乔治·拉塞尔在5号弯锁死轮胎撞上护墙，安东内利成为维斯塔潘唯一剩下的威胁。然而进入最后一圈时，他的W17赛车在出最后一个减速弯时突然减速，让兰多·诺里斯（+0.639秒）和奥斯卡·皮亚斯特里（+1.529秒，从第17位发车）在1号弯前接连超越。

    安东内利以第四名冲线，落后维斯塔潘2.574秒，仅领先阿尔派车队的皮埃尔·加斯利0.353秒。

    赛后接受天空体育F1记者克雷格·斯莱特采访时，安东内利澄清，切换能量部署模式并非问题所在——他试图管理身后维斯塔潘的差距，反而打乱了赛车的能量回收循环。

    安东内利解释道：“不，我认为能量部署的调整是好的，因为如果我在14号弯前超越马克斯，那是在减速弯后保持领先的最佳选择。但因为我想要保留超车模式，我松了油门又重新给油以保持一点速度，这打乱了整个电池能量回收，我在减速弯时电量归零。”

    他补充道：“所以我在进入1号弯时损失了一秒多，不仅无法紧咬马克斯并给自己争取获胜的机会，还被两辆迈凯伦赛车超越，丢掉了领奖台。所以这大概是我的失误，但当时也非常困难，因为我认为自己是在做正确的事——保持在后并保留超车模式。”

    这个失误带有讽刺意味，因为安东内利周四还称赞梅赛德斯可能是进入对动力敏感的內华达站时，围场中电能管理最高效的赛车。然而在最后15圈中，梅赛德斯和红牛都不想在1.9公里长的长直道上过早领跑，引发了一场围绕超车模式的紧张猫鼠游戏。

    安东内利说：“我的目标是一直保持在后面直到最后，正如你所看到的，我和他之间有很多来回。我没有强行做任何事，只是试图保持在后面，保留超车模式，给自己在最后——尤其是最后两段直道——最好的机会。”

    尽管梅赛德斯离开拉斯维加斯时未能带走奖杯——安东内利第四、拉塞尔第九——这位20岁的车手依然享受了这场战术上的较量。“这是一场有趣的比赛；我们彼此之间都很接近，有很多来回拉锯，背后也有很多策略，”他说。“只是以这样的方式收尾，实在有些遗憾。”

    Kimi Antonelli explains mistake that left him “smoked by the two McLarens” in Vegas

    Kimi Antonelli explains how a late battery harvesting mistake cost him a Las Vegas GP win shot and left him fourth

    For Mercedes fans. All the latest from the Silver Arrows, with F1 news, driver updates, car development and race analysis from Brackley and Brixworth.

    Kimi Antonelli looked primed to cap a charge from sixth on the grid with a dramatic last-lap victory at the 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix. Instead, one split-second pedal adjustment drained his battery dry - handing Max Verstappen breathing room and dropping the Mercedes driver off the podium entirely.

    After topping Friday qualifying before a five-place power unit penalty relegated him to sixth, Antonelli used an alternative medium-to-soft tyre strategy to slice back into contention.

    When team-mate George Russell locked up into the Turn 5 barrier late in the race, Antonelli stood as Verstappen’s lone remaining threat. Yet heading onto the final lap, his W17 abruptly slowed exiting the final chicane, allowing Lando Norris (+0.639 seconds) and Oscar Piastri (+1.529s from 17th on the grid) to sweep past into Turn 1.

    Antonelli crossed the line fourth, 2.574s behind Verstappen and just 0.353s ahead of Alpine’s Pierre Gasly.

    Speaking afterwards to Sky Sports F1’s Craig Slater, Antonelli clarified that switching his energy deployment map was not to blame - his attempt to manage the gap behind Verstappen disrupted the car's regeneration cycle.

    "No, I think the change of deployment was good because it was the best option to then stay ahead after the chicane in case I was overtaking Max before Turn 14," Antonelli explained. "But because I wanted to keep the overtake mode, I lift off and reapply some throttle to keep a little bit of speed, and that messed up the whole battery harvesting, and I stayed in the chicane at zero.

    "So I lost more than a second going into Turn 1, and not only I couldn't stay close to Max and give myself a good shot for the win, but I got smoked by the two McLarens as well and lost the podium," he added. "So probably it was a mistake on my side, but in the moment it's also very difficult because I thought I was doing something right to stay behind and keep the overtake."

    The error carried an ironic twist after Antonelli spent Thursday praising Mercedes as likely the grid’s most efficient package in electrical energy management heading into power-sensitive Nevada. Across the final 15 laps, however, neither Mercedes nor Red Bull wanted to lead too early down the 1.9-kilometre Strip straight, triggering a tense game of cat and mouse over overtake mode.

    "My goal, my objective was to stay behind all the way to the end, and as you could see there was a lot of back and forth between me and him," Antonelli said. "I didn't force anything, and I was just trying to stay behind, keep the overtake to give myself the best shot at the end, especially for the last two straights."

    Even with Mercedes leaving Las Vegas without a trophy - Antonelli fourth and Russell ninth - the 20-year-old still enjoyed the tactical scrap. "It was a fun race; we were all close to each other, it was a lot of back and forth and a lot of strategy behind it," he said. "It was just a shame to finish off like this."
