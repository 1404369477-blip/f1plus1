    拉斯维加斯排位赛中那场“疯狂”的牵引效果实际价值

    在拉斯维加斯的一级方程式排位赛中，车手安东内利通过其他车辆的牵引获得了关键优势，尽管他最终因罚分无法从杆位发车，但在排位赛中他是最快的。牵引效果如此强大，以至于他在最后一圈几乎与林德布拉德的小红牛车队赛车并驾齐驱，最终仅落后0.6秒。安东内利承认，如果没有牵引，他可能无法取得这样的成绩。

    在拉斯维加斯的F1排位赛中，从另一辆车中获得牵引至关重要，没有什么能像基米·安东内利的最佳圈速那样，展示出‘疯狂’的收益。

    尽管安东内利由于赛道处罚无法从杆位发车，但在排位赛中他是最快的，尽管他‘对赛车没有感觉’并且Q3阶段的最后一圈比预想的更接近阿维德·林德布拉德的小红牛车队。

    然而，牵引效果如此强大，安东内利在林德布拉德启动圈速内两秒处起步，最终仅落后0.6秒，并在两条最长的直道上获得了惊人的尾流。

    他从中受益是显而易见的，安东内利也迅速承认了这一点：‘我以为我完蛋了，但这里的牵引太强大了，我能够仅仅通过它来提升成绩。’

    然而，他从麦拉伦车队老板安德烈亚·斯特拉所说的“一个非常极端的案例”中获得的程度令人印象深刻。

    牵引和没有牵引之间的差异很大，从0.5-1.0秒不等，具体取决于你比较的是谁的圈速。奥斯卡·皮亚斯蒂甚至说“如果你没有做到这一点，你甚至都不用出去”。

    维斯塔潘和拉塞尔在Q3的各自最佳圈速中，从领先他们三秒多的车辆那里获得了轻微的牵引，而兰多·诺里斯在跑出比队友皮亚斯蒂快两秒的圈速时，也得到了很好的牵引。

    但没有人能接近安东内利。当他通过整个赛道逼近林德布拉德时，安东内利在所有四个直道上都获得了强大的牵引和直线速度优势。

    这是一个他本不应该出现在的地方，如果不是因为他明智地退出了混乱的多车追逐战，争夺赛道位置。

    这使安东内利在计时器耗尽之前开始最后一圈的时间紧迫，也意味着他的轮胎准备受到了影响，但这种方式给了他意想不到的巨大回报。他主动出击，掌控了自己的局势，显然也有些运气在他得到回报的时候。

    当所有九辆车在最后一节Q3的比赛中几乎同时开始他们的圈数，安东内利排在最后——在赛道上，安东内利在Strip上大约有四辆车，每多一辆车在队列中都会带来一点额外的牵引力，这超出了林德布拉德的主要尾流。

    “牵引力是疯狂的，”领先安东内利的驾驶员皮埃尔·加斯利说。

    “如果你看看安东内利的最后一段，你就明白有多强大。我从未在任何一辆车上感受过这样的力量。”

    不出所料，安东内利在每一段直道上都比前四名快了0.9-1.4秒，但同时在每个弯道部分也是最慢的，刹车最早，最低速度慢了几公里每小时，总共损失了大约0.6-1.0秒在污染的空气中。

    与图表上的其他三人相比，他在The Strip上就比他们快了半秒以上，然后在16号弯到终点线的跑道上快了0.2-0.5秒（诺里斯的麦克拉伦在这里直道上特别慢，比安东内利慢20公里每小时）。

    他的最高速度优势由于每个人的引擎开始超级剪切并即使在全油门下也减速而略有减弱。

    这就是安东内利说自己是“仅靠最后一段赛道”才取得最快成绩的原因——这比队友乔治·拉塞尔的最佳成绩快了0.480秒，比马克斯·维斯塔潘最快的第三段赛道快了0.908秒。

    这超过了安东内利在第一、二段赛道上比维斯塔潘和兰多·诺里斯慢了大约六十分之一秒的劣势，在那里红牛和麦克拉伦比奔驰车队都快。

    维斯塔潘表示“在直线上的速度提升简直疯狂”并且说“我在Q3的第一圈几乎已经是最快了”——这简直不是被拖车辅助的安东内利的对手。尽管安东内利在通过弯道时在脏空气中行驶的负面影响方面做得很好，但这个布局在这一年中可以说是最友好的了。

    这本来可能是一个安东内利只排在第四快的排位赛，如果一切正常进行的话。然而，正如他所说：“那是一种你认为你完蛋了，但实际上却成功了的情况。”

    英文原文

    Getting a tow from another car was absolutely critical in Formula 1 qualifying in Las Vegas, with nothing demonstrating how “crazy” the gain could be like Kimi Antonelli’s session-topping lap.

    Though Antonelli will not actually start from pole due to a grid penalty, he was fastest in qualifying despite having “zero feeling with the car” and spending most of his final lap in Q3 much closer than intended to Arvid Lindblad’s Racing Bulls.

    So powerful was the tow effect, though, that Antonelli benefitted significantly overall by starting the lap within two seconds of Lindblad, finishing it barely 0.6s behind and getting a stunning slipstream down the two longest straights.

    That he benefited was obvious, and Antonelli was swift to acknowledge it: “I thought I was screwed but the tow is so big here that I was able to just gain with that.”

    However, the full extent of how he gained from what McLaren team boss Andrea Stella called a "really extreme case" of slipstreaming was remarkable to see.

    The difference between a tow and no tow was vast, anywhere from 0.5-1.0s depending on whose laps you compare. Oscar Piastri even said “you might as well not even go out if you don't get that right”.

    Verstappen and Russell were picking up slight tows from cars just over three seconds ahead on their respective best laps in Q3, while Lando Norris had a good tow from running within two seconds of team-mate Piastri on his fastest.

    But none of them came close to Antonelli. As he closed on Lindblad through the lap, Antonelli ended up with a strong tow and straightline speed advantage on all four straights.

    It was a place he would not have been in had he not had the good sense to back out of a messy, multi-car fight for track position on his outlap.

    That did put Antonelli in a race against time to start his final lap before the timer ran out on the session, and meant his tyre preparation was compromised, but it paid him back big time in a way Antonelli had not intended. He was proactive, took control of his situation, and there was obviously some fortune in how he was then rewarded.

    As all nine cars on their final Q3 runs started their laps within 17s of each other, and Antonelli was last in the queue – on the Strip Antonelli had four cars within about six seconds, and each extra car in the queue added a little more of a tow benefit on top of the primary slipstream from Lindblad.

    “The tow was insane,” said Pierre Gasly, who was one of the drivers ahead of Antonelli.

    “If you look at the last sector of Antonelli, you just understand how powerful. I've never, ever felt that in a car.”

    Unsurprisingly, Antonelli was the quickest of the top four on every straight by an overall gain of 0.9-1.4s depending on the comparison, but the slowest through every corner section at the same time, braking earliest and running a few km/h slower minimum speed totalling a loss of around 0.6-1.0s in the dirty air.

    He seemed to gain over half a second down The Strip alone compared to the other three on the graph above, then between 0.2s and 0.5s on the run out of Turn 16 to the finish line (Norris’s McLaren being particularly slow on the straight here and 20km/h down on Antonelli).

    His sheer top speed advantage was augmented by a slightly reduced effect as everyone’s engines began superclipping and slowing down even while on full throttle.

    That’s why Antonelli said qualifying fastest was “only thanks to the final sector” – which was 0.480s quicker than team-mate George Russell’s best and a mammoth 0.908s clear of Max Verstappen’s fastest sector three.

    This more than offset the six tenths or so Antonelli lost to Verstappen and Lando Norris in the first two sectors, where the Red Bull and McLaren were faster than both Mercedes.

    Verstappen said “it's just insane how much you gain on the straights” and that “my first lap in Q3 was pretty much as good as it can be” – it was just no match for a tow-assisted Antonelli. And though Antonelli did a good job to mitigate the negative effect of running in dirty air through the corners, this layout is about as friendly to that as any could be all year.

    This could easily have been a qualifying session in which Antonelli was only fourth-fastest had it played out normally. Instead, as he said: “It was just one of those times where you think you're screwed, but actually it works out.”
