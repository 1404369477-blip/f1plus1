    瓦塞尔怒斥法拉利车手拉斯维加斯再冲突

    法拉利车队领队瓦塞尔称，勒克莱尔在拉斯维加斯大奖赛上与汉密尔顿的接触“不可接受”，并表现出“对车队缺乏尊重”；距墨西哥站的争执仅三周，车队将再次召开危机会议。

    弗雷德·瓦塞尔怒斥法拉利最新一次“不职业”的冲突：“这和墨西哥站是同一套说辞”

    查尔斯·勒克莱尔与刘易斯·汉密尔顿在墨西哥站发生冲突仅两场比赛之后，法拉利车队领队弗雷德·瓦塞尔在拉斯维加斯双方再度爆发冲突时已忍无可忍

    查尔斯·勒克莱尔，法拉利，刘易斯·汉密尔顿，法拉利

    法拉利车队领队弗雷德·瓦塞尔表示，查尔斯·勒克莱尔在拉斯维加斯大奖赛上与刘易斯·汉密尔顿的接触“不可接受”，并表现出“对车队缺乏尊重”；距两人在墨西哥站的争执仅三周，这支F1车队将再次召开危机会议。

    瓦塞尔不得不与勒克莱尔和汉密尔顿就他们在墨西哥代价高昂的小摩擦进行谈话，而在拉斯维加斯街道赛第38圈他的两辆赛车再次纠缠后，他还将再次这样做，其中勒克莱尔因在前翼悬挂在汉密尔顿内侧而受到指责，导致这位英国车手打转。

    勒克莱尔因这次碰撞被罚10秒，而汉密尔顿则跌至第10位，最终以第8名冲过终点线。在罚时被加到比赛时间后，勒克莱尔被列为第10名。

    在墨西哥之后如此短的时间内，想到他的车手再次纠缠以及可能需要进行新的谈话来澄清气氛，这让瓦塞尔感到厌倦。

    “查尔斯，比赛结束后我要见你，在其他任何事情之前，”瓦塞尔在冷却圈期间通过车队无线电说道。

    当被问及那次讨论是什么样子时，瓦塞尔意味深长地回答说：“那不是讨论。是我在说话。”

    “我认为他道歉了，他明白了，但结果就摆在那里。你犯了错，你说：‘我的错，对不起。’但你[仍然]犯了错。”

    他补充说：“这是不可接受的。这是对车队、对赞助商的不尊重，和墨西哥站之后说的话一样。我认为我们在圣保罗进行了明确的讨论。今天这不够职业。我认为这可能是紧张局势的升级。”

    在汉密尔顿提出投诉后，法拉利已经就勒克莱尔挤压这位英国车手的激烈方式与他进行了沟通。拉斯维加斯类似椭圆赛道的尾流竞速赛要求车手在穿越车阵时具备一定程度的信任和团队合作，因为互不相让也不是选项。这场比赛成了一个痛苦的提醒：勒克莱尔和汉密尔顿之间缺乏信任。

    “我们在比赛中给夏尔传递了信息，让他在以正确方式竞争时考虑车队，但在这种比赛中你无法冻结位置，因为你处在尾流中，”瓦塞尔解释道。“如果你减速，你身后的人就会超越你。而所有其他车队在比赛中能够互相超越20次。你无法想象我的沮丧，但对家里所有人来说更重要。”

    当被问及现在是否是时候制定更严格的指导方针或规则时，他说：“这不是规则的问题。除了必须尊重你的队友之外，你还能做什么不同的事情？在这种比赛中，你必须超越你前面的车。你不能留在后面。这完全无法管理。你只需要在是你的队友时以尊重的方式去做，承担更少的风险。他们只需要以专业的方式行事。”

    刚刚在墨西哥和巴西之间进行了这样的谈话，目前还不完全清楚瓦塞尔现在能做什么不同的事情，这种想法让这位法国人感到无能为力。

    “这就是为什么我想明天再次进行讨论，”他补充道。“而且，也要试着消化这个情况。我认为比赛后两分钟不是和他们讨论的合适地点。我看到夏尔有点低落。我告诉了他我必须说的话。我明天必须和他讨论这件事。”

    Fred Vasseur furious with latest "unprofessional" Ferrari clash: "It's the same speech as Mexico"

    Just two races after Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton clashed in Mexico, Ferrari team boss Fred Vasseur was at the end of his tether when hostilities broke out again in Las Vegas

    Charles Leclerc, Ferrari, Lewis Hamilton, Ferrari

    Ferrari team boss Fred Vasseur says Charles Leclerc's contact with Lewis Hamilton in the Las Vegas Grand Prix was "unacceptable" and showed "lack of respect for the team", with the Formula 1 team set for fresh crisis talks just three weeks after their spat in Mexico.

    Vasseur had to hold talks with both Leclerc and Hamilton for their costly Mexico skirmish and will do so again after his two cars tangled on the streets of Las Vegas on lap 38, with Leclerc to blame for leaving his front wing dangling on the inside of Hamilton, sending the Briton into a spin.

    Leclerc received a 10-second penalty for the collision, while Hamilton dropped to 10th and eventually crossed the line in eighth. Leclerc was classified in 10th place after the penalty had been added to his race time.

    Coming so soon after Mexico, the thought of his drivers tangling again and the prospect of fresh talks to clear the air had worn thin on Vasseur.

    "Charles, I want to see you at the end of the race, before anything else," Vasseur said on the team radio during the cooldown lap.

    When asked what that discussion was like, Vasseur tellingly replied: "It was not a discussion. I spoke.

    "I think he apologised, he understood, but the result is there. You make a mistake, you say: 'My mistake, I'm sorry'. But you [still] made a mistake."

    He added: "It's unacceptable. It's a lack of respect for the team, for the sponsors, the same speech as after Mexico. I think we had a clear discussion in Sao Paulo. Today it wasn't professional. I think it's probably the escalation of tension."

    Following complaints from Hamilton, Ferrari had already been in touch with Leclerc about the aggressive way he was squeezing the Briton. Las Vegas' oval-like slipstreaming race required a degree of trust and teamwork for drivers to move through the pack of cars, as not racing each other was not an option either. The race became a painful reminder that trust between Leclerc and Hamilton is absent.

    "We passed the message to Charles during the race to think about the team when he was fighting in the right way, but you can't freeze the position on this kind of race, because you are in the slipstream," Vasseur explained. "If you slow down, the guy who is behind you is overtaking you. And all the other teams were able to overtake each other 20 times during the race. You can't imagine the frustration for me, but more important for all the guys at home."

    When asked if it's now time for tougher guidelines or rules, he said: "It's not a matter of rules. What can you do differently, except that you have to respect your team-mates. In this kind of race, you have to overtake the car in front of you. You can't stay behind. It's completely unmanageable. You just have to do it with respect when it's your team-mate, to take less risks. They just have to act in a professional way."

    Having just had this very talk between Mexico and Brazil, it is not entirely clear what Vasseur can now do differently, a thought which has left the Frenchman feeling powerless.

    "That's why I want to once again have a discussion tomorrow," he added. "And also, to try to digest the situation. I think it's not the right place to discuss with them two minutes after the race. I saw Charles a bit down. And I told him what I have to say. I've got to discuss it with him tomorrow."
