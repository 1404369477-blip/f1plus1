    After failing to claim a victory in the first 10 grands prix of the season, the reigning constructors' champions have come into form, with Lando Norris claiming three pole positions at the last four races and only being denied a third win by the unfortunate timing of a virtual safety car last time out in Madrid.

    The team's official preview for this weekend's race in Baku, which takes place on Saturday rather than the usual Sunday slot, revealed there will be new parts, and that their rotating 'H-Wing' will return to the car after not being used in Madrid.

    Norris lost the lead of the Spanish GP after an unlucky sequence of events which saw a missed a pit-stop opportunity under a VSC, followed by a very slow stop

    "As part of the team's continued journey throughout the 2026 season, the team will bring to Baku a set of upgrades aimed to improve the aerodynamic performance of the MCL40," the team said.

    "Additionally the H-Wing, that debuted in Monza, will be back in action on a track where the drag reduction can be a factor.

    "As has been the approach in previous races this season, all new parts will be evaluated throughout practice before the team makes final decisions regarding the MCL40's Qualifying and Grand Prix setup for the weekend."

    McLaren chief technical officer and chief designer Rob Marshall explained the team are keen to continue their development push despite their title hopes being extremely thin, with Kimi Antonelli dominating the Drivers' Championship and his Mercedes team well clear in the constructors' standings.

    "With regulatory continuity into next season and the competition at the front so close, our motivation to keep developing the car remains high," Marshall said.

    "We must balance that with work on next year's car, but our immediate focus is on delivering more performance and fighting for more victories in 2026, while carrying forward everything we learn."

    The Baku circuit, which features the longest straight on the F1 calendar alongside several slow corners, creates a challenge that Marshall expects to create entertaining competition between the top teams.

    "Baku will provide another valuable test of the MCL40 across a very different range of characteristics," Marshall added.
