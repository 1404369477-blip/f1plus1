    梅赛德斯为乔治·拉塞尔在拉斯维加斯的拖车计划失败

    拉斯维加斯大奖赛排位赛中，梅赛德斯车队的拖车计划未能成功，乔治·拉塞尔在对抗马克斯·维斯塔潘的比赛中需要更好的发车位。

    为什么梅赛德斯为乔治·拉塞尔制定的拖车计划在拉斯维加斯失败了

    基米·安东内利找到了完美的尾流并取得了最快成绩，而他的队友却没有。这就是拖车游戏如何塑造了拉斯维加斯赛道上的排位赛

    对于梅赛德斯车迷来说，这里有关于银箭的最新消息，包括一级方程式新闻、车手更新、赛车发展和从布拉克利和布里克斯沃斯进行的比赛分析

    拉斯维加斯大奖赛的排位赛不仅取决于实际的赛车和车手表现，也取决于尾流，这一点在观察梅赛德斯车队时非常明显。

    基米·安东内利在终点线以1分33.893秒的速度最快，但因为引擎更换而受到五位的发车位置罚分。乔治·拉塞尔在争夺车手锦标赛第二名的战斗中需要更好的发车位，他排在第三位，错过了他想要的拖车。

    为什么拖车在这里如此重要？

    霓虹灯照亮的拉斯维加斯赛道以长直道为主，车手们表示，由于需要穿过冷而密集的空气，今年跟随其他车辆的效果是他们从未见过的。

    ‘说实话，滑流效应比我们之前在任何类别中经历的都要极端，’拉塞尔在排位赛后说。

    维斯塔潘，谁在第二阶段起跑时占据杆位，表示拖车帮助他在一圈中获得了“大约七到八十分之一秒的时间，但实际上在弯道中并没有获得那么多时间。”

    拉塞尔表示，他在最后一部分赛道中仅对安东内利失去了七十分之一秒，而对维斯塔潘则失去了近一秒的时间。

    兰多·诺里斯解释了为什么单次拖车仍然是一种赌博：“你不仅仅是因为它而获得优势，因为在你超过前车后，你与前面车辆的距离会更大。所以，最好是跟随很多人的尾流在车队中。”

    两辆梅赛德斯之间发生了什么？

    安东内利在第五弯道的准备圈中减速，当时车辆并排三行，看起来就像我们周六晚上会看到的比赛。他坚称这是他自己的决定。

    "关于第五弯道，完全是我自己的责任，" 安东内利说。"我实际上并不清楚我的时间控制有多紧。我可能本可以做得不同，但事后看来，结果还是不错的。

    他开始最后一圈时落后前方车辆大约1.5秒，并承认他之所以处于领先位置，"完全是多亏了最后一段赛道"。他还说，梅赛德斯在排位赛中"总是被安排在前面"。

    安东内利，梅赛德斯，拉塞尔，梅赛德斯

    拉塞尔解释了为什么计划如此困难。

    “因为每个人都想要这么做，所以非常难以协调。而且，一半的赛车在做准备圈，另一半的赛车没有，这也使得情况变得更加困难，”他说。

    “如果你可以选择，你会在九辆车的一组中排在最后，如果完美选择的话，大约有三秒的差距。但这是每个人都追求的目标。所以这有点像2019年的蒙扎。”

    他还指出安东内利差点就完全错过了：“如果他晚开始一圈一秒，他就无法完成这一圈。”

    问他是否想要队友的帮助，拉塞尔说：'我们会私下讨论这个问题，我们可以谈谈。' 安东内利也表示同意：'正如乔治所说，我们会讨论这个问题。'

    梅赛德斯车队老板托托·沃尔夫表示，拉塞尔'处于完美的位置'，比前面的车慢四秒。天空体育评论员雅克·维伦纽夫不同意，坚持认为'车队没有帮助他'，并称安东内利的做法'有点恶劣。'

    还有其他人被坑了吗？

    伊萨克·哈德杰承认红牛'在最后一圈看起来有点愚蠢'，而法拉利车队完全没有帮助其车手。查尔斯·勒克莱尔表示，保持轮胎温度是首要任务。

    对这场比赛意味着什么？

    拉塞尔与维斯塔潘一起站在发车区的第一排，维斯塔潘在车手积分榜上领先他17分。在这种强大的拖动下，没有人期望能够逃脱。

    安东内利说：“如果蒙扎很困难，那么在这里可能就是不可能的。””，拉塞尔也表示同意：“只要在顶尖的七位之内，你就有机会竞争。”

    Why Mercedes' tow plan for George Russell fell apart in Las Vegas

    Kimi Antonelli found the perfect slipstream and went fastest, while his team-mate was left without one. Here is how the tow game shaped qualifying on the Strip

    For Mercedes fans. All the latest from the Silver Arrows, with F1 news, driver updates, car development and race analysis from Brackley and Brixworth.

    Qualifying for the Las Vegas Grand Prix was decided as much by slipstreams as it was by actual car and driver performance, and this was very clear when looking at Mercedes.

    Kimi Antonelli went fastest with a 1m33.893s at the chequered flag, but takes a five-place grid penalty for an engine change. George Russell, who needed the better grid slot in his fight with Max Verstappen for second in the championship, was third and missed out on the tow he wanted.

    Why does a tow matter so much here?

    The neon-lit Las Vegas circuit is dominated by long straights, and the drivers say the effect of following another car this year is like nothing they have seen thanks to the cold and dense air the cars need to push through.

    "The slipstreams are more extreme than I think we've ever experienced in any category, to be honest," Russell said after qualifying.

    Verstappen, who starts on pole, said a tow in Q2 gained him "like seven or eight tenths on the lap while not actually gaining that much in the corner".

    Russell said he lost seven tenths to Antonelli in the final sector alone, and almost a second to Verstappen.

    Lando Norris explained why a single tow can still be a gamble: "You don't just gain from it because then you have a bigger gap to the car ahead after you pass. So it's better just to be in the pack in a slipstream from a lot of people."

    What happened between the two Mercedes?

    Antonelli backed off on his preparation lap at Turn 5, with cars running three wide in what looked like something we would be watching on Saturday night. He insisted that was his own decision.

    "About Turn 5 was fully on me," Antonelli said. "I didn't really know how tight I was with timing. I probably would have done differently, but in hindsight it still worked out."

    He started his final lap around 1.5 seconds behind the car in front, and admitted he was on top "only thanks to the final sector". He also said Mercedes had "been sent always at the front in qualifying".

    Andrea Kimi Antonelli, Mercedes, George Russell, Mercedes

    Russell explained why it was so hard to plan.

    "It's very difficult to orchestrate because everybody's going for it. And also the fact that half the cars were doing prep laps and half the cars were not also made it difficult," he said.

    "If you could choose, you would be at the back of a pack of nine cars with around a three-second gap, if you could perfectly choose. But that's what everyone's chasing. So it's kind of like Monza 2019."

    He also pointed out how close Antonelli came to missing out altogether: "If he started the lap a second later, he wouldn't have got the lap in."

    Asked whether he had wanted help from his team-mate, Russell said: "We'll take it offline and we can talk about it." Antonelli agreed: "As George said, we will talk about it."

    Mercedes boss Toto Wolff said Russell "had the perfect position", four seconds behind the car in front. Sky pundit Jacques Villeneuve disagreed, insisting "the team did not help him" and calling Antonelli's approach "a little bit nasty".

    Did anyone else get caught out?

    Isack Hadjar admitted Red Bull "looked a bit stupid out there for the final run", while Ferrari failed to tow its drivers whatsoever. Charles Leclerc said keeping heat in the tyres came first.

    What does it mean for the race?

    Russell starts on the front row alongside Verstappen, who is 17 points ahead of him in the drivers' standings. With the tow this strong, nobody expects to escape.

    "If Monza was difficult, here it's going to be probably impossible," Antonelli said of pulling away. Russell agreed: "Anywhere in the top seven positions and you're in the fight."
