    拉斯维加斯湿地练习：F1车队收获有限

    拉斯维加斯大奖赛周末以湿滑练习赛开场，车队和车手未能获得预期测试数据，但有限的反馈仍揭示了当前的一些限制。

    拉斯维加斯大奖赛周末以（非常）湿滑的练习赛开场，这并非F1车队或车手所期望的，而本周末其余时间预计将是干燥天气。

    尽管如此，缺乏跑圈和有限的反馈仍让我们了解到当前的一些限制，以及第二次练习赛排名中的一些意外结果在多大程度上可能代表后续走势。

    周五首次练习赛前所未有地没有出现任何计时圈，这令人遗憾地反映出拉斯维加斯今年在湿地条件下变得更糟。

    拉斯维加斯大道赛道过去就已因低温、低抓地力和高速布局而成为非常棘手的挑战，而在湿地条件下，光滑的表面意味着水容易积聚其上并引发水滑现象。

    但哈斯车队车手奥利·贝尔曼表示，这已经变成了“灾难的配方”，因为2026款赛车抓地力低下，而且湿地胎和半雨胎都太硬、难以升温——这就是为什么几乎没人在第一节练习中尝试开始飞驰圈。

    多支车队反馈，无论如何都无法让轮胎升温——以至于倍耐力甚至提高了车队在第二节练习中可用的轮胎加温毯温度，试图在条件相似的情况下让第二节练习多跑一些。

    新秀阿尔维德·林德布拉德首次体验拉斯维加斯，他说“即使在低速弯以100公里/小时行驶也真的很吓人，四个轮子都在打滑，不知道会不会撞墙”，并补充道：“我能最接近地类比的是在冰面上开拉力赛车对比一级方程式赛车。”

    而梅赛德斯车队在第二节练习中跑出最快圈速的乔治·拉塞尔表示，2026款赛车的一个特定特性——在直道上同时打开前后翼的主动空气动力学系统——与拉斯维加斯的长直道很不匹配，因为这意味着轮胎在弯道之间又会变得太冷。

    “由于到处都是慢速弯区域，轮胎上没有任何垂直载荷，所以轮胎在直道上比过去三年降温幅度大得多，”他说。“而且它们本来就已经很凉了。”

    第一节练习的条件太差，小红牛车队领队艾伦·珀曼说“没有任何可学的东西，连安装圈都算不上”。

    第二节练习在里程方面好得多，车手们合计完成了400多圈，但实际上这节练习也没什么用。珀曼称这只是在把冷却调整到正确窗口的练习，否则“我认为没有任何真正有意义的东西可学”。

    预计周末剩余时间将是干燥的，所以即使雨早早停了，车手们也没有获得有价值的经验。湿地赛道太危险，据贝尔曼说，尽管90%的赛道是干的，光头胎根本不可行——如果在其他任何赛道这么湿，车队会换上光头胎并跑得“快得多得多”。

    其他人也有同感。阿斯顿·马丁车队的佩德罗·德·拉·罗萨表示，“你学到的东西显然无关紧要”，法拉利副领队杰罗姆·丹布罗西奥称周五的练习“会有点像是重新开始”，而迈凯伦车手奥斯卡·皮亚斯特里则表示，第二节自由练习“对本周剩余时间来说绝对不太相关”。

    这让周六和周日变成了一种迷你冲刺赛周末，因为在这种赛制下，车队在确定排位赛之前能跑的赛道时间非常少——这至少带来了一个有趣的看点：有些车队可能会判断失误，从而可能导致名次出现混乱。

    阿尔派车队没有人把皮埃尔·加斯利在练习赛中拿到第二名——距离第一名仅0.071秒——特别当回事。虽然那是一个好看的飞驰圈，在一个不寻常的日子里看到一支弱队排名如此之高通常也令人愉快，但这个成绩更多是因为在逐渐变干的赛道上把握住了时机，而非其他因素。

    但即使这已经是本周末阿尔派在成绩表上能取得的最高位置，车队仍然相信拉斯维加斯将是其近期低迷状态结束的地方。

    “我们刚刚经历了相当糟糕的六场比赛——六场比赛里我们只拿到一分，”董事总经理史蒂夫·尼尔森承认道。

    “拉斯维加斯是我们认为并希望会适合我们的赛道。所以我们期待明天和比赛日的干地运行，我们抱有希望。”

    阿尔派的常规主要竞争对手小红牛车队也有同样的预期。

    “我预计他们会很快，”佩尔曼说。

    “他们在蒙扎很快[加斯利在干地情况下拿到令人震惊的杆位]，在巴库也很快，而这是一条非常相似的赛道。”

    但佩尔曼补充说“自那两场比赛以来，我们确实取得了一些进步”。

    而在阿尔派恩“糟糕”的那段时间里，小红牛在争夺车队第五名的战斗中从仅领先九分，变成了在还剩三场比赛时稳握45分优势。

    不少车队都带来了升级，即便只是小升级，在赛季如此深入、又在拉斯维加斯这样独特的赛道上——但这些部件也成了条件的牺牲品。

    迈凯伦认为在兰多·诺里斯的赛车上冒险使用新底板并不明智，红牛则选择不测试前翼升级。两队都将在预计为干地的第三次练习赛中再次尝试。

    小红牛在前翼、底板边缘和后角进行了调整，但佩尔曼表示“在这些条件下我们无法评估它们”。

    真正获得一些有用里程的是升级后的梅赛德斯涡轮增压器，它正在多辆赛车上运行：基米·安东内利的厂队赛车以及所有客户车队。

    “对我们来说最重要的是新引擎，”威廉姆斯车手亚历克斯·阿尔本说道。

    更多是校准设置，而不是其他任何东西。但看起来不错。看起来就像他们所说的那样。

    尽管周五的价值如此之低，但值得注意的是，马克斯·维斯塔潘在第二次练习赛中只需要九圈就对红牛赛车感到不满。

    在最初抱怨引擎升温时的动力响应后，维斯塔潘很快对赛车本身更加担忧。

    技术总监皮埃尔·瓦什表示红牛有“大量工作要做”，并且马克斯“不太高兴”（尽管伊萨克·哈贾尔很高兴）。

    “关键在于赛车平衡，”瓦赫说道。“他对赛车的表现并不满意。”

    “我们正在努力弄清楚问题出在哪里。”

    在阿斯顿·马丁车队周五分别获得第10名和第11名之后，费尔南多·阿隆索是否想借机强调雨天是均衡器，并展望一番爆冷击败强敌的可能性？

    不。他完全没这个意思。

    他说：“今天并不是很有代表性的一天。我认为有一半的车手都没有跑任何圈数。所以这就是为什么我们的排名会稍微高一些。”

    阿隆索觉得拉斯维加斯直道的长度意味着即便是下雨也弥补不了本田的短板，他说“差距相当大”。

    他计划利用周五剩余的练习时间来研究正赛长距离的轮胎策略，并考虑到可能出现的颗粒化问题，因为他已经预料到自己在排位赛或起步阶段取得的任何成果都会在直道上迅速被抵消。

    阿隆索说：“在第一圈我们无论如何都会丢掉大部分位置。所以我认为我们需要把注意力集中在轮胎上。”

    英文原文

    A day of (very) wet running at the start of a Las Vegas Grand Prix weekend that's otherwise expected to be dry wasn't really what Formula 1 teams or drivers were looking for.

    Still, the lack of running and limited feedback told us plenty about some current limitations - and just how representative some surprises in the FP2 order are likely to be of what follows.

    An unprecedented absence of timed laps in Friday's first practice session was a sad reflection of how Las Vegas has been made even worse in the wet this year.

    The Las Vegas Strip circuit was already a very tricky challenge in the past due to low temperatures and a low-grip, high-speed track layout, and in the wet the smooth surface means water pools easily on top of it and causes aquaplaning.

    But it's become a "recipe for disaster", Haas driver Ollie Bearman said, with the low-grip 2026 cars and both wet and intermediate tyres that are so stiff and hard to warm up - which is why hardly anybody even attempted to start a flying lap in FP1.

    Several teams reported that there was nothing that could be done for generating tyre temperature - so much so that Pirelli even bumped up the blanket temperatures teams could have for FP2, to try to get a bit more running in the second session in case conditions were similar.

    Rookie Arvid Lindblad, experiencing Vegas for the first time, said it was "honestly scary to drive even at 100km/h in a low-speed corner, just four wheels sliding, not knowing if you're going to go into a wall" and added: "The closest I can relate to it was driving a rally car on ice versus Formula 1."

    And Mercedes' FP2 pacesetter George Russell said one specific 2026 feature - the active aero that opens both front and rear wings on the straights - is a bad match for Vegas's long straights because it means the tyres get too cold again between corners.

    "With the SM zones everywhere, you're not putting any vertical load in the tyre, so the tyres are getting substantially cooler in the straight than they have been in the last three years," he said. "And they were already cool."

    Conditions were so poor in FP1 that Racing Bulls team principal Alan Permane said "there wasn't anything to learn, not even an installation lap".

    FP2 was much better in terms of mileage as the drivers managed more than 400 laps between them, but actually this session wasn't much use either. Permane called it an exercise in getting the cooling in the right window, otherwise "I don't think there was anything really meaningful to learn".

    The rest of the weekend is expected to be dry so there were no valuable lessons for the drivers even though the rain stopped early. The track was so treacherous in the wet that, according to Bearman, slicks were simply not viable even though 90% of the circuit was dry - and that if it was that wet on any other track, teams would bolt on slicks and go "much, much quicker".

    Others felt the same. Aston Martin's Pedro de la Rosa said it was "obviously irrelevant what you learned", Ferrari deputy team principal Jerome d'Ambrosio said Friday's running "will be a bit of a reset", and McLaren driver Oscar Piastri said FP2 was "definitely not super-relevant for the rest of the week".

    It turns Saturday and Sunday into something of a mini-sprint weekend, given how little track running there is in that format before teams commit to qualifying - which at least raises the fun prospect of some getting it wrong and there being the potential for a mixed-up order as a result.

    No one at Alpine was taking Pierre Gasly's second place in practice - just 0.071 seconds from first - particularly seriously. While it was a fun lap to watch and generally nice to see an underdog that high up on an unusual day, the result was more about timing on a drying track than anything else.

    But even if that's as high as it will appear on a results sheet this weekend, Alpine's still confident Vegas is going to be the place its recent slump ends.

    "We've come off the back of a fairly dire six races - we've got one point in six races," managing director Steve Nielsen acknowledged.

    "Vegas is the track we think and hope will suit us. And so we're looking forward to dry running tomorrow and race day and we're hopeful."

    Alpine's usual main rival Racing Bulls has the same expectation.

    "I expect them to be quick," said Permane.

    "They were quick in Monza [where Gasly took a shock pole in the dry], they were quick in Baku, and this is a very similar kind of track."

    But Permane added "we've certainly made some steps since those two races".

    And during Alpine's "dire" spell, Racing Bulls has gone from being just nine points ahead in their battle for fifth in the constructors' championship to a surely-safe 45 points clear with three races left.

    A decent number of teams have brought upgrades, even if they are just minor ones, this deep into the season on a track as unique as Vegas - but these parts were victims of the conditions too.

    McLaren didn’t feel it was sensible to risk running a new floor on Lando Norris's car, and Red Bull opted against trialling a front wing update. Both will try again in FP3 when it is meant to be dry.

    Racing Bulls had tweaks across the front wing, floor board and rear corner but Permane said "we can't assess them in these conditions".

    What did get some useful mileages was the upgraded Mercedes turbocharger that's running in several cars: Kimi Antonelli's works machine plus all the customer teams.

    "The main thing for us was the new engine," said Williams driver Alex Albon.

    "More calibration settings than anything else. But it looks good. It looks like what they said it would do."

    Though Friday was of so little value, it was telling that Max Verstappen only needed nine laps in FP2 to be unhappy with Red Bull's car.

    Having initially complained about the power response while the engine came up to temperature, Verstappen quickly became more concerned by the car itself.

    Technical director Pierre Wache said Red Bull has a "lot of work to do" and that Max was "not very happy" (although Isack Hadjar was).

    "It's about the car balance," said Wache. "He was not happy with how the car behaved.

    "We are trying to understand where it's coming from."

    Did Fernando Alonso fancy a chance to talk up the rain being a leveller and the prospect of some giant-killing after the Aston Martins finished Friday 10th and 11th?

    No. He really didn't.

    "It was a not very representative day," he said. "I think half of the grid didn't put any laps in. So that's why we are a little bit higher."

    Alonso felt the length of the Las Vegas straights meant even rain wouldn't make up for Honda's shortcomings, saying "the deficit was quite big".

    He's planning to spend Friday's remaining practice session figuring out tyre tactics for long runs in the race, with potential graining in mind, as he's already expecting anything he achieves in qualifying or at the start to be swiftly negated on the straights.

    "In lap one we will lose most of the places anyway. So I think we need to concentrate on the tyres," Alonso said.
