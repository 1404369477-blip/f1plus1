    拉塞尔称因过于坦诚而受罚

    拉塞尔表示，与维斯塔潘争执的最大教训是他在面对F1媒体时“过于开放和坦诚”。

    乔治·拉塞尔表示，与马克斯·维斯塔潘争执给他带来的最大教训是，他在面对F1媒体时“过于开放和坦诚”。

    拉塞尔在上周末拉斯维加斯练习赛中两人发生冲突后批评了维斯塔潘，这促使这位梅赛德斯车手宣称：“我不会就这样接受有人这样算计我，然后还像他们比所有人都更大更好一样四处走动。”

    这不是拉塞尔第一次感到有必要公开对维斯塔潘采取强硬立场。两年前在阿布扎比，他说维斯塔潘“带着不必要的愤怒和近乎暴力的情绪发飙”，并指责他是霸凌者，此后两人之间还有各种其他赛道内外的小冲突。

    不过在拉斯维加斯，分站冠军维斯塔潘确实笑到了最后，他嘲讽拉塞尔撞上护栏——维斯塔潘从后视镜中看到这一幕，立即在车队无线电中做出反应，随后又在多次采访中加倍强调。

    他称拉塞尔远低于自己的水平，并暗讽拉塞尔在赛道外话太多，而在卡塔尔周四的媒体活动中，拉塞尔不可避免地被问到了这件事。

    拉塞尔很平静，他说自己觉得这些评论“挺好笑”，而且鉴于维斯塔潘赢了而拉塞尔没赢，他“完全可以说自己想说的话”——他在两个回答中表达了大致相同的意思，表明他明确希望把这件事翻篇，不再多说什么。

    此前，天空体育的乔什·萨蒂尔问他，他对拉斯维加斯那起事件的评论，是否是他注意到维斯塔潘今年其他所作所为累积的结果。

    拉塞尔停顿了很长时间，权衡如何回答，最终选择解释为什么如果是任何一位车手，他在赛道上也会有同样的反应——不过也许能说明问题的是，他强调了“在赛道上”这部分。

    最有趣的是，也是他所谓的“新教训”，是拉塞尔两次偏离话题，暗示他现在需要更加注意自己在媒体上的言辞。

    当被问及是否想回击维斯塔潘的言论时，拉塞尔说：“我想赢。就这么简单。这不是赛道外的口水战。也许我过去在与你们交流时过于坦率和诚实，这是我的错。

    “我觉得我和所有媒体及记者都保持着良好的关系，但有时你会忘记，你并不是在跟一个人说话。我们现在在对话，而你忘了，实际上，我并不是真的在跟你说话。我是在对着电视上的数百万人说话。

    “所以那种良好的关系实际上可能稍微妨碍了我，我想，这就是我们生活的这个世界里的人生教训。”

    这甚至导致他突然提起了两年前发生的事情，当时作为他在2024年阿布扎比站关于维斯塔潘言论的一部分，拉塞尔在常规转播活动中接受了情绪激动的电视采访。

    拉塞尔当天还与文字媒体进行了一次即兴简报，其中大部分内容都被用来强调他关于维斯塔潘的信息。

    拉塞尔与天空体育F1的泰德·克拉维茨以及前F1电视主持人威尔·巴克斯顿的采访，后来被相关转播方描述为是由拉塞尔本人主动促成的。

    克拉维茨在书中写道，拉塞尔告诉他：“我要把话还给马克斯。我受够他在媒体上说我的坏话了，我要揭穿他的霸凌手段。我知道你们只应该问我一个问题，但别管那个了，你想问多少问题我都愿意。”

    在本周的《Up To Speed》播客中，布克斯顿表示，在谈到维斯塔潘时，拉塞尔“全是嘴皮子，没有裤子”（光说不练），并讲述了他自己版本的2024年阿布扎比采访，布克斯顿声称拉塞尔说过“你能不能就问我关于马克斯的事？”并且“明显准备了些什么”。

    然而，拉塞尔对这些交锋被转述的方式提出了反驳。

    在回答关于维斯塔潘的来回交锋如何“完全可以避免”且“有点不必要”时，拉塞尔表示“从这些情况中是可以吸取教训的”，并重申“最大的”教训是“也许我对媒体太过开放和诚实了”。

    “我记得几年前在阿布扎比，当我和[泰德·克拉维茨]以及[威尔·布克斯顿]就整个情况进行了公开交谈时，”拉塞尔说。

    “我说，‘就在镜头前问我这个问题吧，别在镜头外谈。’于是他们在镜头前问我，然后走开，把事实扭曲成稍微不同的东西。”

    “你多少会觉得这有点厚脸皮，因为那是事实，但并不是现实中的样子。”

    “所以就像我说的，这对我来说只是个人的学习。”

    英文原文

    George Russell's biggest lesson from feuding with Max Verstappen is that he has been "too open and honest" when speaking to Formula 1 media.

    Russell criticised Verstappen in Las Vegas last weekend after a clash between the pair in practice, which prompted the Mercedes driver to declare: "I'm not just going to accept somebody to screw me over like this, and walk around like they're bigger and better than everyone else."

    It is not the first time Russell has felt compelled to take a strong, public stance against Verstappen. Two years ago in Abu Dhabi he said Verstappen "lashes out with unnecessary anger and borderline violence" and accused him of being a bully, and there have been various other on-track and off-track run-ins between the pair around that.

    In Vegas, though, race winner Verstappen quite literally had the last laugh when he mocked Russell for going off into the barriers - which Verstappen saw in his mirrors, reacted to immediately over the team radio, then doubled down afterwards in multiple interviews.

    He called Russell far below his level and took a dig at how much Russell talks off-track, which Russell has now inevitably been asked about during Thursday's media activities in Qatar.

    Russell was calm and said he found the comments "quite amusing" and he "can afford to say what he wants" given Verstappen won and Russell did not - a sentiment he expressed across two answers in much the same way, indicating a clear desire to draw a line under the issue and not add much more.

    This followed when asked by The Race's Josh Suttill if his comments on the incident in Vegas was the cumulative result of other things he's noticed Verstappen has done this year.

    Russell took a big big pause weighing up how to answer, opting to explain why he would have reacted the same on-track if it was any driver - though it was perhaps telling he emphasised the on-track part.

    Most interesting, and his 'new lesson', was how Russell twice deviated into implying he needs to be more careful what he says in the media now.

    Asked whether he wanted to pay back Verstappen's words, Russell said: "I want to win. It's as simple as that. It's not a war of words off-track. I maybe have been guilty of being just too open and honest when I speak with you guys.

    "I feel with all of the media and the journalists I have a good relationship with, and you sometimes forget that you're not talking to one person. We're having a conversation now, and you forget actually, I'm not really talking to you. I'm talking to millions of people on the television.

    "So that good relationship has actually probably hindered me a little bit, and that's, I guess, life lessons in this world we live."

    This even led to referencing something that happened two years ago out of the blue when, as part of his Abu Dhabi 2024 remarks about Verstappen, Russell gave impassioned television interviews as part of the usual broadcast activities.

    Russell also held an impromptu briefing with written media the same day, the majority of which was used to emphasise his Verstappen message.

    Russell's interviews with Sky Sports F1's Ted Kravitz and ex-F1 TV host Will Buxton have since been described by the broadcasters in question as being prompted by Russell himself.

    Kravitz wrote in his book that Russell told him: "I'm going to give it back to Max. I've had enough of him bad-mouthing me in the press, and I'm going to call him out on his bullying tactics. I know you're only supposed to ask me one question, but never mind that, I'm up for as many questions as you like."

    This week on the Up To Speed podcast Buxton said Russell is "all mouth and no trousers" when it comes to Verstappen and told his own version of the Abu Dhabi 2024 interviews, as Buxton alleged Russell said "can you just ask me about Max?" and had "clearly prepared something".

    However, Russell pushed back against how these encounters have been relayed.

    In an answer about how the Verstappen back and forth was "all avoidable" and "a bit unnecessary", Russell said that "there's lessons just to be taken away from these situations" and reiterated that "the biggest" is "maybe I am just too open and honest with the media"

    "And I remember in Abu Dhabi a few years ago when I was having an open conversation with [Ted Kravitz] and [Will Buxton] about the whole situation," said Russell.

    "I said, 'Just ask me the question on camera rather than having the conversation off camera.' So they ask me the question on camera, and then they go away and they spin the truth into something slightly different.

    "You kind of think like that's a little bit cheeky, because it is the truth, but it's not how it was in reality.

    "So as I said, that's just the learning for me personally."
