    汉密尔顿：F1多元化需更进一步

    汉密尔顿表示，F1在过去五年已证明改变是可能的，如今需要为下一代进一步扩大机会。其Mission 44基金会发布新报告《点燃包容》，提出三项建议，帮助更多来自代表性不足背景的年轻人进入并留在F1。

    刘易斯·汉密尔顿表示，改善F1多元化的工作“远未完成”，Mission 44基金会同时提出了进一步变革的建议。

    这位七届世界冠军的Mission 44基金会提出三项建议，帮助更多来自代表性不足背景的年轻人进入并留在F1；汉密尔顿对2021年以来这项运动出现的变化感到“鼓舞”，并“为我们的成就感到自豪”，同时认为还可以做得更多。

    刘易斯·汉密尔顿表示，F1在过去五年“已证明改变是可能的”，如今在增加下一代机会方面“需要更进一步”。

    汉密尔顿委员会提出变革建议五年后，这位七届世界冠军的Mission 44基金会发布了一份题为《点燃包容》的新报告，回顾了黑人、女性以及来自低收入背景的年轻人的进展。

    报告承认了自2021年以来这项运动取得的“重大进步”，其中包括签署多元与包容宪章——全部11支车队、F1和FIA均为签署方——设立工程奖学金以及推出F1学院。这项运动上月还发布了首份社会影响报告。

    汉密尔顿对这些成就表示欢迎，同时也觉得“仍有许多事情要做”。

    “我想首先说声谢谢。感谢各车队、一级方程式（F1）以及国际汽联（FIA），他们签署了这项运动历史上第一份多元与包容宪章，”这位法拉利车手在报告中说道。

    “感谢车队内部那些设立了2021年尚不存在的奖学金、实习岗位和导师计划的人们。感谢每周将这项运动带入课堂的教师、慈善机构和志愿者们。

    “如今当我走过围场时，我看到的女性更多了，黑人和棕色人种的面孔更多了，看起来像看台上车迷的人比我从开始职业生涯以来的任何时候都多。

    这令人振奋，推动此事的人们值得称赞。然而，仍有许多工作要做。

    “我们已经打开了大门，但还没有学会统计有多少人走进来，又有多少人留下来。”

    “这项运动的首份社会影响报告展示了正在发生的诸多事情。这份报告要问的是，这一切加起来意味着什么。这是一项以千分之一秒衡量表现的运动。我们也可以衡量这一点。”

    早在2023年，刘易斯·汉密尔顿就解释了他的使命：在赛车运动领域改变来自服务不足社区的年轻人的生活。

    Mission 44 的报告提出了三项建议。

    第一项是每年公布一份覆盖整个赛车运动的图景，按性别、族裔和社会经济背景说明谁加入、谁晋升、谁离开。

    第二项，报告呼吁将人才投资——首先是学徒薪资——排除在赛车运动的预算帽之外，并且 F1、国际汽联、各车队和商业合作伙伴随后“在未来五年内投入至少 5000 万英镑的新资金，帮助来自代表性不足背景的年轻人进入赛车运动职业，并支持他们留下来”。

    第三项建议是，每支车队都应承诺“每年公布一定数量的带薪、可及的职业生涯早期岗位，到 2030 年建成数千条进入赛车运动的带薪通道”，同时“对自动化筛选进行偏见测试并披露其使用情况”。

    汉密尔顿补充道：“所以这就是我的请求。致每一位车队领队、每一位首席执行官，以及一级方程式和FIA的领导者们：让我们把这份报告中的三项建议付诸实施。”

    F1上个月发布了其首份全运动社会影响报告。

    报告显示，2025年，来自71个国家的超过50万年轻人参与并受益于一级方程式在教育和社区外展方面的项目，同时超过2500名年轻人在F1赛道参加了活动。

    报告还宣布将其工程奖学金延长三年，至少持续到2028/29学年，并承诺再提供240万英镑的资金。

    该报道还补充称，这项运动将“加强其在治理、衡量和报告方面的方法，以更好地理解成果、分享进展，并最大化其社会影响工作的长期价值”。

    一位F1发言人表示：“我们为那些有助于激励下一代一级方程式车手和参与者的项目和倡议感到自豪，也为它们如何持续增加我们这项运动中的参与机会和可及性感到自豪。

    “在汉密尔顿委员会提出建议之后，这项运动于2024年签署了其有史以来第一份多元与包容（D&I）宪章，并在2025年首次开展了全运动范围的数据收集工作，以了解我们社会影响项目的全球覆盖情况。

    “这表明，仅在2025赛季，全球就有超过50万名年轻人参与了教育、STEM和激励项目，并且我们这项运动中提供了超过1000个结构化的技能和人才发展机会。

    “随着一级方程式赛车在全球舞台上的持续发展，我们深化和演进社会影响力的雄心也在不断增强。我们与F1各车队、国际汽联以及这项运动的广大成员一道，致力于在这一基础上继续建设，并期待分享我们下一阶段的工作。”

    在表达对未来取得更多进展的希望时，汉密尔顿表示：“从工程奖学金和多元与包容宪章，到激励超过50万名年轻人，我们帮助推动了真正的进展。我为我们所取得的成就感到自豪，但我们远未完成。”

    “一级方程式赛车正在激励全世界数百万年轻人。但如果没有机会，激励就毫无意义。我们不能告诉年轻人要胸怀大志，然后当他们试图将这些梦想变为现实时却不打开大门。”

    “F1、国际汽联和每支车队都必须加大力度、加快步伐。我们需要真正的投资、更多的机会和更强的问责制。我们已经证明改变是可能的。现在我们需要走得更远。人才就在那里，而我们这项运动的未来取决于能否释放他们的潜力。”

    Lewis Hamilton says work improving diversity in F1 'nowhere near finished' as Mission 44 outlines recommendations for further change

    The seven-time champion's Mission 44 foundation outlines three recommendations to help more young people from under-represented backgrounds get into and stay in F1; Hamilton "heartened" by changes seen in the sport since 2021 and "proud of what we've achieved" and believes more can be done

    Lewis Hamilton says Formula 1 has "shown that change is possible" to improve diversity in the sport over the past five years and "now we need to go further" in increasing opportunities for the next generation.

    Five years after the Hamilton Commission made its recommendations for change, the seven-time world champion's Mission 44 foundation released a new report titled 'Igniting Inclusion' reviewing progress for Black people, women and young people from lower-income backgrounds.

    The report acknowledged the "significant steps forward" made in the sport since 2021, which include the signing of a diversity and inclusion charter - which all 11 teams, F1 and the FIA are signatories to - the creation of an engineering scholarship and the launch of F1 Academy. The sport also published its first social impact report last month.

    Hamilton welcomed those achievements and also feels "there is much still to do".

    "I want to start by saying thank you. To the teams, to Formula 1 and to the FIA, who signed the first Diversity and Inclusion Charter in the history of this sport," said the Ferrari driver in the report.

    "To the people inside the teams who built scholarships, placements and mentoring schemes that did not exist in 2021. To the teachers, charities and volunteers who take the sport into classrooms every week.

    "When I walk through the paddock now, I see more women, more Black and Brown faces, more people who look like the fans in the grandstands than at any point since I started.

    "That is heartening, and the people behind it deserve credit. And yet, there is much still to do.

    "We have opened the door, but we have not yet learned to count how many people walk through it, or how many stay.

    "The sport's first Social Impact Report shows how much is happening. This report asks what it adds up to. This is a sport that measures performance to the thousandth of a second. We can measure this too."

    Back in 2023, Lewis Hamilton explained his mission to transform the lives of young people from underserved communities in the world of motorsport

    Mission 44's report makes three recommendations.

    The first is for the publication of one sport-wide picture each year of who joins, who is promoted and who leaves, by gender, ethnicity and socio-economic background.

    Secondly, the report calls for talent investment, starting with apprentice salaries, to be excluded from the sport's budget cap and that F1, the FIA, the teams and commercial partners then "invest at least £50m of new money over the next five years in helping young people from under-represented backgrounds into motorsport careers, and supporting them to stay".

    The third recommendation is for every team to commit to "a published number of paid, accessible early-career places each year, building thousands of paid routes into motorsport by 2030", while "automated screening is tested for bias and its use disclosed".

    Hamilton added: "So here is what I am asking. To every team principal, every chief executive and the leaders of Formula 1 and the FIA: let's make the three recommendations in this report happen."

    F1 last month published its first sport-wide social impact report.

    It showed that more than 500,000 young people across 71 countries accessed and benefitted from Formula 1 programmes in education and community outreach in 2025, while over 2,500 young people took part in activities at F1 race tracks.

    It also announced a three-year extension to its engineering scholarship until at least 2028/29, committing to a further £2.4m in funding.

    That report added the sport will "strengthen its approach to governance, measurement and reporting to better understand results, share progress and maximise the long-term value of its social impact work".

    An F1 spokesperson said: "We are proud of the programmes and initiatives which help inspire the next generation of Formula 1 and how they continue to increase access and opportunity across our sport.

    "Following recommendations from the Hamilton Commission, the sport signed its first ever D&I charter in 2024, and in 2025, conducted its first ever sport wide data gathering exercise to capture the global reach of our social impact programmes.

    "This showed that in the 2025 season alone, more than 500,000 young people engaged in education, STEM and inspiration programmes globally and over 1,000 structured skills and talent development opportunities across our sport.

    "As Formula 1 continues to grow on the global stage, so too does our ambition to deepen and evolve our social impact. We are committed, alongside the F1 teams, the FIA and broader members of our sport to continue building on this foundation and look forward to sharing the next phase of our work."

    Expressing his hopes for more progress in future, Hamilton said: "From engineering scholarships and the Diversity and Inclusion Charter to inspiring over 500,000 young people, we've helped drive real progress. I'm proud of what we've achieved, but we're nowhere near finished.

    "Formula 1 is inspiring millions of young people around the world. But inspiration means nothing without opportunity. We can't tell young people to dream big and then not open the doors when they try to make those dreams a reality.

    "F1, the FIA and every team must push harder and move faster. We need real investment, more opportunities and greater accountability. We've shown that change is possible. Now we need to go further. The talent is out there, and the future of our sport depends on unlocking it."
