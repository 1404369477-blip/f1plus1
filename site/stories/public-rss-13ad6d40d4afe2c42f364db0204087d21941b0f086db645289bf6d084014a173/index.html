    维斯塔潘批评拉斯维加斯超车过多：这不是F1

    拉斯维加斯大奖赛出现352次超车和35次领跑易主，维斯塔潘批评这种比赛方式“不是F1”，称感觉像在玩《马力欧卡丁车》。

    拉斯维加斯大奖赛超车频繁，车手们对这场比赛的评价出现分歧，一些人批评它“非常人工化”和“不是F1”。

    根据F1的数据，拉斯维加斯站共有352次超车，其中包括35次领跑位置易主。但数量并不总等于质量，发车格上的车手们给出了不同评价。

    批评2026年规则的最大声音维斯塔潘在赛后新闻发布会上开场时开玩笑地试图阻止自己搬出他最喜欢的一个与今年规则作比较的说法。

    “我大概不该说。我大概不该说。我记得我在玩《马力欧卡丁车》时曾这样赢过一次，”维斯塔潘说。

    “有时候你掉到后面，会获得尾流，然后就像被追上来一样。每次你掉到后面，你都会跑得更快。”

    “所以那是我最后一次有这种感觉。”

    尽管从外面看显得很疯狂，但维斯塔潘在方向盘后感到极其舒适，即使他反复失去领先位置。

    “我一点也不紧张，因为，我不知道，感觉就像在外面巡航一样，只是在正确的时候尝试使用电池，或者防守，”维斯塔潘说。

    “但就观赏性而言，当你从旁观者的角度看，大概是的，你会喜欢它。”

    他告诉Canal+：“就我个人而言，我们这样比赛并不是一级方程式赛车应有的样子。电视上看起来很棒，但这不应该是它的样子。”

    亚历克斯·阿尔本作为观众看完了整场比赛，因为他的威廉姆斯赛车在起步前就因刹车过热而彻底损坏，他说自己的感受“复杂”。

    “对于普通观众来说，这确实是一场非常精彩的比赛，”阿尔本说。

    “我觉得前四圈之后，我就无聊了。大部分时间这像是一场电动方程式比赛。它给了我一种大集团骑行的感觉，这很独特，所以我不反对。

    “为什么不举办像这样的比赛，并混合一下呢？我们有一些赛道，纯粹的速度就能获胜。我们还有其他像摩纳哥这样的赛道，那里什么都不会发生。为什么不举办像这样接力赛式的比赛呢？

    “很刺激，但结尾几乎有些虎头蛇尾，基米[安东内利收油导致部署错误，使他退出了与维斯塔潘的领先争夺]，但这仍然是一场精彩的比赛。”

    在与新秀拉斐尔·卡马拉发生碰撞被淘汰后，塞尔吉奥·佩雷斯在他14号弯撞车地点旁边用笔记本电脑观看了比赛的后半段。

    “是啊，很好，非常人工化”是他对这场比赛的讽刺性总结。

    他曾批评“超车模式太人工化了”，这导致他突然追近了与卡马拉之间约一秒的差距，而卡马拉没有意识到佩雷斯就在他正后方，便转向了弯道。

    佩雷斯因卡塔尔站被罚退后五个发车位，因为赛会干事判定“在这次操作过程中，佩雷斯的前轴从未至少与卡马拉的后视镜齐平”。

    车手们一直批评2026年的超车模式不够强大，但在拉斯维加斯，借助尾流效应，速度差变得极其悬殊。

    “从模拟来看，超车效果非常小，但当然还有尾流效应，那非常大，”奥利·贝尔曼解释道。

    “我们进行这类比赛有点令人失望，因为即使你失去尾流，你每圈也会损失大量时间，仅仅因为独自在赛道上就会损失很多比赛时间。

    “你在排位赛中看到了，每个人都像卡丁车一样一起出来利用尾流。说实话，这有点可笑。”

    这也导致车手们玩弄超车模式激活线，确保他们在激活点处于对手身后，以便在下一圈获得超车模式。

    “我花了很长时间夹在两辆奥迪赛车之间，我有很多超车机会，但我选择留在后面，因为我知道如果我冲到前面，我就会失去这次超车机会并损失大量比赛时间，”贝尔曼解释道。

    这也是让安东内利错失领奖台的原因，他减速以保持在维斯塔潘身后，结果却触发了部署问题，使他在迈凯伦赛车面前毫无防守之力。

    没必要找缺点吗？

    不过有些车手很享受这场比赛。其中包括卡洛斯·塞恩斯，他获得了本赛季最好的第八名，并且觉得没必要对这场比赛挑毛病。

    塞恩斯说：“我认为这对F1来说是美好的一天，我觉得每个人都乐在其中，没必要去找它的负面。”

    “这对一级方程式来说就是一场精彩的比赛，我觉得我们都很享受。”

    刘易斯·汉密尔顿称这是“一场非常伟大的比赛”，而兰多·诺里斯则让人有理由相信，当F1在2030年进入新的V8时代时，这场比赛可能会变得更加激动人心。

    诺里斯说：“从比赛的角度来看，从旁观者的角度，我确信这大概是F1有史以来最精彩的一场比赛。”

    “你永远不知道会发生什么。超车会凭空出现。问题是，有时你对别人的接近速度能达到20-30公里/小时，尤其是对我们来说，有些超车根本不算超车，你按下加速按钮就超过去了。

    “但比赛仍然很精彩。你仍然在为位置而战，其中有很多好的方面。

    “但我确信，如果没有电池的那种情况，你仍然会有同样多的超车，而且我们作为车手会更享受，因为我们可以全力推进、全速前进，不必考虑能量回收和电池，不必考虑释放和不释放以及所有这些事情。

    “我想说的是，我们本可以表现得更好。所以，想象一下2030年。天哪，那将是你见过的最精彩的比赛。”

    英文原文

    The racing in Formula 1’s overtake-filled Las Vegas Grand Prix has split opinion among drivers, with some criticising it as “very artificial” and “not F1”.

    According to F1’s figures, there were 352 overtakes, including 35 changes for the lead in Las Vegas. But quantity doesn’t always equal quality, and there were mixed verdicts from the grid.

    Arch-2026 rules critic Max Verstappen began his post-race press conference jokingly trying to stop himself from reaching for one of his favourite comparison points to this year’s rules.

    “I probably shouldn't say it. I probably should not. I remember winning one like this when I was playing Mario Kart,” Verstappen said.

    “Sometimes you drop back and you get drafts, and there's like a catch-up. Always when you're dropping back, you go faster.

    “So that's the last time that I felt something like this.”

    Despite it appearing frenetic from the outside, Verstappen found it extremely comfortable behind the wheel even as he repeatedly lost the lead.

    “I was not nervous at all because, I don't know, it just felt kind of cruising out there, just trying to press the battery whenever it was correct or defend,” Verstappen said.

    “But for the show, when you look at it from the outside, probably yes, you like it.”

    He told Canal+ “for me, personally, this isn't Formula 1 the way that we are racing. It looks great on TV, but this isn't how it should be”.

    Alex Albon, who watched the whole race as a spectator as his Williams was terminally damaged by overheating brakes before the start, said he had “mixed” feelings.

    “For the casual viewer, it was a really great race,” Albon said.

    “I think after the first four laps, I got bored. It was a Formula E race for the most part. It gave me the peloton vibes, which are unique, so I'm not against it.

    “Why not have races where it's like this and have a mix? We have the tracks where pure pace wins. We have other tracks like Monaco where nothing happens. Why not have races like this where it's a baton race?

    “Good excitement and almost an anti-climactic finish with Kimi [Antonelli lifting off and causing a deployment error that dropped him out of the lead fight with Verstappen], but it's still a great race.”

    Once he was eliminated after a clash with rookie Rafael Camara, Sergio Perez spectated the latter half of the race on a laptop by the site of his crash at Turn 14.

    “Yeah, great, very artificial” was his sarcastic summary of the race.

    He’d criticised an “overtake mode [that] is so artificial” which led to him suddenly closing a gap of around a second to Camara, who didn’t realise Perez was right behind him and turned into the corner.

    Perez was given a five-place grid penalty for Qatar as the stewards judged that at “no point during the manoeuvre was the front axle of Perez at least alongside the mirror of Camara”.

    Drivers have been criticising overtake mode in 2026 for not being powerful enough, but in Vegas, with the slipstream effect, the speed differentials became extreme.

    “From simulations, the overtake effect is very small, but then of course you have the slipstream effect as well, which is incredibly big,” Ollie Bearman explained.

    “It's a bit disappointing that we're doing these types of races because even when you lose the slipstream, you lose so much time every lap that you lose a lot of race time just from being alone on track.

    “You saw it in qualy, everyone coming out together like go-karts to take the slipstream. It's a bit of a joke, really.”

    It also led to drivers playing with the overtake mode activation line, ensuring they were behind their rival at the activation point so they’d get overtake mode for the following lap.

    “I spent a lot of time sandwiched between the two Audis, and I had a lot of opportunities to pass, but I opted just to stay behind because I knew that if I went in front, I would lose the overtake and lose a lot of race time,” Bearman explained.

    That’s also what cost Antonelli a podium as he slowed to remain behind Verstappen, only for that to trigger a deployment problem and leave him defenceless to the McLarens.

    No need to find a bad side?

    Some drivers enjoyed the race however. Among them was Carlos Sainz, who finished a season’s best eighth, and didn’t feel the need to find fault with the race.

    “I think it was a good day for F1, I think everyone enjoyed themselves, no need to find a bad side to it,” Sainz said.

    “It was just a good race for Formula 1, and I think we all enjoyed it.”

    Lewis Hamilton called it “a really great race”, while Lando Norris gave reason to believe this race could get even more exciting when F1’s new V8 era arrives in 2030.

    “From a racing point of view, from the outside, I'm sure it probably was the best race ever in Formula 1,” Norris said.

    “You never know what's going to happen. There were overtakes out of nowhere. The thing is, sometimes you have a closing speed of 20-30km/h to people, especially to us, and some of the overtakes weren’t overtakes, you press boost and you go past someone.

    “But the racing was still good. You're still fighting for position, and there are a lot of good things in there.

    “But I'm sure if you didn't have the battery how it was, you would still have just as many overtakes, and we would enjoy it even more as drivers because we would be able to just push and go flat out and not have to think about regen and battery and not deploying and deploying and all of this stuff.

    “We could have been even better, is what I'm trying to say. So, imagine in 2030. Oh my God, that's going to be the best race you've ever seen.”
