    FIA文件揭示小红牛赛车在拉斯维加斯大奖赛获胜的空气动力学更新

    马克·休斯和乔治奥·皮奥拉指出，小红牛在内华达州对前翼端板进行了细微调整，并对侧箱进行了持续改进。

    FIA文件揭示了红牛赛车在拉斯维加斯大奖赛上赢得胜利的空气动力学更新

    F1技术周报和FIA文档9详细说明了红牛赛车降低俯冲平面和侧箱修复如何帮助维斯塔潘赢得拉斯维加斯胜利

    对于红牛车迷来说，关注红牛车队，了解F1新闻、车手更新、技术洞察和米尔顿凯恩斯的比赛分析

    马克斯·维斯塔潘在拉斯维加斯大奖赛上的胜利就像一场高速的电池部署下的象棋比赛，但赢得比赛的基石却是一个更加安静的空气动力学修复。

    正如Formula1.com的Tech Weekly撰稿人马克·休斯和乔治奥·皮奥拉所指出的，红牛在内华达州对前翼端板进行了细微的调整，同时对其侧箱进行了持续的重构——优化从车鼻到地板的气流，为维斯塔潘提供一辆能够在50个激烈、尾流密集的回合中生存下来的赛车。

    官方FIA文件9（汽车展示提交，发布于11月19日）展示了红牛如何与竞争对手形成鲜明对比。梅赛德斯削减了后翼翼尖，修剪了后视镜支架，并缩小了发动机罩出口以减少阻力，而法拉利优化了其前地板以提高Strip效率。相比之下，红牛提交了一个单独的更新，严格归类于性能——局部负载：前翼端板下沉平面的重新定位。

    “为了更好地保护前翼端板脚部以及下游表面的气流稳定性，下沉平面已被降低并减少了弦长，”红牛在其FIA提交中写道。

    为什么在2026年规则下，一个小小的翼尖如此重要。

    红牛车队在去年九月的巴库重大升级中最后一次修改了其前翼端板。对于拉斯维加斯站，工程师们将下沉平面降低到垂直端板的下方，并将其长度修剪成紧凑的形状，这与姐妹车队小红牛已经采用的解决方案非常相似。

    尽管视觉上改动不大，但下沉平面有三个重要的功能。除了增加前轴下压力外，它还能消除尖端涡流，这些涡流会加速前轮胎尾流的湍流，同时将流经车轮顶部的气流从侧面绕过的空气分开 - 将更干净的气流输送到车身两侧。

    同样重要的是，在2026年的主动空气动力学法规下，休斯和皮奥拉指出，修改后的翼尖在主前翼襟翼旋转时，在直线和转弯模式之间急剧切换时稳定气流。

    这次前端的调整并非孤立发生。在巴库，红牛车队引入了更高、更瘦的侧箱，以及修改后的地板和扩散器。四周后在墨西哥城（FIA文件12，10月30日），工程师们修改了侧箱与地板的分割线 - 将巴库之前的规格中弯曲的下车身部分移植到更高的巴库上箱，以封闭地板边缘通道，并在散热器进气口下方恢复局部负载，同时保持稳定性。

    在圣保罗和拉斯维加斯赛道上保持不变，那些混合式下侧翼板也揭示了红牛早期头疼的问题：在底盘内部增加了内部结构锥，据信可以防止面板在重力的空气动力学负载下变形。

    将练习中的挫败感转化为连续七个领奖台

    在内华达州验证新的前翼端板变得混乱。雨洗掉了周四FP1和FP2有意义的测试，迫使红牛依赖干燥的FP3比较 - 包括伊萨克·哈德贾在练习中更换中段翼片 - 在对两辆车的资格赛和比赛进行降低俯冲平面之前。维斯塔潘甚至表示，从巴西带来的“奇怪的问题”在最后练习中让他不满意，坚持认为问题超出了常规平衡。

    然而，红牛在预赛最后的设置调整在关键时刻解锁了这套方案。维斯塔潘在Q3中跑出第二快的圈速 - 比基米·安东内利慢0.258秒 - 通过梅赛德斯冠军的五处引擎处罚继承了杆位，并且挡住了梅赛德斯和麦克拉伦的挑战，以0.639秒的优势击败了兰多·诺里斯。

    更广泛的趋势不言而喻。自从红牛在巴库建立了基金会以来，维斯塔潘在连续七个大奖赛中都完成了领奖台，并收集了他所有的2026年胜利——这使他以304分的成绩排名第二，同时使红牛在车队积分榜上领先于麦克拉伦9分（440比431），在前往卡塔尔之前，红牛以9分的优势排在第三位。

    FIA filings reveal the Red Bull aero update behind Max Verstappen’s Las Vegas GP win

    F1 Tech Weekly and FIA Doc 9 detail how Red Bull's lowered dive-plane and sidepod fixes aided Verstappen's Vegas win

    For Red Bull fans. Keeping watch on Red Bull Racing, with F1 news, driver updates, technical insight and race analysis from Milton Keynes.

    Max Verstappen’s Las Vegas Grand Prix victory played out like a high-speed chess match on battery deployment, yet the race-winning foundation rested on a much quieter aerodynamic fix.

    As Formula1.com’s Tech Weekly contributors Mark Hughes and Giorgio Piola highlighted, Red Bull paired a subtle front wing endplate tweak in Nevada with an ongoing sidepod rework - refining airflow from nose to floor to give Verstappen a car capable of surviving 50 intense, slipstream-heavy laps.

    Official FIA Document 9 (Car Presentation Submissions, issued 19 November) shows how sharply Red Bull diverged from its rivals. Mercedes stripped rear wing winglets, trimmed mirror stays, and narrowed its engine-cover exit to slash drag, while Ferrari optimised its front floorboard for Strip efficiency. Red Bull, by contrast, filed a single update categorised strictly under Performance – Local Load: a repositioned front wing endplate dive-plane.

    "In order to better protect the flow stability of the front wing endplate footplate as well as surfaces downstream, the dive-plane has been lowered and reduced in chord length," Red Bull wrote in its FIA submission.

    Why one small winglet matters under 2026 rules

    Red Bull had last revised its front wing endplates during September’s major Baku upgrade. For Las Vegas, engineers dropped the dive-plane lower down the vertical endplate and trimmed its length into a compact shape closely mirroring the solution already run by sister team Racing Bulls.

    Though modest visually, the dive-plane performs three important jobs. Beyond adding front-axle downforce, it sheds tip vortices that accelerate turbulent front-tyre wake outward while splitting airflow travelling over the top of the wheel from air skirting its side - feeding cleaner flow down the car’s flanks.

    Just as importantly under 2026’s active-aerodynamics regulations, Hughes and Piola noted the revised winglet stabilises airflow during the abrupt shift between straight-line and cornering modes whenever the main front wing flaps rotate.

    That front-end reset did not happen in isolation. In Baku, Red Bull introduced taller, slimmer sidepods alongside a revised floor and diffuser. Four weeks later in Mexico City (FIA Document 12, 30 October), engineers modified the sidepod-to-floor split line - grafting the curved lower bodywork from the pre-Baku specification onto the taller Baku upper pod to close off the floor-edge channel and recover local load beneath the radiator inlet while preserving stability.

    Run unchanged through Sao Paulo and Las Vegas, those hybrid lower sidepods also revealed a clue to Red Bull’s earlier headaches: internal structural cones added inside the lower bodywork, believed to stop the panels from deforming under heavy aerodynamic load.

    Turning practice frustration into seven straight podiums

    Validating the new front wing endplate in Nevada proved messy. Rain wiped out meaningful Thursday running across FP1 and FP2, forcing Red Bull to lean on dry FP3 comparisons - including a mid-session wing swap for Isack Hadjar - before committing to the lowered dive-plane on both cars for qualifying and the race. Verstappen even flagged "weird issues" carried over from Brazil that left him dissatisfied through final practice, insisting the trouble went beyond routine balance.

    Still, Red Bull’s late pre-qualifying set-up adjustments unlocked the package when it counted. Verstappen clocked the second-fastest lap in Q3 - 0.258 seconds behind Kimi Antonelli - inherited pole via the Mercedes champion’s five-place engine penalty, and fended off both Mercedes and McLaren to beat Lando Norris by 0.639s.

    The broader trend speaks for itself. Since Red Bull introduced its Baku foundation, Verstappen has finished on the podium in seven consecutive grands prix and collected all four of his 2026 victories - lifting him to 304 points in second overall while keeping Red Bull nine points clear of McLaren (440 to 431) for third in the constructors’ standings heading into Qatar.
