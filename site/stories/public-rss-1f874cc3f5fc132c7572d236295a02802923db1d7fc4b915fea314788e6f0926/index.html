    F1车手称拉斯维加斯湿地像溜冰场

    拉斯维加斯大奖赛周四练习赛受降雨影响，低抓地力街道赛道让车手们对倍耐力雨胎的表现感到担忧，但所有车手都未撞墙。

    “时速100公里时很吓人”——F1车手对拉斯维加斯“溜冰场”感到震惊

    周四在F1拉斯维加斯大奖赛基本被雨水冲掉的练习赛，只是进一步证实了人们对雨胎性能的担忧

    降雨影响了周四拉斯维加斯大奖赛在Strip周边低抓地力街道赛道上的运行，最大的收获不是谁最快，而是所有F1车手都让赛车远离了护墙，这一点令人印象深刻。

    在巴西经历了F1今年首个真正的雨战周末、在排位赛和正赛条件下使用了全雨胎和半雨胎之后，车手们担心倍耐力的雨胎系列在没有高速弯、轮胎更难升温的赛道上会表现如何。

    迈凯伦车队的兰多·诺里斯在圣保罗站后表示：“我觉得可怕的是，如果你去拉斯维加斯却无法让轮胎达到任何温度。”小红牛车手利亚姆·劳森补充道：“如果我们身处像拉斯维加斯这样的赛道又下雨，说实话，我认为我们会有麻烦。”

    诺里斯和劳森没等多久，他们的预测就得到了验证。2025年拉斯维加斯只有22个雨天，因此降雨影响大奖赛的可能性微乎其微。在这座痴迷于概率的城市里，这相当于在拉斯维加斯大道两旁的任何轮盘赌桌上，押中两个数字的拆分注。

    这场降水如期而至，且雨量充沛，使得周四的首次练习赛完全泡汤，甚至没有车手做出圈速，因为大多数车手都只在使用全雨胎的情况下完成了一圈探路圈，很大程度上是出于好奇。

    二练则更具看点，起初使用全雨胎，随着干燥的行车线开始形成，随后换上了半雨胎。这条布满油污和胎胶、画有标线的赛道被小红牛新秀阿维德·林德布拉德形容为“溜冰场”，这也是他的拉斯维加斯首秀。车手们在每一段（stint）的头几圈尤其挣扎，他们努力将轮胎调整到合理的工作温度区间。

    “这确实让我震惊，”林德布拉德说。“我们已经说过很多次，这些轮胎不好。我认为车手们去年就抱怨过雨胎在这里不起作用。它们在我们今年去的很多赛道上都不起作用。”

    “但这是我坐在车里从未有过的体验。说实话，即使在低速弯以100公里/小时行驶也很可怕。四个轮子只是打滑，不知道会不会撞墙。显然，因为是街道赛道，所有那些油漆和墙壁让情况更糟。这对我来说是非常新的体验，说实话，我能联想到的最接近的情况是驾驶拉力赛车在冰面上，而不是F1赛车。”

    更有经验的车手也支持林德布拉德的看法。“是的，当我看到没有撞车或什么都没发生时，我总是很惊讶，因为你自己在那里经常会有那种时刻，”威廉姆斯的亚历克斯·阿尔本说。“但这是全年最棘手的赛道，全年最低的抓地力，这些车在湿滑赛道上，打滑非常严重。”

    第一次练习赛后，倍耐力决定将其全雨胎的轮胎保温毯温度从40摄氏度提高到60摄氏度，这在一定程度上帮助了初始升温，但阿尔本觉得还不够。“我认为这些轮胎太冷了。我认为我们需要更热的保温毯。我不知道为什么我们把保温毯温度设得这么低，因为我们仍然在使用它们，所以温度高一点也没什么区别，”他说。

    迈凯伦车队的奥斯卡·皮亚斯特里补充道：“起步时几乎不可能。即使我们换上中性胎（半雨胎），头几圈也比全雨胎更糟，而且棘手得多。所以，在那种条件下绝对不容易。”

    不过并非所有人都同意，红牛车队的伊萨克·哈贾尔实际上表示他相当享受使用半雨胎的时段。“在第一节练习中，非常困难。这就是我们没怎么跑的原因。而第二节练习则相当轻松，”这位法国人说道。

    “我觉得全雨胎太极端了。半雨胎运作得完美，说实话还挺好玩的。我开得很开心。”

    在早期研发工作和多次湿地轮胎测试之后，轮胎供应商倍耐力计划最早于下个赛季推出更新版轮胎。

    "It was scary at 100km/h" - F1 drivers shocked by Las Vegas 'ice rink'

    Thursday's largely washed-out practice at Formula 1's Las Vegas Grand Prix only further confirmed concerns over the performance of the wet tyres

    With rain affecting Thursday's Las Vegas Grand Prix running on the low-grip street circuit around the Strip, the biggest takeaway was not who was fastest, but the impressive fact that every Formula 1 driver kept it out of the barriers.

    Following F1's first proper wet weekend of the year in Brazil, using both the full wets and intermediates tyres in qualifying and race conditions, drivers were concerned over how Pirelli's wet weather range would work on a circuit without high-speed corners, where tyres are much harder to get up to temperature.

    "I think the scary times are if you go to Vegas and you can't get any tyre temperature," McLaren's Lando Norris said after the Sao Paulo race, with Racing Bulls driver Liam Lawson adding: "If we're on a track like Vegas and it rains, honestly, I think we're in trouble."

    Norris and Lawson didn't have to wait long for their predictions to be put to the test. Vegas only saw 22 days of rain in 2025, making rainfall affecting the grand prix a long shot. In a city obsessed by odds, it's the equivalent of hitting a split bet on two numbers at the any roulette tables lining the Strip.

    That precipitation duly arrived in copious amounts, making Thursday's first practice session a complete wash, with no drivers even logging a lap time as most of the pack restricted itself to one exploration lap on full wet tyres, largely out of curiosity.

    FP2 was more action-packed, starting on the full wets before moving to intermediates as a dry line started forming. The oily, grimy track lined with paint proved an 'ice rink', as Racing Bulls rookie Arvid Lindblad described his Vegas debut. Drivers particularly struggled on their first couple of laps of a stint as they wrangled their tyres into a reasonable temperature range.

    "It did shock me," Lindblad said. "We've said many times that these tyres are not good. I think the drivers already complained last year that the wet tyres didn't work here. They weren't working on a lot of tracks we went to this year.

    "But this was an experience I've never had before in a car. It was honestly scary to drive even at 100 km/h in a low-speed corner. Just four wheels sliding, not knowing if you're going into a wall. Obviously it doesn't help with all the paint and walls because it's a street track. It was a very new experience for me and honestly the closest I can relate to it was driving a rally car on ice versus Formula 1."

    More experienced drivers backed up Lindblad's opinion, too. "Yeah, I'm always surprised when I see no crashes or nothing, because you feel like you have those moments a lot out there yourself," said Williams' Alex Albon. "But the trickiest track of the year, lowest grip of the year, these cars in a wet track, it's a lot of sliding."

    After FP1, Pirelli decided to increase the tyre blanket temperature for its full wet tyres from 40C to 60C, which somewhat helped the initial warm-up, but Albon felt it didn't go far enough. "I think these tyres are too cold. I think we need hotter blankets. I don't know why we run so cold on the blankets, because we still use them, so it doesn't make a difference if we run them a bit hotter," he said.

    McLaren's Oscar Piastri added: "Pretty impossible at the start. Even when we put the inters on, for the first couple of laps it was worse than the wet and a lot more tricky. So, definitely not easy out there."

    Not everyone agreed, though, as Red Bull's Isack Hadjar actually said he quite enjoyed the intermediate running. "In FP1, it was very difficult. That's why we didn't really drive. And then FP2 was fairly easy," the Frenchman said.

    "I think the extreme tyre was too extreme. The intermediate was working perfectly and it was good fun, honestly. I had good fun driving around."

    Following early development work and a number of wet weather tyre tests, tyre supplier Pirellis is planning to introduce updated tyres as early as next season.
