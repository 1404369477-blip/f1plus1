    本田F1可靠性问题再现，新故障或难在赛季内查明

    本田的可靠性问题再度出现，其中一项新故障的根源可能在F1赛季结束前都无法查明。阿斯顿·马丁与本田在巴西遭遇三连赛中的第三次退赛，斯特罗尔连续第二场比赛退赛。

    本田令人担忧的可靠性问题再度出现，其中包含一项新故障，其根源可能在F1赛季结束前都无法查明。

    阿斯顿·马丁与引擎合作伙伴本田在巴西遭遇三连赛中的第三次退赛，兰斯·斯特罗尔连续第二场比赛退赛。

    斯特罗尔在巴西大奖赛周末期间出现两次独立的电池问题，周日正赛未跑到半程即退赛。

    这意味着斯特罗尔本赛季20场比赛仅完赛6场，而队友费尔南多·阿隆索则有7次退赛。

    最近出现了一些乐观的迹象——相对而言，考虑到这个新联盟的首个赛季如此灾难性——阿隆索在五场比赛前的马来西亚站得分，在奥斯汀尽管首圈就遭受了严重的底板损伤却出人意料地拥有不错的速度，并在巴西的雨战中表现出色。

    在那里，阿隆索轻松地为阿斯顿·马丁和本田带来了他们今年最好的排位赛表现，首次进入Q3并以第八位发车。

    但这一点被严重削弱了，因为显著的脆弱性重新出现，这更接近本田2026年初糟糕的开局状态。

    可靠性问题也断送了阿隆索在墨西哥的机会，在那里他看起来相当有竞争力，比平时更接近前十。这个问题与能量回收系统内部的冷却系统有关，而在巴西，斯特罗尔出现的电池问题敲响了明确的警钟。

    本田首席赛道工程师折原慎太郎承认，“我们知道发生了什么，但不知道根本原因是什么”。

    他承认找到根本原因可能需要几周时间，但根据现有数据，已经需要为下一站拉斯维加斯站采取某种应对措施，同时本田正在追查深层原因，以便在2027年妥善解决。

    “我们需要为明年找到这个错误的真正根本原因，”他说。

    “否则，我们会一直遇到同样的问题。”

    这次故障与墨西哥站发生的情况不同，墨西哥站的问题导致了MGU-K故障。但主题是相似的：在墨西哥，折原承认本田引擎在不止一个方面缺乏稳健性，而那里的故障在模拟中并未显现。

    现在本田知道发生了什么，但不知道为什么。这是两个问题之间令人担忧的贯穿线。这也让人回想起赛季初，当时本田能够识别出现问题的明显灾难性后果，并能大致看出问题来自哪里，但不知道问题的确切根源，因为与它在测功机上经历的情况相比，这个问题是突然出现的。

    特别令人担忧的是，这似乎确实是本田在赛季中期达到的水平的一种倒退。

    其性能显然仍然不足，而且即使全年通过升级试图缩小差距，其引擎在内燃机方面仍然动力不足。

    但至少已经发展到了这样一个阶段：赛车能够完成比赛，这使得阿隆索在阿斯顿·马丁赛车大幅升级时能够获得一些积分。

    在夏休期前后，阿隆索连续五场完赛，而斯特罗尔也在夏休期前的三场比赛中取得了今年六次完赛中的两次。

    折原承认，可靠性在赛季中期确实更好，但最近几场比赛中，这已被“几个问题”所取代。

    这一趋势的部分原因可能与部件的里程数有关——部件只是到了其使用寿命周期的终点——或者只是本田为了性能而更用力地运行引擎并超出了极限。

    但就巴西站具体出现的电池问题而言，现在判断触发原因还为时过早。

    本田本赛季在发动机综合性能方面并未取得所期望的进展，充其量只是在非常特定的情况下实现了部分性能目标。

    因此，可靠性变差却没有明显收益，这让本田承受压力，并在其不得不克服诸多逆境的一年即将结束之际，再次招致不幸的大量负面审视。

    这也给本田正在进行的2027年研发工作带来了变数，届时它需要在性能和可靠性两方面都迈进一步。

    到目前为止所表明的一切是，新赛季开始时不会有一个重大升级。已经有一个在全年分阶段引入进一步升级的计划。但这项工作已经深入进行中。

    故障排查会占用人力资源、在F1引擎开发限制下受限的台架测试时间、受引擎成本上限约束的资金，以及单纯的时间，因为距离2027年初的时钟正在滴答作响。

    首先是墨西哥的冷却问题，无论那究竟是什么，现在是电池方面的这个问题，使本田的注意力偏离了一条它理想情况下本应走得更远的道路。

    本田首席执行官三部敏宏在巴西，折原表示这对员工有激励作用——“但另一方面，我们本周末两次遇到电池问题”。

    这并不完全是本田F1部门希望在大老板面前展示自己的方式。但正如折原所指出的，三部至少“了解赛道边正在发生什么”。

    “他会把这种经验带到日本方面，也会促进我们的研发，”折原说。

    如果曾经有任何可能，让人对本田为达到必要标准还需做多少工作而产生自满情绪，那么这位首席执行官亲眼看到2026年已如此深入时仍会遇到的那类问题，就会把这种情绪彻底消除。

    英文原文

    Honda's worrying return of reliability problems includes a new issue, the root cause of which it might not trace before the end of the Formula 1 season.

    Aston Martin and engine partner Honda suffered their third DNF of the triple-header in Brazil, when Lance Stroll retired for the second race in a row.

    Stroll had two separate battery problems over the course of the Brazil weekend and didn't make it to mid-distance in Sunday's grand prix.

    It means Stroll has finished just six of the 20 races held this season, while team-mate Fernando Alonso has had seven retirements of his own.

    There have been some flashes of optimism - in relative terms, given how disastrous this first season of the new alliance has been - of late as Alonso scored points in Malaysia five races ago, had surprisingly good pace at Austin despite sustaining significant floor damage on the opening lap, and excelled in the wet in Brazil.

    There, Alonso comfortably gave Aston Martin and Honda their best qualifying of the year, getting into Q3 for the first time and lining up eighth on the grid.

    But this has been badly undercut by a return of significant fragility that's closer to how badly Honda started 2026.

    Reliability problems also put paid to Alonso's chances in Mexico, where he had looked quite competitive and closer to the top 10 than normal. The issue was related to the cooling system inside the energy recovery system, whereas in Brazil the emergence of battery problems for Stroll sounded a clear alarm bell.

    Shintaro Orihara, Honda's lead trackside engineer, acknowledged that "we know what happened, but we don't know what is the root cause".

    He admitted it may take a few weeks to find that, but already from the data there needs to be some kind of countermeasure for the next race in Las Vegas, while Honda traces the underlying cause so it can be addressed properly for 2027.

    "We need to find the actual root cause of this error for next year," he said.

    "Otherwise, we keep having the same issue."

    This was a different kind of failure to what had occurred in Mexico, which had led to MGU-K problems. But the theme is similar: in Mexico, Orihara had admitted to a lack of robustness within Honda's engine in more ways than one, and the fault there hadn't shown up in simulation.

    Now Honda knows what happened but doesn't know why. That's a worrying through-line between the two issues. It also harks back to the start of the year, when Honda could identify the obvious catastrophic consequences of the problems that were emerging and could see broadly where they came from, but didn't know exactly what the problem's root was, because it had come out of the blue compared with what it had experienced on the dyno.

    What's particularly alarming is that this does seem to be a regression from where Honda had got to in the middle of the season.

    Its performance was obviously still lacking, and its engine is still underpowered on the internal combustion side, even with upgrades through the year to try to trim the deficit.

    But it had at least got to a point where its cars were finishing races, which allowed Alonso to score some points when the Aston Martin was heavily upgraded.

    Either side of the summer break, Alonso had five straight finishes, and Stroll got two of his six finishes this year in three races before the summer break too.

    Orihara acknowledged that reliability was simply better in the middle of the season, but that has been replaced by "several issues" in recent races.

    Some of this trend could be related to the mileage of parts - components simply coming to the end of their life cycle - or to just a case of Honda trying to run the engine harder for performance and going over the edge.

    But with the battery issue that emerged in Brazil specifically, it's too early to say what the trigger was.

    Honda has not made the progress it would have hoped for this season in terms of the all-round performance of the engine, and at best has only achieved some of its performance targets in very specific circumstances.

    So the fact that reliability has got worse, without a clear upside, puts Honda under pressure and brings an unfortunate amount of negative scrutiny again, right at the end of a year in which it has had to come through quite a lot of adversity.

    It is also a curveball for the development work Honda is doing on 2027, when it needs to take a step in both performance and reliability.

    Everything that has been indicated so far is that there will not be a major step at the start of the new season. There's already a plan to phase in further upgrades through the year. But that work will already be deeply under way.

    Troubleshooting takes away human resource, limited dyno hours which are restricted under F1's engine development limits, money that comes under the engine cost cap, and simply time because the clock is ticking to the start of 2027.

    First the cooling issue in Mexico, whatever that was exactly, and now this on the battery side, takes Honda's focus away from a path that it would ideally be a lot further down.

    Honda CEO Toshihiro Mibe was in Brazil, which Orihara said has a galvanising effect on the staff - "but on the other hand, we had a battery issue twice this weekend".

    It was not exactly how Honda's F1 operation would have liked to present itself in front of the big boss. But, as Orihara noted, Mibe at least "knows what is happening trackside".

    "He will bring this experience to the Japanese side and also boost our development," said Orihara.

    If there was ever any possibility of complacency creeping in about how much work Honda has to do to reach the necessary standard, the CEO seeing first-hand the kind of problems still being encountered this deep into 2026 will have stamped it out.
