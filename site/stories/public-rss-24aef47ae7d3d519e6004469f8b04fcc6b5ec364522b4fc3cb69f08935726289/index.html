    格罗斯让：梅赛德斯没有「挖坑」，而是托举安东内利更上一层楼

    前哈斯车手罗曼·格罗斯让在F1官方播客《超越发车格》中表示，安东内利天赋出众，但从外部看，梅赛德斯和他身边管理团队的支持同样关键：新秀赛季后半段陷入低谷时，车队「没有挖个坑把他推进去，而是帮他提升」；今年澳大利亚站开局不顺，车队也「没有互相指责」，而是重建赛车、支持他。（来源：Formula 1）

    罗曼·格罗斯让在最新一期《超越发车格》中，剖析了梅赛德斯在基米·安东内利惊艳的2026赛季中所扮演的角色。

    罗曼·格罗斯让解释了梅赛德斯在幕后给予基米·安东内利的支持，如何帮助这位意大利车手把表现提升到新的层次，并领跑车手积分榜。

    在经历了起伏的新秀赛季之后，安东内利借助车队在本赛季初段的统治力，赢下前六站中的五场，掌控了积分榜，领先队友乔治·拉塞尔。

    除了巴塞罗那站退赛和银石站第15名之外，他一直稳定地跑进前三，这进一步帮助他建立起领先这位英国车手81分的可观优势。

    在F1官方播客《超越发车格》的最新一期节目中，前哈斯车手格罗斯让表示，他相信安东内利的出色成绩来自两方面：一是他自身的能力，二是梅赛德斯一贯力挺他的方式，即便在他的低谷时期也是如此。

    「他天赋惊人，是一名了不起的赛车手，但我从外部看到的是，梅赛德斯和他身边的管理团队做得有多好。」他说。

    「去年开局不错，赛季后半段很艰难，之后他回来了。车队不是挖个坑把他推进去，而是真正帮助他提升，给了他大量支持，让他知道车队永远会站在他身后。

    「今年澳大利亚站开局并不如他所愿，但没有任何人互相指责。他们重建了赛车，支持他。我觉得作为车手，当你从共事的人那里得到这种程度的信任，意义重大，也会帮助你成为真正的自己。」

    安东内利本赛季高光时刻不少，但最重要的或许是在蒙扎主场从发车区末尾一路冲到冠军，展现出可能让他成为史上最年轻世界冠军的实力。

    被问到是否认为这位20岁车手是世代天才时，格罗斯让回答：「我觉得基米就在那个级别。我觉得他驾驶赛车的方式与外面很多人都非常不同。

    「这种方式效果非常好，而且除此之外，似乎没有什么压力能影响到他，这非常了不起。到现在，他应该已经体会过领跑世界锦标赛、为梅赛德斯比赛的压力了。

    「基米喜欢活在好的混乱里。他不介意身边的喧闹、骚动和车迷。有些人就是这样，能从中汲取能量；而对另一些人来说，这会消耗他们的能量。在这一点上，我觉得基米非常出色。

    「我就需要一些独处的时间，我常常属于那一类人。我喜欢独处一会儿，让大脑关机，但我觉得基米能在公众场合做到这一点，比如和朋友聊天。这不会消耗他的能量，反而会给他能量——就像睡个午觉能给别人补充能量一样。」

    （来源：Formula 1 官网，摘自 F1 官方播客《超越发车格》）

    Romain Grosjean dissected the role Mercedes have played in Kimi Antonelli’s stunning 2026 season in the latest episode of Beyond The Grid.

    Romain Grosjean has explained how the support Mercedes have offered Kimi Antonelli behind the scenes has enabled the Italian to unlock a new level of performance and lead the Drivers’ Championship.

    Following a rollercoaster rookie campaign, Antonelli capitalised on the team’s dominance in the early part of this season to win five of the opening six rounds and take control of the standings, leading his team mate George Russell.

    Consistent top-three finishes – with the exception of a DNF in Barcelona and P15 at Silverstone – have further aided him in building a considerable advantage of 81 points over the Briton.

    On the latest episode of the official F1 podcast Beyond The Grid, former Haas driver Grosjean shared his belief that Antonelli’s remarkable results come from a combination of his skill and the way in which Mercedes have regularly championed him, even at his low points.

    “He's incredibly talented and an amazing race car driver, but I think what I see from the outside is how good the management and Mercedes was around him,” he said.

    “Good start last year, rough second part of the season, came back and instead of digging a hole and putting him into that, they actually helped him elevate and gave him so much support that he knew the team was always going to be behind him.

    “Australia didn't really start the way he wanted this year, but there wasn't any finger pointing. They rebuilt the car, they supported him and I think as a driver, when you get that level of confidence from the people you work for, that means a lot and that helps you to become who you are.”

    Antonelli has enjoyed plenty of highlights this season, but arguably the most significant was his charge from the back of the grid to victory in his home race at Monza, demonstrating the calibre that could see him become the youngest-ever World Champion.

    Asked whether he considers the 20-year-old to be a generational talent, Grosjean replied: “I think Kimi is up there. I think he has a way of driving the car that is very different from quite a lot of people out there.

    “It works amazingly well and on top of that, it seems like no pressure can get to him, which is very impressive. By now he would have found out the pressure of leading a world championship and racing for Mercedes.

    “Kimi loves living in the good chaos. He doesn't mind the noise and the agitation and the fans around him. Some people are like that and they can just take energy of that. In some other people, that takes energy from them. And I think Kimi for that is fantastic.

    “I just need some time alone, and often I'm one of those. I like to be alone for a bit and turn off my brain, but I think Kimi does manage to do that in public, like talking with friends. It doesn't take him any energy. It's just like it gives him energy – like a nap could do to someone else.”
