    F1新引擎冻结规则可能提前回归的罕见原因

    F1承诺从2030年开始实施新的引擎规则，这可能导致在那时之前回归引擎冻结规则。通用汽车原计划从2029年开始为凯迪拉克制造V6引擎，但由于规则变化，这一计划已被搁置。

    一级方程式赛车从2030年开始承诺其新的引擎规定，这预示着在此之前将恢复引擎冻结规则——但原因非常特殊。

    一级方程式赛车之前因成本原因，在2007-13年的大部分V8时代冻结了引擎开发，然后在2022-25年的空气动力学规定期间，在COVID大流行期间的财务压力和全新2026年引擎的到来之间冻结了引擎开发。

    这次，对于当前引擎规定的最后一年半可能出现的引擎冻结，实际上是由如果采取行动，新来者将在2030年获得优势的前景所驱动的。

    通用汽车计划从2029年开始为凯迪拉克按照当前的V6规则制造自己的引擎。即使今年有关于V8回归的很多讨论，GM也预计将在规则变更的原有预期下使用V6两年。

    现在情况并非如此，通用汽车已经搁置了V6项目——它表示已经在单缸上做了一些初步工作。由于V8保持了相同的缸径尺寸，这些工作并没有浪费，尽管一些竞争对手对通用汽车是否真的打算在当前规则变得岌岌可危后建造V6表示怀疑。

    无论如何，凯迪拉克将继续成为法拉利的客户，直到2030年的规则变更。

    但是，将凯迪拉克发动机的首次亮相推迟到V8规则中，又产生了新的问题。

    由于当前制造商不想让凯迪拉克从现在到2030年仅仅将资源全部投入到V8中而获得优势，因此出现了V6发动机开发冻结的前景。

    如果当前的引擎一直开发到2029年底，现有的制造商将同时做两件事：改进今天的V6和设计V8。

    通用汽车只做一件事，它将在2030年到来，已经把整个预算都花在了那个重要的引擎上。

    大多数制造商最终会将大部分，如果不是全部，资源转向2030年，以优先考虑新时代，并为所有车队建造和维护引擎仍然是一项艰巨的任务。

    但如果梅赛德斯或红牛或任何其他车队陷入激烈的锦标赛争夺中，他们将有义务继续寻找引擎改进中还能找到的任何收益。

    2030年的完整规则可能要到2027年中旬才能完成，因此《赛车》杂志了解到，当前的引擎可能从2028年中旬开始冻结。

    那时制造商拥有的任何性能，他们将保持到2029年底。

    这有其自身的后果。现在落后的任何制造商实际上有半年的时间来赶上。在那之后，直到V8引擎到来，排名将锁定。

    这次冻结将停止ADUO，这是一个旨在帮助陷入困境的制造商的追赶系统，但它可能不会永远消失。

    在这个时代，它并没有完全按照预期工作，但帮助任何下次失利的制造商的想法仍然很可能会被写入规则中。

    正如我们在之前的引擎冻结时代所经常看到的那样，制造商总是热衷于在可靠性修改或其他冻结规则允许的任何范围内推动每一个可能的边界。所以，即使有冻结，也不要期待2028年和2029年的引擎性能会完全静止。

    与此同时，任何在引擎上遇到困难的人——现在特别指本田——突然需要在还可能尝试追赶竞争者的时候，和时间展开一场更加紧张的比赛。

    英文原文

    Formula 1 committing to its new engine regulations from as soon as 2030 is set to mean the return of engine freeze rules before then - but for an unusual reason.

    F1 previously froze engine development for cost reasons for the majority of its last V8 era over 2007-13 and then for the duration of the 2022-25 aero regulations between the financial strains of the COVID pandemic and the arrival of the all-new 2026 engines.

    This time, the prospect of a new engine freeze for probably the last year and a half of the current engine regulations is actually driven by the prospect of a newcomer having an advantage for 2030 if action isn't taken.

    General Motors had planned to build its own engine for Cadillac, to the current V6 rules, from 2029. Even with so much talk this year about the prospect of V8s returning, GM would have expected to get two years' use out of a V6 under the original expectation of when the rules would change.

    That's now not the case, and GM has shelved the V6 - on which it says it had done some initial single-cylinder work. Since the V8 keeps the same cylinder dimensions, that work isn't wasted, though some rivals are sceptical about whether GM ever really intended to build a V6 once it was clear the current rules' days were numbered.

    Regardless, Cadillac will remain a Ferrari customer until the 2030 rule change now.

    But delaying the Cadillac engine debut until the V8 rules creates a new problem.

    The prospect of a V6 engine development freeze has arisen because current manufacturers don't want Cadillac to have an advantage from just throwing its resources fully at the V8 from now until 2030.

    If the current engines kept developing right up to the end of 2029, the existing manufacturers would be doing two jobs at once: improving today's V6 and designing the V8.

    GM would only be doing one, and it would arrive in 2030 having spent its whole budget on the engine that matters.

    Most manufacturers would be switching most, if not all, of their resources onto 2030 eventually to prioritise the new era, and building and maintaining engines for all their teams is still a big task.

    But if Mercedes or Red Bull or whoever were locked in a tight championship fight, they would be competitively obliged to keep seeking whatever gains could still be found in engine improvements.

    The full 2030 rules probably won't be finished before mid-2027, so The Race understands the current engines could be frozen from mid-2028.

    Then whatever performance the manufacturers have by then, they keep until the end of 2029.

    That has consequences of its own. Any manufacturer that's behind now has, in effect, a season and a half to catch up. After that, the order is locked in until the V8s arrive.

    That freeze will halt ADUO, the catch-up system that's meant to help struggling manufacturers, but it probably won't be gone for good.

    It hasn't quite worked as intended in this era, but the idea of helping any manufacturer that drops the ball next time is still likely to be enshrined in the regulations.

    And as we saw so often in the previous engine freeze eras, manufacturers are always keen to push every boundary possible in what development they can get away with under the guise of reliability modifications or whatever other leeway the freeze rules allow. So don't expect engine performance to be completely static over 2028 and 2029 even with a freeze.

    And in the meantime, anyone struggling with their engine - which right now means Honda in particular - is suddenly in a much tighter race against time to get on terms with the competition while it's still possible to try to catch up.
