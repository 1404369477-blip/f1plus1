    拉斯维加斯大奖赛2026：赢家与输家

    The Race评选2026年F1拉斯维加斯大奖赛的赢家与输家，法拉利双车手汉密尔顿与勒克莱尔因内斗碰撞双双受罚，拉塞尔争胜未果撞墙退赛。

    一场持续不断的领先争夺战是拉斯维加斯大奖赛的最大看点，但参与其中的车手应对方式却大相径庭。

    以下是我们评选出的这场F1拉斯维加斯史上最精彩比赛中的赢家与输家：

    在这个轮对轮厮杀近乎疯狂的比赛日里，最容易出事的车手似乎注定是法拉利队友刘易斯·汉密尔顿和夏尔·勒克莱尔。

    两人一路杀入领先车阵后，在汉密尔顿因与兰多·诺里斯争夺时切弯被罚10秒之前，两人就因互相缠斗而愈发不满。

    但即便如此，两人最终还是发生了不必要的碰撞——这让汉密尔顿名次大幅下滑，也让勒克莱尔吃到了罚单。

    在墨西哥站事件后，两人公开表示法拉利队内一切良好，但拉斯维加斯站表明，仍有许多问题需要解决。——乔恩·诺布尔

    有机会争胜却在缠斗中处于下风，最终赛车撞墙退赛，拉斯维加斯站几乎就是拉塞尔整个赛季的缩影。

    他与维斯塔潘的较量，感觉有点像一个小得多的弟弟试图去摔赢一个大得多、年长得多的哥哥。即便拉塞尔看似占得上风，维斯塔潘也能轻松反扑。而在其余时间里，拉塞尔面对逆境显得有些无可奈何。

    在试图反超队友基米·安东内利时发生撞车，他说道：“我知道如果我想赢得这场比赛，就必须在最后几圈前处在第二的位置。”

    “基米当时没有超车模式，我选择了一次高风险的超车动作，并付出了代价。但我不后悔。当然，我对此感到失望。但我本可以保守一些，那样我会以第三名完赛。”

    他为了争取到能与维斯塔潘抗衡的位置而撞车，这在一定程度上印证了这位红牛车手的评价——拉塞尔不在他的水平上，即便我们加上一个前提：这是就今年目前的情况而言。——杰克·本尼恩

    在这样一场混乱的比赛中，领先位置的争夺出现了卡丁车级别的多次交替，像维斯塔潘这样的赛车猛兽最终胜出，并不令人意外。

    维斯塔潘的判断非常出色，他保持冷静，在驾驶和策略上都胜过了两位梅赛德斯车手，赢得了六场比赛中的第四场胜利。——JS

    一切似乎都在朝着安东内利有利的方向发展，他采用的非对称策略让他在最后一段换上软胎，看起来使他成为维斯塔潘争夺胜利的主要威胁，这场较量将在冲刺到终点线的过程中展开。

    但在这个他出色地驾驭了2026款动力单元各种特性的年份里，安东内利却犯了错。

    他为了与维斯塔潘保持理想差距而收油，结果却打乱了他的能量部署——因为能量管理算法的反应是在他不想消耗过多电力时却消耗了过多电力。

    这一结果让他对迈凯伦双雄兰多·诺里斯和奥斯卡·皮亚斯特里失去了势头，意味着他在本可以再夺一场精彩胜利的这一天错失了领奖台。 - JN

    考虑到迈凯伦在拉斯维加斯的直线速度“糟糕透顶”，诺里斯和皮亚斯特里双双登上领奖台对迈凯伦而言是了不起的超额成就。

    尽管存在这一弱点，两人都跑出了非常聪明的比赛，充分利用了列车中的行驶，并在超车位置上做出了明智的选择。

    这对皮亚斯特里尤其令人鼓舞，他能够从第17位冲到第三位，这是自五月初迈阿密站以来他首次登上大奖赛领奖台。 - JS

    林德布拉德在比赛初期一度高居第三，但后来在安全车下打滑，犯下了赛车界的一大忌讳。

    他说自己经历了一次奇怪的状态变化，称这是“我经历过的最奇怪的事情”，但他也补充说，他想为所发生的事情承担责任。

    他最大的遗憾是什么？“我可以在接下来几场比赛中争夺第八、第九、第十名。那太棒了。作为新秀赛季，驾驶中游赛车争夺第三、第四、第五名，这样的机会并不多。我觉得这才是让我难受的地方。”

    林德布拉德觉得他最近在墨西哥排位赛中错过了类似的机会。你能看出他非常渴望一场头条表现，来证明这个许多人尚未注意到的强劲赛季。——JB

    目前来看，车队积分榜第五名似乎仍然遥不可及，但阿尔派在拉斯维加斯已经做得不能再多了，因为小红牛的两辆赛车都遇到了麻烦。

    皮埃尔·加斯利一度跑到第三位，并凭借一场精彩的表现以第五名完赛，在终点线前与安东内利展开争夺，尽管由于过热问题，他在50圈中的45圈里都调低了动力输出。

    阿尔派将对此进行复盘，但最终加斯利还是很满意，即便在他到达第三位的那一刻，他觉得自己本可以为胜利而战。

    弗朗哥·科拉平托的比赛则更像是一场过山车，他需要一些帮助，得益于包括夏尔·勒克莱尔（罚时）和拉塞尔（撞车后以第六名完赛）在内的多位车手在比赛后期出现的问题。

    但他从早期使用旧软胎毫无抓地力的困境中恢复过来——起步时他上升了三个位置，但很快又丢了回去——最终稳稳进入前十，无论前方车手遇到什么问题。

    距离小红牛车队的制造商积分差距是27分，还剩两场比赛。- JB

    这是第一场真正让人感觉哈贾尔有争胜威胁的比赛，但在早期安全车后重新起步时，他的前翼立刻弯曲，在灾难性的情况下崩盘了。

    如果你以为他赛后会很沮丧，他的言论简直令人不安。

    他说：“这甚至无关积分或领奖台，只是破坏了我的乐趣。”

    “我来这里是为了和世界上最优秀的车手比赛，结果却落到后面，只能独自驾驶。这正在消磨我开车的动力。真是太烦人了。我想我永远不知道自己本可以取得什么样的成绩。”

    但你可以选择对此持更加积极的看法。他提出了一个可以说更好的比赛策略，即在前方不断交换位置以试图拉开差距，而维斯塔潘似乎在赛道上否决了这一策略。他也是迄今为止对胜利最具威胁的一次，而且正如在巴库那样，他在这类街道赛道上证明了自己是一个真正的竞争者。

    从结果来看是输家，但在表现上绝对不是。——JB

    比赛结束时，肯定会有非常多的人盯着计时塔问：卡洛斯·塞恩斯到底是怎么跑到第八名的？

    这一切的基础是令人印象深刻的开场几圈，塞恩斯从第18位升到了第11位。

    此后，他在安全车之后一直紧咬领先集团，然后在终点线前抓住机会，超越了被阻挡的拉塞尔（拉塞尔被汉密尔顿挡在后面），拿下本赛季最好的第八名，远超目前威廉姆斯赛车应有的位置。——JS

    A relentless lead battle was the defining feature of the Las Vegas Grand Prix, but the way the drivers involved in it dealt with it varied drastically.

    Here's our pick of winners and losers from Formula 1's most entertaining Vegas race yet:

    On a day of frantic wheel-to-wheel racing there seemed almost an inevitability that the drivers most likely to hit trouble would be Ferrari team-mates Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc.

    Having hauled themselves into the train of cars at the front, frustrations were growing between the pair about battling each other prior to Hamilton picking up his 10-second penalty for cutting the track in a fight with Lando Norris.

    But even after that, the pair ended up needlessly colliding - dumping Hamilton down the order and earning Leclerc a penalty.

    After all the public talk of things being rosy between the Ferrari duo after their Mexico incident, Las Vegas has shown there is still a lot that needs to be fixed. - Jon Noble

    Contending for the win but inferior in battle, and ending the race in the wall, Vegas just about sums up Russell's season.

    His battle with Verstappen felt a bit like a much, much younger brother trying to wrestle a much older, bigger one. Even when Russell looked to have the upper hand, Verstappen stormed back with ease. And the rest of the time Russell looked a bit hapless in the face of adversity.

    On his crash, while trying to repass team-mate Kimi Antonelli, he said: "I knew if I wanted to win the race, I needed to be in second going into the last couple of laps.

    "Kimi didn't have the overtake mode and I went for a high-risk move and paid the price. But I don't regret it. Of course, I'm disappointed with it. But I could have been conservative and I would have finished in third."

    The fact that he crashed trying to even get in a position to fight Verstappen lends some credence to the Red Bull driver's assessment that Russell is not on his level is true, even if we add the caveat that that's as things stand this year. - Jack Benyon

    In such a chaotic race with karting-levels of passes for the lead, it's little surprise that a racing animal such as Verstappen emerged on top.

    Verstappen judged things beautifully, keeping a calm head, outdriving and out-strategising both Mercedes drivers for a fourth victory in six races. - JS

    It all seemed to be coming to Antonelli as his offset strategy that put him on softs for the final stint appeared to leave him as Verstappen's main threat for the win, as part of a battle that would play out in the dash to the line.

    But in a year where he has been brilliant in managing the quirks of these 2026 power units, Antonelli got it wrong.

    Having lifted off to keep the ideal gap to Verstappen, that ended up messing up his deployment - as the response from the energy-management algorithms was to burn too much power when he didn't want it to be used up.

    The result was a loss of momentum to McLaren duo Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri that meant he missed out on a podium on a day when he had it in his hands to pull off another brilliant win. - JN

    Considering McLaren had "horrendous" straightline speed in Las Vegas, Norris and Piastri earning a double podium is a fantastic overachievement for McLaren.

    They both drove really clever races despite that weakness, making the most of running in the train and being smart with where they made their overtakes.

    That was particularly encouraging for Piastri, who was able to charge from 17th to third for his first grand podium since Miami in early-May. - JS

    As high as third in the early running, Lindblad later committed one of racing's faux pas by spinning under the safety car.

    He said he'd had a weird shift which was "the strangest thing I've experienced", but did caveat that he wanted to take the blame for what had happened.

    His biggest regret? "I can race for P8, P9, P10 in the next couple of races. That's fantastic. Not often you get to race for P3, P4, P5 in a midfield car in your rookie season. I think that's what hurts me."

    Lindblad felt he missed a similar chance in Mexico qualifying recently. You can tell he's desperate for a headline performance to back up what has been a strong season that many people haven't yet noticed. - JB

    It still feels like fifth place in the constructors' championship is out of reach, but Alpine couldn't have done much more in Las Vegas, as both Racing Bulls cars hit trouble.

    Pierre Gasly ran as high as third, and pulled off a majestic drive to fifth fighting Antonelli to the flag, despite running 45 of the 50 laps with the power turned down due to an overheating issue.

    Alpine will review that, but ultimately Gasly was happy even if at the point he reached third he felt he could fight for the win.

    Franco Colapinto's race was much more of a rollercoaster, and he needed some help from late issues from various drivers including Charles Leclerc (penalty) and Russell (crash to finish sixth).

    But he recovered from having no grip on used softs early on - he gained three places at the start but lost those quickly - to a sure-thing top 10 regardless of the issues of those ahead.

    The constructors' points gap to Racing Bulls is 27 with two races to go. - JB

    This was the first race where it really felt like Hadjar was a threat for victory, and it fell apart in catastrophic circumstances as his front wing buckled right after the restart following the early safety car.

    If you expected him to be sombre after the race, his comments were borderline troubling.

    "It's not even about the points or the podium, it's just ruining my fun," he said.

    "I'm here to race the best guys in the world and I end up at the back and having to race alone. It's just ruining my motivation to drive. It's just so annoying. I guess I never know what kind of result I could have had."

    But you can choose to take a much more positive view of this. He suggested what would have arguably been a better race strategy, to keep swapping positions at the front to try and build a gap, which Verstappen seemingly vetoed on track. He was the most threatening for a win that he's looked so far too and, as in Baku, he showed on these sorts of street circuits that he's a proper contender.

    A loser from the result, but certainly not in performance. - JB

    There will have been an awful lot of people looking at the timing tower at the end of the race asking: how on earth did Carlos Sainz end up eighth?

    The bedrock of that was an impressive set of opening laps as Sainz went from 18th to 11th.

    Thereafter, he kept sight of the leading pack after the safety car, then picked off a baulked Russell (who was caught behind Hamilton) with an opportunistic move just before the line to take a season's-best eighth, far above where the current Williams should be. - JS
