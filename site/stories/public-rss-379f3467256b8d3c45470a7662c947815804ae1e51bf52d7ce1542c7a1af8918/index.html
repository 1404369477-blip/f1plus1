    弗雷德·瓦瑟尔支持FIA对勒克莱尔与汉密尔顿碰撞的裁决

    在2026年拉斯维加斯大奖赛中，查尔斯·勒克莱尔在7号弯道撞上刘易斯·汉密尔顿导致其失控后，法拉利车队负责人弗雷德·瓦瑟尔支持了国际汽联的裁决，并明确表示责任所在。

    弗雷德·瓦瑟尔支持FIA裁决后查尔斯·勒克莱尔与刘易斯·汉密尔顿在拉斯维加斯的碰撞

    弗雷德·瓦瑟尔支持FIA的"非常明确"的处罚，指责查尔斯·勒克莱尔在拉斯维加斯导致刘易斯·汉密尔顿失控

    对于法拉利车迷来说，这是最新的Scuderia 法拉利 F1新闻，包括车手更新、技术分析和来自马拉内洛及以外的赛事反应。

    在 stewards 对车队中的某位车手进行处罚时，一级方程式车队老板通常会迅速采取措施减轻打击。但在2026年拉斯维加斯大奖赛中，查尔斯·勒克莱尔在7号弯道撞上刘易斯·汉密尔顿导致其失控后，法拉利车队负责人弗雷德·瓦瑟尔采取了相反的做法——支持国际汽联的裁决，并明确表示责任所在。

    在内华达州度过的一个受伤的夜晚后，F1电视采访了瓦瑟尔，直接问他是否只有勒克莱尔一个人违反了团队指令，还是两位车手在争夺第六名的激烈战斗中都负有责任。

    “不，我认为……国际汽联的决定非常明确，”瓦瑟尔回答道。

    刘易斯·汉密尔顿，法拉利，查尔斯·勒克莱尔，法拉利

    裁判的官方判决几乎没有留下任何解释的空间。根据国际体育条例附录L，第四章，第二条第d）款，裁判在文件60中指出，16号赛车在7号弯从44号赛车相当远的后方发起了内侧切入。

    应用FIA驾驶标准指南，官员判定勒克莱尔的前轴在弯顶处至少没有与汉密尔顿的镜子并排 - 结论是勒克莱尔"没有获得在7号弯的权利，并对由此产生的碰撞负有全部责任"。

    由此产生的10秒时间处罚使勒克莱尔在终点线时降至第10位，而汉密尔顿恢复到第7位 - 这使得法拉利从原本在发车线上排名第10和第11的赛车中冲入领先集团，却只得到了微薄的一分。

    瓦瑟尔："我们又一次摧毁了比赛"

    让瓦瑟尔最恼火的不只是失去的分数，还有时机。就在墨西哥城第一圈发生纠缠后的三周后，就在法拉利的维修区在五圈前通过无线电向勒克莱尔发出警告后不久，同样的故障再次发生。

    “我知道他已经道歉了，我知道他很抱歉，但今天我们又一次摧毁了比赛，我们本可以做得更好，”瓦瑟尔说。 “我不知道比赛的结果会怎样，因为比赛非常混乱，但我们不能像这样丢分。而且，不仅仅是分数，还有每个人的动力，工作的回报，努力的回报，这对我是巨大的挫折。”

    瓦瑟尔没有浪费任何时间来表达他的愤怒，在冷却圈期间打破车队无线电，首先召唤勒克莱尔。在之后接受F1电视采访时，他确认了最初的会议已经举行，同时暗示在情绪冷却后会有进一步的谈话。 “我已经和查尔斯今晚讨论过了，但现在我最好消化我的挫折，”他补充道。

    后来在拉斯维加斯面对书面媒体时，瓦瑟尔澄清了第一次遭遇有多么直率：“那不是讨论。我只是说了。” 他拒绝了在尾流严重的比赛中实施严格车队指令的呼吁，他认为问题归结于基本的专业性：“这不是规则的问题。你能做些什么不同的事情，除了你必须尊重你的队友？”

    Fred Vasseur backs FIA ruling after Charles Leclerc's Las Vegas clash with Lewis Hamilton

    Fred Vasseur backs the FIA's "quite clear" penalty blaming Charles Leclerc for spinning Lewis Hamilton in Las Vegas

    For Ferrari fans. The latest Scuderia Ferrari F1 news, driver updates, technical analysis and race reaction from Maranello and beyond.

    Formula 1 team bosses usually rush to soften the blow when stewards penalise one of their drivers. After Charles Leclerc clipped Lewis Hamilton into a spin at Turn 7 during the 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix, Ferrari team principal Fred Vasseur took the opposite approach - endorsing the FIA’s ruling and leaving no doubt over where responsibility lay.

    Speaking to F1 TV after a bruised night in Nevada, Vasseur was asked directly whether Leclerc alone had defied team instructions or if both drivers shared fault in their heated battle for sixth place.

    "No, I think... the decision of the FIA was quite clear," Vasseur answered.

    Lewis Hamilton, Ferrari, Charles Leclerc, Ferrari

    The stewards’ official verdict left equally little room for interpretation. Reviewing video and onboard footage under Appendix L, Chapter IV, Article 2 d) of the International Sporting Code, the stewards noted in Document 60 that Car 16 launched its inside lunge at Turn 7 from a considerable distance behind Car 44.

    Applying the FIA Driving Standards Guidelines, officials determined Leclerc’s front axle was not at least alongside Hamilton’s mirror at the apex - concluding Leclerc "had not earned the right to the corner at Turn 7 and was wholly to blame for the resulting collision".

    The resulting 10-second time penalty dropped Leclerc to 10th at the flag, while Hamilton recovered to seventh - leaving Ferrari with a meagre point return from a race where both cars had surged into the lead pack from 10th and 11th on the grid.

    Vasseur: “Once again we destroyed the race”

    What aggravated Vasseur most was not just the lost points, but the timing. Just three weeks after clear-the-air talks following their Lap 1 tangle in Mexico City - and moments after Ferrari’s pitwall had radioed a warning to Leclerc five corners prior - the same breakdown repeated itself.

    "I know that he apologised, I know that he was sorry, but once again we destroyed the race today, that we could have done much better," Vasseur said. "I don't know what could be the outcome of the race because it was quite chaotic, but we can't lose points like this. And more than points, it's the motivation of everybody, the reward of the job done, the reward of the effort, and this - it's a huge frustration for me."

    Vasseur wasted zero time making his anger felt, breaking onto team radio during the cooldown lap to summon Leclerc "before anything else". Speaking to F1 TV afterwards, he confirmed the initial meeting had already taken place while hinting further talks would follow once emotions cooled. "I already had the discussion with Charles tonight, but now it's better for me to digest my frustration," he added.

    Later addressing written media in Las Vegas, Vasseur clarified just how blunt that first encounter had been: "It was not a discussion. I spoke." Rejecting calls to impose strict team orders in a slipstream-heavy race, he argued the issue came down to basic professionalism: "It's not a matter of rules. What can you do differently, except that you have to respect your team-mates?"
