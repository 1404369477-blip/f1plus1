    韩泰或加入F1轮胎供应合同竞标

    据报道，世界拉力锦标赛轮胎供应商韩泰可能参与F1独家轮胎供应合同的竞标，与倍耐力和普利司通展开竞争，招标程序将于明年开启。

    韩泰或加入F1轮胎供应合同竞标

    倍耐力将在明年F1招标程序开启时面临来自世界拉力锦标赛轮胎供应商以及普利司通的竞争

    韩泰是世界拉力锦标赛的独家轮胎供应商

    这是F1全球影响力和人气持续增长的又一鲜明例证：又一家有实力的竞争者出现，竞标其独家轮胎供应合同。

    倍耐力于2011年接替普利司通成为F1唯一轮胎供应商，在其头十年的大部分时间里，当续约时刻到来时，它几乎未遇到任何有组织的挑战。近年来情况发生了变化。普利司通在2010年以成本及全球金融危机后的“战略调整”为由退出F1后，在2023年最近一次招标过程中重新参与竞标，并且据了解正在为下一次招标准备提案。

    如今，Motorsport.com获悉，世界拉力锦标赛的独家轮胎供应商韩泰正在为明年启动的、涵盖2028年后F1时代的招标流程组建竞标团队。该公司曾在2018年竞标2020-23年的合同但未成功，该合同随后因疫情延长了一年。

    倍耐力目前的合同期为2025-28年，因为该公司——与FIA和F1一起——去年6月共同行使了一年续约选择权。在上一次招标过程中，本赛季向新规则过渡期间的技术稳定性被认为尤为重要。鉴于2030年即将到来的变化，潜在竞标者必须向FIA保证他们能够实现这一点。

    韩泰已经在F1大奖赛周末中扮演过一个小角色，曾在2021年和2022年担任W系列赛的供应商。这家韩国公司涉足赛车运动已超过三十年，期间曾多次担任DTM的独家供应商，以及地区性F3和F4系列赛的供应商。2022至2026年，它为Formula E的Gen3时代赛车提供轮胎，之后普利司通接过了该合同。

    韩泰竞赛部门首席执行官右——图中他与现代汽车的Andrew Wheatley一同出现在日本拉力赛上——在过去两个赛季中已到访过多场大奖赛，与F1相关方会面。

    2025年，它被任命为世界拉力锦标赛的独家轮胎供应商；今年，它又被国际汽联指定为该管理机构‘全球合作伙伴计划’的成员。

    包括韩泰竞赛部门首席执行官Steven Cho在内的代表们，在过去两个赛季中已出席过多场大奖赛，最近一次是上周末在拉斯维加斯；据了解，该公司已经用一辆基于F2的‘骡车’进行了原型轮胎测试。

    近年来，该公司已为国际汽联（FIA）的两项重大世界锦标赛提供轮胎，并与管理机构建立了更紧密的联系，这将使其此次申办比上一次更具可信度。电动方程式（Formula E）是一张强有力的名片，因为其环保目标要求仅使用两种不同的轮胎配方，且均由可持续材料制成。在上一次招标过程中，可持续性是倍耐力的制胜一招，因为该公司承诺从2024年起为F1引入经森林管理委员会认证的轮胎。

    尽管总部位于韩国，韩泰在德国汉诺威设有研发设施，并在匈牙利拥有一家工厂，目前该工厂正在进行4.5亿欧元的扩建。倍耐力在其位于土耳其伊兹米特的工厂生产包括F1轮胎在内的赛车运动产品，与普通公路轮胎同线生产。

    任何进入招标过程的公司都面临重大挑战，因为它将在当前技术规则实施的第四年才进入，因此必须提出非常有说服力的理由，证明其至少能与现有产品匹敌。尽管倍耐力的轮胎多年来经常受到批评，但这是独家供应性质所决定的。

    当前的轮胎需要满足一套复杂且往往相互矛盾的要求：它们必须在热性能和结构上足够坚固，同时具有可预测的退化曲线，以促进观赏性和策略多样性。过去15年的经验表明，很容易在任一方向上过犹不及。

    国际汽联主席穆罕默德·本·苏拉耶姆在6月宣布续约时表示：“倍耐力多年来一直是国际汽联F1世界锦标赛的重要合作伙伴，在我们这项运动的最高水平上持续提供高标准的表现、创新和安全性。续约至2028年底为锦标赛提供了稳定性。”

    这一点，再加上倍耐力品牌在赛道周围的普遍存在，清楚地描绘了任何竞标者必须提供的东西：雄厚的财力，以及一种保证——当他们的轮胎装上时，除了胎侧上的名字之外，没有人会注意到任何区别。

    Hankook tipped to enter race for F1 tyre supply contract

    Pirelli is set to face competition from World Rally Championship tyre supplier as well as Bridgestone when the Formula 1 tender process opens next year

    Hankook is sole tyre supplier to the World Rally Championship

    It's another vivid demonstration of Formula 1's ever-growing global reach and popularity that another serious contender has emerged to bid for its exclusive tyre-supply contract.

    Pirelli took over from Bridgestone as F1's sole tyre supplier in 2011 and for much of its first decade it faced little in the way of concerted challenge when renewal time rolled around. That has changed in recent years. Having walked away from F1 in 2010, citing costs and a "strategic realignment" following the global financial crisis, Bridgestone threw its hat in the ring during the most recent tender process in 2023, and is known to be preparing a pitch for the next one.

    Now Motorsport.com understands that Hankook, exclusive tyre supplier to the World Rally Championship, is assembling a bid for the tender process which opens next year for the post-2028 F1 era. It was previously an unsuccessful bidder in 2018 for the contract covering 2020-23, which was then extended by a year owing to the pandemic.

    Pirelli's current contract runs from 2025-28, since the company – along with the FIA and F1 – mutually exercised a one-year extension clause last June. During the last tender process, technical stability during the transition to a new formula this season was regarded as a particularly important. Given the impending changes in 2030, potential bidders will have to assure the FIA they can achieve this.

    While the FIA assesses tenders based on the bidder's technical capacity, sustainability credentials, and the operational capacity to manage the supply process seamlessly, F1 also has a voice in the final decision on commercial grounds. Pirelli is a conspicuously big spender on trackside signage as well as facilitating VIP perks such as the ‘Hot Laps' programme.

    Hankook has already played a small role in grand prix weekends as a supplier to the W Series in 2021 and 2022. The South Korean company has been involved in motor racing for over three decades, at various times being exclusive supplier to the DTM, along with regional F3 and F4 series. It supplied Formula E's Gen3-era cars from 2022-26 before Bridgestone took on that contract.

    Hankook Competition CEO Steven Cho (right), seen here with Hyundai's Andrew Wheatley on Rally Japan, has visited several grands prix to meet F1 stakeholders over the past two seasons

    It was appointed sole tyre supplier of the World Rally Championship in 2025 and this year it was named by the FIA as a member of the governing body's ‘global partner programme'.

    Representatives including Hankook Competition CEO Steven Cho have attended several grands prix over the past two seasons, most recently last weekend in Las Vegas, and it's understood the company has already conducted testing of prototype tyres with an F2-based ‘mule car'.

    Having supplied two major FIA world championships in recent years, along with establishing much closer ties with the governing body, it would make this a much more credible bid than the previous one. Formula E is a strong calling card since its environmental aims call for just two different tyre compounds, both made from sustainable materials. Sustainability was Pirelli's finishing move in the last tender process, since it undertook to introduce Forest Stewardship Council-certified tyres to F1 from 2024 onwards.

    Although based in South Korea, Hankook has an R&D facility in Hanover, Germany, and a factory in Hungary currently undergoing a €450million expansion in Hungary. Pirelli makes its motorsport products, including the F1 tyres, alongside regular road tyres in its factory in Izmit, Turkey.

    Any company entering the tender process faces a significant challenge because it will be bidding to arrive in year four of the current technical regulations, so it would have to build a very convincing case that it could at least match the existing product. While Pirelli's tyres have come in for regular criticism over the years, this is in the nature of having exclusivity.

    The current tyres are expected to deliver on a complex and often contradictory set of requirements: they must be thermally and structurally robust, but with a predictable degradation curve to facilitate the spectacle and strategic variety. Experience of the past 15 years demonstrates how easy it is to overshoot either way.

    Hankook was Formula E's supplier through the Gen 3 era so, besides single-seater experience, it is accustomed to the kind of trackside signage deals that come with the territory

    "Pirelli has been an important partner to the FIA F1 world championship for many years, providing a consistently high standard of performance, innovation and safety at the highest level of our sport," said FIA president Mohammed Ben Sulayem when the contact extension was announced in June. "This extension through to the end of 2028 provides stability for the championship."

    That, along with the prevalence of Pirelli branding around the tracks, paints a clear picture of what any bidder has to offer: deep pockets, and a guarantee that when their tyres go on, nobody will notice any difference apart from the name on the sidewalls.
