    安东内利在新加坡大奖赛尽管四次违规却逃过处罚

    2026年新加坡大奖赛结束后，官方计时图显示冠军车手安东内利在62圈比赛中累计四次越界违规，但未受到处罚。

    尽管有四次违规，基米·安东内利如何在新加坡大奖赛赛道限界处罚中逃脱

    一个关于出站的技术性条款阻止了基米·安东内利在新加坡大奖赛中因国际汽联的处罚而失去领奖台

    对于梅赛德斯车迷来说，这里是关于银箭的最新消息，包括来自布拉克利和布里克斯沃斯的F1新闻、车手更新、赛车发展和比赛分析

    在2026年新加坡大奖赛的发车旗落下后，F1粉丝和赛道观察员在官方计时图形确认冠军领跑者安德烈亚·基米·安东内利在62圈比赛中累积了四次单独的赛道限界违规时，感到深深分歧。

    根据国际汽联制定的处罚指南，在一场大奖赛中累积四次违规应该触发自动五秒时间处罚。如果这项处罚被应用于安东内利的比赛时间，他艰苦争得的第三名成绩将瞬间消失，使他落后于麦凯轮车队的兰多·诺里斯，严重复杂化了他进入美国大奖赛时的积分领先局势。

    然而，当最终成绩公布时，安东内利仍然稳坐在领奖台上毫发无损。深入官方国际汽联文件和体育规则，可以确切地了解到为什么这位梅赛德斯车星逃脱了处罚——以及为什么这个决定引发了激烈的争论。

    文件88中详细列出的四次违规

    在滨海湾街道赛道的赛道限制监控主要集中在前2、7、16和19弯。根据国际汽联文件88，该文件由赛事裁判在当地时间22:53发布，安东内利被指控在四次不同场合离开了赛车道。

    第一击：20小时33分44秒在弯道2（圈速：1分37.395秒）

    第二击：20小时35分21秒在弯道2（圈速：1分37.605秒）

    第三击：21小时1分39秒在弯道19（圈速：1分37.221秒）

    第四击：21小时9分33秒在弯道7（圈速：出站圈）

    根据FIA一级方程式规则的附录L第四章第二条第2(c)款和第B1.8.6款，表面上似乎有四个条目符合时间处罚的门槛。追踪系统记录了针对车号12的四次红色违规标记，这立即激起了竞争对手粉丝基础的强烈不满，他们质疑冠军车手是否受到了特殊待遇。

    安东内利逃脱的核心解释直接源于他在7号弯的第四次记录违规行为。

    根据FIA的官方处罚指南，比赛中超车线有特定的解释："车手不能将时间设置在白色线条之外"。累积违规进程——第三次违规将获得黑白警告旗，第四次则触发五秒处罚——旨在惩罚车手在竞争性计时飞驰圈中反复获得微小优势的行为。

    安东内利的第四次违规发生在21小时09分33秒，当时他正在进行维修区后的出站圈。由于他处于出站圈，没有在计时表上建立或记录任何竞争性圈速。根据国际体育法典第12.4.1.e条款的严格解释，文件88中发出的唯一命令是删除显示的圈速。

    由于出站圈没有代表性的圈速可以从中扣除，并且因为7号弯道处没有在赛道外获得持续的体育优势或超越，裁判没有将此事件视为可能导致比赛中时间处罚的竞争性处罚。

    程序上的失误：缺失的黑白色旗帜

    在出站圈的技术性细节之外，一个次要的程序因素保护了安东内利免受赛后处罚：正式的警告结构。

    根据FIA的标准操作协议，在为随后的第四次违规行为分配时间处罚之前，赛事控制必须正式向车手出示黑白色旗帜。在涉及安全车阶段、维修区窗口混乱和通过脏空气的激烈比赛中，安东内利从未在计时器上被正式显示黑白色警告。在没有触发赛事控制中规定的中间警告的情况下，立即发出五秒的赛后处罚将违反FIA自己的执法协议。

    现在，安东内利虽然合法地保留了他在领奖台上的位置，但这一事件再次引发了对他驾驶精准度的审查。在整个2026赛季中，意大利人在赛道限制的遵守上一直是一个持续的挑战，由于他过于激进的弯道进入风格，导致几圈资格赛和比赛时段受到影响。

    值得注意的是，如果安东内利在标准飞驰圈而不是出站圈时越过了7号弯的白线，那么5秒的降级是不可避免的。相反，这位年轻的梅赛德斯车手带着102分的积分优势前往奥斯汀，尽管整个车队的维修区都在更加密切地关注他的赛车位置。

    How Kimi Antonelli escaped a Singapore GP track limits penalty despite four violations

    How a technicality on an out-lap prevented Kimi Antonelli from losing his Singapore GP podium to an FIA penalty

    For Mercedes fans. All the latest from the Silver Arrows, with F1 news, driver updates, car development and race analysis from Brackley and Brixworth.

    Following the chequered flag at the 2026 Singapore Grand Prix, Formula 1 fans and paddock observers were left deeply divided when official timing graphics confirmed that championship leader Andrea Kimi Antonelli had accumulated four separate track limit violations during the 62-lap race.

    Under the FIA’s established penalty guidelines, reaching four strikes in a grand prix is supposed to trigger an automatic five-second time penalty. Had that sanction been applied to Antonelli's race time, his hard-fought third-place finish would have instantly evaporated, demoting him behind McLaren's Lando Norris and severely complicating his championship lead heading into the United States Grand Prix.

    Yet, when the final classification was published, Antonelli remained untouched on the podium. A deep dive into the official FIA documentation and the governing body’s sporting code reveals exactly why the Mercedes star escaped sanction - and why the decision has sparked fierce debate.

    The four violations detailed in document 88

    Track limit monitoring at the Marina Bay Street Circuit was focused primarily on Turns 2, 7, 16, and 19. According to FIA Document 88, published by the race stewards at 22:53 local time, Antonelli was cited for leaving the racing surface on four distinct occasions:

    Strike one: Turn 2 at 20h33m44s (Lap time: 1m37.395s)

    Strike two: Turn 2 at 20h35m21s (Lap time: 1m37.605s)

    Strike three: Turn 19 at 21h01m39s (Lap time: 1m37.221s)

    Strike four: Turn 7 at 21h09m33s (Lap time: Out-lap)

    On paper, four entries under Appendix L, Chapter IV, Article 2(c) and Article B1.8.6 of the FIA Formula 1 Regulations appeared to meet the threshold for a time penalty. The tracking system recorded four red violation markers against car number 12, triggering immediate outrage among rival fan bases that questioned whether the championship leader was receiving preferential treatment.

    The core explanation behind Antonelli’s escape lies directly in the nature of his fourth recorded infraction at Turn 7.

    According to the FIA’s official Penalty Guidelines, track limits during a race carry a specific clarification: "a driver cannot set a time outside the white lines". The cumulative strike progression - where the third offence earns a black-and-white warning flag and the fourth triggers a five-second penalty - is designed to punish drivers for repeatedly gaining a micro-advantage on competitive, timed flying laps.

    Antonelli’s fourth breach at 21h09m33s occurred on an out-lap immediately following a pitstop. Because he was on an out-lap, no competitive lap time was being established or logged on the timing sheets. Under the strict interpretation of Article 12.4.1.e of the International Sporting Code, the only mandate issued in Document 88 was the deletion of the lap times shown.

    Since an out-lap has no representative lap time to strike from the record, and because no lasting sporting advantage or overtake was gained off track at Turn 7, the stewards did not treat the incident as a competitive strike that would escalate to an in-race time penalty.

    The procedural breakdown: The missing black-and-white flag

    Beyond the out-lap technicality, a secondary procedural factor protected Antonelli from a post-race penalty: the formal warning structure.

    Under standard FIA operational protocol, race control must formally display a black-and-white flag to a driver upon their third track limit violation before a time penalty can be assigned for a subsequent fourth breach. Amid a frantic mid-race phase involving safety car periods, pit-window scrambles, and battles through dirty air, Antonelli was never officially shown the black-and-white warning on the timing monitors. Without that mandated intermediary warning being triggered in race control, issuing an immediate five-second post-race penalty would have breached the FIA’s own enforcement protocol.

    Now, while Antonelli legally retained his podium, the incident has reignited scrutiny over his driving precision. Track limit compliance has been an ongoing struggle for the Italian across the 2026 season, with several qualifying laps and race stints compromised by his aggressive corner-entry style.

    It is important to note that had Antonelli crossed the white lines at Turn 7 on a standard flying lap rather than an out-lap, the five-second demotion would have been unavoidable. Instead, the young Mercedes driver heads to Austin with his 102-point championship cushion intact - though with the entire paddock watching his car placement far more closely.
