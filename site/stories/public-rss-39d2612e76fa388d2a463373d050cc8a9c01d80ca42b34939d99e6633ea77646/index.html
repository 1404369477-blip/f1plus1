    F1意大利大奖赛前瞻：排位赛爆冷，正赛悬念丛生

    加斯利为阿尔派拿下杆位，令蒙扎排位赛爆出近年最大冷门之一，而围场普遍认为周日的正赛还将带来更多意外。

    凭借皮埃尔·加斯利为阿尔派拿下杆位，一级方程式意大利大奖赛已经带来了近年来最大的震惊之一。

    但在一个充满能量部署难题和强大尾流的周末，意外很可能还没有结束——因为大家的共识是，正赛本身应该还会抛出更多的惊喜。

    围场里没有人确切知道接下来会发生什么，也不清楚最终局势将如何展开，忽上忽下的缠斗、性能的起伏波动以及潜在的风险都被摆上了台面。

    不过，在一些将影响我们应做好心理准备的比赛类型的关键因素上，大家是有共识的。

    今年我们已经多次看到，那些让2026年赛车“能量饥渴”的赛道是如何成为周日比赛打乱秩序的催化剂。

    不同的能量部署模式和电池动力的差异使超车变得容易，并为所谓的“悠悠球式比赛”打开了大门。

    这种现象在开场几圈往往被放大，因为赛车挤在一起时变化可能更加极端，能量管理也更加混乱。

    再加上强大的尾流效应，以及通往1号弯的长直道，难怪奥斯卡·皮亚斯特里认为比赛初期可能会出现一些小动作。

    他说：“比赛的前几圈可能会相当混乱——而且不一定是最快的赛车会一直领跑。

    虽然比赛通常最终会从早些时候这种忽上忽下的疯狂中稳定下来，因为车手们会逐渐收敛到统一的能量输出方式上，但在蒙扎这一点并不一定会发生，因为赛车在那里能量非常匮乏。

    车手们在2026年学到的一个教训是：一旦你开始与某人缠斗，就会损失大量圈速，因为能量输出会受到影响——所以攻击别人是有风险的，可能让你掉回车阵之中。

    迈凯伦车队领队斯特拉认为，要找出最佳的应对方式并不容易，因为任何想要提升名次的人都要面对潜在的后果。

    他说：“这场比赛中，如果你开始随意使用加速（boost）并去超车，你可能反被超车，并在整场比赛中开始损失总时间。”

    “但与此同时，如果你更快，你可能想要超越。贴近前车会让你速度更快——而一旦完成超越，你可能会比跟在后面时更慢。这会非常有意思。”

    卡洛斯·塞恩斯认为，车手们将不得不权衡自己想采取多大程度的进攻性策略。

    “我认为这取决于车手们在管理加速压力时能有多自律。”他说。

    “这最终将取决于你愿意为了开始进入借助增压按钮的忽快忽慢式竞速而放弃多少比赛节奏，以及你希望在不按增压、完全按照能量部署系统自己的意愿行事的情况下保持多么理想的效率。

    “这在很大程度上取决于你周围有哪些车手，以及谁愿意卷入这种忽快忽慢的缠斗。”

    对于车手来说，一个关键的考量是在进攻超车时需要投入多少能量，但同样需要认真考虑的是如何保护自己不遭到后方车手的攻击。

    能量匮乏的问题意味着超车模式所提供的额外能量将有助于车手紧咬前车——因此可能会导致出现一些‘火车’（多辆赛车首尾相接排成长队）的情况。

    要防止后方形成排队，以及避免随之而来的被卷入忽前忽后缠斗的风险，关键在于打破那一秒的差距——一旦差距缩小到一秒之内，前车就会触发超车模式。

    据威廉姆斯车手阿尔本表示，这将成为决定比赛结果的关键。

    “我认为这会是一场有趣的比赛，”他说。“从我看到的情况来看，尽量贴近前车会更快，而一旦失去超车模式，至少对我们来说，就会损失性能。”

    “所以，每个人都会试图摆脱那种尾流牵引。这将是那种局势不断反转的比赛之一。”

    阿尔本表示，这场比赛对车队来说会变得和对车手一样关键，需要去帮助管理——因为他认为，这种猫捉老鼠的博弈可以靠基地里的工作人员来协助。

    “心理战已经取代了身体上的对抗，”他说。

    “这更像是回到工厂进行GPS数据分析，能给我们即时反馈，告诉我们需要做什么、需要切换什么模式，来预判周围赛车在做什么。

    “这在很多层面上是战术层面的，但与此同时，你有时会感到无力。你不能再单纯地防守了。”

    这些战术的一部分应该涉及一些队友之间的协同配合。

    从第一排发车的皮埃尔·加斯利和拉塞尔身边没有队友可以提供帮助，但两辆法拉利将并排在第二排发车。

    这就是为什么刘易斯·汉密尔顿在排位赛后呼吁“我们必须作为一个团队来合作”，他对排位赛的表现感到失望——他在第三节排位赛（Q3）的首次飞驰圈没有获得牵引，这意味着他在关键的最后一圈没有正确的刹车参照点。

    在赞德沃特的一场混乱比赛之后，这场比赛对法拉利来说是一次巨大的考验，考验它能否让两位车手协同合作，在这个必须交出成绩的压力前所未有之高的周末取得理想结果。

    关键问题之一是，考虑到上述所有因素，加斯利能否将在周六取得的惊人成绩转化为周日的“以下克上”。

    爆冷夺得杆位者的过往战绩参差不齐。那些在湿地上凭借一次灵光一现的轮胎选择拿下杆位的车手，往往在正赛中会掉回原位——这也在意料之中，因为干地上他们的赛车性能根本跟不上。

    但那些在干地上爆冷夺杆的车手，正赛拿到好成绩的历史先例倒是相当不少。

    关键问题将是阿尔派在正赛条件下是否拥有同样强劲的赛车。A526 是一辆效率很高的赛车，相对顶尖车队缺乏下压力在蒙扎赛道并不会成为劣势。

    阿尔派的极速很高，这应该有利于加斯利在防守时发挥，但不会像他在2020年意大利大奖赛领先时那样轻松（当时得益于时机恰到好处的安全车），他当时能与卡洛斯·塞恩斯的迈凯伦保持距离。

    基于我们上面列出的所有原因，现在对手超越他的机会比当时多得多。

    “今年的蒙扎是一条对能量需求很高的赛道，有四个超车区域。车手无法守住所有区域，所以更快的赛车应该能够相对轻松地取得进展。”梅赛德斯赛道工程总监安德鲁·肖夫林在周末前针对基米·安东内利从队尾发车追赶时指出。

    英文原文

    Formula 1’s Italian Grand Prix has already served up one of the biggest shocks of recent years thanks to Pierre Gasly’s pole position for Alpine.

    But on a weekend of energy deployment headaches and a very powerful slipstream, the surprises may well not be over - as the consensus is that the race itself should throw up some more.

    No one in the paddock is exactly sure of what is to come and how things will play out in the end, with yo-yo racing, performance swings and the potential for jeopardy all on the agenda.

    But there is agreement on some key factors that will influence the type of race we should be braced for.

    We have seen plenty of times this year how tracks that leave the 2026 cars energy-starved are a catalyst for shaking up the order on Sundays.

    Different deployment patterns and offsets of battery power make passes easy and open the door for what’s become known as yo-yo racing.

    This phenomenon is often exacerbated in the opening laps as the variability can be more extreme with cars bunched together and energy management more haphazard.

    Throw into the mix how powerful the slipstream is, plus the long run to Turn 1, and it’s no wonder Oscar Piastri thinks there could be some shenanigans early on.

    “The first few laps of the race could be quite chaotic - and not necessarily the quickest car is going to be leading all the time,” he said.

    While races often eventually settle down from early yo-yo madness as drivers converge on where they deploy power, it is not definite that will happen at Monza because of how energy-poor the cars are there.

    One lesson drivers have learned in 2026 is that once you start battling someone, that costs a lot of laptime as energy deployment is compromised – so attacking someone runs the risk of dropping you into the pack.

    McLaren team boss Andrea Stella reckoned it was not easy to work out how best to approach things, because anyone wanting to move up the order faces potential consequences.

    “It's a race in which if you start to play with the boost, and overtake cars, you may get overtaken and you may start to lose overall time in the race,” he said.

    “But at the same time, if you're faster, you may want to pass. Being close to the car ahead will make you fast - and if you pass you may be slower than you were after. It will be very interesting.”

    Carlos Sainz reckoned that drivers were going to have to weigh up how aggressive they wanted to be.

    “I think it depends how disciplined people are with their boost press,” he said.

    “It will all come down to how much race time you want to give up by starting to get into yo-yo racing with the boost presses, and how optimal you want to stay without pressing the boost and just doing whatever the deployment wants to do.

    “It's going to be quite a bit depending on who you have around you and who is willing to get into a yo-yo battle.”

    While a key consideration for drivers will be how much to go on the offensive to move forward, as much thought is going to have to go into protecting yourself from behind, too.

    The energy starvation problem means that the extra energy on offer from overtake mode is going to help keep drivers in touch with the cars ahead – so could lead to some trains.

    Key to stopping a queue forming behind, with all the risks of then getting dragged into a yo-yo battle, will be breaking the one-second gap where overtake mode is triggered.

    This is going to be decisive to the outcome, according to Williams driver Alex Albon.

    “I think it's going to be a fun race,” he said. “From what I've seen, it's quicker to stay as close as you can to the car in front, and the moment you lose overtake mode, at least for us, you lose performance.

    “So, everyone's going to be trying to break that tow. It's going to be one of those kinds of swinging-around races.”

    Albon suggested that this game would become as critical a thing for teams to help manage as it was for the drivers – as he reckoned the cat-and-mouse games would be helped by staff back at base.

    "The mental warfare has overtaken the physical combat,” he said.

    “This is more like GPS data analysis back at factory, giving us immediate response times of what we need to do and what mode switches we need to change to anticipate what cars are doing around us.

    “This in many ways is tactical, but at the same time you feel sometimes helpless. You cannot just defend anymore.”

    Part of those tactics should involve some team-mates working together.

    Front row starters Pierre Gasly and George Russell don't have their team-mates nearby to help them, but the Ferraris are starting alongside each other on the second row.

    It's why Lewis Hamilton urged "we do have to work as a team" after a qualifying session where he was disappointed by the execution - he was left without a tow on his first Q3 run, and that meant he didn't have the right braking references for his crucial final lap.

    After a messy Zandvoort, this race is a huge test for Ferrari and whether it can get its drivers to work together to get the result on a weekend where the pressure to deliver has never been higher.

    One of the key questions is whether Gasly can convert his shock Saturday result into a giant-killing on Sunday, taking into account all of the above factors.

    The track record for surprise polesitters is mixed. Those who take their poles in wet conditions via an inspired tyre call in qualifying often slip back on race day, as you’d expect given they don’t have the car performance to match in the dry.

    But there is a good history of those who take a surprise pole in the dry bringing home a strong result on race day.

    The key question will be whether Alpine has that same strong car in race conditions. The A526 is a very efficient car, with its lack of downforce relative to the top teams not a penalty around Monza.

    The Alpine has a good top speed that should serve Gasly well when defending, but it won’t be as comfortable as when he was leading the 2020 Italian Grand Prix (thanks to a well-timed safety car) and he could keep Carlos Sainz’s McLaren at arm’s length.

    For all the reasons we’ve listed above, there are far more opportunities for rivals to pass him than there were back then.

    “This year Monza is an energy-starved circuit with four overtaking zones. A driver cannot defend all of them, so a faster car should be able to make progress relatively straightforwardly,” Mercedes trackside engineering director Andrew Shovlin pointed out ahead of the weekend in relation to Kimi Antonelli making progress from the back of the grid.
