    威廉姆斯让塞恩斯与阿尔本充当测试车手

    威廉姆斯在赛季末的F1分站周末牺牲部分环节，让塞恩斯和阿尔本为2027年赛车进行试验性工作，两人周五练习运行与其他车手不同的项目。塞恩斯表示自己不在意第15或第17名，更看重能为明年学到多少。

    威廉姆斯组合卡洛斯·塞恩斯和亚历克斯·阿尔本可以说已成为世界上资历最过剩的测试车手，因为车队牺牲了本赛季最后几个F1周末的部分环节来帮助其2027年赛车。

    随着威廉姆斯的叙事几乎完全转向为明年取得进展，塞恩斯和阿尔本在周五练习中执行着与其他车手非常不同的项目，并且更多地谈论试验性工作的价值超过完美准备某个周末。

    当被The Race问及这是否让他在这段时间成为测试车手时，塞恩斯说：“我是。我告诉车队，我不在乎第15名或第17名完赛。

    “更重要的是我能学到多少、他们能学到多少，以便让我自己、车队和赛车在明年处于更好的位置。”

    尽管威廉姆斯车队2026年的问题已经广为人知，但有必要理解核心症结——以及威廉姆斯在解决这些问题时面临的现实——才能体会它眼下将塞恩斯和阿尔本变成心甘情愿的、被美其名曰的测试车手这一做法为何是正确的。

    新规则加上围场中最好的引擎，本应帮助威廉姆斯在塞恩斯2025年两次登上领奖台的基础上更进一步，但人们对2026年实现这一目标的期望很早便被放弃了。

    它是唯一一支未能让赛车赶上1月巴塞罗那试车的车队，而迟到达巴林的赛车同样超重且速度慢。此后在相对竞争力上的好转微乎其微，因此威廉姆斯在11支车队中积分排名第九，单圈速度方面也仅领先阿斯顿·马丁和凯迪拉克，且随着赛季推进，在这两项指标上都与这两队越来越接近（就阿斯顿·马丁而言则是被其反超）。

    尽管此后赛车重量已经降了下来，但更深层的缺陷在于其高底盘倾角空气动力学理念。阿斯顿·马丁走了类似路线，并在赛季中通过B版赛车放弃了这一理念，而威廉姆斯认为那样做代价太大。

    九月份在巴库带来的升级让赛车达到了重量限制，这在很大程度上是对2027年的投资，因为其底盘将沿用下去；而据车队领队詹姆斯·沃尔斯所说，威廉姆斯“在风洞里留了相当一部分性能没有释放”，以避免把宝贵资源浪费在本赛季回报较小的方向上。

    “先变差、再变好”的现实早在几个月前就已被接受。九月份在主场马德里赛道，当威廉姆斯正在准备其最后一次重大的减重升级、并希望至少能更接近积分区时，塞恩斯说：“这离我们今年想要达到的位置差得远，离我们承诺的也差得远。”

    “我们最大的希望就是把所有鸡蛋都放进明年的篮子里。”

    这件事常规的一面在于，不再纠缠于2026年赛车的持续研发，而是尽早把风洞和计算流体力学（CFD）的重点转向2027年。不常规的一面在于，威廉姆斯把部分大奖赛周末变成了测试环节。

    在赛车的性能上限实际上已经明确且固定的情况下，威廉姆斯车队认为，优化某些赛事的表现已不那么重要。这适用于前期工作——阿尔本表示“甚至我们在工厂做的模拟器工作，我们几乎不花时间在今年的赛车上，而是把所有时间都花在明年的赛车上”——但随后在赛道上进行实验，将真实赛车与格罗夫基地的设计和模拟工作联系起来。

    塞恩斯解释道：“我们正在进行正规的调校测试实验，也是为了我对赛车和车队的了解和经验，感受变化有多大，并尝试与模拟器做一些关联工作：赛车在现实中的表现与在模拟器中的表现对比，与我们的模型对比，以及模型预期它会做什么。”

    “所以作为车手，一旦没有什么可争夺的，就像现在这样，你总是可以通过大型测试项目获得经验和学习，因为通常在F1中，测试非常有限。”

    最明显的例子出现在美国大奖赛的练习赛中。首席赛道工程师保罗·威廉姆斯表示，车队“在本周末的性能与长期学习之间做了平衡”，为2027年收集数据，因为美洲赛道“非常适合空气动力学数据收集”。

    威廉姆斯的第一次自由练习赛运行计划与其他任何车队都不同。使用硬胎时，每辆赛车都进行了五轮：先跑一个出场圈，随后在计时段一和计时段二以比赛速度跑一圈，然后直接驶入维修区，而不是冲过终点线。之后每辆赛车在车库中停留六到九分钟，再进行下一轮。

    阿尔本这样做了四次，塞恩斯做了五次，而其他所有车手加起来只完成了六次这样的圈数。两位威廉姆斯车手直到大约48分钟时的中胎运行才开始完整的飞驰圈，而且两人都没有使用软胎，也没有进行连续圈数的运行。其他每辆赛车这两项都做了。

    由于他唯一的中胎飞驰圈因赛道限制被删除，阿尔本以落后榜首40.416秒的成绩结束了本节练习。但威廉姆斯带走了远比一次正常练习所能提供的更多受控数据，用于其2027年相关性工作，实际上将第一次自由练习赛变成了F1不再允许的季中测试的一种微型版本。

    威廉姆斯在如何运行赛车方面也承担了更多风险，以找出其真实工作窗口到底有多宽。

    技术总监马特·哈曼表示：“我们正在尝试新事物，不仅是赛车上的物理硬件，还包括系统和流程，并挑战我们承担的风险，以观察反馈如何。”

    “我们在赛车的运行方式上不断突破极限，比如冷却方面的一些东西。我们试图在赛车上展现出更多的侵略性，这对我们很有好处，不仅是为了今天你们在这里看到的表现，也是为了我们工厂模型的关联性。”

    “待在模型中间很容易，但我们需要努力触及边缘，这就是我们正在做的。”

    在竞争更激烈的车队中，通常会限制风险以避免影响周末剩余的比赛。威廉姆斯失去的要少得多——但它在关键时刻会开启这种状态，比如在英特拉格斯湿滑的排位赛中最大化表现，让阿尔本进入前十发车。

    同样地，尽管塞恩斯表示凭实力拿分“有点不太可能”，但如果威廉姆斯能在排位赛中比平时高出几个名次、强势起步并守住位置，那么下周的拉斯维加斯仍是剩下的例外。因此预计他们会在那里进行正常的练习计划。

    “（积分）今年是渺茫的可能，我会在拉斯维加斯为此奋战，”塞恩斯说。

    “我会竭尽全力为车队拿下那最后的积分。

    “但在其他所有地方，我几乎把它看作提升明年赛车的测试周末。”

    尽管从竞争结果来看，这些比赛基本已无悬念，但它们仍为威廉姆斯车队提供了提升的关键机会——比如维修区停站。

    阿尔本表示，这能让比赛团队保持敏锐，并给予他们明确的动力去尽可能努力地工作，他提到威廉姆斯在墨西哥站本赛季首次拿下全场最快维修区停站。

    夏休期以来各车队的维修区停站用时分布

    已剔除各车队最快和最慢的10%维修区停站数据

    其进站中位时间已从夏休前的3.28秒（11支车队中第九快）降至此后的2.79秒，因此有相当多的证据能在更广层面上支持这一进步。

    然而，如果2027年的赛车无法带来威廉姆斯所需的那一步提升，这一切都无关紧要，塞恩斯承认这是他最大的担忧。

    “今年我们在三个关键领域失败了，”他说。“第一是让赛车按时抵达赛道。第二是控制赛车重量。第三是让空气动力学概念正确。”

    “前两个，我相信我们明年会解决。第三个是让我担忧的，或者说我们不得不投入更多努力的。而第三个是最重要的，因为它是产生性能的那一个。”

    原因是威廉姆斯失去了地效时代末期的一项优势，当时塞恩斯表示其升级完全达到了预期效果。

    “用这些赛车，我们遇到了更多问题，”他说。“并非所有交付的东西都表现如预期。

    “所以当我在模拟器上测试并看到我们为明年产生的数据时，我脑中会有一定程度的疑虑，因为我们今年产生的一些数据并没有像地效赛车那样完全一一对应。”

    英文原文

    Williams duo Carlos Sainz and Alex Albon have become arguably the most overqualified test drivers in the world as the team has sacrificed parts of the final Formula 1 weekends this season to help its 2027 car.

    As the Williams narrative has turned almost entirely to making progress for next year, Sainz and Albon have been conducting very different programmes in Friday practice to other drivers, and talking more about the value of experimental work outweighing that of absolutely nailing the preparation of a given weekend.

    Asked by The Race if that made him a test driver for this part of the year, Sainz said: "I am. And I told the team that I don't care about finishing 15th or 17th.

    "It's more how much can I learn, how much can they learn, in order to get myself and the team and the car in a better place for next year."

    Though Williams's 2026 problems are well established it is important to understand the core issues – and the reality Williams faced in addressing them – to appreciate what it is doing right now in turning Sainz and Albon into willing, glorified test drivers.

    New rules and the best engine on the grid were meant to help Williams build on Sainz's two podiums in 2025 but hopes of this being achieved in 2026 were abandoned very early.

    It was the only team that failed to get a car to January's Barcelona shakedown, and the car that arrived late in Bahrain was also overweight and slow. The turnaround since has been minimal in relative performance, hence Williams is ninth of the 11 teams on points, and on single-lap pace, ahead of only Aston Martin and Cadillac in both metrics and slipping closer to both (and behind in the case of Aston Martin) as the season has worn on.

    Though the weight has since come off the car, the deeper flaw was its high-rake aerodynamic concept. Aston Martin took a similar route and abandoned it with an in-season B-spec car, Williams decided doing that would cost too much.

    The Baku upgrade in September that got the car to the weight limit was largely an investment in 2027, as its chassis carries over, and Williams has "left quite a bit of performance in the tunnel at home" according to team boss James Vowles to avoid wasting precious resource on a smaller return this season.

    An 'it'll get worse before it gets better' reality was embraced months ago. At his home race in Madrid in September, when Williams was preparing its final major weight-cutting upgrade and was hoping it would at least get closer to scoring points, Sainz said: "That's nowhere near where we wanted to be this year, nowhere near what we promised.

    "Our biggest hope is putting all the eggs in next year's basket."

    The conventional element to this is not bothering with ongoing 2026 car development and shifting windtunnel and CFD focus to 2027 as early as possible. What's unconventional is how Williams is turning part of grand prix weekends into test sessions.

    With the car's performance ceiling effectively clear and fixed, Williams has decided it matters less how well it optimises certain events. This applies to the work in advance – Albon said "even the simulator work we're doing at the factory, we're spending almost no time on this year's car, and spending all our time on next year's car" – but then at the track running experiments that link the real car to the design and simulation work at its Grove base.

    Sainz explained: "We are doing proper set-up test experiments, also for my knowledge and experience of the car and the team, how big the changes feel, and trying to do a bit of correlation work with the simulator: what the car does in real life versus what it does in the simulator, what it does versus our models, and what the model is expecting it to do.

    "So as a driver, once there is nothing to fight for, like there is right now, there is always experience and learning that you can do with big test items, because normally in F1, testing is super limited."

    The clearest example came in practice for the United States Grand Prix. Chief trackside engineer Paul Williams said the team had "balanced off performance for this weekend versus long-term learning", collecting data for 2027 because the Circuit of the Americas is "very good for aero data collection".

    Williams's FP1 run plan was unlike any other team's. On hard tyres, each car made five trips of an out-lap followed by a lap driven at pace through the first two sectors, before peeling into the pits instead of crossing the line. Each car then spent six to nine minutes in the garage before the next run.

    Albon did this four times and Sainz five, while the rest of the field managed six such laps between them. Neither Williams driver started a full flying lap until the medium-tyre runs about 48 minutes in, and neither ran the soft tyre or a run of consecutive laps. Every other car did both.

    With his only flying laps on mediums deleted for track limits, Albon ended the session 40.416 seconds off the pace. But Williams left with a far bigger bank of controlled data for its 2027 correlation work than a normal session would give, effectively turning FP1 into a micro-version of the sort of in-season test F1 no longer allows.

    Williams has also been taking more risk with how it runs the car, to find out how wide its operating envelope really is.

    "We're trying new things, not just physical hardware on the car, but also systems and processes, and challenging the risk that we take to see what the responses are," said technical director Matt Harman.

    "We're pushing the envelope of the car in where we run it, some things on the cooling and bits and pieces like that. We're trying to exercise a little bit more aggression in the car, and that serves us well, not just for what you see here today, but for the correlation of our models back at the factory.

    "It's very easy to be in the middle of the model, but we need to be trying to get to the edges, and that's what we're doing."

    Teams in a closer fight usually limit the risks to avoid compromising the rest of the weekend. Williams has far less to lose – but it switches it on when it matters, like maximising the wet qualifying in qualifying at Interlagos to get Albon inside the top 10 on the grid.

    Likewise, though Sainz says points on merit are "a bit out of the question", Las Vegas this coming week is the remaining exception if Williams can qualify a few places higher than usual, make a strong start and hold on. So expect to see it conduct a normal practice programme there.

    "[Points are] a remote possibility this year, which I'm going to fight for in Vegas," said Sainz.

    "I'm going to try everything to get those last points for the team.

    "But everywhere else, I see it almost as testing weekends to improve next year's car."

    And though the races are largely a foregone conclusion in terms of the competitive result, they still afford Williams key opportunities to improve – like pitstops.

    Albon said this is keeping the race team sharp and giving it a clear motivation to keep working as hard as possible, pointing to Williams getting the fastest pitstop of the race for the first time this season in Mexico.

    Its median stop has dropped from 3.28 seconds before the summer break, ninth-best of the 11 teams, to 2.79s since, so there is a decent body of evidence to back up this improvement more broadly.

    However, none of this matters if the 2027 car does not deliver the step Williams needs, and Sainz admits that is his biggest concern.

    "There's three key areas where we've failed this year," he said. "One is getting the car on time to the track. Two would be getting the car on weight. And number three would be getting the aero concept right.

    "The first two, I trust that we're going to solve next year. Number three is the one that worries me, or let's say that we are having to put more effort on. And that number three is the most important one, because it's the one that generates performance."

    The reason is that Williams has lost one of its strengths from the end of the ground-effect era, when Sainz says its upgrades delivered exactly what was predicted.

    "With these cars we're having more problems," he said. "Not everything that is being delivered is performing as expected.

    "So it leaves a certain level of doubt in my head when I test the simulator and see the numbers that we're producing for next year, because some of the numbers that we've produced this year haven't exactly gone one to one like they did with the ground-effect car."
