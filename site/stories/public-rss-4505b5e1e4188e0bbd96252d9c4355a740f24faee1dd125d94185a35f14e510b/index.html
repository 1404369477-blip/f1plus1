    F1 V8引擎未来计划进展：FIA与制造商达成初步协议

    尽管社交媒体帖子被删除引发疑虑，但F1回归V8引擎的计划正与FIA推进至下一阶段。FIA主席穆罕默德·本·苏莱姆在Instagram上暗示即将有突破。

    一级方程式回归V8引擎的计划正在与FIA的协商中稳步推进，尽管周二社交媒体上删除的帖子引起了人们的猜测。

    在FIA、FOM和引擎制造商就V8计划进行会议后的第二天，FIA主席穆罕默德·本·苏莱姆在周二暗示了一个突破。

    在一篇发布在Instagram上的帖子中，伴随着V8引擎的声音，本·苏莱姆写道：“你们说了，我们听了。V8引擎即将到来。”

    这是由穆罕默德·本·苏莱姆（@mohammed.ben.sulayem）分享的帖子。

    这条帖子引起了一些轰动，因为它附带了其他在FIA渠道发布后被删除的社会媒体图形，这些图形指向了一些事情已经确定下来。

    这些帖子提到了关于从2030年开始引入V8引擎的原则性协议已经达成。

    这些帖子只存在了一段时间，但仍然足够被其他渠道捕捉和分享，并保持在线状态。

    不清楚为什么这些帖子出现后又消失了，但一个可能的解释是需要将V8规则流程正式化，在所有各方就绪后，再发布任何官方声明。

    这需要决策关键利益相关者的支持，包括国际汽联（FIA）、自由媒体公司（FOM）和制造商。

    《赛车》杂志了解到，在周一的会议上，对V8概念达成了广泛的一致意见，该概念将基于2.1升涡轮增压动力单元。

    然而，关于整体规则包的许多细节仍需最终确定，制造商对所提议的各个方面并不完全和谐一致——这表明在规则最终确定之前还需要更多的工作。

    这方面的讨论预计将继续进行，以期在年底前就广泛达成一致——这是F1和国际汽联公开设定的目标，以概述规则。

    下一个重大步骤将是FIA世界汽车运动理事会的下一次会议，该会议将于12月10日在上海举行，以讨论新规则的框架。

    一个需要确定的重要问题是V8引擎何时到来。目前的规则计划运行至2031年，但本·苏莱姆一直致力于将新引擎的切换应用于2030年。

    为了实现这一点，需要制造商和车队的大力支持，而这只有在有具体的提案供他们投票时才可能实现。

    本·苏莱姆最近表示，引入更简单、更便宜的引擎将有助于加速新规则的到来。

    “当你让它变得更简单时，你可以将其价格减半。然后如果研发成本减半，加上简单性，生产时间可以更短，”他说。

    正如《The Race》所揭示的，2.1升的选择被青睐，因为它是最简单、最便宜的方式，让制造商适应他们当前的V6涡轮增压发动机。

    新的动力单元将使用可持续燃料，配备更小的涡轮和降低的增压压力，对电能的依赖性远低于以前。

    目前的350kW电动机将降低至200kW，其中只有100kW始终可用。另外100kW将用于超车系统。

    随着V8引擎的变革，FIA还计划采取积极的措施来降低F1赛车的重量。目标是将当前赛车的重量减少约80公斤，使其低于700公斤。

    英文原文

    Formula 1’s plans to return to V8 engines are on track to progress to the next stage with the FIA, despite intrigue over deleted social media posts that appeared on Tuesday.

    Just one day after a meeting between the FIA, FOM and engine manufacturers to discuss the V8 plans, FIA president Mohammed Ben Sulayem teased a breakthrough on Tuesday.

    In a post that appeared on Instagram and was accompanied by the sound of a V8 engine, Ben Sulayem posted “You spoke, we listened. V8s coming soon.”

    A post shared by Mohammed Ben Sulayem (@mohammed.ben.sulayem)

    That post created some buzz as it was accompanied by other social media graphics that were published on FIA channels, before being deleted, which pointed to things being set in stone.

    These referenced an agreement in principle having been reached for the introduction of V8s from 2030.

    These posts were only up for a short time, but that was still long enough for them to be captured and shared by other channels and remain online.

    It is not clear why these posts appeared and then disappeared, but one possible explanation is the need to put the V8 rules process on a more formal footing, with all parties lined up, before making any official statements.

    This would require support from the key stakeholders in the decision, which includes the FIA, FOM and the manufacturers.

    The Race understands that at Monday's gathering there was broad agreement for the V8 concept, which will be based around a 2.1-litre turbo power unit.

    However, a lot of the details surrounding the overall rules package still need to be finalised, with manufacturers not in total harmony about every aspect of what is being proposed - which suggests more work is needed before regulations can be finalised.

    Discussions on that front are set to continue in a bid to try to get things broadly agreed before the end of this year - which is the timeframe that has been openly targeted by F1 and the FIA to get the rules outlined.

    The next big step is set to be the next gathering of the FIA’s World Motor Sport Council, which takes place in Shanghai on December 10, to discuss the framework of the new rules.

    One major issue to set in stone is when the V8s will arrive. The current rules are planned to run until 2031, but Ben Sulayem has long targeted the switch to the new engines to come into play for 2030.

    For that to happen, it would require the full backing of manufacturers and teams, which could only realistically happen if there are concrete proposals for them to vote on.

    Speaking recently, Ben Sulayem suggested that getting simpler and cheaper engines in play would open the door for fast-tracking the arrival of the new rules.

    "When you make it simpler, you can make it half of the price. Then if the R&D goes down by half, with the simplicity, the production time can be shorter,” he said.

    As revealed by The Race, the 2.1-litre choice has been favoured because it is the simplest and cheapest way for manufacturers to adapt their current V6 turbos.

    The new power units will run on sustainable fuels with a smaller turbo with reduced boost pressure, and have a far lesser reliance on electrical power.

    The current 350kW electric motor is set to decrease to 200kW, with only 100kW available all of the time. The other 100kW will be used for an overtake system.

    As well as the changes in power units coming with the V8s, the FIA also plans an aggressive push to bring the weight of F1 cars down. Sights are set on reducing the current car mass by around 80kg, which could get them below 700kg.
