    威廉姆斯2025赛季超支62.7万英镑原因

    威廉姆斯因公司重组时将资产转入新的有限责任合伙企业，其财务团队与FIA对相关成本的理解存在分歧，被认定在2025赛季成本上限中构成62.7万英镑的“轻微超支违规”。FIA承认这并非有意为之，威廉姆斯接受了罚款和空气动力学测试处罚。

    威廉姆斯为何在2025赛季F1成本上限中违规

    FIA在2025年财务报告中发现约60万英镑超支后，威廉姆斯接受了罚款和空气动力学测试处罚

    威廉姆斯在2025赛季违反F1成本上限规定，原因在于其财务团队与FIA裁决人员对相关成本的理解存在分歧，涉及车队重组时将资产转入新的有限责任合伙企业。

    该格罗夫车队被认定在2025赛季成本上限中构成62.7万英镑的“轻微超支违规”，不过FIA承认，鉴于其对所涉成本的理解，这并非有意为之。

    威廉姆斯随后接受了这一违规行为，处罚是空气动力学测试额度被削减1.5%，外加一笔未公开的罚款。

    据车队领队詹姆斯·沃尔斯所说，威廉姆斯认为公司重组会被算作财务和法律活动，而这两类在2025年成本上限规则下都属于豁免项。国际汽联并不同意。

    “我们现在已经把这件事彻底复盘了一遍，弄清楚本应该怎么做才不一样。显然，正因为这件事，我们本应该采取不同的做法，”沃尔斯表示。

    “尽管规则在字面解释上并不清晰，但国际汽联的立场非常明确，就是以下这点：因为你们把合同从一个业务实体转移到了另一个业务实体，对他们来说这实际上就是采购，而采购属于被纳入的活动。”

    “对我们来说，我们将其定义为一种财务和法律行为，因为它实际上就是拿一份合同并在上面改个名字。我能理解他们的解释，这也是我们接受违约的原因。”

    “但我希望这能帮助你们理解，在那种情况下，为什么这其实是微妙之处，而非清晰明了。”

    从英国公司注册处的日志来看，情况是这样的：威廉姆斯——自1977年以来一直以威廉姆斯大奖赛工程有限公司的名义运营——设立了一家新的有限责任合伙企业：威廉姆斯大奖赛工程服务有限责任合伙企业，并将价值2780万英镑的资产和负债转移到了这家新公司。

    威廉姆斯大奖赛工程有限公司是该有限责任合伙企业中占99%份额的合伙人，而BCE GP有限责任公司——一家据信由多里尔顿资本管理的控股公司——则注册为占1%份额的少数合伙人。通过这样做，威廉姆斯大奖赛工程有限公司获得了一定程度的法律隔离，例如，通常不会对该有限责任合伙企业累积的任何债务承担责任。

    沃尔斯表示，这是为了确保车队具备一定程度的未来适应性，也是威廉姆斯车队为跟上时代而需承担的一长串开支中的又一项。因此，62.7万英镑超支的大部分都用于组建新的有限责任合伙企业，以及将法律文件和供应合同转移至新公司。

    他解释道：“威廉姆斯的公司架构自1977年以来基本没有变化，在现代环境下确实不太适用。”

    “我们一开始就打算对国际汽联保持公开透明，全程都是这么做的，但我们认为这些成本属于公司重组，不应计入成本上限开支。”

    “这是一次性的公司重组。真正感兴趣的人可以在我们的公司注册处报告中看到。你不会每年都重组公司。这种事一辈子做一次——理想情况下，只做一次。只不过这是一家有着50年历史的公司，在这方面不太适应需求。”

    从根本上说，之前那里是一个单一的垂直结构，而从根本上拥有这种结构有很多缺点。

    卡洛斯·塞恩斯，威廉姆斯，詹姆斯·沃尔斯，威廉姆斯

    所以，你会看到围绕它已经创建了若干其他公司。这样只是更高效，但其中涉及的成本相当可观。

    如果你想想作为一家企业你必须做什么，你可能会有——我是说，在我们的情况下，由于这一点，你不得不将接近2000份与各种供应商及其他方的合同从一家公司迁移到另一家公司。这是一项非常痛苦、漫长的任务。

    “随之而来的还有一些法律方面的事务。同时，根据英国法律，你还必须完成一些合规工作。最终这是一个耗时颇多的过程——我们大概在六个月前启动了这一流程，这或许能让你了解它有多漫长。”

    “但在此期间，我们不得不动用财务和法律人员，并临时加强人手以度过那段时间。”

    沃尔斯解释说，车队直到国际汽联“大约三、四周前”联系车队、指出可能存在超支时，才知道违规一事——他再次表示，在管理机构说明立场之前，车队一直认为自己合规。

    他表示，威廉姆斯不应因所受处罚而受到太大影响，并指出空气动力学测试削减1.5%（相当于两个月内约六小时风洞占用、1.2小时“吹风”时间、5次风洞运行和30次CFD几何测试）只是“小菜一碟”。

    "换个角度看，发车格上一个位置相当于5%的空气动力学测试限制（ATR），而我们只有1.5%的差距。所以，这还不到三分之一的位置。

    "而且我相当希望我们明年能做得更好，让自己在发车格上重新靠前。那将是15%，差不多在这个范围内。所以现实情况是，从大局来看，这只会是些小菜一碟。"

    Why Williams breached the 2025 F1 cost cap

    Williams took a fine and an aero testing penalty after the FIA spotted a £600k overspend in 2025's financial reports

    Williams' breach of Formula 1's cost cap regulations for the 2025 season was down to a difference in interpretation between its own finance team and the FIA adjudicators, relating to the company's restructure as the team moved assets into a new limited liability partnership.

    The Grove squad was found to have committed a "minor overspend breach" of £627,000 in the 2025 cost cap, although the FIA conceded that this was unintentional given its interpretation of the costs involved.

    Williams subsequently accepted the breach, which comes with a 1.5% penalty to its aerodynamic testing allowance, and an undisclosed fine.

    According to team principal James Vowles, Williams believed that a company restructure would count as a financial and legal activity, both of which are classed as exemptions under the 2025 cost cap regulations. The FIA did not agree.

    "We've gone back through it exactly now and what should we have done differently. And clearly, we should have done something differently as a result of it," Vowles stated.

    "And although the regulations aren't clear in black and white in its interpretation, the FIA is very clear on their perspective, which is the following: because you've moved contracts from one business to the next, that effectively for them is procurement, which is an included activity.

    "For us, we defined it as a financial and legal activity because it's literally taking a contract and changing a name on it. I can understand their interpretation, which is why we've accepted the breach.

    "But I hope that helps you understand why it's subtleties, not clarity, really, in that circumstance."

    As seen from its logs on Companies House, the scenario is that Williams - having traded as Williams Grand Prix Engineering Ltd since 1977 - had set up a new LLP: Williams Grand Prix Engineering Services LLP, and moved £27.8m worth of assets and liabilities into the new company.

    WGPE Ltd is the 99% partner in the LLP, with BCE GP LLC - a holding company understood to be managed by Dorilton Capital - registered as the 1% minority partner. By doing this, WGPE Ltd has a degree of legal separation in place and, for example, will usually not be liable for any debts accrued by the LLP.

    Vowles says that this is about ensuring a degree of future-proofing for the team, and another in a long line of expenses involved in bringing Williams up to date. As such, the majority of the £627,000 overspend has been tied up in incorporating the new LLP, and in transferring any legal documents and supply contracts to the new company.

    "The company structure of Williams was largely unchanged from 1977, and wasn't really fit for purpose in a modern world," he explained.

    "We went into it with every intention of being open and transparent with the FIA, which is what we held all the way throughout, but believed those costs, because it's company restructuring, not to be a cost cap expense.

    "This is a one-off company restructuring that's taking place. You can, for those really interested, you can see it in our Companies House report. You don't restructure your company every year. You do it once every - well, ideally, you do it one-off. It's just it's a 50-year-old company that wasn't quite fit for purpose in that regard.

    "Fundamentally, it's a single vertical structure is what was there previously, and there's many downsides to fundamentally having that.

    Carlos Sainz, Williams, James Vowles, Williams

    "So, you'll see there's a number of other companies have been created around it. It's just more efficient, but the costs involved in it are fairly significant.

    "If you think about what you have to do as a business, you probably have – I mean, in our case, it was close to 2,000 contracts with various suppliers and otherwise that you have to migrate from one company to the next as a result of it. It's a very painful, lengthy task.

    "There's some legal that goes with it. There's some compliance you have to do in UK law as a result of it as well at the same time. It was a process in the end that took - we initiated it probably around about six months, which probably gives you an idea as to how lengthy it is.

    "But it was using finance and legal personnel for a period of which we had to bolster up to get us through that period."

    Vowles explained that the team didn't know about the breach until the FIA contacted the team "about three, four weeks ago" to flag the potential overspend - again, feeling satisfied that it had been compliant until the governing body explained its stance.

    He says that Williams shouldn't hurt too much with the applied penalty, noting that the 1.5% reduction in aero testing (which relates to about six hours of wind tunnel occupancy, 1.2 hours of 'wind on' time, 5 wind tunnel runs, and 30 CFD geometry tests in a two-month period) is "small beans".

    "To put things in perspective, one position on the grid is 5% of ATR, and we've got a 1.5% delta. So, it's less than a third of a position.

    "And I'm rather hoping that we do a better job next year and put ourselves back up the grid. And that would be 15%, something in that ballpark. So the reality is it's going to be small beans in the grand scheme of things."
