    克里斯蒂安·霍纳透露F1生涯中的恐慌症斗争

    克里斯蒂安·霍纳透露他在F1生涯中与恐慌症的秘密斗争，并感谢医生帮助他识别浅呼吸和焦虑。

    克里斯蒂安·霍纳透露F1生涯中的恐慌症斗争

    克里斯蒂安·霍纳感谢医生帮助他在多年的F1维修区压力下识别浅呼吸和焦虑

    克里斯蒂安·霍纳，红牛车队车队负责人

    对于红牛车迷来说，关注红牛车队，了解F1新闻、车手更新、技术洞察和米尔顿凯恩斯的比赛分析

    在光鲜的维修区围板、领奖台香槟和不懈的政治操纵背后，一级方程式赛车运动对那些驾驶着数百万美元操作的个体施加了残酷、往往看不见的代价。二十年来，克里斯蒂安·霍纳作为红牛车队的领军人物，展现出了坚定不移的外表，在激烈的冠军争夺战和残酷的赛道战争中保持着绝对的冷静。

    然而，在与《卫报》的一次深刻对话中，这位52岁的前车队负责人揭开了他幕后所承受的严重生理压力的序幕，详细描述了慢性压力最终如何导致可怕的恐慌发作。

    误将恐慌发作归咎于'喝太多咖啡'

    管理一支赢得冠军的车队，跨越繁忙的赛历，其不懈的要求最终对身心造成了实际的损害。霍纳回忆起他经历了令人担忧的心血管和呼吸系统症状，他最初试图将这些症状归咎于常规疲劳或咖啡因摄入过多。

    “我不知道那是什么，”霍纳在描述攻击开始时承认道，“你突然感到头晕，对呼吸变得敏感，你觉得有些奇怪的事情正在发生。我把它归咎于在一个压抑、闷热、雷雨交加的日子里喝太多咖啡。”

    红牛车队车队经理克里斯蒂安·霍纳

    当这些情况再次发生时，霍纳寻求了医学评估，结果发现根本原因不是身体疾病，而是心理过载。

    “然后它又发生了。我进行了检查，但身体上没有任何问题，”霍纳透露，“他们说，'我们认为你有焦虑症。你的呼吸非常浅。'这是我的身体在告诉我，你在这里的压力很大。我现在能够识别这些症状并让它过去，然后呼吸。”

    “身份”的空虚和对Toto Wolff的赞扬

    在2025年7月意外离开红牛车队后，压力的生理表现最终被突如其来的身份真空所加剧。在米尔顿凯恩斯担任了20年的核心决策者，突然被从这个充满活力的环境中移除，引发了一段深刻的自我反思时期。

    在反思心理冲击时，霍纳意外地对他最激烈的职业对手、梅赛德斯车队负责人Toto Wolff表示敬意，Wolff曾公开谈论自己与临床抑郁症和治疗的经历。

    “Toto公开谈论我们这个年龄段的人可能会遇到的问题非常勇敢，”霍纳承认。“我虽然没有经历过这些问题，但在这个时期我对人们是如何[受苦的]有了更深的理解。”

    Christian Horner，红牛车队负责人，Toto Wolff，梅赛德斯-AMG车队负责人兼首席执行官

    Horner强调，当表现优异的专业人士突然失去职业目标时，心理上的影响可能是毁灭性的。

    “许多中年人的身份是通过他们的工作来定义的。突然间，你失去了这一切，”Horner解释道。“你是谁？你是什么？你的目标是什么？你要么蜷缩成一团，要么振作起来站起来。”

    尽管Horner坚持认为一级方程式赛车仍然是激发他职业热情的主要领域，但离开赛道为他提供了一个前所未有的生活方式的重置。在连续21个赛季中错过了无数个人里程碑之后，Horner得以在伦敦度过赛车周末，庆祝女儿Olivia的13岁生日，带她去西区观看音乐剧《汉密尔顿》

    询问他真正怀念体育运动的哪些元素时，霍纳毫不犹豫地指出了界限。

    "人、团队、竞争。我仍然热爱这项运动，" 霍纳总结道。"但我并不怀念旅行和政治。"

    Christian Horner reveals secret battle with panic attacks during F1 career

    Christian Horner credits doctors with helping him recognise shallow breathing and anxiety after years of F1 pitwall stress

    Christian Horner, Team Principal, Red Bull Racing

    For Red Bull fans. Keeping watch on Red Bull Racing, with F1 news, driver updates, technical insight and race analysis from Milton Keynes.

    Behind the polished pitwall pitboards, podium champagne, and relentless political maneuvering, Formula 1 extracts a grueling, often invisible toll on the individuals steering its multi-million-dollar operations. For two decades, Christian Horner presented an unflappable exterior as Red Bull Racing's figurehead, projecting absolute composure across intense title showdowns and bitter paddock wars.

    However, in a deeply revealing conversation with The Guardian, the 52-year-old former team principal pulled back the curtain on the severe physiological distress he weathered behind the scenes, detailing how chronic stress eventually culminated in terrifying panic attacks.

    Mistaking panic attacks for 'too much coffee'

    The relentless demands of managing a championship-winning team across a sprawling calendar eventually took a tangible physical toll. Horner recalled experiencing alarming cardiovascular and respiratory episodes, which he initially tried to dismiss as routine fatigue or excessive caffeine intake.

    "I didn't know what it was," Horner admitted when describing the onset of the attacks. "You suddenly feel dizzy and conscious of your breathing, and you think there's something strange going on. I put it down to having had too much coffee on an oppressive, muggy, thundery day."

    Christian Horner, Team Principal, Red Bull Racing

    When the episodes recurred, Horner sought medical evaluation, only to find that the root cause was psychological overload rather than a somatic illness.

    "Then it happened again. I got checked out, and there was nothing physically the matter," Horner revealed. "They said, 'We think you have anxiety. Your breathing's very shallow.' It was my body telling me you're pushing pretty hard here. I'm able to recognise the symptoms now and let it go, and breathe."

    The "identity" void and praising Toto Wolff

    The physical manifestations of stress were eventually compounded by an abrupt identity vacuum following his unexpected departure from Red Bull in July 2025. After spending 20 years as the central decision-maker in Milton Keynes, being suddenly removed from that high-octane environment triggered a period of profound existential reflection.

    Reflecting on the psychological shock, Horner paid unexpected tribute to his fiercest career rival, Mercedes team principal Toto Wolff, who has spoken publicly about his own history with clinical depression and therapy.

    "Toto was very brave to talk openly about such a factor for people our age," Horner acknowledged. "I hadn't experienced those issues, but I had an appreciation during this period of how people could [suffer]."

    Christian Horner, Team Principal, Red Bull Racing, Toto Wolff, Team Principal and CEO, Mercedes AMG

    Horner emphasised that when high-performing professionals have their vocational purpose abruptly severed, the psychological fallout can be devastating.

    "The identity of many middle-aged guys is through their work. Suddenly you have that stripped and taken from you," Horner explained. "Who are you? What are you? What is your purpose? You either curl up into a ball or brush yourself down and get up."

    While Horner maintains that Formula 1 remains the primary arena that excites him professionally, stepping away from the paddock has provided an unprecedented lifestyle reset. After missing countless personal milestones over 21 consecutive seasons, Horner was able to spend race weekend in London celebrating his daughter Olivia's 13th birthday, taking her to the West End to watch the musical Hamilton.

    Asked what elements of the sport he genuinely misses, Horner was unequivocal about where the line is drawn.

    "The people, the team, the competition. I still love the sport," Horner concluded. "But I don't miss the travel and politics."
