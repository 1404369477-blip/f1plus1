    科拉平托发声：阿尔法罗密欧车队员工遭受网络攻击

    科拉平托在摩纳哥接受Motorsport.com独家采访时，详细讨论了社交媒体上针对阿尔法罗密欧车队员工的攻击事件。

    '界限被跨越' – 法兰科·科拉平托在针对阿尔派 F1 员工的网络骚扰后公开发声

    科拉平托感到有必要录制一段视频信息来向他的粉丝表达。据阿根廷人所说，网络骚扰不仅针对其他 F1 车手，还针对 阿尔派 车队的成员

    在 2026 年一级方程式赛季的媒体活动中，法拉利科·科拉平托一直面临着一个反复出现的话题：阿根廷人感到有必要在社交媒体上发声反对骚扰

    科拉平托在摩纳哥接受 Motorsport.com 的独家采访时详细讨论了这个问题，以及在此之后许多赛车周末的活动中

    上一次新加坡大奖赛之后，然而，阿尔卑斯车手感到有必要更进一步，直接通过视频信息向粉丝群体表达。在许多事情中，他表示感激所有粉丝的支持，但补充说，将仇恨指向他人对他的F1职业生涯毫无帮助。

    “首先，我一直非常感激所有支持我的粉丝，”科拉平托在被Motorsport.com问及本周末美国大奖赛前的声明时说。

    “但我认为在每一个体育项目和粉丝群体中，都有不好的一面，就是那些发表负面评论、不帮助运动员发挥最佳水平的人。对我来说，最近这已经越过了几条底线。

    “我认为可能是最后一件事，就是发表一个我像那样在视频中讲话的声明。这始终会是积极的，所以我非常乐意去做。”

    解释他所说的某些界限最近已被跨越的含义时，科拉平托透露，他自己的团队成员也成为了社交媒体上的目标。科拉平托认为这是不可接受的，这促使他再次公开发言。

    “特别是我的机械师们受到了很多恶意攻击，”这位23岁的年轻人透露。“我的工程师们也是如此，他们是我团队中希望我表现良好并帮助我发挥的人。我每天都和他们一起工作，看到他们收到这样的信息，这让我感到非常痛苦。”

    当被问及这些是团队官方Instagram账号上的评论还是发送给团队成员的个人消息时，科拉平托回答道：“我没有他们的手机，但据我所知，他们收到了信息和评论。再次强调，这对我来说已经越过了界限。”

    在实践中，这包括为科拉平托的赛车工作的机械师，他们从一节排位赛到下一节排位赛对赛车的前翼等进行调整。

    他们因此被错误地认为是艰难的一节练习的责任者，随后在他们的账户上收到了恶意的消息，尽管Motorsport.com了解阿尔派已经采取了措施来最小化影响。

    团队成员被建议保持他们的个人社交媒体账户私密，不要展示任何车队标志，以最小化收到不欢迎消息的风险。

    然而，这并没有解决根本问题，这正是科拉平托再次向粉丝发出紧急呼吁的原因。他也强调，这个问题不仅限于F1，而是影响整个社会。

    “我可能在过去的10场比赛中都谈到了这个问题，”他说。 “我尽力了，但我认为它一直在跨越一些界限。所以这是我能做的最后一件事，制作一个视频，我认为它有所帮助。”

    “你也看到了，Max也是。他也对此发表了评论。这在我们周围发生得很多。这是我们如今在体育界以及生活的许多不同领域面临的问题。艺术家、演员，无处不在。”

    “就我个人而言，这并没有对我造成太大的伤害。我不太关注那些评论，我不会太在意。但对一些人来说，这确实[影响了他们]。这就是为什么我们需要关注它。我们需要改进它，希望这个视频能稍微改善一下。”

    'Lines were crossed' – Franco Colapinto speaks out after online abuse targeting Alpine F1 staff

    Colapinto felt compelled to record a video message addressing his fans. According to the Argentine, the online abuse was directed not only at fellow F1 drivers, but also at Alpine team members

    It has become a recurring topic during Franco Colapinto's media sessions throughout the 2026 Formula 1 season: the Argentine feeling the need to speak out against abuse on social media.

    Colapinto discussed the issue at length during an exclusive interview with Motorsport.com in Monaco, as well as during many of the race weekends that have followed since.

    After the Singapore Grand Prix last time out, however, the Alpine driver felt the urgency to go a step further and address his fanbase directly in a video message. Among other things, he said he appreciated all the support from his fans, but added that directing hate towards others does nothing to help his own F1 career.

    “First of all, I've always been very grateful for all the fans supporting me,” said Colapinto, when asked by Motorsport.com about his statement ahead of this weekend’s United States Grand Prix.

    “But I think in every sport and fan base, it's got a bad side as well, the hate side of people putting bad comments and not helping athletes perform at their best. To me, it crossed a few lines lately.

    “I think it was probably the last thing I could do, to put out a statement of me talking in a video like that. It was always going to be positive, so I was keen to do it.”

    Explaining what he meant when he said that certain lines had recently been crossed, Colapinto revealed that even members of his own team had been targeted on social media. Colapinto found that unacceptable, prompting him to speak out once again.

    “Especially my mechanics got a lot of hate,” revealed the 23-year-old. “My engineers as well, people from my team that want me to do well and that help me to perform. I'm working with them daily and seeing them getting these kind of messages, it just hurts me in general.”

    Asked whether these were comments on the team's official Instagram account or personal messages sent to team members, Colapinto replied: “I don’t have their phones, but from what I understand, they receive messages and comments. Again, it just crossed the line for me.”

    In practice, this includes mechanics who work on Colapinto's car and make adjustments to, for example, the front wing from one qualifying segment to the next.

    They are then wrongly held responsible for a difficult session and subsequently receive hateful messages on their accounts, although Motorsport.com understands that Alpine has taken measures to minimise the impact.

    Team members are advised to keep their personal social media accounts private and not show any team logos, in order to minimise the risk of receiving unwanted messages.

    Nevertheless, that does not address the actual problem at its root, which is precisely why Colapinto has once again made an urgent appeal to fans. He also stressed that the problem is not limited to F1, but affects society as a whole.

    “I've been talking about this for the last 10 races probably,” he said. “I do my best, but I think it kept crossing some lines. So this was the last thing I could do, making a video, and I think it helped.

    “You saw it with Max as well. He also made a statement about it. It's happening a lot around us. It's an issue that we’ve got in sports nowadays, in sports and in many different areas of life. Artists, actors, it's everywhere.

    “Personally, it doesn’t hurt me too much. I don't look at those comments too much, I don't put too much attention. But for some it does [affect them]. And that’s why we need to put attention on it. We need to improve it and hopefully this video does improve it slightly.”
