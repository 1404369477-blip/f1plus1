    威廉姆斯放弃2026赛季空气动力学升级，为2027赛季蓄力

    威廉姆斯车队领队詹姆斯·沃尔斯表示，车队放弃2026赛季的空气动力学升级、将资源留给2027赛季，这一决定“痛苦至极”。威廉姆斯原本希望2025赛季在幕后取得的进展能延续到新规则之下，但全新赛车的巨大工作量堵塞了格罗夫工厂的系统，最终赛车交付延迟、超重，空气动力学开发也因此不足。

    “痛苦至极”——威廉姆斯放弃2026赛季赛车升级，以利于2027赛季

    威廉姆斯一级方程式车队负责人詹姆斯·沃尔斯不得不做出残酷且反直觉的决定，为车队布局2027赛季

    威廉姆斯F1车队领队詹姆斯·沃尔斯表示，车队放弃2026赛季空气动力学升级、转而将资源留给2027赛季，这一决定“痛苦至极”。

    威廉姆斯一段时间以来一直被迫着眼长远，但原本希望2025赛季在幕后取得的进展能延续到新规则之下。然而，威廉姆斯遭遇了巨大的警醒：打造一辆全新赛车所需的巨大工作量堵塞了格罗夫工厂的系统，最终赛车交付延迟、超重，空气动力学开发也因此不足。

    威廉姆斯投入了大量资源，使其底盘达到重量限制，并在阿塞拜疆引入了一个新样例，该样例将延续到明年。但车队迄今只拿到12分，沃尔斯不得不做出艰难决定，将车队剩余成本上限资源中的一部分圈定用于优化2027年赛车项目。

    这意味着决定不基于车队在风洞中看到的一些空气动力学收益来生产新部件。沃尔斯在巴西大奖赛对天空体育表示：“我们故意没有花钱，我们在风洞中锁定了更多空气动力学成果，却没有带到赛道上，实际上是为了把成本上限的美元省到明年。”

    出于同样原因，车队在备件方面也捉襟见肘，正如它最不想做的事情就是把钱花在生产很快就要过时的赛车部件上，因此“把一切都推到里程极限”。

    最近，威廉姆斯车队也将一些周五练习赛当作变相的测试来对待，收集数据为2027年的研发提供参考，代价是牺牲了当周末的性能优化，这对于已经将重心转向下一年的车队来说是一种相对常见的策略。

    “我们在奥斯汀和墨西哥所做的工作也是为2027年做的准备工作，”沃尔斯解释道。“理解这套套件、我们经历的空气动力学研发机制，这有助于我们抢占先机。”

    “我们清楚，以目前这辆赛车，只有运气站在我们这边时才会有积分入账，而背后的现实是，投资未来对我们更有利。”

    "It's horribly painful" - Williams drops 2026 car upgrades to benefit 2027

    Williams Formula 1 chief James Vowles has to make brutal, counterintuitive decisions to set the team up for 2027

    Williams F1 team boss James Vowles says it was "horribly painful" for the squad to forego 2026 aero upgrades and save the resources for 2027 instead.

    Williams has been forced into playing the long game for some time now, but was hoping its progress behind the scenes in 2025 would carry over into the new regulations. Instead, Williams faced a huge wake-up call as the huge workload required to build an all-new car clogged the Grove factory's systems, arriving with a car that was late, overweight and as a result aerodynamically underdeveloped.

    Williams poured a lot of resources in getting its chassis to the weight limit, introducing a new example in Azerbaijan that will be carried over next year. But with the squad only managing 12 points thus far, Vowles has had to make the tough decision to ringfence some of the team's remaining cost cap resources into optimising the 2027 car project.

    That has meant deciding against producing new parts off of some of the aerodynamic gains the team was seeing in the windtunnel. "We've purposely not spent money, we had more aero locked away in the wind tunnel that we have not brought to the track, really saving the cost cap dollars for next year," Vowles told Sky at the Brazilian Grand Prix.

    "It's horribly painful. I can't tell you how painful it was to sit in the meeting and make those decisions, because it's just not the way you think. You want every millisecond on track."

    The team is also marginal on spare parts for the same reason, "pushing everything to the limit of mileage" as the last thing it wants to do is spend money on producing soon-to-be obsolete car parts.

    More recently, Williams has also been treating some Friday practice sessions as glorified test runs, gathering data to inform its 2027 development at the expense of optimising performance on the weekend itself, which is a relatively common strategy for teams who have already switched focus to the following year.

    "The work we've been doing in Austin [and] Mexico was preparation work for 2027 as well," Vowles explained. "Understanding the package, the aero development mechanisms we've gone through, it helps us get ahead of the curve.

    "We're aware that with this car at the moment, points are only going to be there if fortune falls our way, and the reality behind it is we're better off investing in the future."
