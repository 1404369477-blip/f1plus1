    卡塔尔方面预计2026年F1大奖赛门票售罄

    尽管中东地区冲突引发安全担忧，卡塔尔摩托车运动联合会主席阿尔·曼奈表示，2026年F1卡塔尔大奖赛预计周日满座，大部分看台和全部款待区已售罄。

    卡塔尔摩托车运动联合会表示，尽管赛前存在地区冲突及相关安全担忧，仍"预计"2026年F1卡塔尔大奖赛将"满座"。

    中东冲突曾令2026年卡塔尔和阿布扎比大奖赛能否举办存疑，巴林和沙特阿拉伯分站此前已在今年早些时候被推迟。

    伊莫拉曾是这两场赛季收官战的备选场地，但该意大利赛道在11月被取消备用安排，F1坚持按计划在卡塔尔和阿布扎比比赛。

    尽管外界担忧该地区安全，卡塔尔摩托车运动联合会（QMMF）主席阿卜杜勒拉赫曼·本·阿卜杜勒拉蒂夫·阿尔·曼奈表示："我们预计周日满座。大部分看台已售罄，全部款待区也已售罄。"

    "我认为只有一个看台（尚未坐满），大概今晚就会售罄，我猜。到目前为止，实际上，我们已经售罄了。"

    阿尔·曼奈——他也是卢赛尔赛道的总裁——表示，MotoGP卡塔尔站对本月初因以色列/美国-伊朗战争而从4月推迟到11月举行，对让观众和参赛者感到安全而言是一个重要的里程碑。

    "这实际上很重要，而且真的令人兴奋，我们这里座无虚席，我想我们创下了最高的上座人数——如你所知，MotoGP已经在这里举办了二十多年（自2004年以来），而那是我们规模最大的一次。

    "我认为锦标赛也对上座率产生了影响，但它证明了信任确实非常重要。"

    2026年卡塔尔MotoGP分站赛在雅马哈及其卫星车队Pramac退出后，由11支车队中的9支参赛，尽管存在安全顾虑，仍吸引了创纪录的55,120名观众。

    阿尔·曼奈表示，他“非常高兴”F1车队没有退出2026年F1比赛。

    他说上座率“远超预期”，并将这一情况与2020年全球大流行相提并论。

    “我觉得这让我想起了新冠时期，当时所有人都想，‘哦，没人会出门，没人会去参加活动’。结果人们反而疯狂地涌向各种活动，”阿尔·曼奈说。

    他补充说，“非常高”的需求意味着卡塔尔摩托车联合会（QMMF）无需提供优惠来吸引观众。

    “我认为这充分说明了如今一级方程式赛车有多么强大，以及我们拥有的车迷群体有多么热爱这项赛事。需求真的非常高。”

    卡塔尔未考虑巴林式的迁移

    阿尔曼奈表示，尽管2026年F1赛季最后两轮存在不确定性、伊莫拉作为应急举办地，卢赛尔赛道始终按赛事照常举行来做准备。

    他说：“当时的情况带来了一些疑虑。但对我们来说，在规划和努力确保我们为比赛做好绝对准备方面，我们从未停止为这些比赛进行准备和规划。”

    “我想不出有什么事情太过棘手，以至于让我们无法达到那个地步。所以我们按计划推进，并假定我们知道将会发生的事件。”

    “而一级方程式或MotoGP，他们从未说过‘我们不来了’。每个人都说‘我们会来，但我们正在评估情况’。情况并非反过来，并不是说‘除非这件事发生，否则我们不来’。”

    “这就是为什么我们从未停止整合我们的计划并执行比赛的各项计划。”

    阿尔·曼奈补充说，卡塔尔大奖赛并未考虑像巴林大奖赛在马来西亚那样，通过在其他国家举办分站来实质性地呈现一场比赛。

    “不，我们是在赛季末，在阿布扎比之前。那从来都不是我们想做的事情。但显然对他们（巴林）来说非常成功，”他说。

    围绕卡塔尔大奖赛的安全担忧集中在伊朗在冲突中的角色上。

    据卡塔尔内政部称，伊朗于2026年2月向卡塔尔发射了66枚导弹，随后在3月和4月又发动了多次袭击。

    阿尔·曼奈表示，他所在赛道的安全措施是基于卡塔尔政府的指导方针制定的。

    “他们最终会告诉我们，可以继续推进，或者不要继续推进。如果他们说‘做吧’，我们就做。”

    他补充说，卢赛尔赛道并未对今年赛事的安全计划做出“重大改变”，因为针对紧急情况所采用的计划与其他赛事“完全相同”。

    英文原文

    The Qatar motorsport federation is "expecting a full house" for the 2026 Formula 1 Grand Prix despite regional conflict and associated safety concerns in the lead up to its running.

    Conflict in the Middle East led to uncertainty over whether the 2026 Qatar and Abu Dhabi GPs would take place, the Bahrain and Saudi Arabian rounds having earlier in the year been postponed.

    Imola was the contingency venue, had it not been able to stage the two season-ending races, but the Italian circuit was stood down in November as F1 committed to continuing its plan to race in Qatar and Abu Dhabi.

    Despite worries over safety in the region, Qatar Motor and Motorcycle Federation (QMMF) president Abdulrahman Bin Abdullatif Al Mannai said: "We are expecting a full house on Sunday. We sold out most of our grandstands, we sold out all the hospitalities.

    "I think there is only one grandstand [not yet full] that should be sold out within probably tonight, I'm guessing. So far, actually, we are a sell-out."

    Al Mannai - who is also president of the Lusail circuit - said that MotoGP's Qatar round, which did go ahead earlier this month having been moved from April to November due to the Israel/US-Iran war, was a significant milestone for making spectators and entrants feel safe.

    "It was actually important and it was really exciting, we had a full house here, I think we had the highest number of attendance - as you know MotoGP has been here for more than two [decades, since 2004] and that was the biggest one we had.

    "I think the championship also had an impact on attendance, but it proved that trust is really important."

    The 2026 Qatar MotoGP round, contested by nine of its 11 teams following the withdrawal of Yamaha and its satellite team Pramac, attracted a record 55,120 spectators despite safety concerns.

    Al Mannai said he was "very happy” that F1 teams did not withdraw from the 2026 F1 race.

    He said that attendance was "much better than expected" and equated the situation to the global pandemic in 2020.

    "I think it reminded me of COVID, when everybody thought, 'Oh, nobody's going to go out, nobody's going to go to events'. And just people went crazy about going to events," said Al Mannai.

    He added that "really high" demand meant the QMMF did not need to offer concessions to attract spectators.

    "I think it says a lot about how strong Formula 1 is today and the fanbase we have and how much they love the event. The demand is really high."

    Qatar didn't consider Bahrain-style relocation

    Al Mannai said that despite uncertainty over the last two rounds of the 2026 F1 season, with Imola as the contingency venue, the Lusail circuit always planned for the event to take place.

    "With the situation, it brought some doubt. But for us in terms of planning and trying to make sure we are absolutely ready for the race, we never stopped preparing and planning for these races," he said.

    "And I cannot think of anything that was too challenging to make us reach that point. So we went ahead with our plans and we assumed the events we know will happen.

    "And Formula 1 or MotoGP, they never said, 'We're not coming'. Everybody said, 'We are coming, but we are assessing the situation'. It wasn't the other way around, it wasn't like, 'We're not coming until this happens'.

    "And that's why we never stopped putting our plans together and executing the plans for the races."

    Al Mannai added that the Qatar GP did not consider essentially presenting a race by holding its round in a different country, as the Bahrain GP did in Malaysia.

    "No, we are at the end of the season, we are before Abu Dhabi. That never came to be something we wanted to do. But obviously it was very successful for them [Bahrain]," he said.

    Safety concerns around the Qatar GP revolve around Iran's role in the conflict.

    Iran fired 66 missiles at Qatar in February 2026 according to Qatar's interior ministry, before numerous attacks in March and April.

    Al Mannai said that his circuit's safety measures were based on the Qatari government’s guidelines.

    "They tell us, go ahead, or don't go ahead, at the end of the day. If they say, 'Do it', we do it."

    He added that Lusail did not make "big changes" to the safety plan for this year's event as "exactly the same plan" is in place for emergencies as for other events.
