    汉密尔顿拉斯维加斯受罚后质疑干事判罚一致性

    2026年F1拉斯维加斯站，汉密尔顿被罚10秒，他在法拉利车队无线电中质疑国际汽联干事判罚的一致性，该无线电内容未在转播中播出。

    汉密尔顿在F1拉斯维加斯站受罚后，在未播出的无线电中质疑干事。

    汉密尔顿在被罚10秒后，在未播出的拉斯维加斯站无线电中质疑国际汽联判罚的一致性。

    致法拉利车迷。来自马拉内罗及更广范围的最新法拉利F1新闻、车手动态、技术分析和比赛反应。

    2026年F1拉斯维加斯站，汉密尔顿的追击之战在比赛前半段遭遇重大挫折，并促使他在法拉利车队无线电中就国际汽联干事判罚的一致性提出尖锐抱怨。

    周五晚上两辆法拉利在Q2被淘汰后，汉密尔顿从第11位起步，在与队友夏尔·勒克莱尔和迈凯伦车手兰多·诺里斯激烈争夺的首个赛段中攀升至积分区。大约在第13圈，为争夺第七名而在7-8号弯减速弯缠斗时，汉密尔顿在与诺里斯争夺中冲入缓冲区并切了弯。

    尽管迈凯伦占据赛车线时弯心处的空间看起来十分狭窄，但赛会干事认定汉密尔顿离开赛道并获得持久优势，因此处以10秒罚时。

    在一段未在全球转播信号中播出的车队无线电中，汉密尔顿对这一处罚的严厉程度表达了明显的不满。

    “这不是很有意思吗？有一场比赛某人不会接受，就会有减轻处罚的情节；他们给他们五秒，却因为我避免了一次事故给我10秒，”汉密尔顿对比赛工程师说。

    为什么汉密尔顿提到“五秒”格外引人注意

    汉密尔顿在发言中没有点名具体车手或大奖赛，但他提到“减轻处罚情节”以及减至五秒的罚时，立刻让人想起他所在车队内部最近的一次争议事件。

    三周前，也就是11月1日的墨西哥城大奖赛上，勒克莱尔在首圈4号弯从赛道外完成了对汉密尔顿的超越。根据一级方程式的驾驶标准准则，离开赛道并获得优势通常会被处以10秒罚时。然而在墨西哥，赛会干事将勒克莱尔的处罚减半至五秒，并明确提到了减轻处罚情节。

    国际汽联赛会干事在墨西哥城的裁决中写道：“基准罚时准则为10秒；但减轻处罚的情节在于，44号赛车在16号赛车内侧时也离开了赛道，而且这发生在第一圈早期。”

    刘易斯·汉密尔顿，法拉利，查尔斯·勒克莱尔，法拉利

    当时这一裁决在围场引发了争论。汉密尔顿——他曾在2025年墨西哥因类似的赛道外违规被罚10秒——在比赛中通过车队无线电争辩说，较轻的处罚未能抵消留在前面对干净空气的益处，而最终赢得比赛的最大维斯塔潘事后也表示，“通常，那个处罚是10秒，现在突然变成5秒了”。

    从规则角度看，这两项裁决取决于具体情境：干事们通常对拥挤的第一圈事件比比赛中段的争夺更为宽容，后者仍默认适用完整的10秒准则。

    尽管如此，汉密尔顿在拉斯维加斯的无线电言论证明，车手们对于这些准则在每个比赛周末被灵活应用的方式仍存有不满——这是国际汽联希望在2027年开始逐步引入全职F1干事时缓解的一个痛点。

    Lewis Hamilton questions stewards in unaired radio message after F1 Vegas penalty

    Lewis Hamilton questions FIA penalty consistency in unaired Las Vegas radio after receiving a 10-second time penalty

    For Ferrari fans. The latest Scuderia Ferrari F1 news, driver updates, technical analysis and race reaction from Maranello and beyond.

    Lewis Hamilton’s recovery drive at the 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix hit a major snag in the opening half of the race - and prompted a pointed complaint over Ferrari team radio regarding FIA stewarding consistency.

    Starting 11th after both Ferraris were eliminated in Q2 on Friday night, Hamilton climbed into the points during a fiercely contested opening stint alongside team-mate Charles Leclerc and McLaren’s Lando Norris. Around Lap 13, while fighting for seventh place through the Turn 7–8 chicane, Hamilton ran into the run-off area and cut the corner while duelling Norris.

    Although space at the apex appeared tight as the McLaren occupied the racing line, stewards determined Hamilton left the track and gained a lasting advantage, imposing a 10-second time penalty.

    In a team radio message not broadcast on the global feed, Hamilton expressed clear frustration with the severity of the sanction.

    "Isn't that interesting? One race where someone won't have it, and they'll be mitigating circumstances; they give them five seconds, they give me 10 for avoiding an incident," Hamilton told his race engineer.

    Why Hamilton’s “five seconds” reference stands out

    Hamilton did not name a specific driver or grand prix in his message, yet his reference to "mitigating circumstances" and a reduced five-second penalty immediately recalled a recent flashpoint inside his own team.

    Three weeks earlier at the Mexico City Grand Prix on 1 November, Leclerc completed an off-track pass on Hamilton at Turn 4 on the opening lap. Under Formula 1’s driving standards guidelines, leaving the track and gaining an advantage carries a standard 10-second penalty. In Mexico, however, the stewards halved Leclerc’s sanction to five seconds, explicitly citing mitigation.

    "The baseline penalty guideline is 10 seconds; however, in mitigation is the fact that car 44 was also off track on the inside of car 16 and this occurred early in the first lap," the FIA stewards wrote in their Mexico City decision.

    Lewis Hamilton, Ferrari, Charles Leclerc, Ferrari

    That ruling sparked debate across the paddock at the time. Hamilton - who had served a 10-second penalty for a similar off-track offence in Mexico in 2025 - argued over team radio during the race that the lesser penalty failed to offset the clean-air benefit of staying ahead, while eventual race winner Max Verstappen also remarked afterwards that "normally, that penalty is 10 seconds, and now suddenly it's five".

    From a regulatory standpoint, the two rulings hinge on context: stewards routinely apply greater leniency to crowded opening-lap incidents than to mid-race battles, where the full 10-second guideline remains the default.

    Still, Hamilton’s Las Vegas radio comments prove there is lingering driver frustration over how flexibly those guidelines are applied from weekend to weekend - a sore point the FIA hopes to ease when it begins phasing in full-time Formula 1 stewards in 2027.
