    安东内利通过了皮亚斯特里曾未能通过的关键考验

    安东内利坦言阿塞拜疆大奖赛周末是他本赛季“迄今为止最糟”的比赛，但他展现了如何在糟糕的周末中限制损失，成功通过了争夺世界冠军途中不可避免的一次考验，避免了皮亚斯特里去年冲击F1总冠军时在巴库遭遇的崩盘命运。

    世界冠军不只靠好日子赢下，糟糕的日子同样决定成败。

    基米·安东内利的阿塞拜疆大奖赛周末——他自己正确地形容为“本赛季迄今最糟糕的周末”——正是这一点的证明，他通过了迟早都会面对的一次考验，而且表现出色。这样做，他避免了去年奥斯卡·皮亚斯特里在冲击一级方程式冠军失败途中遭遇的命运。

    尽量减少这种糟糕表现是夺冠方程式的一部分，但更关键的是在出问题时控制损失。我们知道安东内利带着高达81分的巨大领先优势进入这个周末，而他在巴库展示的是他懂得如何使用这个优势。这正是皮亚斯特里没能做到的——他2025年带着31分的领先优势来到巴库，虽然不那么悬殊，但依然健康。

    皮亚斯特里在排位赛中撞车，发车前抢跑，明知即使排名暴跌也难逃处罚，随后又在第一圈的5号弯撞车。唯一的慰藉是迈凯伦队友、冠军争夺对手兰多·诺里斯没有充分利用这个机会，但即便如此，这标志着皮亚斯特里的夺冠征程开始瓦解。

    在那一年巴西大奖赛的比赛周末，我曾问及他的困境，他提到了阿塞拜疆站，说那个周末“可以说有一些其他因素可能悄悄混了进来”，影响了他的表现。很难不得出这样的结论：他指的是蒙扎“木瓜规则”事件的余波——当时因为队友诺里斯进站较慢，他被车队下令让诺里斯超越。

    即使没有车队指令令人困惑这一复杂因素的干扰，安东内利也本可能轻易遭遇类似的局面，尤其是考虑到他经验尚浅，此前他在第一节排位赛中不必要地撞上了护墙。

    这是一个重大失误，因为在排位赛这么早的阶段完全没必要冒险。这个失误或许是为了缩小与梅赛德斯队友乔治·拉塞尔之间速度差距而用力过猛的结果，或许也与他今年一贯倾向于过度贴近赛道边缘的老毛病有关，但尽管他本不该犯这个错误，他却以完全正确的心态投入了正赛。

    他最终 Recovery 到第五名有安全车的帮助，但这是一场稳健、执行到位的比赛：他在与奥利·比尔曼的碰撞中无辜受损、车底板受伤之后，又在第一次安全车重启的1号弯混乱中保持冷静，刻意留出了余量，稳住了自己的头脑。

    安东内利说：“我开得有点像我奶奶，完全就是避免受损的安全模式，完全不冒险。在做动作之前，我都会先确认自己有把握才去做。”

    这展现出一种与他缺乏经验不相称的成熟——对于学习速度没那么快的车手来说，这种智慧往往要付出高昂代价、经历惨痛教训才能换来。他后来详细解释了自己这样做的理由，同时也表示，在大奖赛刚开始时，他很快就放下了想要复制蒙扎式翻盘的幻想。虽然他在排位赛后表示自己“已经没有什么可输的了”，但赛后接受采访时，他解释了为什么实际上并非如此。

    安东内利说：“如果我有蒙扎那站或者之前几场比赛那样的节奏，结果就会不一样。那就会是全力进攻。但我很早就意识到，首先，当一长串车都处在尝试超车的状态下，超车会难得多。而且我的节奏也不如之前。所以心态很快就转变了。

    而且看到那么多人犯错，你也会稍微收敛一点。我只是利用了其他所有人的失误，就这样向前赶了上去。

    梅赛德斯车队领队托托·沃尔夫以及他身边的人肯定会反复向他灌输这种处理方式，但只有安东内利自己才能锁定一种与平时让他受益匪浅的进攻型心态相悖的思维模式。结果就是他在积分榜上落后拉塞尔15分而不是25分。这是一个沉重的打击，但还不算最糟的。

    假如他当时过于激进，发生撞车或与对手碰撞退赛，那损失的不只是积分，还有他会带进赛季末冲刺阶段的心理创伤。相反，安东内利以自己形容为八成力度的表现完成了这场比赛，对自己的处理方式感到满意，并能在接下来的马来西亚大奖赛上向拉塞尔发出挑战。

    他说：“下周我一定要重新确立顺序，确保回到我平时的水平，因为整个周末我都开得像个老奶奶。所以这不是平时的基米。不过幸运的是，还有下一场比赛在等着我们，我非常期待。”

    整个2026年，安东内利凭借摧枯拉朽般的速度和不懈的稳定性一次次展现了他的实力。而在巴库，他展示了成为冠军所需素质中同样重要的另一面。

    英文原文

    You win world championships on your bad days as much as you do on your good ones.

    Kimi Antonelli's Azerbaijan Grand Prix weekend, which he rightly described as "the worst by far" of the season, was proof of that as he passed a test he would inevitably face at some point with flying colours. In doing so, he avoided a fate that befell Oscar Piastri in last year's unsuccessful pursuit of the Formula 1 title.

    Minimising such performances is part of the title-winning equation, but it's essential to limit the damage when things go wrong. We knew Antonelli had the comfort of a gargantuan 81-point lead heading into the weekend, but what he demonstrated in Baku was he understands how to use it. That's something Piastri failed to do with the more slender, but still healthy, 31-point lead he took to Baku in 2025.

    Piastri crashed in qualifying, moved before the start and knew a penalty was inevitable despite plummeting down the order, then crashed at Turn 5 on the first lap. The only saving grace was that McLaren team-mate and title rival Lando Norris didn't make the most of his opportunity, but even so this represented the start of Piastri's campaign unravelling.

    I asked him during that year's Brazilian GP weekend about his struggles and it was Azerbaijan that he mentioned as a weekend when "you could argue there were some other things that maybe crept in" and compromised him. It's difficult to escape the conclusion that he was pointing to the hangover of the 'papaya rules' incident from Monza where he was ordered to let Norris past after his team-mate's slow pitstop.

    Even without the complicating factor of such team-orders bafflement, Antonelli could so easily have let something similar happen, especially given he's still so inexperienced, after hitting the wall needlessly in Q1.

    That was a significant error given there was no reason to take risks so early in qualifying. That blunder was perhaps the consequence of overreaching to close the pace deficit to Mercedes team-mate George Russell, perhaps because of his longstanding tendency to flirt a little too much with the extremities of the track this year, but while ideally he wouldn't have made the error he took exactly the right mentality into the race.

    His recovery to fifth was aided by the safety car, but it was a steady, well-executed drive in which he deliberately left a margin and kept his head after sustaining floor damage through no fault of his own after contact with Ollie Bearman in the Turn 1 chaos of the first safety-car restart.

    "I was driving a bit like my grandmother, just damage-safe mode, just didn't take a risk," said Antonelli. "Before making a move, I was making sure that I could go for it."

    This showed wisdom that belies his inexperience that is often hard-won and at great cost by drivers who aren't such fast learners. He later elaborated on his reasons for this, as well as indicating that he quickly set aside any pretensions of a Monza-style recovery early in the grand prix. While he suggested he had "nothing to lose" after qualifying, when he spoke following the race he explained why that wasn't really the case.

    "If I had the same pace as at Monza or in previous races, it would have been different," said Antonelli. "It would have been full attack. But I realised very early that, first of all, it was a lot harder to overtake with a train of cars all in overtake mode. And then I didn't have the same pace as I had previously. So the mindset changed very quickly.

    "And also seeing so many people doing mistakes, you kind of pulled back a little bit. I just took advantage of everyone else's mistake and just moved forward."

    Mercedes team principal Toto Wolff and others around him will have drummed that approach into him, but only Antonelli himself could lock into a mindset at odds with the attacking one that usually serves him so well. The result was losing 15 points to Russell in the championship rather than 25. A big hit, but not as big as it could have been.

    Had he been too aggressive and crashed or collided with a rival and retired, the points loss would be multiplied by the mental scars he would carry into the run-in. Instead, Antonelli could finish a race he described as driving at 80% satisfied with his approach and able to lay down the gauntlet to Russell for the coming race in Malaysia.

    "I'll make sure next week I re-establish the order and just make sure that I will be back at my usual level because this weekend I was driving more like a grandmother," he said. "So it wasn't the usual Kimi. But, luckily, we have another race coming up and I'm really looking forward to it."

    Antonelli has showed his class time and again during 2026 with his pulverising pace and relentlessness. In Baku, he showed a different aspect that's just as important in the make-up of a champion.
