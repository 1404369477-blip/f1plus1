    拉塞尔回应维斯塔潘言论：觉得好笑但坚持己见

    拉塞尔回顾了与维斯塔潘在拉斯维加斯大奖赛期间的言语交锋，以及自己在正赛中的关键失误。

    乔治·拉塞尔：马克斯·维斯塔潘的言论“好笑”，但我坚持我说过的话

    乔治·拉塞尔回顾了他与马克斯·维斯塔潘的言语交锋，以及自己在F1拉斯维加斯大奖赛上的关键失误

    两年前差不多也是这个时候，两人曾有过一次重大争执；上周在拉斯维加斯，他们再次发生冲突。起因是练习中的一次阻挡事件，这让这位英国车手非常愤怒——维斯塔潘在最后一弯超越拉塞尔，毁掉了这位梅赛德斯车手的飞驰圈，而这是荷兰人向来不太在意打破的一条不成文规矩。

    拉塞尔随后将赛车插入内线，把维斯塔潘挤出了赛道。

    最终结果是，在赛季这个平静的时期，维斯塔潘和拉塞尔在赛道外的竞争关系被毫无必要地重演了一遍，虽然还算有点娱乐性。

    “他每周都和很多车手这样，大家都接受了，而我不会就这样接受有人这样坑我，然后还像自己比所有人都更大更好一样四处走动。总得有人站出来对抗那家伙，”拉塞尔说。

    所以，当拉塞尔试图超越队友基米·安东内利时，把梅赛德斯赛车撞上了墙，而当时他正跟在领跑的维斯塔潘身后，这位四届世界冠军在赛后无法掩饰自己的窃喜。“他在赛道旁边话很多。老实说，对我来说这真的无所谓，因为他远低于我的水平，所以对我来说，去尝试挑战那个根本没有任何意义。所以，这并不困扰我。今天因果报应真是个婊子。”

    几天过去，情绪已经冷却，虽然拉塞尔在与钢铁般坚韧的荷兰人的心理博弈中可能赢不了太多，但他坚持自己说过的话。

    拉塞尔在被追问这个话题时若有所思地说：“我觉得赛后的一些评论挺有意思的，但说到底，他跑了一场不可思议的比赛并赢了下来，所以他完全有资格想说什么就说什么。”

    “说到底，我想我坚持练习赛后说过的话，但他是那个真正上场赢下比赛的人。我没有。”

    “无论换成哪位车手，我在赛道上的回应都会是一样的，因为你知道，如果你进入最后一个弯道、准备自己的飞驰圈，要是有人在那个弯道超越了你，你的下一圈就毁了。

    所以你要么决定试着夺回位置、像平常一样开始自己的圈，要么就接受你会失去下一圈，因为你离前车太近了。”

    拉塞尔本可以在赛道上回应，通过赢下比赛来做到这一点，他和维斯塔潘在一圈又一圈的较量中展开了类似椭圆赛道般的超车盛宴。但在落后于安东内利之后，拉塞尔在4号弯脏兮兮的内侧试图超越队友时冒了太大的风险，结果撞上了墙。

    这位英国车手承认，他太渴望在赛季末取得一个重大成绩，以至于在经历了残酷的一个赛季后，他可能为了再拿下一场能提振士气的胜利而冒了太多风险。

    “显然，我在维加斯的失误发生在我试图超越队友的时候，”他说。“我认为事实是，我目前的风险刻度可能有点太高了，因为我只是想要去争取这些重大成绩，在赛季末的记分板上留下一些亮点。

    “如果那是赛季的第一场比赛，我绝不可能在还剩两圈时去尝试那个动作。但赛季还剩两场比赛，我有点想去拼一下。”

    “这并非出于任何沮丧情绪。只是愿意冒更多风险去争取胜利。事后看这算是个错误吗？是的。但正如我所说，我本可以保守一些，我有信心那样能拿到第三或第二。而且我大概会对自己感到沮丧，因为我没有去尝试那个动作。”

    回顾他与维斯塔潘的最新争执，拉塞尔暗示自己在向媒体表达想法时过于坦率，他说这是整个事件中他“最大的收获”。

    “也许我一直错在过于开放和坦诚，”他补充道。“我觉得我和所有媒体、记者都保持着良好关系，有时你会忘记自己不是在跟一个人说话。我是在对着电视上的数百万人说话，所以我认为这种良好关系实际上可能反而稍微妨碍了我。我想，这就是我们所在这个世界的人生教训。

    “竞争在赛道上，那才是我想要展开较量地方。我非常尊重马克斯的驾驶天赋以及他在赛道上取得的一切成就。真的就是这样。我并不想和任何人关系不佳。那真的不是我的为人。”

    George Russell: Max Verstappen comments "amusing", but I stand by what I said

    George Russell has reflected on his war of words with Max Verstappen and his crucial mistake at Formula 1's Las Vegas Grand Prix

    Two years after their last major spat around this time of the year, Russell and Verstappen clashed again last week in Vegas after an impeding incident in free practice that infuriated the Briton, with Verstappen ruining the Mercedes driver's lap by passing him into the last corner - an unwritten rule that the Dutchman doesn't tend to mind breaking.

    Russell responded by sending his car up the inside, pushing Verstappen off.

    The end result was a needless, though moderately entertaining at this quiet time of the season, reprise of Verstappen's and Russell's off-track rivalry.

    "He does this week in week out with many drivers and everybody accepts it, and I'm not just going to accept somebody to screw me over like this and walk around like they're bigger and better than everyone else. Somebody's got to stand up to that guy," Russell said.

    So, when Russell sent his Mercedes into the wall while trying to pass team-mate Kimi Antonelli, right behind leader Verstappen, the four-time world champion couldn't hide his glee post-race. "He speaks a lot next to the track. And honestly, for me, it doesn't really matter because he's so far below my level that it doesn't really make any sense for me anyway, to try and challenge that. So, it doesn't bother me. And today karma is a bitch."

    A few days on, tempers have cooled and while Russell may not win too many mind games against the steely Dutchman, he stands by what he said.

    "I found some of the comments post-race quite amusing, but at the end of the day, he drove an incredible race and won it, so he can afford to say what he wants, really," Russell mused when grilled on the topic.

    "At the end of the day, I guess I stand by what I said after practice, but he was the one who went out and won the race. I didn't.

    "How I responded on track would have been the same if it was any driver, because you know if you're going into the last corner, preparing for your lap, if somebody does overtake you into that corner, your next lap is screwed.

    So you either have a decision just to try and retake the position and start your lap like normal or just accept you're going to lose that next lap because you're too close to the car in front."

    Russell could have responded on-track instead, by winning the race as he and Verstappen battled lap after lap as part of an oval-like overtaking bonanza. But having fallen behind Antonelli, Russell took too big a risk trying to overtake his team-mate on the dirty inside of Turn 4 and veered into the wall.

    The Briton admitted he is so eager for a big result at the end of the season that he's probably taking too many risks to try and get one more morale-boosting win after what has been a brutal campaign.

    "Obviously, my mistake in Vegas came when I was trying to overtake my team-mate," he said. "I think the truth is my risk dial is probably a bit too great at the moment because I'm just kind of wanting to go for these big results and get some highlights on the board towards the end of the season.

    "If that was race one of the season, there's no way I would have gone for that move with two laps remaining. But with two races to go in the season, I kind of wanted to go for it.

    "It wasn't out of any frustration. It was just willing to risk more to get a victory. Was it a mistake in hindsight? Yes. But as I mentioned, I could have been conservative and I'm confident I would have finished third or second. And I probably would have been frustrated with myself that I didn't go for the move."

    Reflecting on his latest spat with Verstappen, Russell suggested he had been too open with the media in saying what he thinks, which he says is his "biggest takeaway" from the whole episode.

    "Maybe I have been guilty of being just too open and honest," he added. "I feel with all of the media and the journalists I have a good relationship with and you sometimes forget that you're not talking to one person. I'm talking to millions of people on the television, so I think that good relationship has actually probably hindered me a little bit. And that's, I guess, life lessons in this world we live in.

    "The competition is on track and that's where I want to have the battles. I respect Max immensely for his driving talents and everything he's achieved on track. And that's that, really. I'm not looking to have poor relationships with people. That's just not really the type of person I am."
