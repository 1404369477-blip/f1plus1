    Mercedes chief Toto Wolff has dismissed suggestions from "couch warriors" that the Silver Arrows failed to treat George Russell fairly during the Italian Grand Prix.

    Russell was narrowly leading the race from his team-mate Kimi Antonelli when Mercedes took a decision to pit the Italian under a virtual safety car.

    The call ultimately benefitted Antonelli as he used his fresh tyres to pull off a stunning victory having started from 19th on the grid, which increased Russell's deficit to the Italian at the top of the standings to 66 points.

    Speaking to Sky Sports F1 ahead of qualifying at this weekend's Spanish Grand Prix, Wolff reiterated his belief that the fact Antonelli was running on a medium tyre and Russell a hard tyre completely justified the decision.

    Highlights from the 2026 Italian Grand Prix.

    "It's absolutely the right thing we did," Wolff said. "He (Russell) was on the hard. It was predicted to be a one-stop as the better strategy.

    "The medium was a compromise; we had to put the medium on Kimi because he started on the hard.

    "There is just no way, and obviously there are some couch warriors that will drop their comments after having watched some other material online… saying that he was penalised is complete bull****."

    Kimi Antonelli and George Russell go wheel-to-wheel for the Italian Grand Prix lead in several epic team-mate battles.

    Russell started the season as the clear title favourite but has been outperformed by Antonelli, with the 20-year-old having dramatically exceeded expectations during just his second F1 campaign.

    The Brit backed Mercedes' decision following the race in Monza, insisting that Antonelli deserved credit for his performance.

    Wolff admitted that "harsh calls" are required to win championships but insisted that there is no "hidden agenda or favouritism" with his team.

    "That's been part of our role in the last 12 years, to manage both sides of the garage well," Wolff said. "Because if you are in a title fight, one is always going to be disappointed after a race and the other one is going to be happy.

    George Russell explains why the team chose not to pit him during the VSC, which potentially could have cost him the win in Monza.

    "I think as long as you're transparent and there is no hidden agenda or favouritism, then it's the best to manage it.

    "But it doesn't mean that we are not making some harsh calls. We are having stern talks on a Sunday morning - what are the various situations we are facing? Behind some pressure from another team, and one of our drivers behind the other one and it's too slow, we're going to invert the cars when it's needed.

    "But it's a decision that you need to take sometimes, in either direction by the way, whoever is in front. The decisions you need to take in order to win a championship, but it's always going to be balanced."

    'We could have looked like idiots, like Ferrari'

    Brundle and Rosberg debate who was at fault for the collision between Hamilton-Leclerc.

    Ferrari shared the headlines with Antonelli following the Italian Grand Prix after a first-lap incident between Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton, which saw the former push his team-mate into the gravel, resulting in the loss of several places.

    Wolff said that the clash made Ferrari look "like idiots" but wanted to avoid being overly critical of his rivals given Antonelli and Russell came close to also leaving Mercedes red-faced.

    The Austrian said: "I can express my opinion now and say it's not on to push your team-mate off in Turn 2, but three or four laps from the end, we had some scary moments between the two of them that we didn't expect in that way because George had a tyre that was shot, and Kimi was a second quicker.

    "Did we expect them to fight like they did, which could have ended up with a car in the gravel?

    "We could have quickly looked like idiots as well, like the Ferraris looked, eventually, so it's a fine margin."
