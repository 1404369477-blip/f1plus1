    卡塔尔大奖赛F1车手面临2026赛季最高胎压要求

    在卡塔尔大奖赛上，F1车手将面对2026赛季最高的最低前轮胎压，这也是赛事周末开始前有史以来的最高规定值。

    卢赛尔赛道的独特特征——包括连续高速弯和激进的 pyramid 式路肩——使其成为对轮胎最具挑战性的赛道之一。

    这是赛季横向能量最高的赛道之一，高速右弯偏多意味着左前胎承受特别大的压力。

    倍耐力历来在卡塔尔采用三种手段来降低轮胎故障风险：带来最硬配方、提高最低胎压，以及规定最长 stint 长度，使每套轮胎不会被用到寿命尽头。

    今年，三项响应参数再次被采集，且压力相比去年略有上调。

    在国际汽联于卡塔尔大奖赛周末前发送给车队的官方通知中，前轮最低胎压设定为29psi，而后轮则设定为27.5psi。

    对前轮而言，这是全年最高值，此前的基准为28psi——该数值是在铃鹿、银石和斯帕设定的。

    对后轮而言，卡塔尔的数值比去年赛前规定高出4psi，但并非2026年的峰值。斯帕和拉斯维加斯均为28.0psi。

    在2025年卡塔尔大奖赛上，倍耐力在进入比赛周末时设定的最低前胎压为28psi，但在周五练习后发现轮胎橡胶出现割伤，于是将胎压提高到29psi——他们认为这是赛车碾过被带上赛道的碎石所致。

    这一次是否需要在比赛周末期间做出类似调整的可能性不能被排除，尤其是因为2026款赛车在主动空气动力学下的表现只有在赛道活动开始后才会变得清晰。

    本赛季最低胎压上升的原因之一，是倍耐力不得不防范直线模式失效的风险——一旦失效，将在直道上给轮胎施加巨大负荷。

    这使得后胎最低胎压被提高，产生了明显变化，在卡塔尔也不例外：去年的基准值为23.5周五后提高到24，如今则设定为27.5psi。

    卡塔尔赛道的挑战性极高，因此倍耐力为确保安全裕度足够大，一个可选方案就是将最低胎压大幅提高。

    然而，这并非车手和车队所乐见的——今年已经出现抱怨，认为胎压总体上最终被设得过高。

    因此，尽管今年光头胎的结构更为坚固，且下压力水平总体更低，倍耐力仍选择再次对轮胎实施最长使用圈数限制。

    该限制被设定为每套轮胎最多使用27圈——比去年的规定多出两圈。

    威廉姆斯车队现场工程师保罗·威廉姆斯表示，赛道特性和轮胎限制让车队面临的情况远非简单直接。

    关于排位赛，他说：“软胎并不是一个明确的首次计时圈轮胎。右前轮难以升温，而左前轮和左后轮在整圈中会过热，因此我们预计出场圈需要艰难地暖胎，并需要两圈冷却圈来重置后轮温度。”

    然后，关于正赛本身，他解释道：“左前轮将成为限制因素，先是过热，然后是磨损，而后轮磨损相对较低。软胎存在左前轮颗粒化风险，而中性胎预计仍会出现高磨损。”

    很可能中性胎和硬胎将成为首选的正赛轮胎，最少两次进站的比赛意味着主要策略挑战是确保最后一次进站不会太早，以免造成代价高昂的额外换胎。

    迈凯伦赛车工程副总监阿德里安·古德温表示：“固定的 赛段长度实际上让轮胎管理变得更简单，因为我们不需要在轮胎寿命结束后继续管理它们。

    “主要的策略要点是不要过早进站，以免被迫采用三次进站的比赛策略。”

    英文原文

    Formula 1 drivers are to face the highest minimum front tyre pressures of the 2026 season - and the highest ever stipulated at the start of a race weekend - at the Qatar Grand Prix.

    The unique characteristics of the Lusail circuit, which features sequences of high-speed corners and aggressive pyramid-style kerbs, makes it one of the most challenging for tyres.

    It is one of the highest lateral-energy circuits of the season, with the bias of fast right handers meaning the front left is punished particularly hard.

    Pirelli has historically used three techniques to minimise the risk of tyre failures in Qatar: bringing its hardest compounds, increasing the minimum pressures, and imposing maximum stint lengths so each set is not taken right to the end of its life.

    This year, all three responses have been taken again, with pressures nudged up compared to last year.

    In the official FIA notes sent to teams ahead of the Qatar weekend, the front minimum tyre pressure is set at 29psi, while the rears have been set at 27.5psi.

    For the fronts this is the highest of the year, with the previous benchmark having been set at 28psi – which was laid down at Suzuka, Silverstone and Spa.

    For the rears, the Qatar number is four psi higher than the pre-weekend stipulation last year but not a peak for 2026. Spa and Las Vegas were both at 28.0 psi.

    At the 2025 Qatar GP, Pirelli headed into the weekend with a minimum front pressure of 28psi but then lifted it to 29psi when it discovered cuts in the rubber after Friday running – which it believed had been caused by cars running over gravel that had been pulled onto the track.

    The possibility of a similar in-weekend response being needed this time around cannot be ruled out, especially because the behaviour of the 2026 cars with active aero will only become clear once track action has got underway.

    One of the reasons minimum tyre pressures have gone up this season is because Pirelli has had to counter the risks of straight mode failures – which would have the consequence of putting huge forces through tyres on the straights.

    This has made a notable difference with the minimum pressures at the rear being lifted and it is no different in Qatar with last year's baseline of 23.5psi (lifted to 24.5psi after Friday) now being set at 27.5psi.

    The challenge of Qatar is so high that one option for Pirelli to be satisfied that safety margins are big enough would be to lift the minimum pressures much higher.

    However, this is not something drivers and teams are keen on – with complaints having already surfaced this year about how high pressures have generally ended up.

    So that is why, despite a more robust construction of slick tyre this year, plus downforce levels generally being less, Pirelli has elected to impose a maximum stint length for tyres once again.

    This has been set at 27 laps for a set of tyres – which is two more than was put in place last year.

    The nature of the track and the tyre limits make things far from straightforward for teams, according to Williams chief trackside engineer Paul Williams.

    For qualifying he said: "The soft is not a clear first-timed-lap tyre. The front-right struggles to warm up while the front-left and rear-left overheat through the lap, so we expect a hard out-lap for warm-up and a double cool-down lap to reset the rear temperatures."

    Then, for the race itself, he explained: "The front-left will be the limit, first through overheating and then through wear, while rear wear is relatively low. The soft carries a front-left graining risk and the medium is still expected to see high wear."

    The likelihood is that the medium and hard will be the preferred race tyres, with the minimum two-stop race meaning the main strategy challenge is making sure that the final stops do not come too early that a costly extra change is created.

    McLaren's associate director of car race engineering Adrian Goodwin said: "The fixed stint lengths actually make it a simpler event for tyres, as we're not trying to manage them beyond end of life.

    "The main strategic point is not stopping so early that you're forced into a three-stop race."
