    凯迪拉克F1车队仍面临挑战，佩雷斯感到沮丧

    佩雷斯对凯迪拉克F1车队在拉斯维加斯的最后一场分站赛事中表现出的运营问题感到沮丧。

    Sergio Perez在拉斯维加斯赛季最后一场主场比赛周末的开始时，心情想要给他的凯迪拉克一级方程式车队一些直言不讳。

    凯迪拉克在F1的第一个赛季结束时仍然排在车队积分榜的末尾，这并不令人惊讶，甚至也不是真正的失望，考虑到建立一个新车队所面临的挑战规模。

    让Perez感到沮丧的是运营方面，他原本希望这部分能够“更加敏锐”一些，但最近却显得“薄弱”。

    这显然让他很在意，因为在拉斯维加斯的周三晚上，他避开了一个关于瓦尔特里·博塔斯是否继续担任他队友的无关紧要的问题，而是提出了自己的观点。

    “我一直说，我认为现在团队有比车手更重要的事情要关注，”佩雷斯说。

    “所以在这方面我很高兴[博塔斯留下来了]。现在我们可以把这种干扰从我们身边移除，并确保我们能够在维修区做出好的停站，做出好的事情，因为最近几场比赛，在运营方面一直很弱。”

    当《赛车》杂志问及这是否只是新车队第一个赛季的现实时，佩雷斯回答道：“我认为目前我们确实期待在运营方面更加敏锐。”

    他补充说：“最近的几个周末对我们来说都很困难。我们在圣保罗有好的机会，在墨西哥也有好的机会，所以我们需要确保在这最后三场比赛中，如果这些机会出现，我们能够非常非常强大。”

    在一些方面，凯迪拉克最近的实际运营表现实际上有所下降。过去两场三连赛的平均维修区停车时间为4.82秒，这是本赛季在该方面的最差表现——尽管这个时期也包括了今年最佳的单次停车，巴顿在奥斯汀的2.65秒轮胎更换。

    但这只是它在过去19次中唯一的低于三秒的维修区停车，其中六次停车时间超过五秒。佩雷斯在巴西的第一次维修区停车时间为9.68秒，这使得他远离了包括奥迪、哈斯、阿斯顿·马丁和威廉姆斯在内的车队，尽管他最终还是在这场比赛中击败了卡洛斯·塞恩斯驾驶的威廉姆斯和拉斐尔·卡马拉驾驶的哈斯。

    巴顿由于在赛前发现了一个交叉螺纹的螺栓，而这个螺栓无法及时修复，因此他没有参加巴西大奖赛，而且他在墨西哥一周前因高速悬挂故障而戏剧性地撞车。

    佩雷斯在拉斯维加斯提出的抱怨不仅仅是关于运营方面。

    他一整年都在公开推动凯迪拉克改进，敦促其人员不要仅仅满足于站在F1的起跑线上，而要专注于向前发展。

    当《赛车》杂志问他是否觉得车队正在满足他的紧迫感时，他给出了一个坚定的回答。

    佩雷斯说：“对我来说，从第一天起就很清楚，我不是为了仅仅在这里参加F1而回来。”

    作为一个新车队，我们感觉很多人只是很高兴能在这里，享受乐趣。

    “我不希望看到这种情况在车队中发生。我想确保我们是一个在这里、在工厂、在赛道、在运营上全力以赴的团队。因为这是我们前进的唯一方式。”

    “竞争非常激烈。我认为这个团队里没有空间给那些只是满足于在这里的人。他们需要离开，因为这是一个我们将全力以赴推动未来几年的团队，以确保我们能够进步。”

    “明年对凯迪拉克来说是一个关键年份，以确保我们能够展示我们的进步，否则我们都知道这个行业的运作方式，压力可以迅速升级。”

    英文原文

    Sergio Perez was in a mood to offer his Cadillac Formula 1 team some home truths at the start of its last home grand prix weekend of the season in Las Vegas.

    Cadillac still propping up the grid at the end of its first year in F1 is no surprise, or even really a disappointment, given the scale of the challenge involved in building up a new team.

    What is frustrating Perez is the operations side, which he hoped would be “a lot sharper” by now and has been “weak” recently.

    It’s clearly on his mind, because on Wednesday night in Las Vegas he pivoted away from an innocuous opening question about Valtteri Bottas staying on as his team-mate to make the point.

    “I've always said, I think the team has much more important things to focus on than drivers for now,” said Perez.

    “So I'm happy in that regard [that Bottas is staying]. Now we can take that distraction out of our way and make sure that we operationally can make good stops, can make good things, because the last few races, operationally, have been weak.”

    Asked by The Race how much that was just a reality of a new team’s first season, Perez replied: “I think right now we did expect it to be a lot sharper on the operational side.”

    He added: “The last few weekends have proved to be difficult for us. We had good opportunities in Sao Paulo, we had good opportunities in Mexico, so we need to make sure that in these last three races, if they [the opportunities] happen, that we can be very, very strong.”

    In some areas, Cadillac’s operational performance has actually declined recently. Its average pitstop time of 4.82 seconds across the last two triple-headers is its worst stretch of the season on that front - though that period also includes its best single stop of the year, a 2.65s tyre change for Bottas at Austin.

    But that was its only sub-three-second pitstop in its last 19, and six of those were five seconds or more. Perez’s 9.68s first pitstop in Brazil dropped him away from racing with a pack that included an Audi, Haas, Aston Martin and Williams - though he did still eventually beat Carlos Sainz’s Williams and Rafael Camara’s Haas in that race.

    Bottas didn’t start the Brazilian GP at all due to a cross-threaded wheelnut discovered pre-race and that couldn’t be fixed in time, and he’d dramatically crashed out in Mexico a week earlier with a high-speed suspension failure.

    The operations side wasn’t the only complaint Perez wanted to raise in Las Vegas.

    He has openly pushed Cadillac to improve all year, urging its personnel not to simply be content with being on the F1 grid and to focus on moving forward.

    Asked by The Race if he felt the team was matching his urgency, he gave an uncompromising answer.

    “For me, it was very clear since day one that I wasn't coming back to Formula 1 just for the sake of being here in F1,” said Perez.

    “Us being a new team felt like a lot of people were really happy just to be here and have a good time.

    “And it's not something I want to see in the team. I want to make sure that we are a team that is pushing 100% in here, in the factory, at track, operationally. Because it's the only way we will be able to move forward.

    “The competition is super-high. And I think there is no room for anyone in this team that is just happy to be here. They need to be moving out because this is a team that we're going to be pushing super-hard for the coming years to make sure we are able to progress.

    “Next year is a crucial year for Cadillac to make sure that we are able to show our progress because otherwise we all know how this business works and the pressure can ramp up extremely fast.”
