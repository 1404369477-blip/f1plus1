    拉斯维加斯大奖赛疯狂对决：20多次领先变化，我们的评价

    一场疯狂的拉斯维加斯大奖赛，马克斯·维斯塔潘的惊人胜利和2026年F1规则的干预，为比赛增添了大量戏剧性。

    我们的团队给出了他们对这场比赛的看法，这场比赛将因其正确和错误的原因而成为一场惊心动魄的比赛。

    那场胜利在维斯塔潘的伟大胜利列表中非常高，而且他的胜利有很多。

    我没有在现代F1比赛中看到过这样的比赛。它和今年早些时候比赛中的一些 yo-yo 赛车有相似之处，但持续时间更长，因为它不仅仅是因为引擎和能量使用。

    我从未在现代看到过如此精彩的F1比赛。它和今年早些时候比赛中早期部分的特点——悠悠球式赛车——有一些相似之处，但之所以能够持续，是因为这不仅仅是因为引擎和能量消耗。

    这是一场由不同的部署策略和紧张、激烈的赛车所增强的尾流盛宴。这也是为什么那么多其他赛车手能够坚持这么久——皮埃尔·加斯利仅以2.9秒之差完成了比赛！

    在那种情况下，维斯塔潘能够如此控制，是对他作为F1最佳车手的赛车技艺的证明。他有时非常坚决和激进，但他也能非常聪明、果断和有分寸。可能比人们给予他的评价还要多。

    这是一次精英级的表演，只有维斯塔潘才能如此自信地完成。

    维斯塔潘反复表示他不会参加印第安纳波利斯500英里赛，但他的拉斯维加斯赛道上的出色驾驶几乎就是次佳选择。

    他完美地掌控了比赛，车内的小失误是他当天唯一的失误。

    维斯塔潘在比赛中观察到梅赛德斯车队改变部署策略的事实，展示了他在驾驶座上惊人的额外潜力，同时还在高速象棋游戏中击败了乔治·拉塞尔和基米·安东内利。

    也许梅赛德斯车队明年会再次改变规则，但如果2027年的赛车性能与现在相似，那么大胆地赌维斯塔潘无法获得第五个冠军可就太冒险了。

    这是一场绝对悲剧性的比赛，让我对2026年的规则产生了悲伤。但如果你超越那些无休止的车队混乱，你就能看到为什么没有任何规则集会永远摧毁F1，为什么粉丝会喜欢它。

    因为只要还有像维斯塔潘这样优秀的车手，锦标赛就处于健康的状态。

    与许多人不同，他在弯道和他人甚至没有想到要超车的位置完成了大部分超车，比如2号弯或5号弯的外侧。他甚至计算了梅赛德斯的车队部署策略，并在比赛中试图从车内对抗它，试图在最后一圈从内部否定它。

    整个比赛中，他与后面的车手有半秒的差距。他被反复超越。他反复摧毁了所有试图领先的人。我越看维斯塔潘，就越觉得他是F1史上最好的车手，这是一个显然非常微妙的争论，但我会感到自在地与任何人争论这一点。

    他的天赋已经在这套糟糕的规则中爆发。在这场看起来像是被一个小孩操控着Hot Wheels玩具赛车进行的比赛中，它的性质如此任意，但维斯塔潘在那里，证明这一切根本不是任意的。我想你可以说他就是那个拥有Hot Wheels的小孩：他确实像那样尽情地享受着无拘无束的乐趣。

    对我来说，他是F1的最佳车手，并且以这样的表现证明了这一点。车队负责人劳伦特·梅基斯说：“只有你。”

    在赛季初， 指赛车在维修区进进出出从未吸引过我，但拉斯维加斯大奖赛的疯狂却有些不同，相当有趣。

    这不仅仅是因为能量偏移导致的虚假的混乱。相反，可能是大尾流效应、轮胎行为和不同的能量部署模式组合，为了一场在F1现代历史上前所未有的比赛打开了大门。

    我不想每场比赛都像这样，但看到F1车手们保持这种椭圆形的超越和反超越，并将其转变为策略而非随机，这带来了一些引人入胜的东西。

    这最终证明这不仅仅是一场彩票式的比赛，因为这将作为维斯塔潘的经典胜利之一而载入史册。精彩的轮对轮比赛，一些巧妙的思考——以及在混乱中保持冷静——是值得赞扬的。

    英文原文

    A wild Las Vegas Grand Prix offered plenty for any fan to get their teeth into.

    A stunning Max Verstappen win and the most obvious case of Formula 1's bizarre 2026 rules intervening in the racing meant lots of drama.

    Our team give their verdicts on what stood out to them in this race, which will go down as a thriller for the right - and wrong - reasons.

    That win goes very, very high in the list of great Verstappen victories - and there have been so many.

    I have not seen an F1 race like that in modern times. It shared some lineage with the yo-yo racing that characterised early parts of races earlier this year but was sustained because it wasn't entirely a function of the engines and energy usage.

    This was a slipstream-fest augmented by different deployment strategies and tense, close racing. It's why so many other cars hung on for so long too - Pierre Gasly finished 2.9s from the win!

    For Verstappen to be so controlled in those circumstances is a testament to the racecraft that is part of what makes him F1's best driver. He's uncompromising and very aggressive at times, but he can also be incredibly smart, incisive and measured. Probably more so than some give him credit for.

    This was an elite performance and one that only Verstappen could pull off with such assurance.

    Verstappen has repeatedly said he won't race at the Indianapolis 500, but it felt like we got the next-best thing with his masterful Las Vegas drive.

    He controlled things beautifully, with his stumble in the car to the podium being his only real wobble of the day.

    The fact that Verstappen clocked Mercedes changing its deployment strategy mid-race showed the impressive extra capacity he had behind the wheel, all while besting George Russell and Kimi Antonelli in a game of high-speed chess.

    Maybe Mercedes turns up next year and moves the goalposts again but, if the cars are similar in performance in 2027 to now, you'd be brave to bet against Verstappen securing title number five.

    This was an absolutely tragic display and one that makes me sad at what these 2026 rules turned out to be. But if you look beyond the yo-yoing peloton nonsense, you can see exactly why no ruleset will ever ruin F1 and why fans love it.

    Because as long as there are drivers as good as Verstappen, the championship is in a healthy place.

    Unlike many others, he did most of his overtaking in the corners and in places others wouldn't have even have thought to pass in, such as the outside of Turn 2 or Turn 5. He even clocked Mercedes' deployment strategy and was trying to combat a way to negate it on the last lap from inside his own car, mid-race.

    He had a half-second gap to the driver behind for the whole race. He was repeatedly passed. He repeatedly destroyed everyone who attempted to stay ahead. The more I watch Verstappen, the more I think he's F1's best-ever driver, a debate which is obviously extremely nuanced, but I'd feel comfortable in my arguments with anyone on that.

    His talent has blasted through this woeful ruleset. In a race that looked like it was being played out with a toddler controlling Hot Wheels, so arbitrary in its nature, Verstappen was there, proving that it wasn't arbitrary at all. I suppose you could say, he is the kid with the Hot Wheels: he certainly had as much unconstrained fun as one.

    For me, he is F1's best driver and showed as such. "Only you," said team principal Laurent Mekies.

    Yo-yo racing never floated my boat earlier in the season, but there was something a bit different about the madness of the Las Vegas GP that was quite enjoyable.

    This wasn't just about some fake shuffling caused by energy offsets. Instead, a likely combination of the big slipstream effect, tyre behaviour and different energy-deployment patterns opened the door for a race that has been like nothing else seen in F1 in modern times.

    I wouldn't want every race to be like this one, but seeing F1 drivers sustain this oval-style of passing and repassing, and then make it tactical rather than random, delivered something that was intriguing.

    And the ultimate proof of it being something more than just a lottery is that this will go down as one of Verstappen's classic wins. Great wheel-to-wheel racing, some clever thinking - and keeping a cool head among the chaos - was something to applaud.
