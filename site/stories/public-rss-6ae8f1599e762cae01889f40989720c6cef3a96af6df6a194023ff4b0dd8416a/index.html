    倍耐力评估缩短卡塔尔站轮胎使用上限

    周五练习显示轮胎磨损与性能衰退高于预期，并出现由砂石造成的深度割伤，倍耐力正评估是否将每套轮胎27圈的使用上限下调。

    15圈就出现100%磨损——F1在卡塔尔面临的轮胎挑战远超倍耐力预期

    周五练习显示轮胎磨损与性能衰退高于预期，并出现由砂石造成的深度割伤，倍耐力正评估是否下调每套轮胎27圈的使用上限

    每年，卢赛尔国际赛道都是F1赛历中对唯一轮胎供应商倍耐力挑战最大的赛道。正因如此，本赛季再次设置了每套轮胎的最大使用圈数上限，此次为27圈。

    这一上限基于倍耐力的赛前模拟，但实际情况比预期更为极端，倍耐力首席工程师西蒙内·贝拉如此解释。

    “关键在于，颗粒化程度和磨损程度都高于我们在赛前预期。每次来到这里，这条赛道带给我们的惊喜都相当令人印象深刻，”贝拉微笑着说。

    “我们注意到的是，在高油量运行期间，C3、C2和C1三种配方都出现了颗粒化。C1在机械耐受性方面稍强一些，颗粒化程度略有降低，而C2和C3的颗粒化程度为中度到重度。

    “这加速了磨损，主要集中在两个区域：TWI 2和3区域，也就是基本朝向轮胎内侧胎肩的位置，然后是轮胎最外侧胎肩。我们有两个高磨损点，并且在高油量运行15圈后已经看到了100%磨损的情况。”

    这些极端局部磨损的情况远早于规定的27圈上限出现，这意味着倍耐力正在评估是否需要进一步降低该上限。然而，答案未必是肯定的，因为除了磨损之外，轮胎性能衰退也证明高于预期。

    “我们正在评估从第一节和第二节练习赛中选出的所有轮胎组。我们会检查它们，逐段分析，显然如果没有完整性问题，我们将保留27圈的限额。我们知道正赛中的颗粒化程度会降低，车队会做更多的管理来延长 连续驾驶圈数长度，”贝拉解释道。

    “所以，如果它们没有引发任何完整性问题的增加，那么我们可以保留27圈。但这是我们实验室正在进行的一项分析，所以我们要弄清楚是继续用27圈还是不用。”

    更高的退化程度可能会让现有的限制对倍耐力来说不那么令人担忧，因为贝拉预计车队不会一直推到每组27圈的允许上限。由于高退化导致车手每圈损失相当多的时间，提前进站可能更有利。

    “你说的没错，退化程度确实高于我们在赛前预期的水平。我认为这将迫使车队更早进站，”贝拉在被 Motorsport.com 问及退化因素时说道。

    “从我们最近做的分析来看，目前并不足以支撑跑27圈的长距离 stint。因为如果延长这么久，性能会明显下降，每圈大约掉0.2秒，这会迫使你换上新的轮胎并提前进站。所以基本上你可以把比赛分成三个更均衡的 stint，而不是27圈、27圈和3圈。”

    当然，如果像去年那样早早出现安全车，后者仍然是一种风险。但即便在这种情况下，贝拉也不认为会出现27圈的 stint，而更可能是三次进站策略。

    “是的，没错。根据我们的模拟，最优策略将是19圈、19圈、19圈。安全车甚至可能最终迫使他们转向三次进站。一次是为了安全车，之后还有两次，而不是只有一次。”

    除了极高的磨损和最大 stint 长度之外，围场里还有一个与轮胎相关的话题：极高的胎压。前轮的最低胎压被设定为29psi，是整个赛季最高的。

    尽管如此，这一点也仍在评估之中，因为赛道上有大量砾石，确实在轮胎上留下了痕迹。

    “我们注意到轮胎上有砾石造成的割伤。我们看到多名车手冲出赛道，把大量砾石带到了赛车线上。我们看到其中一些割伤相当深，已经深入轮胎结构，”贝拉透露。

    “也因此出现了红旗，而在这样的赛道上，这是一个额外的变量，可能在爆胎等方面引起我们的关注。”

    实际操作上，这意味着倍耐力也在评估是否需要进一步调高胎压，若调高将创下现代F1时代的最高纪录。

    “我们正在评估所有变量，如有必要，我们也会增加胎压。”

    然而，倍耐力更倾向于不增加胎压，因为这样做可能会加剧颗粒化和局部磨损。

    “我们现在的胎压接近30psi，所以基本上就是局部化了。你减小了轮胎的接地面积，基本上就是把磨损集中在一个点上，”贝拉解释道。

    这些点目前是TWI 2、TWI 3和TWI 6。TWI代表胎面磨损指示器，其中2、3和6位于轮胎胎肩附近。进一步增加胎压会减小接地面积，可能将磨损集中在更小的区域，并使其向轮胎中心偏移。

    “这就是为什么如果可能的话，我不会增加压力。但如果出于诚信原因我们必须这样做，那我们显然会进行评估。如果你继续增加压力，你会把颗粒化更多地推向内侧，更靠近中心。而TWI 6由于高载荷本来就承受着压力，所以基本上你有高横向载荷，并且你让轮胎的这个部分承受了很大压力。”

    正如贝拉总结的那样：“一个接一个的惊喜。”

    100% wear in 15 laps – F1’s tyre challenges in Qatar far more extreme than Pirelli expected

    Pirelli is evaluating whether to reduce its 27-lap tyre limit after Friday practice revealed higher-than-expected wear and degradation, as well as deep cuts caused by gravel

    Every year, the Lusail International Circuit represents the biggest challenge on the Formula 1 calendar for its sole tyre supplier, Pirelli. It is precisely for that reason that a maximum stint length is once again in place this season, with the limit this time set at 27 laps per set of tyres.

    The latter was based on Pirelli's pre-event simulations, but in practice, the situation is proving even more extreme than expected, as Pirelli's chief engineer Simone Berra explained.

    “The point is that the graining level and the wear level are higher than what we expected pre-event. It's quite impressive, the surprises that this circuit gives us every time we come here,” Berra smiled.

    “What we have noticed is that during the high fuel running, the graining is on all three compounds, C3, C2 and C1. C1 is slightly more resilient in terms of mechanical resistance and the graining level is slightly reduced, while on C2 and C3 the graining level is moderate to heavy.

    “That is accelerating the wear, mainly in two regions: the TWI 2 and 3 area, so basically towards the inside shoulder of the tyre, and then on the very outside shoulder of the tyre as well. We have two points of high wear and we have seen cases of 100% wear already during the high fuel running after 15 laps.”

    Those cases of extreme localised wear occurred well before the prescribed 27-lap limit, meaning Pirelli is evaluating whether that limit needs to be reduced further. However, the answer is not necessarily yes, given that, in addition to the wear, tyre degradation is also proving higher than expected.

    “We are evaluating all the sets that we have selected from FP1 and FP2. We are going to check them, to section them, and obviously if there is no integrity issue, we will keep the 27 laps. We know that the graining level will reduce for the race and the teams will do much more management to extend the stint length,” Berra explained.

    “So, if they don't induce any increase in terms of integrity issues, then we can keep the 27 laps. But it's an analysis that is ongoing in our laboratory, so we are going to understand if we continue with 27 or not.”

    Higher degradation could make the existing limit less of a concern for Pirelli, as Berra does not expect teams to push right up to the permitted limit of 27 laps per set. With drivers losing a considerable amount of time per lap due to the high degradation, it could be more beneficial to pit earlier.

    “It's true what you said, so the degradation is higher than what we expected pre-event. And I think this will force the teams to stop earlier,” Berra said when asked about the degradation factor by Motorsport.com.

    “I don't think it's really powerful at the moment, looking at the last analysis we have done, to do a 27-lap stint. Because if you extend for so long, there will be a clear drop in performance of 0.2 seconds per lap, and that forces you to take a new tyre and stop earlier. So basically you can divide the race into three more balanced stints instead of having 27, 27 and 3 laps.”

    The latter is, of course, still a risk if there is an early safety car, as was the case last year. But even in that scenario, Berra does not expect to see 27-lap stints, but rather a three-stop strategy.

    “Yes, correct. From our simulation, the optimum strategy will be 19, 19, 19. A safety car can even force them towards three stops in the end. One for the safety car and two later, instead of just one.”

    In addition to the extremely high wear and the maximum stint length, there is another tyre-related talking point in the paddock: the extremely high tyre pressures. For the front tyres, the minimum pressure has been set at 29psi, the highest of the entire season.

    Nevertheless, this is also still being evaluated, as the large amount of gravel on the track has quite literally left its mark on the tyres.

    “We have noticed gravel cuts in the tyres. We've seen multiple drivers going off the track and bringing a lot of gravel onto the race line. We've seen some of those cuts quite deep into the construction,” Berra revealed.

    “There was also a red flag due to this and it's something that, on a circuit like this, it's an additional variable that can create some attention from our side in terms of punctures, etc.”

    In practical terms, this means Pirelli is also assessing whether the tyre pressures need to be increased even further, which would take them to a record high in the modern F1 era.

    “We are evaluating all the variables and, if necessary, we will increase the pressure as well.”

    However, Pirelli would prefer not to increase the tyre pressures, as doing so could exacerbate the graining and localised wear.

    “We are almost running 30psi, so you basically localise it. You reduce the contact patch of the tyre and you localise the wear on a single point, basically,” Berra explained.

    Those points are currently TWI 2, TWI 3 and TWI 6. TWI stands for tread wear indicator, with 2, 3 and 6 located towards the shoulders of the tyre. Increasing tyre pressure further would reduce the contact patch, potentially concentrating wear over a smaller area and shifting it towards the centre of the tyre.

    “That's why I wouldn't increase the pressure, if possible. But if we have to do it for integrity reasons, then we’re obviously going to evaluate it. If you continue to increase the pressure, you move the graining more inboard, more towards the centre. And TWI 6 is under pressure anyway due to the high loads anyway, so basically you have high lateral loads and you stress this part of the tyre a lot.”

    As Berra summed it up: “Surprise after surprise after surprise.”
