    诺里斯：F1需要更好的雨胎，拉斯维加斯雨战会“很吓人”

    世界冠军兰多·诺里斯希望倍耐力在2027年推出大幅改进的雨胎，他认为现有雨胎在拉斯维加斯这类低下压力赛道上无法正常工作。

    兰多·诺里斯：F1需要更好的雨胎，拉斯维加斯雨战会“很吓人”

    领先的F1车手们希望倍耐力能对其湿地轮胎系列做出重大改进

    世界冠军兰多·诺里斯希望F1轮胎供应商倍耐力在2027年推出大幅改进的雨胎，因为他预计现有雨胎在拉斯维加斯这类低下压力赛道上无法正常工作。

    在马来西亚和新加坡经历部分受雨水影响的比赛周末后，F1的2026款赛车在巴西大奖赛首次经历真正意义上的湿地排位赛和正赛，倍耐力的全雨胎和半雨胎配方都在圣保罗接受了测试。排位赛因多个弯道积水而中断超过一小时，打滑（aquaplaning）风险促使车手在Q1使用全雨胎，随后换上半雨胎。

    这场比赛在湿地胎下开始并结束，中间有一段干燥期，消除了此前对2026代赛车在湿滑条件下表现的担忧。但尽管巴西站呈现了一场精彩的表演，倍耐力的雨胎在正确的工作窗口内基本按预期工作，车手们仍然认为今年的硬质湿地胎需要在低温性能上改进，因为他们在 赛段开始时和前往发车格的圈中难以将赛车保持在赛道上。

    由于英特拉格斯是赛历上最容易让轮胎进入工作范围的赛道之一，几位车手担心倍耐力的产品在并非如此的赛道上不够稳健，比如即将到来的拉斯维加斯街道赛道。

    国际汽联的官方天气预报尚未公布，但其他预测显示周四晚间第一节自由练习的降雨概率超过50%，之后条件更可能干燥。

    由于拉斯维加斯周末气温远低于10摄氏度，且赛道缺乏任何高速弯道来给轮胎施加横向负荷，迈凯伦车手诺里斯表示湿地练习会相当“可怕”，因为湿地胎在太冷时无法工作。

    当被 Motorsport.com 问及对本规则周期首个真正湿地周末的评判时，诺里斯说：“轮胎变冷时，情况相当糟糕。在前往发车格的圈里，当它们变得非常冷时，感觉糟透了。如果进入合适的工作窗口，它们就很好。所以，我认为可怕的时刻是如果你去拉斯维加斯却无法让轮胎升温。你需要比我们这里好得多的轮胎，因为那会非常糟糕。”

    巴西站亚军马克斯·维斯塔潘补充道：“我认为主要是工作范围有点太小了。热的时候还可以，但冷的时候，它们真的像石头一样。这是我们需要努力解决的问题，要在全雨胎（extreme wet）和半雨胎（intermediates）上生成更大的工作范围。我认为这会大大有助于更好地让轮胎进入工作状态，然后甚至能在更大的雨中行驶。”

    威廉姆斯车手卡洛斯·塞恩斯也认为倍耐力的整个轮胎系列都需要改进。“我认为雨胎和半雨胎的水平不够好，我们需要与倍耐力合作找到更好的轮胎，因为在混合条件下，半雨胎在后轮仍然是非常弱的轮胎；等它升温时，前轮已经不行了。

    “而全雨胎说实话，[在排位赛中]我们出现了大量水滑，后轮抓地力严重不足，所以这两款轮胎仍然缺少一些开发。”

    倍耐力面临一项艰巨任务：要为F1 2026年全新规则套件开发雨胎，这些规则包含高扭矩动力单元和主动空气动力学，会给轮胎施加巨大作用力。与此同时，倍耐力还在追逐一个不断变化的目标，即车队究竟能产生多少下压力，而赛季中激烈的研发进展已经超出了这家供应商的预期。

    在休赛期，国际汽联在湿地条件下实施了一种部分直道模式以缓解安全担忧，现在只有前翼会被激活。随后在4月，管理机构还降低了湿地条件下的电力输出。据信这两项措施对2026年赛车在湿地中相对安全地比赛的能力产生了很大影响，但它们显然是在倍耐力不得不确定其2026年轮胎系列之后才出台的。

    F1首次真正使用全雨胎和半雨胎进行测试来得如此之晚，以至于倍耐力无法将巴西站的教训纳入2027年的考量，因为这家意大利轮胎制造商已经敲定了下赛季的认证。

    但在今年早些时候的早期开发工作之后，倍耐力得以在上个月于马尼库尔进行关键的湿地测试，试验其在2026年全年研发的各种解决方案。在这条法国赛道上，梅赛德斯储备车手弗雷德里克·维斯蒂进行了为期两天的测试，其中一天在湿地条件下测试了多种采用不同配方和结构的新型半雨胎。

    倍耐力的赛道工程主管西蒙内·贝拉在墨西哥表示：“我们测试了一些方案，尤其是针对半雨胎的方案，最终得到了我们想在下个赛季引入的不错选择。可以肯定的是，我们的想法是改进明年的雨胎产品。”

    “我们在配方上做了工作，在胎面厚度上做了工作，也在不同结构上做了工作。所以，我们在轮胎的不同方面做了大量工作，希望把最佳方案结合起来。我们现在正在处理数据，接下来将对新的雨胎配方规格进行认证。”

    在圣保罗排位赛后，倍耐力赛车运动负责人达里奥·马拉富斯基坚称，公司已为2027年“取得了一些进展”。他说：“当然，我们非常认真地对待了一些车手的质疑，并在这一年里推进了研发。我们知道总有改进的空间，我们相信我们为明年向前迈出了一些步伐。”

    “获得这些信息（来自巴西）非常有价值。遗憾的是，它直到赛季末才出现。我们已经完成了明年的雨胎研发。我不想透露明年雨胎会发生什么，但我们是根据赛季初掌握的信息以及我们在铃鹿进行的一些测试来完成雨胎研发的，所以这对我们来说有助于确定今年的优先事项。”

    “我们在马尼库尔进行了一次积极的测试，很快我们就会公布这次湿地测试的结果。”

    Lando Norris: F1 needs better rain tyres as wet Las Vegas would be "scary"

    Leading Formula 1 drivers are hoping Pirelli can make significant improvements to its wet-weather range of tyres

    World champion Lando Norris hopes Formula 1 tyre supplier Pirelli will field much-improved rain tyres in 2027 as he expects the current range won't work properly at a low-downforce circuit like Las Vegas.

    Following partially rain-affected weekends in Malaysia and Singapore, F1's 2026 cars went through their first proper qualifying and grand prix held in wet conditions at the Brazilian Grand Prix, with both Pirelli's full wet and intermediate compounds being put to the test at Sao Paulo. Qualifying had to be halted for over an hour due to standing water in several corners, with the risk of aquaplaning prompting drivers to run full-wet tyres in Q1 before switching to intermediates.

    The race both started and finished on intermediates, with a dry middle spell, dispelling early fears over how the 2026 generation of cars would run in the wet. But while Brazil put on an entertaining spectacle and Pirelli's rain tyres largely worked as expected in the right operating window, drivers still felt this year's stiffer intermediates need work on how they perform in low temperatures as they struggled to keep their cars on the track at the start of a stint and on laps to the grid.

    With Interlagos being one of the easiest circuits on the calendar to get tyres into the working range, several drivers voiced concerns that Pirelli's offering won't be robust enough on circuits where that isn't the case, like the upcoming Las Vegas street circuit.

    The FIA's official weather forecast is not available yet, but other predictions suggest the chance of rain is over 50% for Free Practice 1 on Thursday evening, with conditions more likely to be dry after that.

    With temperatures dipping well below 10C over the Vegas weekend and the circuit lacking any high-speed corners to put lateral load into the tyres, McLaren driver Norris said a wet practice would be quite "scary" because the intermediates don't work when they're too cold.

    "When the tyres get cold, it's pretty nasty," Norris said when asked by Motorsport.com for his verdict on F1's first proper wet-weather weekend of this rules cycle. "On laps to the grid, when they get very cold, they felt horrible. If they get into a good window, they're good. So, I think the scary times are if you go to Vegas and you can't get any tyre temperature. You need a much better tyre than what we have here because that's going to be horrible."

    Brazil runner-up Max Verstappen added: "I think it's mainly that the working range is a bit too small. When they're hot, it's okay, but when they're cold, they're really like a rock. That's something that we need to try and work on, to generate a bigger working range on the extreme wet [tyres] and the intermediates. I think that would help a lot with switching on the tyre better and then being able to run even in heavier rain."

    Williams driver Carlos Sainz also felt Pirelli's entire range needed improvements. "I think the level of the wet and the inter is not good enough and we need to work with Pirelli on finding a better tyre because in the mixed conditions the inter is still a very weak tyre at the rear; by the time it warms up the front is gone.

    "And the extreme honestly, [in qualifying] we had so much aquaplaning and a big lack of rear grip, so still missing a bit of development on these two tyres."

    Pirelli faced a difficult task developing rain tyres for F1 2026's all-new regulations suite with their high-torque power units and active aerodynamics, putting significant forces through the rubber. It meanwhile chased a moving target of just how much downforce the teams would be able to generate, with fierce in-season development already ahead of the supplier's projections.

    Over the off-season the FIA implemented a partial Straight Mode in the wet to ease safety concerns, with only the front wing now being activated. Then, in April, the governing body also reduced electric output in wet conditions. Those two measures are believed to have made a big difference to the 2026 cars' ability to race in the wet relatively safely, but they obviously arrived well after Pirelli had to define its 2026 tyre range.

    F1's first proper running on both the extreme wet tyres and intermediates is coming so late in the season that Pirelli can't take Brazil's lessons into account for 2027, as the Italian tyre manufacturer has already finalised its homologations for next season.

    But following earlier development work at the start of the year, it was able to conduct crucial wet-weather testing in Magny-Cours last month, trialling the various solutions it worked on over the course of 2026. At the French circuit, Mercedes reserve driver Frederik Vesti conduced two days of testing, including one day in the wet to trial various new intermediate tyres featuring different compounds and constructions.

    "We tested some solutions, especially for the intermediates, and we ended up with good options that we would like to introduce for next season," Pirelli's trackside engineering chief Simone Berra said in Mexico. "For sure the idea is to improve the wet-weather product for next year.

    "We worked on the compounds, we worked on the tread thickness, we also worked on different structures. So, we worked a lot on different tyre aspects and we would like to combine the best solutions. We are working on the data now and we are going to homologate new wet-weather compound specifications."

    Speaking after qualifying in Sao Paulo, Pirelli's head of motorsport Dario Marrafuschi was adamant the company has "made some steps" for 2027. "Of course, we took quite seriously the scepticism from some drivers, and we addressed the development during the year. We know there is always room for improvement and we believe we made some steps ahead for next year," he said.

    "It is a great value having this information [from Brazil]. Unfortunately, it is coming only towards the end of the season. We have already closed the wet development for next year. I don't want to disclose what will happen on the wet next year, but we made the wet development based on the information we had at the beginning of the season and some of the testing we did in Suzuka, so that was good for us to address the priorities for this year.

    "We had a positive test in Magny-Cours, and quite [soon] we will be disclosing the outcome of this wet test."
