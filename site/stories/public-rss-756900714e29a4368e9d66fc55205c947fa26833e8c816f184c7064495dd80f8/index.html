    FIA 探索在F1 V8引擎中恢复DRS系统并推动通过系统

    尽管上周宣布的框架确立了F1下一款动力单元法规的关键要素——一款2.1升涡轮增压V8，其电气组件显著减少——但许多细节仍需在未来几个月内解决。其中一个关键问题是如何促进超车。

    国际汽联探索在F1 V8引擎中恢复DRS系统并推动通过系统

    独家对Motorsport.com发表谈话，国际汽联单座赛车总监Nikolas Tombazis透露了管理机构如何能在2030年结合空气动力学和电气超越辅助系统

    上周宣布的框架确立了下个一级方程式动力单元法规的关键要素——一个2.1升涡轮增压V8，其电气组件显著减少——但在接下来的几个月里，仍有许多细节需要完善

    其中关键未解决的问题是如何促进超越。对于2026年，国际汽联引入了超越模式，但这主要是由于这套规则设定极其复杂的结果。

    为了使当前的动力单元规定能够有效实施，汽车需要具有显著更低的阻力，这就是为什么引入了带有直线模式区域的主动空气动力学。由于每位车手都可以打开前后翼片，DRS作为超越辅助工具消失了，国际汽联不得不提出一个替代方案，结果就是超越模式。

    目标是使下一轮规则周期中的整个概念大大简化。国际汽联单座赛车总监尼科拉斯·托马齐斯在接受Motorsport.com独家采访时确认，直线模式（SM）区域将消失，并且管理机构正在研究更熟悉的概念。

    幕后，目前正在努力确定从2030年起在超越辅助工具方面需要什么。国际汽联正在调查几个选项，包括推杆加速和DRS的组合。

    “在比赛条件下，电能将肯定更多地集中在推杆加速上，而较少地集中在能量回收上。由于与当前引擎相比，电能部分在尺寸上会小得多，这意味着所有与能量使用相关的规定都将被消除或大幅简化，”托马齐斯告诉Motorsport.com。

    “所以是的，这将是超车的基本点，但我们仍在进行超车和近距离比赛的模拟。”

    “这些模拟旨在确定在推杆加速方面需要什么——是像IndyCar那样的每场比赛固定秒数，还是不同的系统——以及是否需要与DRS的回归相结合。”

    “如果我们需要添加DRS并与一些电气部件一起使用，我们将非常乐意这样做，”Tombazis说。”

    “只是想说明：在下一项规定下，我们不再需要SM模式区域，因此我们将保留拥有更多类似DRS的设备来补充电气部件的选项，以确保我们有正确的近距离比赛水平。”

    机械师正在对马克斯·维斯塔潘的小红牛18赛车DRS系统进行维修

    虽然正在进行模拟，但尚未确认推杆和DRS是否会真正结合，尽管托马齐斯确认这种结合现在已经在考虑之中。

    “可能会，是的。我没有说它将完全那样，但确实可能会有DRS和推杆两种功能。”

    根据最初的提案，电气组件将提供高达200kW的动力，其中100kW用于正常使用，另外100kW用于推杆加速。

    通过可能将DRS添加到其中，国际汽联希望找到正确的平衡：超车动作理想情况下应该再次在刹车区域完成，而不是在直道的一半处。

    "是的，我想说的是，DRS是解决这个问题的已知方案。这不像是我们需要发明的东西。我们只需要看看，A，我们是否需要它，B，我们需要多少。"

    "我们显然不希望出现一种情况，即汽车毫无竞争地飞驰而过，我们希望有比赛。我们希望汽车进入弯道时并排而行，并展开竞争，但我们有工具基本上以正确的方式调整它，以适应2030年。"

    FIA explores DRS return alongside push-to-pass system for F1’s V8 engines

    Speaking exclusively to Motorsport.com, FIA single-seater director Nikolas Tombazis reveals how the governing body could combine aerodynamic and electrical overtaking aids in 2030

    Although the framework announced last week establishes the key elements of Formula 1’s next power unit regulations – a 2.1-litre turbocharged V8 with a significantly reduced electrical component – many details still need to be worked out over the coming months.

    One of the key remaining questions is how to facilitate overtaking. For 2026, the FIA introduced overtake mode, but that is primarily a consequence of how incredibly complex this ruleset is.

    For the current power unit regulations to work, the cars needed to have considerably less drag, which is why active aerodynamics were introduced with straight mode zones. With every driver able to open their front and rear wings, DRS disappeared as an overtaking aid and the FIA had to come up with an alternative, which turned out to be overtake mode.

    The aim is to keep the whole concept considerably simpler for the next rules cycle. FIA single-seater director Nikolas Tombazis confirmed in an exclusive interview with Motorsport.com that straight mode (SM) zones will disappear and that the governing body is looking into more familiar concepts.

    Behind the scenes, work is currently underway to establish what will be needed in terms of overtaking aids from 2030 onwards. The FIA is investigating several options, including a combination of push-to-pass and DRS.

    "The electrical power in race conditions will definitely be more focused on push-to-pass and less on the energy recovery. Because the electrical part will be so much smaller in dimensions compared to the current engines, it means that all the regulations that relate to the energy usage will be either eliminated or hugely simplified," Tombazis told Motorsport.com.

    "So yeah, it will be a fundamental point for overtaking, but we are still doing simulations about overtaking and close racing."

    Those simulations are intended to establish exactly what is needed in terms of push-to-pass – a fixed number of seconds per race, as IndyCar has, or a different system – and also whether it needs to be combined with the return of DRS.

    "If we need to have an addition of DRS to go together with some electrical part, then we will be more than happy to do that," Tombazis said.

    "And just to say: we won't need to have SM mode zones anymore under the next regulations, and therefore we would retain the option to have a more DRS-like device to complement the electrical part to make sure that we have the right level of close racing."

    Mechanics work on the DRS of Max Verstappen, Red Bull RB18

    While simulations are ongoing, it has not yet been confirmed that push-to-pass and DRS will indeed be combined, although Tombazis did confirm that such a combination is now on the table.

    "It could be, yes. I didn't say that it will be exactly like that, but yeah, there could be both [DRS and push-to-pass]."

    Under the initial proposal, the electrical component would deliver up to 200kW, with 100kW available for normal use and the other 100kW for push-to-pass.

    By potentially adding DRS to that, the FIA wants to strike the right balance: overtaking moves should ideally once again be completed in the braking zone rather than halfway down a straight.

    "Yes, and what I'm saying is that DRS is a known solution for that. It's not like something we'd need to invent. We just need to see, A, if we need it, and B, how much of it we would need.

    "We clearly don't want to create a situation where cars just fly past each other without any fight, we want there to be racing. We want cars to be entering corners next to each other and fighting, but we have the tools to basically tune it in the right way for 2030."
