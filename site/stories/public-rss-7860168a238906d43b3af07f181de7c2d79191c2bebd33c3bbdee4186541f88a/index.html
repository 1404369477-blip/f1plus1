    法拉利拉斯维加斯排位赛双车无缘Q3

    法拉利在拉斯维加斯排位赛中两位车手均未能进入Q3，创下本赛季最差排位赛表现。勒克莱尔因7号弯失误中止Q2第二圈，最终排名第11（因皮亚斯特里罚退从第10位起步），汉密尔顿则受能量部署问题和Q1车损影响。

    法拉利原本预计在拉斯维加斯的长直道上会很艰难，但两位车手都未能进入Q3，这标志着其本赛季至今最差的排位赛表现。

    夏尔·勒克莱尔在7号弯出现失误后被迫中止Q2第二圈，就此断送机会。他最终排名第11快（因奥斯卡·皮亚斯特里受罚，他从第10位起步），紧随其后的刘易斯·汉密尔顿则受到能量部署问题和Q1中车损的影响。

    但法拉利的问题要深得多，因为极速不足叠加车手在排位赛中让轮胎升温的持续难题，共同决定了他们的命运。

    而且，正如勒克莱尔在排位赛后接受媒体采访时公开承认的那样，他在7号弯的失误源于对速度不足的日益不满以及与维修区持续存在的沟通问题。

    “我认为这是在一连串挫败之后发生的，我们在管理一切、交通和其他事情上犯了很多错误，这些错误不断累积，”他解释道。

    “这没有帮助，尤其是在这样一条一切都取决于轮胎的赛道上。你必须把轮胎带入正确的工作窗口。

    “我更多是在看后视镜，试图自己弄明白交通状况，这让人很难做任何事。”

    法拉利众所周知的动力单元弱点在拉斯维加斯尤其代价高昂。

    “我只是非常失望，”他说。“我们原本有所期待，但在直道上损失了一秒——这太疯狂了。这是意料之中的，而这让事情变得非常、非常困难。

    “在这样的赛道上，这是本赛季对动力最敏感的赛道，所以我们预料到会很艰难。但我原本期望能进入第三节排位赛（Q3）。”

    汉密尔顿表示，法拉利并非没有意识到拉斯维加斯是一个更具挑战性的周末，但他也认同结果比预期更糟。

    “我们知道长直道之类的东西会很有挑战性，但在直道上的差距似乎比我预期的要大一些，”他说。

    “这并非无关紧要。在直道上损失很大。然后我不知道，就抓地力而言，感觉我们并没有很强的抓地力。我们很难让轮胎进入工作状态，而且我们就是有点找不到方向。”

    然而，汉密尔顿觉得，尽管存在轮胎和极速问题，但考虑到损伤带来的空气动力学损失和能量管理问题，赛车仍有潜力进入Q3。

    “我们在Q1的第一圈或第二圈损失了一块底板。赛车平衡发生了变化，我们损失了百分之三到四秒（积分）的下压力，”他说。“在那之后，我们更多是在打滑中挣扎，基本上从一开始就是这样。

    “我最后那一圈真的很好，但我用现有的条件做到了最好。但后来我们在部署上损失了大约百分之六秒。我想如果没有那个损失，我们本可以进入Q3。但除此之外，我对我的圈速很满意。”

    还有更多执行层面的问题吗？

    另一个受到关注的方面是车队如何执行排位赛，因为法拉利在赛道位置上陷入挣扎，并且没有选择像许多其他车队那样玩尾流游戏。

    勒克莱尔表示，法拉利没有走那条路线，是因为考虑到今年在轮胎管理上挣扎的程度，它不认为一个好的尾流能带来接近让轮胎处于完美工作窗口内的好处。

    “我的意思是，那并没有被讨论过，”他说。“我们认为轮胎就是一切。保持轮胎温度对我们来说是最重要的。”

    “我们知道这是我们的弱点。我们必须推进。即使在冷却圈，我们也必须推进以保持轮胎高温。

    “我不知道其他人是否面临和我们一样的问题，但当你遇到那个问题时，就很难等两辆车，并把握好时机，因为那需要你开得比你能开的更慢。对我们来说，保持那些轮胎的温度非常非常棘手。”

    交通管理方面也再次出现了沟通问题，但勒克莱尔想更好地了解车队在练习中遇到的GPS问题是否在排位赛中造成了困难。

    “我不知道今天的原因是什么，”他说。“我知道今天早上我们在GPS上有很多问题。如果今天那是问题，那就可以理解。”

    “否则，我们只需要确保在那些关键时刻把一切做对。”

    他补充道：“如果是GPS问题，那不一定来自我们，那就是我们无能为力的事情，今天很痛苦。”

    “但如果问题来自我们，我确实同意我们需要审视它并显然加以改进，因为特别是在这样的关键时刻，我们需要在场，而我们没有做到。”

    英文原文

    Ferrari braced for a tough time on the long straights of Las Vegas, but neither driver making it into Q3 marked its worst qualifying showing of the Formula 1 season so far.

    Charles Leclerc had wrecked his chances when he was forced to abort his second Q2 run after a mistake at Turn 7. He ended up 11th fastest (he starts 10th thanks to Oscar Piastri's penalty), while right behind him Lewis Hamilton was not helped by deployment problems and some car damage that he picked up in Q1.

    But Ferrari’s problems ran much deeper because a combination of its lack of top speed allied to the ongoing issues its drivers have faced in firing tyres up for qualifying that consigned them to their fate.

    And, as Leclerc openly admitted when he spoke to the media after qualifying, the error he made in T7 was a consequence of growing annoyance at the lack of pace and ongoing communication problems with the pit wall.

    “I think that is following a lot of frustration, building up with a lot of mistakes with the way we managed everything, traffic and other things,” he explained.

    “That doesn't help, particularly on a track like this where it's all about tyres. You've got to put tyres in the right window.

    “I was more looking in my mirrors and trying to understand the traffic myself, that it makes it very difficult to do anything.”

    Ferrari's well-known power unit weakness was particularly costly in Vegas.

    “I'm just very disappointed,” he said. “We were expecting, and we lost one second down the straight - which is crazy. It's expected, and that makes it very, very difficult.

    “On a track like this, it's the most power-sensitive track of the season, so we expected to have a hard time. But I was expecting to go through Q3.”

    Hamilton said Ferrari wasn't blind to Las Vegas being a more challenging weekend, but he agreed the outcome was worse than anticipated.

    “We knew long straights and stuff was going to be challenging, but the gap seems to be a little bit more than I anticipated in the straightline,” he said.

    “It's not insignificant. It's a big chunk on the straights. And then I don't know, just grip-wise, it didn't feel like we had a tonne of grip. We were struggling to get the tyres working and we were just kind of nowhere."

    Hamilton felt, however, that even in spite of the tyre and top speed problems, the potential was still within the car to make it to Q3 – taking into account the aero deficit from damage and energy management issues.

    “We lost a piece of the floor on the first or second lap of Q1. The car balance shifted and we lost three or four tenths [points] of downforce,” he said. “After that we were struggling more so with sliding, basically from the get-go.

    “My lap was really good at the end, but I did the best I could with what I had. But then we lost something like six tenths just in deployment. I think without that we would have been in Q3. But otherwise, I was happy with my lap.”

    More execution problems?

    Another element that came under the spotlight was how the team executed qualifying, as Ferrari struggled for track position and didn't elect to play the slipstream game that many others were doing.

    Leclerc said that Ferrari had not gone down that route because it did not think a good tow would offer anywhere near the benefits of having tyres in the perfect operating window, considering how much it has struggled this year in managing its rubber.

    “I mean, that was not discussed,” he said. “We thought that tyres were just everything. Keeping just the temperature in the tyres was the most important for us.

    “We know it's our weakness. We've got to push. Even in the cool-down laps, we've got to push to keep the tyres high.

    “I don't know if the others are facing the same issues as we do, but when you have that problem, then it's very difficult to wait for two cars, and to time it well, because that requires you to go slower than what you can. It's very, very tricky for us to keep those tyres up.”

    There were also communication problems again with traffic management, but Leclerc wanted to understand better if GPS problems the team had suffered in practice had played a part in making things difficult in qualifying.

    “I don't know what was the reason for that today,” he said. “I know this morning we had a lot of problems on GPS. If that was a problem today, then it's understandable.

    “Otherwise, we just need to make sure that in those critical moments we get everything right.

    He added: “If it's a GPS issue, which doesn't necessarily come from us, then it's something that we cannot do much, and today was painful.

    "But if it comes from us, I do agree we need to look at it and obviously improve, because particularly in the critical moments like this, we need to be there, and we didn't.”
