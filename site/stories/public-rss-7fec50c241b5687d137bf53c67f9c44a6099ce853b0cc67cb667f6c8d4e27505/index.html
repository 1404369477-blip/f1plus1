    前红牛顾问赫尔穆特·马尔科投资了一家奥地利人工智能初创公司

    马克斯·维斯塔潘、红牛车队、赫尔穆特·马尔科、红牛车队

    赛道之外的赛车文化。车手生活方式、时尚、旅行、设计和那些让赛车不仅仅是一项运动的故事。

    前红牛赛车运动顾问赫尔穆特·马克奥成为了一家奥地利人工智能初创公司的投资者，该公司允许人们留下个人信息，在亲人去世后传达给他们。

    这位83岁的老人在去年赛季结束后，结束了他在米尔顿凯恩斯车队超过二十年的职业生涯，于10月20日在维也纳曼德勒东方酒店举办的启动仪式上被公布为Lebensstimmen GmbH的支持者。

    这家总部位于格拉茨的公司将在国际上以Words That Remain的名义运营，其平台被描述为一种数字化的临终规划。用户在生前录制或写下信息，然后在去世后传递给指定的接收者。

    该公司还提供了一种工具，帮助悲伤的家庭整理自己的话语，并推出了一款名为“Fabertar”的人工智能悼词撰写者，该软件与维也纳圣斯蒂芬大教堂的牧师托尼·法贝尔共同开发。Lebensstimmen表示，“Fabertar”明确标明为AI，并不旨在取代神父或演讲者，而是帮助那些无人可用的情况。

    马克奥，他建立了红牛的青训项目，并帮助四位冠军车手塞巴斯蒂安·维特尔和马克斯·维斯塔潘成长，将这次投资与他自己在赛车界的职业生涯联系起来。

    "赛车运动从未留给运气；它通过不断使用新技术来造福人们的勇气而得到改进。这正是我在Lebensstimmen中认识到的态度，马克奥在新闻稿中说。

    这家公司由Philipp Berkessy、Markus Pinter和Lukas Stern创立，并计划在2027年第一季度在奥地利和国际上全面推出其平台。

    "每个人都有应该保留的话语。通过Lebensstimmen，我们首次使临终规划变得个性化、数字化和合法化。今天，一种新的告别文化开始了，它始于奥地利，"Berkessy说。

    Helmut Marko invests in AI start-up after Red Bull F1 exit

    Former Red Bull adviser Helmut Marko has invested in an Austrian AI start-up

    Max Verstappen, Red Bull Racing, Helmut Marko, Red Bull Racing

    Motorsport culture beyond the track. Driver lifestyle, fashion, travel, design and the stories that make racing more than a sport.

    Former Red Bull motorsport adviser Helmut Marko has become an investor in an Austrian artificial intelligence start-up that lets people leave personal messages to be delivered to loved ones after they die.

    The 83-year-old, who stepped down from his role at the Milton Keynes outfit at the end of last season after more than two decades with the company, was unveiled as a backer of Lebensstimmen GmbH at a launch event at the Mandarin Oriental hotel in Vienna on 20 October.

    The Graz-based company, which will operate internationally under the name Words That Remain, describes its platform as a form of digital end-of-life planning. Users record or write messages during their lifetime, which are then passed on to chosen recipients after their death.

    The company also offers a tool to help grieving families put their own words together and has launched an AI eulogist called the "Fabertar", developed with Toni Faber, the cathedral pastor of St Stephen's in Vienna. Lebensstimmen says the "Fabertar" is clearly labelled as AI and is not designed to replace priests or speakers, but to help where none are available.

    Marko, who built Red Bull's junior programme and helped bring through four-time champions Sebastian Vettel and Max Verstappen, linked the investment to his career in racing.

    "Motorsport was never left to chance; it was improved through the courage to consistently use new technologies for the benefit of people. That is exactly the attitude I recognise in Lebensstimmen," Marko said in the press release.

    The business was founded by Philipp Berkessy, Markus Pinter and Lukas Stern, and plans to launch its platform fully in Austria and internationally in the first quarter of 2027.

    "Every person has words that should remain. With Lebensstimmen, we are making end-of-life planning personal, digital and legally secure for the first time. Today, a new culture of farewell begins, and it begins in Austria," Berkessy said.
