    法拉利认为书面规则无法管理勒克莱尔和汉密尔顿

    赛后媒体采访中，弗德里克·瓦瑟尔表示，与书面媒体的对话几乎与墨西哥城的对话相同。

    为什么法拉利认为书面规则无法帮助管理查尔斯·勒克莱尔和刘易斯·汉密尔顿

    法拉利车队负责人弗雷德里克·瓦瑟尔坚持认为这主要是尊重的问题。墨西哥碰撞事件再次发生让他感到“比你想象的还要沮丧”

    与弗雷德里克·瓦瑟尔进行的赛后媒体会议在多个方面几乎与墨西哥城的对话如出一辙。

    在两次场合，与书面媒体的讨论都在法拉利接待区的后面一个小而几乎舒适的房间里进行，部分原因是F1车队在前往飞地赛事时不能依赖欧洲赛季期间使用的接待单位。

    就像在墨西哥一样，媒体的关注度很高，就像在赫尔曼诺斯·罗德里格斯赛道一样，一个话题主导了整个对话：查尔斯·勒克莱尔和刘易斯·汉密尔顿之间的碰撞。

    在瓦瑟尔到来之前，当新闻官试图创造更多空间时，一位同事评论说，这个房间氛围相当轻松。新闻官笑了笑说：“嗯，对我们来说，这也是一个非常轻松的夜晚……”

    当然，这是讽刺的。这次，两辆红色赛车之间的碰撞并没有让法拉利损失一个可能的一二名完赛，与墨西哥不同，但这并没有减轻瓦瑟尔的沮丧。

    “说实话，你们无法想象我的沮丧，但这对所有在家的人来说更为重要，”法拉利车队负责人说。

    除了‘挫败’这个词，在这次会议中出现的另一个词是‘无能为力’。在墨西哥的碰撞之后，在圣保罗举行了建设性的讨论，所有各方随后表示，这些讨论非常富有成效，并且已经确立了界限。

    查尔斯·勒克莱尔，法拉利，刘易斯·汉密尔顿，法拉利

    核心信息是，车手的利益永远不能优先于2000名在马拉内洛的团队成员的利益，并且车手永远不能通过损害团队的行为让这些团队成员和赞助商失望。

    当Motorsport.com询问，从某种意义上说，瓦瑟尔是否感到无能为力，因为他已经清楚地传达了这一信息，但一旦面罩放下，这仍然证明是不够的，法拉利车队负责人回应说：‘这是一个很好的观点…’

    “这就是为什么我想明天再次进行讨论，也试图消化一下情况，”瓦瑟尔周六晚上说。

    “我认为在比赛两分钟后与他们讨论不是合适的地方。我看到查尔斯有点沮丧，我就告诉他我想说的话。我必须明天和他讨论剩下的部分。”

    瓦瑟尔立即通过车队无线电清楚地表明，他希望在勒克莱尔做其他任何事情之前——比如与媒体交谈——与他见面，但当Motorsport.com询问时，发现周六晚上发生的事情并没有那么多的对话。

    “不，那不是讨论，我只是在说话。我认为他道歉了；他理解了，但结果就在那里。你犯了错误。你可以说我错了，对不起，但你还是犯了错误。”

    因此，更实质性的讨论将在本周进行，尽管问题仍然存在，什么可以帮助并需要做来防止类似情况再次发生——尤其是如果法拉利想在2027年挑战冠军的话。

    查尔斯·勒克莱尔，法拉利，刘易斯·汉密尔顿，法拉利

    最明显的答案似乎就是行动规则或严格的指导方针，但瓦瑟尔明确表示，在他看来，把事情写下来并不一定能保证成功。

    “说实话，这不是规则的问题。不是规则的问题。你除了要尊重你的队友外，还能做些什么不同的事情？”

    根据瓦瑟尔的说法，最终归结于那个最后一点：无论情况如何，都要尊重你的队友。

    这位法国人明确表示，这必须是一种贯穿于每一种情况的思维方式，而不仅仅是一张包含规则的纸。在他看来，为每一种情况制定规则是不可能的，而且规则也无法阻止在拉斯维加斯发生的事情。

    “我认为今天的比赛，没有人会写出一个这样的场景。没有人能想象出这样的比赛。他们只需要以专业的方式行动。”

    在这两种情况下，可以说赛道布局至少起到了一定的作用。勒克莱尔在墨西哥表示，弯道的起始部分鼓励了这样的战斗，而在拉斯维加斯，尾流如此强大，以至于车手几乎被迫在直道上相互超越。

    这些是有效的观点，但瓦瑟尔不愿意接受它们作为减轻情况的理由，尤其是因为其他队友设法避免了问题。

    在这类比赛中，你不能冻结位置，因为你在尾流中。如果你减速，后面的车手就会超过你。但其他所有车队在比赛中都互相超过了20次。安东内利和拉塞尔，诺里斯和皮亚斯特里，甚至伊萨克超过了马克斯。

    这正是瓦瑟尔周六晚上还不相信书面规则是解决方案的原因。未来几天这一立场将如何演变还有待观察，但在拉斯维加斯，车队负责人继续强调尊重是最重要的。

    然而，整个情况的无能为力感在于，同样的信息在圣保罗被传达过，但仅仅两周后历史就重演了……

    Why Ferrari believes written rules won't help manage Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton

    Ferrari team principal Frederic Vasseur insists it is primarily a matter of respect. The fact that things went wrong again after the clash in Mexico has left him "more frustrated than you can imagine"

    The post-race media session with Frederic Vasseur was, in several respects, almost a carbon copy of the conversation in Mexico City.

    On both occasions, the discussion with the written press took place in a small, almost cosy room at the back of the Ferrari hospitality area, partly because Formula 1 teams cannot rely on the hospitality units used during the European part of the season when travelling to flyaway races.

    Just as in Mexico, there was considerable media interest, and just as at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez, one topic dominated the conversation: the clash between Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton.

    Before Vasseur arrived, as the press officer tried to create a little more space, a colleague remarked that the room had quite a relaxing atmosphere. The press officer laughed: "Well, it's a very relaxing evening for us as well…"

    It was, of course, sarcastic. This time, the collision between the two red cars had not cost Ferrari a potential one-two finish, unlike in Mexico, but that did nothing to ease Vasseur's frustration.

    "Honestly, you can't imagine the frustration for me, but it’s even more important for all the guys at home," the Ferrari team principal said.

    Apart from the word 'frustration', another word that came up during the session was 'powerless'. Following the clash in Mexico, constructive discussions were held in Sao Paulo, with all parties subsequently saying that those talks had been very productive and that the limits had been established.

    Charles Leclerc, Ferrari, Lewis Hamilton, Ferrari

    The central message was that a driver's interests must never take precedence over those of 2,000 team members back in Maranello, and that the drivers must never let those team members and sponsors down through actions that harm the team.

    When Motorsport.com asked whether, in a sense, Vasseur felt powerless, given that he had clearly communicated that message but it had still proved insufficient once the visors came down, the Ferrari team principal responded: "That is a good point…

    "And that's why I want to once again have a discussion tomorrow, also to try to digest the situation," Vasseur said on Saturday night.

    "I think it's not the right place to discuss it with them two minutes after the race. I saw Charles a bit down and I told him what I have to say. I've got to discuss the rest with him tomorrow."

    Vasseur immediately made it clear over the team radio that he wanted to see Leclerc before the Monegasque did anything else – such as speaking to the media – but when asked by Motorsport.com, it emerged that what happened on Saturday evening had not so much been a conversation.

    "No, it was not a discussion, I spoke. I think he apologised; he understood, but the result is there. You do a mistake. You can say, my mistake, I'm sorry, but you still did a mistake."

    The more substantial discussions will therefore take place this week, although the question remains what will help and what is needed to prevent a repeat of the same situation – particularly if Ferrari wants to challenge for the title in 2027.

    Charles Leclerc, Ferrari, Lewis Hamilton, Ferrari

    The most obvious answer would seem to be rules of engagement or strict guidelines, but Vasseur made it clear that, in his view, putting things in writing would not necessarily guarantee success either.

    "Honestly, it's not a matter of rules. It's not a matter of rules. What can you do differently, except that you have to respect your teammates?"

    According to Vasseur, it ultimately comes down to that last point: showing respect for your team-mate regardless of the situation.

    The Frenchman made it clear that this must be a mindset that carries through into every situation, rather than simply a sheet of paper containing rules. In his view, it is impossible to devise a rule for every scenario, and rules would not have prevented what happened in Las Vegas either.

    "I think the race of today, nobody will write a scenario like this. Nobody can imagine a race like this. They just have to act in a professional way."

    In both cases, it could be argued that the track layout played at least a minor role. Leclerc said in Mexico that the opening sequence of corners encouraged such battles, while the slipstream in Las Vegas was so powerful that drivers were almost forced to overtake one another on the straights.

    Those are valid points, but Vasseur was unwilling to accept them as mitigating circumstances, particularly because other team-mates had managed to avoid problems.

    "You can't freeze the position on this kind of race, because you are in the slipstream. If you slow down, the guy who is behind you is overtaking you. But all the other teams, they were able to overtake each other 20 times during the race. Antonelli and Russell, Norris and Piastri, even Isack overtook Max."

    That is precisely why Vasseur was not yet convinced on Saturday evening that written rules were the solution. It remains to be seen how that position evolves over the coming days, but in Vegas the team principal continued to emphasise respect above all else.

    The sense of powerlessness surrounding the whole situation, however, is that the same message was delivered in Sao Paulo, only for history to repeat itself just two weeks later…
