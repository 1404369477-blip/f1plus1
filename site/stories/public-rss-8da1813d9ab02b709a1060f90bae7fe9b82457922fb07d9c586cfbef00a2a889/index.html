    F1在美国仍‘非常小’ - 多门纳利

    尽管近年来F1在美国地区增长巨大，但F1首席执行官斯蒂法诺·多门纳利认为该赛事在美国仍然‘非常小’。多门纳利在接受The Race联合创始人Darren Cox采访时表示，尽管F1在美国的粉丝数量已达5200万，但与其它美国体育相比，仍有巨大的潜力待挖掘。

    一级方程式首席执行官斯蒂法诺·多梅尼卡利认为，尽管近年来该地区取得了巨大增长，但锦标赛在美国仍然“非常小”。

    多梅尼卡利在接受《赛车》联合创始人达伦·科克斯采访时表示，这次采访是在Axios和《赛车》的《超越赛车：创新与领导力投资》活动上进行的，该活动在拉斯维加斯举行，是F1赛历上的三场美国赛事之一。

    当自由媒体公司在2017年完成对F1的收购时，美国只有一场赛事，美国人对其他主要市场的兴趣落后。

    但现在，根据F1自己的数据，截至去年年底，美国有5200万粉丝，比前一年增长了11%，与十年前的情况完全不同。

    F1还与美国苹果公司签订了为期五年的合作协议，成为其新的美国广播合作伙伴，该公司表示，这在上个月已经使观众数量同比增长了13%——尽管对其影响存在争议。美国球迷也能在Netflix上通过特别流媒体合作观看今年的加拿大大奖赛。

    尽管有这种多方面的增长，但多门卡利仍然觉得与美国的其他运动相比，还有很多未被挖掘的潜力。

    “今天我对我的团队说，我们在美国很小，但我们正在以相当高的速度增长，”多门卡利在Axios和《赛车》联合创始人Darren Cox以及《赛车》的《超越赛车：创新与领导力投资》活动中告诉Darren Cox。

    “但当我们每天醒来，除了你已有的新闻外，还有F1的新闻，还有你对NFL、NBA以及其他这个国家热爱的所有运动的兴趣，因为它是文化的一部分。它是你与生俱来的感性的一部分。”

    “我们需要进入学校，我们需要进入大学，因为幕后，那些了解这项运动的人都知道，有一个不可思议的机会来谈论技术，谈论这项运动的复杂性水平。”

    “我们的重点是吸引人们，谈论我们作为一项运动，作为一个行业。我在谈论行业，因为每个车队背后都有一个不可思议的供应链阵容，一个令人难以置信的技术阵容，每个人都想参与其中。”

    “所以对我们来说，有如此多的角度来吸引人们的兴趣。这很迷人。我敢说没有多少平台能做这件事。”

    “因此，这是我们需要通过合作伙伴的协作不断确保的事情：无论出于什么原因吸引每个人，但随后进入了解我们是谁的知识，因为这是基础，为了可信，为了长期吸引那些可能是因为与我们的运动无关的兴趣而进入的人。”

    Netflix的《生存之战》也对F1在美国的增长起到了关键作用。

    当时，由于Covid疫情，人们需要通过电脑或电视等设备保持联系，[《生存之战》]在那些我们当时没有机会如此相关的市场中，对于提高我们对体育运动的认知起到了至关重要的作用，”多梅尼卡利补充道。

    自由时代与伯尼·埃克莱斯顿领导下的旧F1运营模式有着重大的区别。

    多梅尼卡利赞扬了埃克莱斯顿如何将F1发展壮大，但同时也指出了他一直致力于让F1摆脱的排他性问题。

    “在过去几年里，一级方程式赛车从以前的样子到现在的样子，确实有很大的不同，”多梅尼卡利说

    “在伯尼·埃克莱斯顿的领导下，采取那样的策略绝对是正确的：去创造原本不存在的东西。去建立一个对技术充满热情、对赛车充满热情、对环游世界充满热情的社区。

    “但这个社区只是专注于赛车运动，仅此而已。带有排他性的负面含义。在排他性的意义上：第一个回答是‘不’，而不是‘是的，如何？’。在自由媒体控制权变更之后，如果我们想要保持相关性，我们就必须采取不同的范式。

    “很多人……生活是美好的，因为空中充满了许多话语，但很多人都在书写。很多人认为我们的运动几年前就死了。这对那些热爱竞争、在赛道上比赛的人来说是一个巨大的机会，去展示当时人们所说的话。当然，并不是所有人。他们是错的。

    “但简单来说，我们认为这是一个很好的机会让我们变得相关，因为我们认为我们拥有所有成为真正优秀的元素，在保持运动成为我们策略中心的同时，尝试随着社交媒体内容的发展而发展。”

    “确保参与是由我们的英雄们，车手们，车队们，人们来完成的。解释幕后的情况。尝试给真正的铁杆粉丝一个谈论运动的机会，同时利用我们的平台与那些对运动一无所知的人群建立联系。”

    “这就是我们现在在这个方面变得相关的理由，因为一级方程式赛车为那些相信我们的合作伙伴提供了一个机会去做他们认为对他们有利的事情。”

    “我必须说，今天我们收到的最大的赞美是：‘一级方程式赛车是你要去的地方’。当你这么说的时候，那意味着很大的事情。”

    英文原文

    Formula 1 CEO Stefano Domenicali believes the championship is still “very small in the US” despite its huge growth in the region in recent years.

    Domenicali was speaking to The Race co-founder Darren Cox at Axios and The Race’s Beyond the Race: Investments in Innovation and Leadership event in Las Vegas - one of three US races on the F1 calendar.

    When Liberty Media completed its takeover of F1 in 2017, there was only one US race and American interest was lagging behind other key markets.

    But now, according to F1’s own figures, it had 52 million fans in the United States as of the end of last year, an 11% increase on the year before and a totally different picture to a decade ago.

    F1 also struck a five-year deal with Apple as its new broadcast partner in the US too, something it said back in August had increased viewership 13% year on year - even if there’s debate around its impact. US fans were also able to watch this year’s Canadian Grand Prix on Netflix in a special streaming collaboration.

    But despite that multi-pronged growth, Domenicali still feels there’s lots of untapped potential compared to other sports in the US.

    “Today I said to my people, we are very small in the US, but we are growing at quite a high speed,” Domenicali told The Race co-founder Darren Cox at Axios and The Race’s Beyond the Race: Investments in Innovation and Leadership event.

    “But we’re going to crush the market when, every day when you wake up, you have the news of Formula 1, on top of the news that you have, on top of the interest in the NFL, the NBA, in all the other sports that the country loves, because it’s part of the culture. It’s part of the sensibility that you were born with.

    “We need to get into the schools, we need to get into the universities, because behind the scenes, and the ones that know this sport know very well, there is an incredible opportunity to talk about technology, talking about the level of complexity of this sport.

    “Our focus is to attract people and talk about us as a sport, as an industry. I’m talking about the industry, because behind every team there is an incredible line-up of supply chains, a line-up of incredible technology, that everyone wants to be involved in.

    “So for us, there are so many angles to attract the interest of people. It’s fascinating. And I would say there are not so many platforms that can do that.

    “Therefore this is something that, through the collaboration of our partners, we need to keep making sure: that everyone is attracted for whatever reason, but then goes into the knowledge of who we are, because that’s fundamental, to be credible, and to stay long-term attractive to the people, who maybe came in because of an interest that was not really related to our sport specifically.”

    Netflix’s Drive To Survive has also been key to F1’s growth in the US.

    “At that time, when there was Covid, when people needed to be connected to a computer or TV or whatever it was, [Drive To Survive] was instrumental in growing the awareness of the sport in markets where we didn’t have the chance to be so relevant at that time,” Domenicali added.

    The Liberty era has been a monumental departure from how F1 was run under the old Bernie Ecclestone-fronted regime.

    Domenicali paid tribute to how Ecclestone was able to build F1 up but highlighted an exclusivity problem that he’s been very keen to pull F1 away from.

    “The evolution of what Formula 1 was before and what Formula 1 is now, in the last couple of years, is definitely different,” Domenicali said

    “It was absolutely the right strategy, under the leadership of Bernie Ecclestone, to do what he did: to build something that was not there. To build a community that was passionate about technology, passionate about racing, passionate about travelling around the world.

    “But it was a community that was just focused on motorsport, full stop. With a negative term of exclusivity. Exclusivity in terms of: the first answer was ‘no’, instead of ‘yes, and how?’. And under the Liberty change of control, we had to do a different paradigm if we wanted to be relevant.

    “A lot of people... and life is beautiful because a lot of words can fly around the air, but a lot of people wrote it. A lot of people thought that our sport was dead, not so many years ago. That is an incredible opportunity for the people that are racing inside, who love the competition, to show what the people were saying at that time. Not everyone, of course. They were wrong.

    “But in simple terms, the reason why we thought it was a great opportunity for us to be relevant is because we thought that we have all the elements to be really good, in terms of keeping the sport at the centre of our strategy, but trying to evolve with the social media content.

    “Trying to make sure that the engagement was done by our heroes, the drivers, the teams, the people. Explaining what is behind the scenes. Trying to give the opportunity not only to the real hardcore fans to talk about the sport, but using our platform to connect with an audience that didn’t know anything about the sport.

    “That’s the reason why now we are relevant in that respect, because F1 is giving an opportunity for the partners who believe in us to do what they believe is right for their interest.

    “And I have to say, today the biggest compliment that we receive is: ‘Formula 1 is the place to be’. When you say that, it’s something big.”
