    维斯塔潘详述他心目中2030年F1规则愿景

    维斯塔潘在赢得一场他直言感觉像“玩马里奥赛车”的拉斯维加斯大奖赛后，阐述了他认为F1在2030年下一次重大规则改革时应追求的方向：更轻的赛车、更接近V8时代的简单感受，以及超车按钮而非能量回收。

    维斯塔潘详述他心目中2030年F1规则愿景

    维斯塔潘概述他理想的2030年F1规则：更轻的赛车、更接近V8时代的简单感受，以及超车按钮而非能量回收

    致红牛车迷。关注红牛车队，获取来自米尔顿凯恩斯的F1新闻、车手动态、技术洞察和比赛分析。

    刚刚赢得一场混乱、出现352次超车的拉斯维加斯大奖赛，而他直言那感觉像“玩马里奥赛车”，马克斯·维斯塔潘随后阐述了他认为F1在2030年下一次重大规则改革到来时真正应该追求的方向。

    在卢赛尔举行的卡塔尔大奖赛前，这位四届世界冠军在接受媒体采访时被问及“纯粹”的一级方程式赛车是什么样子，以及在当前规则周期结束后，F1是否应该依赖DRS、超车模式或一键超车系统。

    维斯塔潘的出发点毫不含糊：减轻重量，摒弃人为的直线超车技巧，不再让电能回收决定车手如何进攻一圈。

    “嗯，我的意思是，赛车肯定要更轻，”维斯塔潘说。“考虑到我们过去开发的所有东西，如果你看从1980年代到2010年，当然有过统治级的赛车、统治级的车队，有时一圈快一秒甚至两秒。

    “现在我不认为你还会看到那种情况，但你仍然可以设计出一套规则，让赛车在直道上阻力超大，这样你就不需要那么依赖DRS了。”

    在2026年的技术规则下，F1确实缩小了尺寸，并将最低重量从800公斤降至768公斤，但转向接近50比50的内燃机与电力分配——在取消MGU-H后将MGU-K输出从120千瓦提升至350千瓦——使得对电池能量回收的依赖大大增加。

    在内华达，维斯塔潘承认这一场面在电视上效果不错，但坚持表示：“对我个人而言，我们比赛的方式并不是F1。电视上看起来很棒，但这不应该是它的样子。”

    对维斯塔潘来说，理想的解决方案并不需要完全禁止超车辅助手段——只需恢复一台在车手右脚之下表现自然的引擎。

    “比如对我来说，超车按钮系统没问题，但它需要是一台容易得多的引擎，”他解释道。“比如每次我做表演圈，开小红牛7之类的，开起来就是很舒服，因为油门响应灵敏，一切都很轻松，车开起来顺畅，车身不抖，没有迟滞，诸如此类。就是比我们现在的车好开得多。

    “所以，只要我们能回到接近那种状态的引擎，我知道它必须带有某种混合动力元素，很可能是涡轮增压器，因为否则在某些赛道上你甚至都不被允许驾驶，因为声音太吵了，这没问题，只要它不凌驾于自然的驾驶元素之上，而目前这种情况当然正在发生。”

    红牛2011年赢得总冠军的小红牛7赛车含车手仅重640公斤——比当今的赛车轻128公斤——并搭载了一台清脆的自然吸气2.4升雷诺V8引擎，转速可达18000转/分，配合紧凑的动能回收系统（KERS）单元。

    国际汽联2030年V8引擎重置——以及传统赛道上的商业隐忧

    维斯塔潘的愿望清单与F1权力掮客们上周正式锁定的方向高度一致。11月20日在拉斯维加斯，国际汽联宣布与F1、各车队及制造商一致达成2030年动力单元框架协议，核心是一台2.1升涡轮增压V8引擎，使用先进可持续燃料，并采用“有限混合动力化”——这是自2013年以来八缸引擎首次回归，也将当前的2026引擎时代缩短了一年。

    在国际汽联世界汽车运动理事会下个月于上海正式批准的前提下，国际汽联主席穆罕默德·本·苏拉耶姆承诺，新规则将使V8引擎与“更轻、更简单、更实惠的赛车相结合，让车手技术成为比赛的核心。”

    马克斯·维斯塔潘，红牛车队，伊萨克·哈贾尔，红牛车队

    维斯塔潘在赛历本身上则务实得多。当被问及2030年更小的赛车能否为回归传统老派赛道与较新的街道赛场地并存铺平道路时，他直接指出现代大奖赛赛程安排背后的商业模式。

    “他们随时可以回去；问题不在这，”维斯塔潘说。“这些赛道举办很多不同的比赛；并不是说它们不能举办一级方程式比赛，但当然，你也必须从F1的角度来看，当你更靠近市中心、邀请嘉宾时，就有机会赚很多钱，所以有很多因素会影响到什么才是他们最好的赛道。”

    “我只是从驾驶的角度来看，当然斯帕对我来说是最好的，但我也能理解，是的，它在森林里，如果你把它和拉斯维加斯或迈阿密相比，是的，我能理解它不在同一水平，但它很美，我喜欢它，开起来太棒了。”

    维斯塔潘的选择有一种巧妙的讽刺。根据F1的欧洲轮换协议，比利时与巴塞罗那-加泰罗尼亚配对，斯帕-弗朗科尔尚赛道已锁定2026、2027、2029和2031年——这意味着维斯塔潘最喜欢的赛道将缺席2030赛季，而这一年正是F1更轻、搭载V8引擎的重置计划预计登陆发车格的时候。

    Max Verstappen details his ideal 2030 F1 Formula

    Max Verstappen outlines his ideal 2030 F1 rules: lighter cars, simpler V8-era feel, and push-to-pass over energy saving

    For Red Bull fans. Keeping watch on Red Bull Racing, with F1 news, driver updates, technical insight and race analysis from Milton Keynes.

    Fresh off winning a chaotic, 352-overtake Las Vegas Grand Prix that he bluntly dismissed as feeling like "playing Mario Kart", Max Verstappen has laid out what he believes Formula 1 should actually chase when its next major regulation overhaul arrives in 2030.

    Speaking to the media ahead of the Qatar Grand Prix at Losail, the four-time world champion was asked what a "pure" Formula 1 looks like - and whether F1 should lean on DRS, overtake modes, or push-to-pass once the current rules cycle wraps up.

    Verstappen’s starting point was unambiguous: trim the weight, ditch artificial straight-line tricks, and stop letting electrical harvesting dictate how drivers attack a lap.

    "Well, I mean lighter cars for sure," Verstappen said. "With all the things that we have developed in the past, if you see from the 1980s to 2010, of course you had dominant cars, dominant teams that were sometimes over a second or two seconds a lap faster.

    "Now I don’t think you will have that anymore, but you can still come up with a formula where maybe a car is just super draggy in a straight line, so you don’t need to rely so much on DRS."

    Under 2026’s technical rules, F1 did shrink dimensions and cut minimum weight from 800 kilograms to 768 kilograms, yet the switch to a near 50–50 combustion-to-electric split - boosting MGU-K output from 120 kW to 350 kW after dropping the MGU-H - has placed a lot of reliance on battery harvesting.

    In Nevada, Verstappen conceded the spectacle worked for television while insisting, "For me personally this isn't Formula 1, the way that we are racing. It looks great on TV, but this isn't how it should be."

    For Verstappen, the ideal fix does not require banning overtaking aids altogether - only restoring an engine that behaves naturally under the driver’s right foot.

    "Like a push-to-pass system for me is fine, but it needs to be a much easier engine," he explained. "Like every time I do a show run, for example in an RB7 or whatever, it’s just so nice to drive because the throttle responds, everything is just easy, it drives off nice, the car is not shaking about, it has no lag, stuff like that. It’s just a lot nicer to drive than what we have currently.

    "So as long as we can get back to an engine close to that, I know it needs to be some kind of hybrid element, probably turbocharger, because otherwise you are not even allowed to drive at some tracks anymore because it’s too loud, which is fine, as long as it’s not overruling the natural driving element, which of course at the moment is happening."

    Red Bull’s championship-winning 2011 RB7 tipped the scales at just 640 kilograms including the driver - 128 kilograms lighter than today’s machinery - and paired a crisp, naturally aspirated 2.4-litre Renault V8 revving to 18,000 rpm with a compact KERS unit.

    FIA’s 2030 V8 reset - and the commercial catch over old-school tracks

    Verstappen’s wishlist aligns closely with the direction F1’s power brokers formally locked in last week. On 20 November in Las Vegas, the FIA announced a unanimous agreement with F1, teams, and manufacturers on a 2030 power unit framework centred on a 2.1-litre turbocharged V8 running on advanced sustainable fuels with "limited hybridisation" - bringing eight-cylinder engines back for the first time since 2013 and cutting the current 2026 engine era short by a year.

    Subject to formal approval by the FIA World Motor Sport Council in Shanghai next month, FIA president Mohammed Ben Sulayem pledged the new rules would marry the V8 to "lighter, simpler and more affordable cars that put driver skill at the heart of the competition."

    Max Verstappen, Red Bull Racing, Isack Hadjar, Red Bull Racing

    Where Verstappen remained far more pragmatic was on the calendar itself. Asked whether smaller 2030 cars could clear a path back to traditional, old-school circuits alongside newer street venues, he pointed straight to the business model driving modern grand prix scheduling.

    "They can always go back; it’s not about that," Verstappen said. "These tracks host a lot of different races; it’s not that they can’t host a Formula 1 race, but you have to, of course, also look at it from F1 side, where you have the opportunity to make a lot of money when you are also closer to a city centre, inviting guests over, so there are a lot of things that come into play to whatever is their best track.

    "I just look at it; of course it’s just a driving perspective, which for me Spa is the best, but that’s also, I can understand, yeah, it’s in the forest, which if you compare that to Vegas or Miami, yeah, I can understand it’s not at the same level, but it’s beautiful, I like it, to drive it’s fantastic."

    There is a neat irony in Verstappen’s choice. Under F1’s European rotational agreement pairing Belgium with Barcelona-Catalunya, Circuit de Spa-Francorchamps is locked in for 2026, 2027, 2029, and 2031 - meaning Verstappen’s favourite track is set to sit out the 2030 season, the very year F1’s lighter, V8-powered reset is scheduled to hit the grid.
