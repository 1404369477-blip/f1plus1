    国际汽联将从2027年开始引入全职F1裁判以提升一致性

    国际汽联宣布将从2027年开始引入全职裁判，以提升F1赛事裁判决定的连贯性。

    国际汽联将从2027年开始引入全职F1裁判以提升一致性

    国际汽联将自下赛季开始逐步引入全职裁判，最终团队将扩大至12-15名全职官员

    F1赛事裁判决定的连贯性一直是赛车场上的讨论话题。虽然体育规则和赛车指南的复杂性是一个因素，但另一个问题不断被提出：是否一个永久性的裁判小组能提升一致性？

    从2027年开始，国际汽联将朝着这个方向迈出一步。该管理机构周四宣布，它将逐步转向全职裁判，第一阶段将从2027年开始。

    意图是招募12到15名全职裁判，尽管这个数字将在一段时间后才能达到。第一批全职裁判将在2027赛季招募，他们将在每场比赛中由通常的兼职裁判和一名车手裁判加入，这名车手裁判作为前赛车手的专家知识和视角将一直是流程的一部分。

    主要目标是提高决策的一致性，尽管根据国际汽联的说法，这个倡议超越了这一点。通过使这个角色全职化，这些裁判也可以在比赛周末之外部署，例如“识别新兴趋势和需要监管明确化的领域”以及培训国际汽联官员。

    这将允许更有效地解决裁判使用的体育规则和赛车指南中的不足。因此，目标不仅是提高比赛中的连贯性，首先是要使规则和指南本身更加稳健。

    这一举措与国际汽联主席穆罕默德·本·苏莱姆一直倡导的相一致：进一步职业化裁判小组。国际汽联认为，超越当前的自愿轮换制度将有助于提高连贯性和一致性。

    这正是FIA想要改进的关键领域之一，同时强调这一举措也补充了FIA远程操作中心的功能，该中心最近在大吉山开业。

    这次对FIA裁判工作的最新重大投资反映了我们致力于在赛车运动中提供最高标准的决策、公平和诚信。全职裁判的招聘是我们长期使命中的一个重要步骤，即在我们FIA一级方程式世界锦标赛中建立一个专门的裁判生态系统，这也是对我们运动未来的投资，”本·苏莱姆说。

    这一发展将有助于在我们裁判结构中建立更大的专业性和连续性，加强我们审查和改进规则、支持未来官员的发展以及确保所有锦标赛中裁判工作的一致性最高的能力。

    FIA现在正在为2027年的分阶段引入制定招聘流程，计划在2028年和随后的年份扩大团队规模，直到达到12到15名全职裁判的目标。

    FIA to introduce full-time F1 stewards from 2027 to improve consistency

    The governing body will begin phasing in permanent stewards next season, with the group eventually expanding to 12-15 full-time officials

    The consistency of stewards’ decisions has been a topic of discussion in the Formula 1 paddock for years. While the complexity of the sporting regulations and racing guidelines is one factor, another question keeps coming up: would a permanent panel of stewards improve consistency?

    From 2027, the FIA will take a step in that direction. The governing body announced on Thursday that it will gradually move towards full-time stewards, with the first phase beginning in 2027.

    The intention is to recruit a panel of 12 to 15 full-time stewards, although that number will only be reached over time. The first full-time stewards will be recruited during the 2027 season, who will be joined at each race by the usual part-time stewards and a driver’s steward, whose specialist knowledge and perspective as a former racing driver will remain part of the process.

    The main objective is to improve the consistency of decisions, although according to the FIA, the initiative goes beyond that. By making the role full-time, these stewards can also be deployed outside race weekends, for example “to identify emerging trends and areas requiring regulatory clarification” and to train FIA officials.

    That would allow for shortcomings in the sporting regulations and racing guidelines used by stewards to be addressed more effectively. The aim is therefore not only to improve consistency during races, but also to make the rules and guidelines themselves more robust in the first place.

    This move is in line with what FIA president Mohammed Ben Sulayem has been advocating for some time: further professionalisation of the stewarding panel. The FIA believes moving beyond the current voluntary, rotating system will help improve continuity and consistency.

    And that is precisely one of the key areas the FIA wants to improve, while emphasising that the move also complements the FIA Remote Operations Centre, which recently opened at Biggin Hill.

    “This latest major investment in FIA officiating reflects our dedication to delivering the highest standards of decision-making, fairness and integrity in motorsport. The recruitment of full-time stewards is a significant step in our longstanding mission of building a dedicated officiating ecosystem in the FIA Formula One World Championship, it is also an investment in the future of our sport,” Ben Sulayem said.

    “This development will help to build greater expertise and continuity within our stewarding structure, strengthening our ability to review and improve regulations, support the development of future officials and ensure the highest standards of consistent officiating across all our championships.”

    The FIA is now setting up the recruitment process for the phased introduction in 2027, with plans to expand the group in 2028 and subsequent years until it reaches the target of 12 to 15 full-time stewards.
