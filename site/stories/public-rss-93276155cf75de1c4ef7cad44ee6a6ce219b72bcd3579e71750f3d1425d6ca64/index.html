    勒克莱尔拒谈法拉利内部交锋规则

    勒克莱尔在卡塔尔大奖赛前的FIA新闻发布会上承认与汉密尔顿的接触“不可接受”，并确认赛前已与汉密尔顿私下沟通，但拒绝透露法拉利内部如何防止类似事件重演。

    查尔斯·勒克莱尔拒绝透露法拉利的“交锋规则”在刘易斯·汉密尔顿碰撞事件后有何内容

    查尔斯·勒克莱尔同意法拉利内部的接触“不可接受”，并确认在卡塔尔大奖赛前已与刘易斯·汉密尔顿私下沟通

    致法拉利车迷。来自马拉内罗及更广范围的最新法拉利F1新闻、车手动态、技术分析和比赛反应。

    查尔斯·勒克莱尔周四走进洛赛尔国际赛车场的FIA新闻发布会时，清楚聚光灯仍会牢牢对准法拉利的车库。在拉斯维加斯大奖赛上让队友刘易斯·汉密尔顿打转五天后——并且承受了车队领队弗雷德里克·瓦塞尔赛后直率的严厉批评——这位摩纳哥车手没有试图回避后续影响，尽管他对详细说明马拉内罗计划如何防止此事再次发生关上了门。

    “我知道弗雷德（瓦塞尔）的立场，作为车手我必须接受，”勒克莱尔说。“我们都是实力强劲的车手，都希望跑在对方前面。不幸的是，最近发生了太多事故。”

    勒克莱尔的悔意出现在2026赛季动荡的最后阶段之后，这段时期反复考验着法拉利“两位一号车手”的理念。此前在蒙扎已有摩擦，随后在墨西哥城又发生首圈4号弯的冲突——当时勒克莱尔在挑战杆位得主汉密尔顿的过程中驶上草地，并被罚时五秒，而马克斯·维斯塔潘趁机穿过并最终以0.799秒的优势夺冠——瓦塞尔于是在圣保罗让两位车手坐下来当面把话说开。

    那次和解只维持了不到一场比赛。在拉斯维加斯，勒克莱尔从第10位发车，正好排在汉密尔顿（第11位）前面，比赛后段他在7号弯的猛烈插入导致七届世界冠军打转，勒克莱尔因此被罚时10秒，两人最终分别以第七和第十完赛，仅带回可怜的七个积分。

    赛车甚至还没抵达封闭停车区，瓦塞尔的怒火就已经清晰可闻；他在冷却圈通过无线电把勒克莱尔叫来，要求他“先来见我，其他事都往后放”。随后面对记者，这位法拉利领队明确表示那次交流完全是单向的：“不，那不是讨论——是我在说！”他称这次碰撞“不可接受”，是“对车队、对赞助商缺乏尊重”，并在接受天空体育意大利台采访时更进一步，警告两位车手之间的状况“正变得难以管理”，原因是“他们两人之间存在人与人之间的误解，而不是他们与车队之间有误解”。

    为什么勒克莱尔不愿讨论法拉利的“交战规则”

    在卡塔尔，勒克莱尔完全站在了他的老板一边，同时明确区分了赛道外的和谐与轮对轮的紧张。

    “解决方案？我不能详细谈论交战规则，”勒克莱尔说。“不过，我认为外界关注度非常高，但最重要的是找到尽可能避免问题的解决方案。我们正在做，但我不能谈论它。我为拉斯维加斯承担了责任，但我同意瓦塞尔的看法，两辆法拉利之间的接触是不可接受的。

    “刘易斯和我已经谈过寻找解决方案，我相信我们在赛道外的关系非常牢固。然后这个问题又在赛道上出现了，这正是我们需要找到解决方案的地方，但正如我所说，我不想谈论它，也不想再回顾那次事件。”

    夏尔·勒克莱尔，法拉利，刘易斯·汉密尔顿，法拉利

    勒克莱尔不愿讨论正式准则，这与瓦塞尔本人在内华达州所持的怀疑态度如出一辙。当被问及法拉利是否终于需要白纸黑字的书面比赛规则时，瓦塞尔予以反驳，坚称：“老实说，这不是规则的问题……你没法把它写在纸上……他们只需要以专业的方式行事。”

    在洛赛尔周五练习赛之前，瓦塞尔在法拉利官方周末前瞻中语气更为平静，以目标为导向。他说：“我们前往卡塔尔，决心作为一个团队做出回应，并交付我们对自己所期望的那种周末。”他强调，法拉利的目标是“让两辆赛车都带回一个扎实的成绩”。

    在积分榜上，法拉利实际上正处于完成其剩余主要车队目标的边缘。梅赛德斯已以666分加冕车队冠军，法拉利则以517分位居第二——领先第三名红牛（440分）77分，领先迈凯伦（431分）86分，而在卡塔尔和下周阿布扎比收官战中还剩下86分。周日只需拿到10分——无论是通过一个前五名完赛，还是常规的双车积分入账——无论对手如何表现，马拉内罗都将锁定第二名。

    然而，就个人而言，荣誉依然岌岌可危：汉密尔顿以267分位列车手积分榜第三进入卢赛尔站——仅领先乔治·拉塞尔和兰多·诺里斯三分——而勒克莱尔以250分落后17分，还剩两场比赛来缩小差距。卡塔尔强制性的27圈轮胎上限将周日的比赛变成一场全速的战略冲刺，法拉利两位明星车手之间的干净执行，将比周四新闻发布室里说的任何话都更能说明问题。

    Charles Leclerc refuses to reveal Ferrari's "rules of engagement" after Lewis Hamilton crash

    Charles Leclerc agrees Ferrari contact is "unacceptable" and confirms private talks with Lewis Hamilton before Qatar GP

    For Ferrari fans. The latest Scuderia Ferrari F1 news, driver updates, technical analysis and race reaction from Maranello and beyond.

    Charles Leclerc walked into Thursday’s FIA press conference at the Losail International Circuit knowing the spotlight would stay locked on Ferrari’s garage. Five days after spinning team-mate Lewis Hamilton at the Las Vegas Grand Prix - and absorbing a blunt post-race dressing-down from team principal Fred Vasseur - the Monegasque driver did not attempt to dodge the fallout, even as he shut the door on detailing how Maranello plans to stop it from happening again.

    "I knew what Fred’s position was, and as a driver I have to accept it," Leclerc said. "We are strong drivers who want to finish ahead of each other. Unfortunately, too many incidents have happened recently."

    Leclerc’s contrition follows a turbulent final stretch of the 2026 campaign that has repeatedly tested Ferrari’s two-No. 1 philosophy. After earlier friction at Monza and an opening-lap Turn 4 clash in Mexico City - where Leclerc cut the grass while challenging polesitter Hamilton and picked up a five-second penalty as Max Verstappen slipped through to win by 0.799 seconds - Vasseur sat both drivers down in São Paulo to clear the air.

    That truce lasted barely one race. In Las Vegas, where Leclerc started 10th directly ahead of Hamilton in 11th, a late-race lunge into Turn 7 spun the seven-time champion and landed Leclerc a 10-second penalty, leaving the pair seventh and 10th for a meagre seven-point haul.

    Vasseur’s fury was audible before the cars even reached parc fermé; he summoned Leclerc over cooldown-lap radio to see him "before anything else". Speaking to reporters afterwards, the Ferrari boss made clear the exchange was strictly one-way: "No, it was not a discussion – I spoke!" Calling the collision "unacceptable" and "a lack of respect for the team, for the sponsors," Vasseur went even further with Sky Italia, warning that the situation between his drivers was "becoming unmanageable" due to "a human misunderstanding between them, not between them and the team".

    Why Leclerc won’t discuss Ferrari’s “rules of engagement”

    In Qatar, Leclerc aligned himself squarely with his boss while drawing a clear line between off-track harmony and wheel-to-wheel tension.

    "The solution? I can't go into detail and talk about the rules of engagement," Leclerc said. "However, I believe there is a very high level of external attention, but the most important thing is to find a solution to avoid problems as much as possible. We are doing it, but I can't talk about it. I took responsibility for Las Vegas, but I agree with Vasseur that contact between two Ferraris is unacceptable.

    "Lewis and I have already spoken to find a solution, and I believe our relationship off the track is very strong. Then the issue came up again on the track, and that’s where we need to find a solution, but as I said, I don’t want to talk about it or go back over the incident."

    Charles Leclerc, Ferrari, Lewis Hamilton, Ferrari

    Leclerc’s reluctance to discuss formal guidelines mirrors Vasseur’s own scepticism in Nevada. Asked whether Ferrari finally needed written racing rules on paper, Vasseur pushed back, insisting: "Honestly, it's not a matter of rules... You can't put it on the paper... They just have to act in a professional way."

    Ahead of Friday practice at Losail, Vasseur struck a calmer, target-driven tone in Ferrari’s official weekend preview. "We head to Qatar determined to respond as a team and deliver the kind of weekend we expect from ourselves," he said, emphasising that the Scuderia’s goal is to "bring home a solid result with both cars".

    On the scoreboard, Ferrari actually stands on the brink of wrapping up its main remaining team objective. With Mercedes already crowned Constructors’ champions on 666 points, Ferrari holds second on 517—a 77-point cushion over third-placed Red Bull (440) and 86 ahead of McLaren (431) with 86 points left across Qatar and next week’s Abu Dhabi finale. Scoring just 10 points on Sunday - whether through a single top-five finish or a routine double-points haul - seals P2 for Maranello regardless of what its rivals do.

    Individually, however, pride remains sharply on the line: Hamilton enters Losail third in the Drivers’ standings on 267 points—just three ahead of George Russell and Lando Norris - while Leclerc sits 17 points back on 250 with two races left to close the gap. With Qatar’s mandatory 27-lap tyre cap turning Sunday into a flat-out strategic sprint, clean execution between Ferrari’s two stars will tell far more than anything said in Thursday’s press room.
