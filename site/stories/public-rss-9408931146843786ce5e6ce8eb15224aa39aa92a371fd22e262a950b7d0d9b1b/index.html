    拉塞尔夺赞德沃特冲刺杆位，但数据向梅赛德斯发出警示

    拉塞尔在荷兰大奖赛冲刺赛中为梅赛德斯拿下杆位，但性能数据显示迈凯伦取得关键进步，表明梅赛德斯可能无法再推迟引进更多升级。

    乔治·拉塞尔在一级方程式荷兰大奖赛冲刺赛排位赛中拿下杆位，让梅赛德斯松了一口气，因为其按兵不动、暂缓升级的策略尚未让车队掉入中游集团。

    法拉利和迈凯伦在为赛车引进研发套件方面一直很激进——双方最新的升级件都出现在赞德沃特——而梅赛德斯则在下一盘长棋。

    自五月的加拿大大奖赛以来，它有选择地只进行了一次重大规格改动，并把研发经费留到赛季末再大笔投入，届时它希望竞争对手的钱袋已经掏空。

    不过，尽管拉塞尔力压兰多·诺里斯的迈凯伦展现出W17依然非常强大，但数据深处却隐藏着一个明显的警告信号：梅赛德斯可能无法再等太久，必须尽快带来更多升级。

    拉塞尔击败诺里斯夺得杆位的优势只有0.041秒，但两人做出单圈的过程非常跌宕起伏——各计时段之间差异巨大。

    两人在中间计时段相差近半秒，基米·安东内利也同样落后，说明这是梅赛德斯车队的整体趋势。

    在2026年一级方程式规则下，这种波动并不罕见，通常来自不同的能量部署策略——即一名车手在某条直道上比另一名车手使用更多能量。

    诺里斯和拉塞尔之间的部分差距可以由此解释，例如在冲线结束单圈时，两车之间存在很大的速度差异。

    当拉塞尔的梅赛德斯在冲向计时线时持续加速到317公里/小时（197英里/小时），诺里斯的迈凯伦则在达到约307公里/小时（191英里/小时）的峰值后速度开始回落。

    但梅赛德斯在这里的优势似乎并不是它在其他路段刻意节省能量所带来的好处，因为两者之间最高速度的唯一真正区别，出现在迈凯伦似乎在本圈起步阶段使用了更多能量的地方。

    事实上，深入分析他们SQ3最佳单圈的遥测数据可以发现，拉塞尔糟糕的中间计时段与在直道上节省能量毫无关系——问题出在过弯的表现上。

    如上面的数据所示（数据来自F1tel.com），梅赛德斯在高速的7号弯和8号弯上一直不如迈凯伦和法拉利——无论是拉塞尔还是安东内利，都无法以接近迈凯伦和法拉利的速度通过这两个弯。

    虽然迈凯伦的升级帮助诺里斯在7号弯以253公里/小时（157英里/小时）的最低速度通过，在8号弯达到219公里/小时（136英里/小时），而拉塞尔则分别降至242公里/小时（150英里/小时）和205公里/小时（127英里/小时）。

    这些差距让诺里斯从当时落后拉塞尔0.2秒，重新追平了拉塞尔。

    在这两个高速弯中的表现表明迈凯伦最近的升级取得了明显进步——因为这与我们今年早些时候在同级别速度区间的弯道中所看到的情况形成了对比。

    例如在银石赛道，诺里斯在科普斯弯比拉塞尔慢7公里/小时（4.3英里/小时）（283公里/小时对276公里/小时），在斯托弯也落后了类似的差距（238公里/小时对231公里/小时）。

    梅赛德斯现在需要弄清楚的是，赛车在赞德沃特7号/8号弯速度上的损失，究竟说明它真的在高速弯上落后了、需要通过一些赛车改进来做出回应，还是存在调校或驾驶方式上的问题导致它暴露了弱点。

    当梅赛德斯在周六下午再次尝试排位赛时，这个问题会得到更清晰的答案。

    英文原文

    George Russell's sprint pole position at Formula 1's Dutch Grand Prix provided relief for Mercedes that its strategy of holding fire on upgrades had not yet dropped it into the pack.

    While Ferrari and McLaren have been aggressive in bringing development packages to their cars – with latest updates from both appearing at Zandvoort – Mercedes has been playing the long game.

    Having selectively had just one major spec change at the Canadian GP in May, it is saving its development spending for an end of season splurge when it hopes the coffers of its rivals are empty.

    But despite Russell's form in edging out Lando Norris's McLaren showing that the W17 is still very strong, tucked away in the data is a clear warning signal that Mercedes may not be able to wait much longer before needing to bring more.

    Russell's advantage over Norris to take pole was a slim 0.041 seconds, but there was a very topsy turvy nature to how they achieved their laps – with big variability between sectors.

    There was nearly half a second of difference between them in the middle sector, with Kimi Antonelli similarly adrift to show that this was a Mercedes trend.

    Under F1's 2026 rules, such variability is not uncommon and often comes from different deployment strategies – where one driver will use more energy on one straight compared to another.

    Some of the difference between Norris and Russell can be explained from this, with there being a big speed difference for example as they run to end the lap.

    As Russell's Mercedes keeps accelerating to 317km/h (197mph) in the chase to the timing line, Norris's McLaren peaks at around 307km/h (191mph) before falling away.

    But the Mercedes advantage here does not seem to be the upside to it having deliberately not used energy elsewhere, as the only real difference in top speeds between them is when McLaren appeared to have used more at the start of the lap.

    In fact, digging into the telemetry of their best SQ3 efforts, the poor middle sector of Russell's was nothing to do with saving energy on the straights – it was performance through the corners.

    As the data above shows, which we have used from F1tel.com shows, Mercedes has been struggling compared to McLaren and Ferrari in the high-speed Turns 7 and 8 – which neither Russell nor Kimi Antonelli can take anywhere near as fast as McLaren and Ferrari.

    While McLaren's upgrade has helped Norris keep his minimum speed up at 253km/h (157mph) through Turn 7 and 219km/h (136mph) at Turn 8, Russell drops to 242km/h (150mph) and 205km/h (127mph).

    Those differences pull Norris back from two tenths of a second adrift at the time, to back on par with Russell.

    The performance through these two high-speed corners points to McLaren having made a clear step with its most recent upgrade – as it is in contrast to what we have seen in turns with such a speed range earlier in the year.

    At Silverstone, for example, Norris was 7km/h (4.3mph) slower than Russell through Copse (283km/h vs 276km/h), and a similar speed adrift at Stowe (238km/h vs 231km/h).

    What Mercedes now needs to understand is whether that speed offset through Turns 7/8 at Zandvoort is a sign that it has genuinely fallen back in high-speed corners so needs to respond with some car improvements, or if there is some set-up or driving approach issue that has left it exposed.

    A clearer answer for that will come when Mercedes has another go at qualifying on Saturday afternoon.
