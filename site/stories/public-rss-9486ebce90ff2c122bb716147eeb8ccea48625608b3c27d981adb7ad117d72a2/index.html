    诺里斯：迈凯伦直道速度糟糕，塑料袋卡底板

    诺里斯在F1拉斯维加斯大奖赛排位赛后称迈凯伦直道速度"糟糕"且"可怕"，他Q3仅列第四，因安东内利受罚正赛将从第三位发车。车队领队斯特拉还提到，诺里斯车底卡入塑料袋损失了下压力。

    诺里斯在错过F1 2026年拉斯维加斯大奖赛杆位后，称迈凯伦的直道速度"糟糕"且"可怕"。

    诺里斯在Q3中排名第四，由于新科世界冠军、杆位得主基米·安东内利受罚，他正赛将从第三位发车，但他对落后0.351秒感到沮丧。

    "我们在直道上就是糟糕，"诺里斯说，"直道上简直可怕。

    "我的这一圈非常好，轻松是我最好的一圈。但和基米相比，我们在最后一段慢了0.8秒。

    “他获得了完美的尾流，而且他做得非常好。但与大多数其他人相比，我们落后太多了。”

    “我必须在距离前车1.5秒的位置开始我的飞驰圈，这意味着在每个弯道，由于损失了太多下压力，我至少会损失0.05到0.1秒。”

    “最终，拥有良好的尾流显然能带来更大的收益，但我们必须弥补直道速度的严重损失，因此不得不损害我们的过弯速度。”

    “所以总的来说，在下压力和阻力方面，我们就是没有找到正确的平衡。这让我们的赛车无法争夺杆位。”

    车队领队安德烈亚·斯特拉还提出了诺里斯一方一个不同寻常的额外减轻因素。

    “我想特别提一下，尤其是在兰多这边，他相当不走运，因为塑料袋卡在了底板里，我们在那里损失了相当多的下压力，”斯特拉解释道。

    “所以，考虑到十分之一秒本会带来很大差别，那个塑料袋上肯定值十分之一秒，这有点可惜。”

    队友奥斯卡·皮亚斯特里在Q3中落后超过一秒，仅列第七，被小红牛的阿尔维德·林德布拉德击败。

    尽管诺里斯将迈凯伦在直道上的劣势描述为“全年都存在的一个问题”，但皮亚斯特里觉得，迈凯伦在拉斯维加斯损失如此之大，让他们“有点措手不及”。

    “我不认为我们预料到这会成为这么大的问题，”他说。“在蒙扎我们还可以，对我们来说奇怪的部分是，这主要是和其他梅赛德斯动力赛车相比。”

    “所以，也许很多人减少了空气下压力，而我们减得不够。但这看起来是我们缺失的相当大的一步。”

    斯特拉表示，迈凯伦“对这条赛道的要求是否适合我们的赛车有一些担忧”，因为车队采用了更短的齿比，而且已知在产生与动力单元供应商梅赛德斯相同的直道性能方面存在弱点。

    斯特拉总结道：“当然，我们在这里看到了与蒙扎和巴库类似的趋势，而且在所有条件下我们都不是特别有竞争力。”

    “混乱”还是执行得当？

    皮亚斯特里称第三阶段排位赛对迈凯伦来说是一场“混乱”的 session，留下了“一些我们可能会复盘并看看能否做得稍好一点的事情”。

    皮亚斯特里补充道：“甚至在这里开启一圈时，赛车之间就有一些差异，它们在进入1号弯时消耗多少能量，如果那是半秒，那可能在圈速后段直道上就是三到四成的差距。”

    “所以这很难做对，但我觉得我的圈速也有点乱。”

    正如安东内利的杆位圈所展示的，这一切都被尾流效应极大地压倒了——如果执行得当，这能带来相当可观的额外圈速。

    “我觉得法拉利就是完美的例子，”皮亚斯特里说。“他们没获得尾流，或者尾流不够大，结果他们在Q2就被淘汰了。

    “说实话，我认为前四名车队中的任何一支，如果没获得尾流，都可能遭遇同样的情况。

    “所以，在这类赛道上驾驶这些赛车时，这是非常重要的一部分，你只能将就着应对，因为也许完美的轮胎升温能让你快个零点一秒或零点二秒，但你看基米，他在最后一个计时段比我快了一秒。”

    “无论你在轮胎上获得多少优势，你总是会在尾流上失去它，而且失去的远不止这些。”

    斯特拉认为迈凯伦很好地执行了那部分。

    “我们在这里做得更好的是，通过在排位赛中占据非常好的位置来减轻这一[弱点]，”斯特拉说。

    “在排位赛中，赛车基本达到了我们想要的状态，这无疑帮了大忙，因为我得说，在这些规则下，跟随一辆车甚至多辆车时的阻力减少比过去更多，因此处在正确位置的激励甚至更高。”

    “我认为这在排位赛中效果很好。”

    英文原文

    Lando Norris called McLaren's straightline speed "shocking" and "horrendous" after missing out on pole position for Formula 1's 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix.

    Norris was fourth fastest in Q3 and will start the race third on the grid thanks to a grid penalty for new world champion and pole winner Kimi Antonelli, but was frustrated to be 0.351 seconds off the pace.

    "We're just shocking in the straights," Norris said. "Like, horrendous in the straight.

    "My lap was very good and it was easily my best lap. But to Kimi, we're eight tenths off in the last sector.

    "He has a perfect tow and he does a very good job. But comparing to most other people, we're too far off.

    "I have to start my lap 1.5 seconds off the car ahead, which means in every corner I'm losing at least a half a tenth to a tenth because of how much downforce I'm losing.

    "There's obviously a bigger gain at the end of the day in having a good slipstream, but we're having to make up for a pretty terrible loss of straightline speed and therefore we have to harm our cornering speed.

    "So we just haven't got the right balance, all in all, in terms of downforce, drag. It's made our life impossible to fight for our pole."

    Team principal Andrea Stella also offered an unusual extra mitigating factor on Norris's side.

    "I would like to mention that especially on Lando's side he has been quite unlucky, because the plastic bag got stuck in the floor and we lost quite a lot of downforce there," Stella explained.

    "So, considering that one tenth of a second would have made a big difference, certainly there was one tenth of a second on a plastic bag, which is a bit of a shame."

    Team-mate Oscar Piastri was over a second off the pace down in seventh, beaten by the Racing Bulls of Arvid Lindblad in Q3.

    And even though Norris described McLaren's deficit on the straights as something that's "been an issue all year", Piastri felt McLaren had been "caught a bit by surprise" by how much it was losing out in Vegas.

    "I don't think we expected it to be such a big problem," he said. "At Monza we were OK, and the strange part for us is it's mostly compared to the other Mercedes-powered cars.

    "So, maybe a lot of people took downforce off, and we didn't take enough off. But it seems like a pretty massive step that we were missing."

    Stella said McLaren had "some concerns in terms of how suitable the demand of this circuit would be for our car" because of the team's shorter gear ratios and its known weakness in generating the same straightline performance as power unit supplier Mercedes.

    "Certainly we have seen here trends that are similar to Monza and Baku, and in all conditions we were not particularly competitive," Stella summarised.

    'Messy' or well executed?

    Piastri called Q3 a "messy" session for McLaren, which left "a few things that we probably will review and see if we can do a slightly better job".

    "Even opening the lap here, there's a bit of a difference between cars, how much energy they use to Turn 1, and if that's half a second, that can be the difference of three or four tenths down the straight later in the lap," Piastri added.

    "So it's difficult to get right, but I think also my laps were a little bit messy as well."

    As Antonelli's pole lap showed, all of this was massively overwhelmed by the towing effect - which was worth a significant chunk of extra laptime if executed correctly.

    "I think Ferrari is the perfect example," Piastri said. "They didn't get a slipstream, or not a big enough slipstream, and they're out in Q2.

    "I think honestly, any of the top four teams could have done the same thing if they didn't get a tow.

    "So, it's a massive part of these kind of tracks in these cars, and you kind of just have to make do because maybe you can gain a tenth or two with the perfect [tyre] warm-up, but you look at Kimi, he's one second faster than me in the last sector.

    "No matter what you gain on the tyres, you're always going to lose it and a lot more on the tow."

    Stella felt McLaren had executed that part well.

    "What we have done better here is to mitigate this [weakness] from having really good positioning in qualifying," Stella said.

    "The car was pretty much where we wanted it to be in qualifying, and this definitely helped, because I have to say that it seems like with these regulations, the drag reduction when you follow one car or even multiple cars is more than in the past, and therefore there is an even higher incentive to be positioned in the right place.

    "I think this worked well in qualifying."
