    汉密尔顿访问拉法口岸后呼吁以色列放行援助卡车

    法拉利车手刘易斯·汉密尔顿周二在埃及拉法口岸考察向加沙运送援助物资的工作，随后呼吁以色列放行援助卡车。

    刘易斯·汉密尔顿：法拉利车队的七届F1世界冠军在访问拉法口岸后，呼吁以色列“放行援助卡车”进入加沙。

    刘易斯·汉密尔顿周二访问了拉法口岸，实地了解向加沙运送援助物资的工作；这位法拉利车手周三在Instagram发帖，呼吁以色列“履行停火协议下的义务”。

    刘易斯·汉密尔顿在访问拉法口岸后，呼吁以色列“放行援助卡车”进入加沙。

    汉密尔顿周二在埃及，实地了解向加沙运送人道主义与救援物资的工作。这位法拉利车手还参观了拉法口岸，并走访了埃及红新月会的援助仓库。

    美国支持的以色列与哈马斯之间的停火协议仍然有效，但并未结束以色列对加沙的打击，同时联合国世界粮食计划署表示，加沙三分之二的人口难以获得足够的食物。

    汉密尔顿此前曾呼吁该地区实现和平，他表示援助物资因“疯狂的理由”被拒绝进入加沙。

    这位七届世界冠军在Instagram上发文称：“过去三年以色列在加沙的种族灭绝行径令人震惊。”

    “人们被剥夺自由、安全和尊严，这是不公正的。任何人都不应被当作生命不那么重要来对待。”

    “我看到绵延数英里的围墙，是为了把他们困在里面而建的。站在外面，感觉就像一座露天监狱，我满脑子想的都是进去帮忙。”

    “在拉法口岸，我看到食品卡车排着队准备出发，但它们经常不被允许进入，或者面临严重延误。我交谈过的排在第一位的那位司机已经等了超过12个小时。”

    在附近的物流中心，我看到了救援人员的辛勤工作，但我也看到了因荒唐理由被拒绝的物资。睡袋、急救包、医疗设备、轮椅，甚至给孩子们的足球。

    我知道很多人都感受到同样的无助。同样觉得需要做点什么，而不是袖手旁观。我不想让我们对他们的苦难习以为常。我不想让我们移开视线。

    周三，以色列总理本雅明·内塔尼亚胡否认以色列在拖延物资进入加沙，并将加沙不断恶化的状况归咎于哈马斯，指责他们拒绝解除武装并截留援助物资用于军事用途。

    2021年，汉密尔顿创立了Mission 44慈善机构，旨在支持年轻人并改善他们获得教育和就业机会的途径。

    他还支持了人道主义和救援倡议，以及为受冲突影响的人们提供帮助的努力。

    汉密尔顿是在上周末参加圣保罗大奖赛并获得第九名后进行了此次访问。他接下来将代表法拉利参加拉斯维加斯大奖赛，第一次练习赛于11月20日星期五举行。

    汉密尔顿分享了与加沙儿童会面的细节

    在首次发帖一天后，汉密尔顿于周四再次通过社交媒体分享了他与从加沙撤离的儿童互动的细节。

    除了分享一组自己与孩子们的合影外，汉密尔顿写道：“在埃及见到一些由@inaraorg支持的从加沙撤离的巴勒斯坦儿童，我的心都碎了。

    “这些美丽、聪明的孩子经历了创伤、悲痛和可怕的伤害，这些是任何孩子都不应该承受的事情。有些孩子在袭击中中弹或严重烧伤，仍在面对复杂的康复之路。但真正打动我的是他们如此坚韧，而且似乎在彼此身上找到了许多快乐。

    Lewis Hamilton: Ferrari's seven-time F1 world champion urges Israel to 'let the aid trucks in' to Gaza after visit to Rafah border

    Lewis Hamilton visited the Rafah border on Tuesday to witness efforts to transport aid into Gaza; Ferrari driver published Instagram post on Wednesday calling on Israel to "fulfil its obligations under the ceasefire"

    Lewis Hamilton urged Israel to "let the aid trucks in" to Gaza after visiting the Rafah border.

    Hamilton was in Egypt on Tuesday to witness efforts to deliver humanitarian and relief aid bound for Gaza. The Ferrari driver also toured the Rafah border crossing and visited the Egyptian Red Crescent's aid warehouses.

    The US-backed ceasefire between Israel and Hamas remains in place but has not ended Israeli strikes on Gaza, while the UN World Food Programme says two-thirds of Gaza's population are struggling to get enough to eat.

    Hamilton - who has previously called for peace in the region - said aid was being denied entry into Gaza for "crazy reasons".

    The seven-time world champion posted on Instagram: "The past three years of Israel's genocide in Gaza is appalling.

    "It's an injustice for people to be denied their freedom, their safety and their dignity. No human being should be treated as though their life matters less.

    "I saw walls stretching for miles, built to keep them in. Standing there, outside what felt like an open-air prison, all I could think about was getting in to help.

    "At Rafah border, I saw food trucks lined up ready to go, but they're regularly not allowed in or face major delays. The first driver in line that I spoke to had already been waiting over 12 hours.

    "At the nearby logistics centre, I saw the hard work of the aid workers, but I also saw what gets rejected for crazy reasons. Sleeping bags, first-aid kits, medical equipment, wheelchairs, even footballs for kids.

    "Israel needs to fulfil its obligations under the ceasefire and let the aid trucks in. No excuse.

    "I know so many people feel that same helplessness. That same need to do something rather than just watch. I don't want us to get used to their suffering. I don't want us to look away."

    On Wednesday, Benjamin Netanyahu, the Israeli Prime Minister, denied Israel was delaying the entry of supplies into Gaza, and blamed Hamas for the worsening conditions in Gaza, accusing them of a refusal to disarm and seizing aid for military use.

    In 2021, Hamilton founded Mission 44, a charity aimed at supporting young people and improving ⁠access to education and employment ​opportunities.

    He has also backed ​humanitarian and relief initiatives, and ​efforts to support people affected by ⁠conflict.

    Hamilton made his visit after competing in the Sao Paulo GP last weekend, where he finished ninth. He will next compete for Ferrari in the Las Vegas GP, with Practice 1 on Friday November 20.

    Hamilton shares details of meeting children from Gaza

    A day after his initial post, Hamilton took to social media again on Thursday to share details of his interactions with children he met who have been evacuated from Gaza.

    Along with sharing a selection of photos of himself with the children, Hamilton wrote: "Meeting some of the Palestinian children evacuated from Gaza and supported by @inaraorg in Egypt broke my heart.

    "Beautiful, bright children who have faced trauma, grief and horrendous injuries, things no child should ever have to endure. Some have been shot or badly burned in attacks and are still facing a complicated road towards recovery. But what really struck me was how resilient they were and how much joy they seem to find in each other.

    "When I asked about their dreams, they spoke about becoming teachers and doctors so they could help others, the way they were helped. After everything they've been through, they still imagine a future caring for other people. That has stayed with me.

    "I met children at school, in hospital and at a mental health clinic helping them work through their trauma. I also went into families' homes and listened to their stories of recovery and being separated from loved ones - their children, their parents, their siblings. The pain of that separation and uncertainty is hard to comprehend.

    "Their stories made me think about the children still in Gaza, who are living in terrible conditions. According to reports, more than 21,500 children have been killed and 45,000 injured since October 2023. Every one of them had a home and a future. We can't forget them.

    "These children are no different from yours. Their lives are just as precious. They should be able to feel safe in a classroom with friends, be with their families and imagine a future. I'm so grateful the young people I met are getting the help they need, but it's heartbreaking that they've had to leave their homes to find safety."
