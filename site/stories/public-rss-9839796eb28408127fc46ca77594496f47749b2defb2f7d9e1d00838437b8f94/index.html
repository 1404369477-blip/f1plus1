    威廉姆斯与阿尔派违反2025年F1成本上限规则

    威廉姆斯和阿尔派两支F1车队在2025年均违反了国际汽联的成本上限规则，威廉姆斯存在轻微超支和程序性违规，阿尔派仅为程序性违规。两队都将面临罚款，威廉姆斯还将受到竞技处罚。

    威廉姆斯和阿尔派两支F1车队在2025年均违反了国际汽联的成本上限规则。

    威廉姆斯存在轻微超支违规以及程序性违规，而阿尔派仅被认定存在程序性违规。

    两队都将面临罚款，威廉姆斯还将受到竞技处罚。

    五家动力单元制造商在2025年均被认定合规。

    2025年，车队成本上限为1.35亿美元，引擎制造商为1.3亿美元，另加通胀调整。

    威廉姆斯的违规属于“轻微超支违规”，即低于2025年成本上限的5%。

    该车队被认定超出成本上限62.7万英镑，占成本上限的0.48%。

    该车队于11月23日与国际汽联签订了接受违规协议。

    作为《预算上限违规协议》（ABA）的一部分，国际汽联成本上限管理机构承认威廉姆斯车队“以合作的态度和诚信的精神行事”；并没有指控威廉姆斯车队有意寻求违反成本上限。

    国际汽联认定，威廉姆斯车队的违规源于对规则的误解。

    该车队的超支是由于程序性违规，即未能提交准确的财务报告。由于威廉姆斯在成本排除和调整方式上的错误，导致其超支。

    作为处罚的一部分，该车队必须在裁决后30天内向国际汽联支付罚款（如未按时支付，则需支付利息）。

    该车队还将从《阿布扎比协议》签署之日起的12个月内（至2027年11月23日），被扣减1.5%的风洞测试时间和计算流体力学（CFD）测试时间。

    该车队于周三（11月25日）发布声明，承认了上述违规行为。

    声明称：“此次违规涉及我们为调整公司结构而产生的行政成本。

    “我们曾认为这些成本不在预算帽范围内。经与国际汽联（FIA）沟通后，我们已同意按照他们的解释解决此事，即这些成本本应计入预算帽。”

    国际汽联已注意到威廉姆斯并未获得任何体育或经济优势，我们也从未寻求过此类优势。国际汽联还注意到，我们在整个审查过程中始终以合作和善意的态度行事。

    诚信是威廉姆斯立身之本的核心，我们现在可以了结此案，继续专注于我们的未来。

    我们一直是并且将继续是成本上限的坚定支持者，而成本上限自然需要准确的监管才能有效。

    成本上限管理机构认定，与威廉姆斯一样，阿尔派恩以善意态度予以配合，并未寻求违反成本上限，也未从中寻求任何优势。

    阿尔派 的违规属于程序性违规，原因是其 2025 年报告中的“不准确之处”。

    该车队承认，它未计入与营销活动相关的成本、非 F1 活动成本、归属于人力资源活动的成本以及员工奖金成本。

    此外，该车队在获得税收抵免或财务收入后，未对成本作出充分调整，也未就库存作出充分调整。

    阿尔派 必须在 12 月 12 日前支付一笔财务罚款，因为它已于 11 月 11 日签署了一份 协议。

    阿尔派 在11月25日发布声明称，尊重“成本上限管理部门的调查结果”。

    “车队接受其在不知情的情况下违反了程序性规定，这是由于全年报告中与豁免和调整相关的成本文件存在不准确之处。”

    声明补充说：“报告中的错误和不准确之处具体涉及非体育和运营成本。”随后详细列出了上述自身违规情况。

    声明继续写道：“车队还要感谢成本上限管理部门和国际汽联在整个过程中的勤勉与理解。”

    “财务规则仍然相对较新、不断演变且复杂，其中一些豁免和调整项可由各车队自行解读。车队认可其在整个业务范围内需要做出的集体努力，并认识到未来可以在哪些方面进行改进。”

    英文原文

    The Williams and Alpine Formula 1 teams both breached the FIA's cost cap rules in 2025.

    Williams committed a minor spending breach as well as a procedural breach, while Alpine was found to have committed a procedural breach only.

    Both teams face financial penalties, and Williams also faces a sporting penalty.

    All five power unit manufacturers were found to be in compliance for 2025.

    In 2025, the cost cap was $135million for teams and $130m for engine manufacturers, plus inflation.

    Williams's breach was a "minor overspend breach", which is less than 5% of the cost cap for 2025.

    The team was found to have breached the cost cap by £627,000 or 0.48% of the cost cap.

    The team entered an ABA - accepted breach agreement - with the FIA on November 23.

    As part of the ABA, the FIA's cost cap administration acknowledged that Williams "acted cooperatively and in a spirit of good faith"; there is no accusation that Williams sought to breach the cost cap willingly.

    The FIA found that Williams's breach arose from a misinterpretation of the rules.

    The team's overspend was due to a procedural breach in which it failed to file accurate financial reports. As a result of errors in how Williams excluded and adjusted its costs, it overspent.

    As part of its punishment, the team will have to pay a financial penalty to the FIA within 30 days of the decision (with interest due if it doesn't do so on time).

    The team will also receive a 1.5% subtraction from its windtunnel testing time and CFD testing time for 12 months from the date of the ABA being executed (until November 23, 2027).

    The team released a statement on Wednesday (November 25) that acknowledged the above breaches.

    It said: "The breach relates to administrative costs we incurred making changes to our company structure.

    "We believed these costs fell outside of the cap. Following discussions with the FIA, we have agreed to resolve the matter in line with their interpretation that the costs should have been in cap.

    "The FIA has noted that Williams did not gain any sporting or financial advantage, and nor did we ever seek to. It has also noted that we acted cooperatively and in good faith throughout the review process.

    "Integrity is central to what Williams stands for and we can now close this case and remain focused on our future.

    "We have always been and will continue to be strong supporters of the cost cap, which naturally requires accurate policing to be effective."

    The cost cap administration found that, like Williams, Alpine had cooperated in good faith and did not seek to breach the cost cap, nor gain any advantage from doing so.

    Alpine's breach was procedural and due to "inaccuracies" in its reporting for 2025.

    The team accepted that it had excluded costs relating to its marketing activities, non-F1 activities, costs attributable to HR activities, and employee bonus costs.

    Furthermore, the team had not made sufficient adjustments to its costs after receiving tax credits or finance income, nor had it made sufficient adjustments regarding inventory.

    Alpine will have to pay a financial penalty by December 12 as it entered an ABA on November 11.

    Alpine released a statement on November 25 that said it respected "the findings of the cost cap administration".

    "The team accepts that it unknowingly committed a procedural breach, which is a result of inaccuracies in the full year reporting documentation for costs related to exclusions and adjustments.

    "The errors and inaccuracies in the reporting were specifically related to non-sporting and operational costs," it added, before detailing its own breaches as above.

    "The team would also like to express its thanks to both the CCA and FIA, for its diligence and understanding throughout the entire process," the statement continued.

    "The financial regulations are still relatively new, constantly evolving and complex, with some exclusions and adjustments being open to each team’s interpretation. The team acknowledges the collective efforts it needs to make across the business and recognises where improvements can be made in future."
