    马德里F1赛事是否提升了经济价值？

    马德里马德里赛道首届2026年西班牙F1大奖赛创造了5.44亿欧元的产值，支持了10948个工作岗位，并产生了9500万欧元的税收。马德里赛道是一个部分街道赛道，位于IFEMA会议中心周围，赛道布局将在2027年进行更改，以提高比赛的品质和超车的频率。

    2026年首届马德里赛道西班牙一级方程式大奖赛创造了5.44亿欧元的产值，支持了10,948个就业岗位，并为税收贡献了9500万欧元，据主办方透露。

    马德里赛道是一部分街道赛道，围绕着IFEMA会议中心建造，赛道布局将在2027年进行调整，以提高比赛的品质和超车的频率。

    大奖赛推广商进行了一项由普华永道（PwC）进行的调查，显示与活动直接相关的每1欧元的经济活动，在马德里更广泛的经济中产生了4.60欧元的收益。

    总经济影响比预期高出16.5%，大奖赛支持的就业岗位比预期多23.7%，总体税收贡献比预测多14.5%。

    与马德里2026年的54.4亿欧元经济活动相比，巴塞罗那大奖赛在2024年为其当地经济活动产生了超过3亿欧元的收益，据其自身报告显示。巴塞罗那赛道首席执行官波尔·吉尔伯特在最近赛道扩建公告中重申了这一点，他表示“一级方程式在每个赛事中为场地产生了超过3亿欧元的经济效益。”

    一级方程式首席执行官斯蒂法诺·多门卡利最近表示，奥斯汀的美国大奖赛在2026年为当地地区产生了“超过10亿美元”的收入，而拉斯维加斯大奖赛在其三届赛事中累计为当地地区产生了超过32亿美元的收入。

    马德里国际展览中心（IFEMA）主席何塞·维森特·德·洛斯·莫索斯表示，这些数据“证实了大奖赛自首届以来为马德里经济和国际形象带来的战略重要性，其产生的经济活动相当于马德里地区GDP的0.16%和马德里市GDP的0.30%。”

    大奖赛吸引了353,000名观众。调查显示，35%的游客来自海外，20%来自西班牙其他地区——这意味着45%的游客来自马德里及其周边地区。

    国际游客在其停留期间的人均花费为2,772欧元 - 与西班牙游客的1,099欧元和马德里居民的659欧元相比 - 这些数字包括估计的平均票价，但票价并未公布。

    因此，大奖赛的国际游客花费是马德里平均国际游客的1.7倍，是访问西班牙的国际游客的2.9倍。国内游客在马德里地区的旅行中花费是国内游客的3.8倍。

    当地数字出版物《Democrata》发现，大奖赛期间酒店价格平均为485欧元 - 比平时高出70%。

    大约49%的游客住在酒店，32%住在短期租赁住宿，19%与家人和朋友同住 - 产生了9,500万欧元的税收。

    近年来，短期租赁一直是当地的一个有争议的问题，因为2025年，西班牙政府打击Airbnb，在全国范围内删除了近66,000个房源，并对该公司非法房源罚款6400万欧元。

    此外，马德里赛道的当地居民抗议了其对环境和社会的负面影响，包括噪音问题。

    调查发现，95.3%的国际与会者表示大奖赛‘提升了马德里在国际上的形象’，98.1%的人会推荐马德里作为旅游目的地，86%的与会者表示他们可能会再次回来。

    普华永道合伙人Jordi Esteve表示：“通过广泛的实地调查，我们发现大奖赛的游客比普通游客花费更多，停留时间更长。这有助于马德里吸引高价值旅游，产生更大的经济活动并支持就业。”

    普华永道在比赛周末的周五至周日之间进行了1,102次面对面调查。

    英文原文

    The inaugural 2026 Spanish Formula 1 Grand Prix at the Madring created €544 million in economic activity, supported 10,948 jobs, and generated €95m in tax revenue, according to its organiser.

    The Madring is a part-street circuit built around the IFEMA conference centre - with the track layout set to be changed for 2027 to improve the quality of racing and frequency of overtaking.

    The grand prix promoter ran a study conducted by PwC showing that, for every €1 of economic activity directly linked to the event, €4.60 was generated across Madrid’s wider economy.

    The total economic impact was 16.5% larger than expected, the grand prix supported 23.7% more jobs than anticipated, and the overall tax contribution was 14.5% more than predicted.

    In comparison to Madrid’s figure of €544m in economic activity for 2026, the Barcelona GP generated over €300m in local economic activity in 2024, according to its own reports. This was reaffirmed by Barcelona circuit CEO Pol Gilbert as part of the track’s recent extension announcement, where he said that “Formula 1 generates an economic impact of more than €300 million per edition” at the venue.

    F1 CEO Stefano Domenicali recently said the US GP at Austin generated “over $1 billion” for the local area in 2026, while the Las Vegas GP has generated over $3.2bn cumulatively for the local area over its three editions.

    IFEMA Madrid chairman Jose Vicente de los Mozos said that the figures “confirm the strategic importance the Grand Prix has gained for Madrid’s economy and international profile from its very first edition, generating economic activity equivalent to 0.16% of the Madrid Region’s GDP and 0.30% of the city of Madrid’s GDP”.

    The grand prix attracted 353,000 attendees. The survey estimates that 35% of visitors were from overseas and 20% travelling from elsewhere in Spain - meaning 45% were from Madrid and its immediate vicinity.

    International visitors spent an average of €2,772 per person over the course of their stay - compared with €1,099 from Spanish visitors and €659 among Madrid residents - with those figures including the estimated average ticket price, which is not stated.

    International visitors to the grand prix therefore spent 1.7 times that of an average international tourist to Madrid, and 2.9 times more than an international tourist visiting Spain. Domestic visitors spent 3.8 times more than a domestic tourist would on a trip to the Madrid region.

    Local digital publication Democrata found that hotel prices averaged €485 - 70% more than usual - for the grand prix.

    Around 49% of visitors stayed in hotels, 32% in short-term rental accommodation, and 19% with family and friends - generating €95m in tax revenues.

    Short-term rentals have been a contentious issue for the local area in recent years as, in 2025, the Spanish government cracked down on Airbnbs, removing nearly 66,000 listings across the country and fining the company €64m for illegal listings.

    Furthermore, residents local to the Madring protested its environmental and societal impact, including noise concerns.

    The survey found that 95.3% of international attendees said the grand prix had ‘enhanced Madrid’s international image’ and 98.1% would recommend Madrid as a tourist destination, with 86% of attendees saying they were likely to return.

    PwC partner Jordi Esteve said: “Through extensive fieldwork, we have found that grand prix visitors spend more and stay longer than the average visitor. This is helping Madrid attract high-value tourism, generating greater economic activity and supporting employment.”

    PwC conducted 1,102 face-to-face surveys between the Friday and Sunday of the race weekend.
