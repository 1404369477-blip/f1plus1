    Spanish GP: Lando Norris beats Kimi Antonelli to first pole position at Madring after thrilling qualifying finale

    Lando Norris edges Kimi Antonelli by 11 thousandths of a second to claim first Madring pole; Max Verstappen beats Lewis Hamilton to third; watch the Spanish Grand Prix from the all-new Madring circuit live on Sky Sports F1 at 2pm on Sunday, with build-up from 12.30pm

    Highlights from qualifying for the 2026 Spanish Grand Prix.

    Lando Norris produced a stunning lap to beat Kimi Antonelli to the first pole position at Formula 1's all-new Madring circuit in a thrilling Spanish Grand Prix Qualifying.

    The McLaren driver hadn't appeared to be in contention for pole at any point of the session, but found a huge amount of pace on his final run to edge out championship leader Antonelli by 11 thousandths of a second.

    Max Verstappen, who had surged into pole contention earlier in the session, edged out Lewis Hamilton for third after the Ferrari driver produced a strong recovery having crashed in final practice earlier on Saturday.

    The remarkable nature of Norris' lap was further highlighted by a near-half second gap to his team-mate Oscar Piastri in seventh.

    Norris said: "I'm shocked! A little bit surprised to be here now. My god, that was probably one of the best laps I've done in my career.

    "Very, very happy. Very proud, very happy for the team. Just one of those laps where everything came together.

    "I had a good idea of what I wanted to do. It was whether my brain let me do it on the last lap. Everything pretty much worked perfectly."

    With overtaking expected to prove difficult around the tight circuit, Norris has given himself a strong chance of claiming a third victory in four races and reducing a 96-point deficit to Antonelli.

    Despite losing out to Norris, Antonelli is well positioned to extend his 66-point championship lead over Russell and move closer to a maiden title, with nine rounds remaining after Sunday's race.

    Red Bull's Liam Lawson, who continues to deputise for the injured Isack Hadjar, was eighth, while Alpine's Franco Colapinto and Racing Bulls' Arvid Lindblad completed the top 10.

    A supreme collective display of driving saw the session pass without any of the red-flag stoppages that had been expected at the hugely challenging new circuit.

    Haas driver Oliver Bearman was unable to participate due to the heavy damage done to his car in a big crash in final practice, but there were only kisses rather than heavy contact with the barriers as the field pushed to the limit in qualifying.

    Ollie Bearman took a huge hit right at the end of P3 at the Spanish Grand Prix.

    With F1 returning to Madrid for the first time since 1981 as the capital city takes over from Barcelona as the Spanish Grand Prix host, the locals were left disappointed as both Williams' Carlos Sainz and Aston Martin's Fernando Alonso were knocked out in Q1, with the former demoted from 17th to 20th by a grid penalty for impeding.

    There had been high expectations for McLaren coming into the weekend given Norris had recorded back-to-back pole positions and race victories either side of the summer break at similar-style circuits before Antonelli won last weekend's Italian Grand Prix.

    However, that pace failed to materialise throughout practice, and Norris' personal challenge was severely heightened when he missed almost the entirety of Friday's second session because of a gearbox failure.

    Mercedes looked as though they may have it all their own way before Verstappen flashed strong pace in Q2 to suggest he was capable of contending for pole.

    Then, Ferrari, who had started the weekend strongly, suddenly rediscovered their pace as Hamilton took provisional pole after the first runs in Q3.

    A thrilling finale was in store, and it begun with Antonelli displacing Hamilton from pole, before the Brit improved but failed to reclaim top spot.

    Attention then turned to Norris and Verstappen, who were both lighting up the timesheet with fast sector times.

    Norris had a massive slide in the final corner that almost took him into the barrier. It cost him around two to three tenths of a second, but he still edged out Antonelli.

    Verstappen produced the fastest middle sector of the session but couldn't keep up his speed in the final part of the lap and had to settle for third.
