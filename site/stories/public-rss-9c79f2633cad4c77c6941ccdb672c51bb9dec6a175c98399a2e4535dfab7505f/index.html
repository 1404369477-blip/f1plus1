    维斯塔潘拉斯维加斯多次夺回领先夺冠

    2026年拉斯维加斯大奖赛出现35次领先易手，其中17次由马克斯·维斯塔潘重新夺回领先，多数对手是乔治·拉塞尔，维斯塔潘最终赢下这场被他视为复仇之战的胜利。

    昨晚谁睡得最好：马克斯·维斯塔潘

    在马克斯·维斯塔潘于一级方程式赛车中赢下的所有比赛里，这一场他一定会记住。因为这不只是又一场胜利，而是一场复仇。

    他们说，2026年拉斯维加斯大奖赛出现了35次领先位置的超越。这听起来可信，但甚至不值得去核实。看过这场比赛的人都知道，领先易手非常频繁。据称其中17次是马克斯·维斯塔潘重新夺回领先，而绝大多数对手是乔治·拉塞尔。

    所以，尽管这位荷兰车手并不太喜欢领先位置来回易手的比赛，但可以肯定，他在短短一个多小时里数十次智胜拉塞尔，对此十分享受——不仅因为他们在争夺比赛领先，还因为他的对手是谁。

    这当然不是从第三次练习赛的冲突才开始的。拉塞尔已经烦扰维斯塔潘有一段时间了，他在媒体上多次发起攻击，其中最不留情的一次出现在2024年阿布扎比大奖赛，当时他说自己已经对马克斯失去了尊重，并称他是个霸凌者。

    你可以说乔治只是说出了别人不敢说的话——甚至可以说他敢于坚持立场是勇敢的。但对维斯塔潘来说，以他的性格，这几乎只会让他更加被激怒，无论拉塞尔或其他人怎么看待他的比赛方式。

    但那次第三次练习赛的冲突也激怒了拉塞尔——他又在媒体上全力开火。有趣的是，这并非发生在排位赛后的新闻发布会上，尽管他和维斯塔潘都出席了那场发布会。在那里，坐在荷兰人旁边的沙发上，乔治被问及对所发生之事的看法，他只说自己认为“这很清楚”。

    但随后，当车手们来到电视采访区时，他在接受天空体育意大利台采访时释放了自己的怒火。

    这位英国车手对天空体育意大利表示：“规则里没有任何条款说不能超越正在开始飞驰圈的人，但当你这么做时，你就是在坑这个人。他每周都和许多车手这样干，大家都接受了，而我不会就这样接受有人这样坑我，还像自己比所有人都更大更强一样四处走动。总得有人站出来对抗那家伙。”

    维斯塔潘大约在同一时间对媒体发言，并不特别想卷入这场口水战，只是表示他根本不在乎：“我甚至不想在那上面浪费时间。对我来说完全不值得。”

    马克斯·维斯塔潘，红牛车队，乔治·拉塞尔，梅赛德斯

    那究竟是因为他当时没听到拉塞尔说了什么，还是单纯想淡化处理，就由大家自行判断了。但他肯定确切发现了乔治选择了什么话来表达自己的感受——或许尤其是因为他那总在阅读的父亲乔斯当时也在赛道现场。

    而且，正如维斯塔潘一贯的风格，他决心首先在赛道上给出回应。场景也堪称完美，拉塞尔与他共享头排发车位。

    这是一场精彩的较量。有人会说引擎算法在很大程度上决定了战局走向，但一次又一次，当拉塞尔设法超越维斯塔潘时，这位荷兰车手总知道如何动用一切手段立即夺回领先，甚至从未让对手领跑超过十几秒——要么在起终点直道上重新夺回领先，要么通过一连串的开场弯角完成反超。

    无论有没有算法，这显然需要技巧。即便维斯塔潘并不喜欢2026年的规则，这场比赛无疑也证明了他在这种类型的竞速中同样强于拉塞尔。哪怕它更像印地赛车椭圆赛道的缠斗，而非“常规”的一级方程式。

    他清楚地知道如何回应拉塞尔的每一次进攻——然后在对手于比赛还剩两圈时冲出赛道后，他笑了。

    “因果报应，真是活该，”他在新闻发布会上说道。但在电视采访区，他显然很享受那些与拉塞尔相关的问题，尽管他不得不重复四五遍。面对递到面前的每一个不同颜色的麦克风，他似乎同样乐于给出越来越犀利的回答。

    “我确实很享受今天有一个人完全没有任何机会，”他说。“他显然在每个弯角都被击败，但他也活该如此。”

    他坚称，自己并没有因为又一次又一次超越的是拉塞尔而获得额外动力，但他的回答表明他完全享受这场复仇。

    “不，”他在Viaplay采访中对荷兰同胞说。“你知道为什么吗？因为他远未达到我的水平，所以那样的评论根本不会激励我。”

    “但这正是他的典型作风：他在赛道外口无遮拦，而在赛道上，整个赛季对他来说都是一场彻底的灾难，尤其是开着那辆车。所以这完全吻合，今天再次如此，简直就是完美的因果报应。”

    两年前在阿布扎比，拉塞尔曾说马克斯“带着不必要的愤怒和近乎暴力的情绪发泄”。这个周末，这位荷兰人几乎没有反驳这一评价——但他的回应已经尽可能强硬，无论是在赛道上还是赛道外。

    维斯塔潘仍然不喜欢混合动力引擎，也不喜欢那种来回拉锯的比赛，他在骨子里依然是个纯粹主义者。但如果要从整个规则时代中挑出一场他格外愿意回味的胜利，那将是2026年的拉斯维加斯。

    不仅因为他击败了谁，更因为他如何做到。

    Who slept best last night: Max Verstappen

    Of all the races Max Verstappen has won in Formula 1, this is one he'll definitely remember. Because it wasn't just another victory. It was one of revenge

    There were 35 overtakes for the lead, they say, in the 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix. Which sounds believable, but isn't even worth checking. Everyone who watched it knows there were a lot. Apparently, 17 of those involved Max Verstappen reclaiming the lead. And the vast majority were against George Russell.

    So even though the Dutchman doesn't enjoy yo-yo racing that much, you can be sure he thoroughly enjoyed outsmarting Russell on dozens of occasions in the space of just over an hour – not just because they were fighting for the race lead, but because of who he was fighting against.

    It certainly didn't start with the FP3 clash. Russell has been annoying Verstappen for a while, with several attacks in the media, the most uncompromising of which came at the 2024 Abu Dhabi Grand Prix, when he said he'd lost respect for Max and called him a bully.

    You could argue that George was simply saying things others were afraid to say – and that it was even brave of him to stand his ground. But for Verstappen, being the way he is, it could hardly have done anything other than fuel him even more, regardless of what Russell or anyone else thinks about the way he races.

    But that FP3 clash triggered Russell, too – and he went all out in the media again. Interestingly, it wasn't at the post-qualifying press conference, which he and Verstappen both attended. There, sitting on the couch next to the Dutchman himself, George was asked for his view of what had happened and simply said he thought "it was pretty clear".

    But he then unleashed his anger in an interview with Sky Sport Italy when the drivers reached the TV pen.

    "There's nothing in the rules to say you cannot overtake someone who is starting the lap, but when you do this, you are screwing this person over," the Briton told Sky Sport Italy. "He does this week in week out with many drivers and everybody accepts it, and I'm not just going to accept somebody to screw me over like this and walk around like they're bigger and better than everyone else. Somebody's got to stand up to that guy."

    Verstappen, speaking to the media around the same time, wasn't particularly keen to engage in a war of words, simply stating that he didn't even care: "I don't even want to waste my time on that. It's not worth it to me at all."

    Max Verstappen, Red Bull Racing, George Russell, Mercedes

    Whether that was because he hadn't heard what Russell had said at the time or was simply trying to play it down is for everyone to judge. But he surely discovered exactly what words George had chosen to express his feelings – perhaps not least because his father Jos, who's always reading, was at the track, too.

    And, as usual for Verstappen, he was determined to deliver his response on the track first and foremost. The setting was perfect, too, with Russell sharing the front row of the grid.

    It was a good fight. Some would argue that engine algorithms played a big part in how it unfolded, but time after time, when Russell managed to find his way past Verstappen, the Dutchman knew how to use every tool available to get back ahead immediately, never even allowing his rival to lead for more than a dozen seconds – either reclaiming the lead on the start-finish straight or through the sequence of opening corners.

    Algorithms or not, it certainly required skill. And even if Verstappen doesn't enjoy the 2026 regulations, this was surely a race in which he demonstrated that he was better than Russell at this kind of racing, too. Even if it resembled IndyCar oval-style battling more than "normal" F1.

    He knew exactly how to answer each and every attack from Russell – and then had a laugh when his rival eventually went off two laps before the finish.

    "Karma is a bitch," was his line in the press conference. But in his TV pen interviews, he was clearly enjoying the Russell-related questions, even though he had to repeat himself four or five times. He seemed to take the same pleasure in delivering increasingly punchy answers to every different-coloured microphone put in front of him.

    "I did enjoy the fact that one particular person had absolutely no chance at all today," he said. "He was obviously getting beaten at every corner the whole time, but he deserves that too."

    He insisted he hadn't gained any extra motivation from the fact that it was Russell he was overtaking again and again, but his answers suggested he was thoroughly enjoying his revenge.

    "No," he told his fellow Dutchmen in the Viaplay interview. "And do you know why? Because he's nowhere near my level, so comments like that don't motivate me at all.

    "But this is typical of him: he has a big mouth off the track, but on the track this whole season has been a complete disaster for him, especially in that car. So it fits perfectly, and today again, it's just perfect karma."

    Two years ago in Abu Dhabi, Russell said Max "lashes out with unnecessary anger and borderline violence". The Dutchman did little to challenge that characterisation this weekend – but his response was as strong as it could have been, both on and off the track.

    Verstappen still doesn't like the hybrid engines or yo-yo racing, and remains a purist to his core. But if there's one victory from this entire regulation era that he'll particularly enjoy remembering, it will be Las Vegas 2026.

    Not just because of who he beat, but how he did it.
