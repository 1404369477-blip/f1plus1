    国际汽联确认F1引擎冻结并限制测试

    国际汽联单座赛车总监尼古拉斯·通巴西斯在接受Motorsport.com独家采访时透露，从2028赛季第13站起，现款F1动力单元的开发将被冻结，同时国际汽联对2030年引擎的台架测试施加限制，以避免两个项目重叠。

    国际汽联确认F1引擎冻结，并在V8回归前施加测试限制

    现款F1动力单元的开发将从2028赛季第13站起冻结。国际汽联还对2030年引擎的台架测试施加了限制，以防止任何重叠

    阿尔维德·林德布拉德，小红牛，安德烈亚·基米·安东内利，梅赛德斯，弗兰科·科拉平托，阿尔派

    国际汽联单座赛车总监尼古拉斯·通巴西斯在接受Motorsport.com独家采访时透露，从2028年中期起，F1制造商将不再被允许对现款动力单元进行研发。

    在拉斯维加斯大奖赛周末期间，Motorsport.com 首次披露了这项措施，其目的是防止制造商不得不同时开展两个项目，同时也防止某些制造商获得不理想的优势。出于同样的原因，在相同时间点之前，大部分关于即将推出的 V8 发动机的工作是被禁止的。

    在实践中，这意味着当前混合动力 V6 动力单元的开发将从 2028 赛季的第 13 场比赛起冻结。从那时起，只允许进行可靠性更新，不再允许进一步的性能升级。

    在此之前，对 2030 年使用的 2.1 升涡轮增压 V8 发动机的开发将实施限制。在 2027 年 10 月 1 日之前，F1 制造商不得进行单缸台架测试；而在 2028 年 7 月 1 日之前，不得进行完整动力单元——换言之，整个 V8——的台架测试。

    这些限制不适用于新进入者，这意味着通用汽车将被允许更早地为其凯迪拉克动力单元进行模拟工作。

    2028年7月1日这一日期大致与当年的第13场比赛重合，因此也与当前动力单元（power units）的引擎冻结（engine freeze）时间重合。通过这种方式，国际汽联（FIA）防止制造商不得不同时开展两个项目，否则将导致高昂成本并使研发竞赛失控。

    该管理机构还希望防止出现这样一种情况：某家制造商——例如本田（Honda）——在冻结生效时仍大幅落后。这与围场内被认为有必要的额外研发升级机会重新审视密切相关，不过据托姆巴齐斯（Tombazis）表示，这方面尚未做出最终决定。

    “我们一直专注于动力单元（PU）框架，坦率地说，我们在ADUO方面有些滞后，”托姆巴齐斯对Motorsport.com表示。

    “我们需要结合我们的意图以及与动力单元制造商讨论的内容来考虑这整个议题，即从某个时间点起冻结当前这一代引擎。那将是2028年中期。出于成本原因，我们希望避免的是他们同时开展两个并行项目。”

    引擎冻结还有另一个原因：如果没有这些限制，在当前规则下拥有极具竞争力动力单元的车队，将能够比那些落后、需要全力以赴提升现有引擎竞争力的车队更早地将重心转向2030年。

    “我们还想避免的是，当前某些动力单元制造商如果目前非常有竞争力，可能会获得显著优势。他们可以更早停止研发，而其他可能正在追赶的人则会处于劣势。

    “所以，我们有这个问题需要解决，而所有这些都与作为讨论点的ADUO有关。因此，我们需要暂停一下，仔细思考我们该怎么做。”

    引擎冻结已得到确认并与所有相关方达成一致，但下一步是确定ADUO的变更，并确保没有制造商在2028年中期仍面临巨大差距，而一旦冻结生效，这一差距将无法再弥补。

    “显然，我们需要在那之前尽可能提供更多趋同的机会。我们不希望某支车队被冻结规则后远远落后。”通巴西斯继续说道。

    “因此，我们有这些与空气动力学研发升级机会相关的议题需要处理，还有一些微妙的事项，我们需要在继续推进之前进行基本评估。”

    FIA confirms F1 engine freeze and imposes testing restrictions ahead of V8 return

    Development of the current F1 power units will be frozen from the 13th race of the 2028 season. The FIA has also imposed restrictions on dyno work on the 2030 engines to prevent any overlap

    Arvid Lindblad, Racing Bulls, Andrea Kimi Antonelli, Mercedes, Franco Colapinto, Alpine

    Formula 1 manufacturers will no longer be allowed to work on the current generation of power units from mid-2028, the FIA's single-seater director Nikolas Tombazis has revealed in an exclusive interview with Motorsport.com.

    First revealed by Motorsport.com over the Las Vegas Grand Prix weekend, the measure is intended to prevent manufacturers from having to work on two projects simultaneously and also to prevent some from gaining an undesirable advantage. For the same reasons, work on the upcoming V8 engines is largely prohibited until that same point in time.

    In practice, this means that development of the current hybrid V6 power units will be frozen from the 13th race of the 2028 season. From that point onwards, only reliability updates will be permitted, with no further performance upgrades allowed.

    Prior to that point, restrictions will be in place on the development of the 2.1-litre turbocharged V8 for 2030. Until 1 October 2027, F1 manufacturers will not be allowed to carry out dyno work with a single cylinder, while no dyno work with the full power unit – in other words, the entire V8 – will be permitted until 1 July 2028.

    These restrictions do not apply to newcomers, meaning General Motors will be allowed to carry out simulation work for their Cadillac power unit earlier.

    The date of 1 July 2028 roughly coincides with the 13th race of that year, and therefore with the engine freeze for the current power units. In this way, the FIA is preventing manufacturers from having to work on two projects simultaneously, which would result in high costs and a development race spiralling out of control.

    The governing body also wants to prevent a situation in which one manufacturer – such as Honda – is still significantly behind when the freeze comes into effect. This is closely linked to the ADUO rethink that is deemed necessary in the paddock, although according to Tombazis, no final decision has yet been made on that front.

    “We've been concentrating on the PU framework and, frankly speaking, we are a bit late on the ADUO side,” Tombazis told Motorsport.com.

    “We need to consider this whole topic in relation to our intention and what we've discussed with the PU manufacturers, so to freeze the current generation of engines from a certain point onwards. That will be mid-2028. What we want to avoid for cost reasons is that they have two parallel programs.”

    There is also another reason for the engine freeze: without these restrictions, a manufacturer with a highly competitive power unit under the current ruleset would be able to shift its focus to 2030 earlier than a manufacturer that is lagging behind and needs to pull out all the stops to make its current engine more competitive.

    “What we also want to avoid is that some of the current PU manufacturers may get a significant advantage if they are very competitive at the moment. They can stop earlier and then others who may be chasing may have a disadvantage.

    “So, we have this topic to address and all of that is related to ADUO as a discussion point. And therefore, we need to just pause and think carefully of what we do.”

    The engine freeze has been confirmed and agreed with all parties involved, but the next step is to establish the changes to ADUO and ensure that no manufacturer is still facing a huge deficit in mid-2028 that can no longer be made up once the freeze comes into effect.

    “Clearly, we need to give the opportunity for as much convergence as possible before that time. We don't want somebody to be frozen and to be way down,” Tombazis continued.

    “So, we have these topics to address which are related to ADUO and there are some delicate matters that we need to basically assess before we move forward.”
