    阿隆索报告引擎故障，本田未发现异常

    阿隆索在拉斯维加斯大奖赛最后一圈通过无线电报告引擎故障并退赛，但引擎供应商本田表示数据中未发现任何问题。

    为什么阿隆索在拉斯维加斯报告“引擎坏了”——但本田找不到问题

    阿隆索在拉斯维加斯因疑似动力单元问题退赛，但引擎供应商本田表示数据中看不到任何异常

    在F1拉斯维加斯大奖赛的最后一圈，阿隆索结束了比赛。这位两届世界冠军在长直道末端锁死轮胎，随后通过无线电告诉车队：“进站，进站，引擎坏了。”

    赛后在接受媒体采访时，这位西班牙车手重申他的阿斯顿·马丁赛车动力单元存在问题：“我觉得引擎从比赛一开始就动力不足。我们在排位赛中就已经看到引擎动力不足，而且我认为整场比赛都是如此。”

    “所以我在直道上损失了很多时间，不仅是对其他车手，对兰斯也是如此。我觉得最后两圈引擎完全放弃了。”

    在随后的几天里，引擎供应商本田检查了所有数据，但值得注意的是，首席工程师折原慎太郎在卡塔尔透露，没有发现任何问题。

    “周末过后，我们已经检查了目前掌握的所有数据，也在维护中对引擎进行了物理检查。目前，从数据和维护检查来看，我们还没有发现引擎有任何问题，但同样，我们将在周五检查引擎在这条赛道上的工作状况，”折原周四对包括Motorsport.com在内的媒体表示。

    后者不仅是为了观察动力单元在卢赛尔国际赛道——一种完全不同类型的赛道——上的表现，也是为了从阿隆索本人那里获得更多反馈，以确定这台特定的动力单元是否仍然适合使用，或者是否需要进行引擎更换。

    “是的，我们仔细分析了他的评论，我们也尊重他的意见，”折原继续说道。“这就是为什么我们检查了所有数据，也检查了发动机状况。从数据来看，目前我们还没有发现任何问题，但正如我所说，我们将在周五再次检查，以获取费尔南多的反馈。”

    本田赛车公司赛道总经理兼首席工程师折原慎太郎

    折原补充说，车手很难准确感受到拉斯维加斯发生的事情，正是因为这条赛道对动力极其敏感且能量匮乏。

    “在拉斯维加斯，很难分析发动机性能如何影响赛车速度，因为存在很大的扭矩效应，而且两辆车的能量管理也不同。我们的指标显示动力本身没有问题，但我们可能应该在这条赛道上再次仔细检查。”

    阿斯顿·马丁首席赛道官迈克·克拉克强调了情况的复杂性，并解释说阿隆索所感受到的糟糕感觉——这让他认为存在动力单元问题——也可能与MGU-K的部署以及该领域的困难有关。

    “我认为我们需要谨慎，因为MGU-K部分要高得多。这意味着一旦MGU-K[部署]出现微小变化，就可能被感觉为内燃机的问题。而且不同位置的MGU-K功率不同。所以很多时候，车手判断起来比过去困难得多，我认为部分原因就来自那里。”

    尽管部分困惑可能确实源于围绕MGU-K部署的不确定性，但折原承认，另一部分可能仅仅来自极度的沮丧。阿隆索和兰斯·斯特罗尔都强调，他们每圈纯粹因为动力不足而损失两秒，这意味着，根据折原的说法，阿隆索的退赛也可能与那种沮丧有关。

    “请问费尔南多。从数据角度看，我们没有看到任何问题，但可能今年我们的引擎性能不足以让车手感到满意或自信，然后可能他们感觉到一些，怎么说，感觉引擎性能不佳。”

    斯特罗尔在卡塔尔的媒体日上再次表达了这种沮丧情绪，表示在这种情况下，阿斯顿·马丁车手甚至无法展开竞争，尤其是在拉斯维加斯这样的赛道上。

    “我们知道赛车需要改进的地方，也知道底盘方面必须提升什么，但去拉斯维加斯这样的赛道比赛，光引擎就落后两秒，我们实在做不了太多。我们只是需要更多动力，希望明年能得到。”

    斯特罗尔对阿斯顿·马丁在2027年底盘方面取得进展持乐观态度，但立即指出，除非动力单元大幅提升，否则这种进展价值不大。

    “银石工厂的每个人都在全力以赴实现目标。然后，就像我说的，引擎方面，无论底盘多好，如果在长直道上缺少两秒的动力，那几乎不可能竞争。这就是我们目前的处境，所以我们真的希望明年能获得更多动力。”

    Why Fernando Alonso reported a "broken engine" in Las Vegas – but Honda can’t find an issue

    Alonso retired in Las Vegas due to a suspected power unit problem, but engine supplier Honda says it cannot see anything in the data

    On his final lap of the Formula 1 Las Vegas Grand Prix, Fernando Alonso called it a day. The two-time world champion locked up at the end of the long straight before telling the team over the radio: “Box, box, broken engine.”

    In the media pen afterwards, the Spaniard reiterated that something was wrong with the power unit in his Aston Martin: “The engine was down from the beginning of the race, I think. We saw already in qualifying that the engine was down and I think it was down the whole race as well.

    “So I was losing a lot of time on the straights, not only to the rest of the field but also to Lance. I think in the last two laps the engine completely gave up.”

    In the days that followed, engine supplier Honda examined all the data, but remarkably, chief engineer Shintaro Orihara revealed in Qatar that no problem had been identified.

    “After the weekend, we have checked all the data that we have now and also in the maintenance we have checked the engine physically. At this moment, we haven't found any issue on the engine from the data and also from the maintenance check, but again, we will check on Friday to see how the engine works at this track,” Orihara told media including Motorsport.com on Thursday.

    The latter is not only to see how the power unit performs at the Lusail International Circuit – a completely different type of track – but also to obtain more feedback from Alonso himself to determine whether this particular unit is still fit for use or whether an engine change is needed.

    “Yes, we carefully analysed his comments and we also respected his comments,” Orihara continued. “That's why we checked all the data and why we checked the engine condition. From the data, at this moment, we haven't seen any issue, but as I mentioned, we will check again on Friday to get some feedback from Fernando.”

    Shintaro Orihara, Trackside General Manager and Chief Engineer of Honda Racing Corporation

    Orihara added that it was difficult for the drivers to feel exactly what was happening in Las Vegas, precisely because the circuit was extremely power-sensitive and energy-poor.

    “In Las Vegas, it was difficult to analyse how engine performance affected car speed, because there was a lot of torque effect and also different energy management across the two cars. Our metrics say that the power itself is okay, but we should probably check carefully at this track again.”

    Aston Martin chief trackside officer Mike Krack underlined the complexity of the situation and explained that the poor feeling Alonso experienced – which led him to believe there was a power unit problem – could also be related to MGU-K deployment and difficulties in that area.

    “I think we need to be careful because the MGU-K part is so much higher. And that means that as soon as you have a small variation in MGU-K [deployment], it could be felt like a problem with the internal combustion engine. And you have different MGU-K power in different places. So very often, it's much more difficult for drivers to judge than it used to be in the past, and I think some of it is coming from there.”

    Although some of the confusion may indeed stem from uncertainty surrounding MGU-K deployment, Orihara acknowledged that another part of it could simply come from sheer frustration. Both Alonso and Lance Stroll stressed that they were losing two seconds per lap purely due to a lack of power, meaning that, according to Orihara, Alonso's retirement could also be related to that frustration.

    “Please ask Fernando. From the data viewpoint, we haven't seen any issue, but probably this year our engine performance is not enough to give satisfaction or confidence to the driver and then probably they feel some, how to say, feeling that the engine performance is not great.”

    Stroll once again expressed that frustration during media day in Qatar, stating that under these circumstances the Aston Martin drivers cannot even put up a fight, particularly at a circuit such as Las Vegas.

    “We know the areas we're going to work on with the car, and what we have to improve on the chassis side, but going to races like Vegas and missing like two seconds with just the engine, there's really not much we can do. We just need a lot more power, and hopefully we get that next year.”

    Stroll is optimistic that Aston Martin will make progress on the chassis side for 2027, but immediately pointed out that such progress will be worth little unless the power unit improves considerably.

    “Everyone at Silverstone is pushing flat out to hit our targets. And then, like I said, the engine, it doesn't matter how good of a chassis you have, if you're missing two seconds of power on the long straights. I mean, then it's going to be almost impossible to compete. That's where we are, so we really hope we get a lot more power next year.”
