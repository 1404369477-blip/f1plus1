    F1的平衡艺术：比你想的做得更好

    Stefano Domenicali在拉斯维加斯描述了Bernie Ecclestone为F1所做的一切，他将其比作在沙漠中建造拉斯维加斯。Bernie建立了大奖赛，并围绕它们建立了一个对技术、赛车和全球旅行充满热情的社区。

    “建造一个原本不存在的东西。”

    这就是斯蒂法诺·多梅尼卡利对伯尼·埃克莱斯顿为一级方程式所做贡献的描述。他在拉斯维加斯中间说出了这句话。那里有沙漠，然后他们建起了拉斯维加斯。

    这个比喻太强烈了。我不会让它浪费掉。

    他们围绕赌博建起了拉斯维加斯，这是一项非常好的生意。然后有人想到你可以让人们来看表演。你可以带孩子来。你可以在一个建立在赌博之上的城市里度过一周，而不需要在桌子上放筹码。而且如果你已经多年来看黑杰克牌桌，你的桌子还在那里。

    伯尼拥有大奖赛。正如斯蒂法诺所说，他围绕大奖赛建立的是‘一个对技术充满热情、对赛车充满热情、对环游世界充满热情的社区’。然后是资格赛：‘但这是一个只专注于汽车运动的社区。’

    超越这一点，简而言之，就是自由时代。给那些可能从未看过赛车的人一个来的理由。并确保对于那些已经连续多年前来的人，这次旅行仍然值得。

    当你面前有两个观众群体时，你如何做到正确的事情？这是自由在面对每一个决策时都要面对的问题。我在拉斯维加斯的Axios x The Race的‘超越极限：创新与领导力投资’活动上只处理了半小时，我很快意识到我将会让某个人失望。

    这是一个超过100位客人的相当排外的房间，主要是美国人，主要是C级高管。他们高兴地告诉我，他们是因为《生存之战》而来。一位父亲特别自豪地告诉我，他的14岁女儿说服他看F1。现在他认为他比她更是一个粉丝。

    我的爸爸带我进入了赛车运动。他的女儿又让他对它产生了兴趣。向伯尼解释这一点吧。

    房间里还有我们自己的斯科特·米切尔-马尔姆、埃德·斯特劳和格伦·弗里曼。他们是最高级别的专业一级方程式记者，几乎和最狂热的一级方程式粉丝一样狂热。

    那么我是应该推动斯蒂法诺在新的引擎上，还是询问托托·沃尔夫和克里斯蒂安·霍纳之间的关系究竟如何？深入了解詹姆斯·沃尔斯对成本上限的提议，或者询问下一部一级方程式电影？

    有一次我实际上对他说，'我现在感觉到的压力可能就是你曾经感觉到的压力'。在我提到埃德和动力系统之前，斯蒂法诺就穿过房间提醒埃德，他很久以前就预测了这次引擎的改动。负责将一级方程式转变为娱乐品牌的人也很乐意和埃德讨论引擎。我们有一个完整的播客来证明这一点。

    这相当困难，不是吗？这种平衡的东西。

    前一天，我询问了自由媒体总裁兼首席执行官Derek Chang，在自由媒体时期F1有哪些改变：生存之战还是成本上限？

    您可以在我们即将在The Race上发布的完整独家视频中看到他关于F1和MotoGP各种话题的更多言论。

    所以当Stefano给了我同样的答案时，我又尝试了一次。

    “哪个更重要？”两个都重要。

    我尝试了第三次。

    如果他可以回到过去只保留一个，他会选择哪个？他从预算帽开始。有那么一瞬间我以为我让他同意了。然后他又提到了Netflix。又是两个都。”

    德雷克理解为什么车队在这两方面都需要说服。如果你是一位花费多年时间建立起给你在赛道上带来优势的预算的车队负责人，从你的角度看这个优势看起来有多‘不公平’？你有多愿意让Netflix的团队进入你的维修区？法拉利和梅赛德斯都缺席了第一系列。他们都签约参加了第二系列。

    自由媒体正确地处理了这些电话。但不要低估了查尔斯·凯里在还没有为最小型车队提供数十亿美元报价，以及有一个在拉斯维加斯看女儿通过电视节目变成一级方程式粉丝的CEO父亲之前，说服人们是多艰难的事情。

    现在，自由媒体经过艰苦的斗争才达成限薪协议，沃尔斯要求改变它。威廉姆斯车队试图赶上在限薪政策实施前就建立了更好设施的车队。沃尔夫希望进行改变，以帮助吸引年轻人加入车队（并留住他们）。所以即使当两个车队负责人意见一致时，他们也很希望斯蒂法诺做两件不同的事情。

    我离开时认为，德里克和斯蒂法诺处理这个几乎不可能的任务的方式，几乎和任何人一样好。我花了半小时试图在人们把我往不同方向拉扯中做到公正。他们每天都要这样做。

    如果你担心自由媒体是否关心一级方程式，我认为你可以从德里克判断他们是否找到了平衡中得到一些安慰：'我不会让11个车队负责人都满意。我不会让每个粉丝都满意。每天，我能否让他们保持兴趣，至少让他们认为我们在努力做正确的事情？我认为这可能就是目标。'

    英文原文

    "To build something that was not there."

    That was how Stefano Domenicali described what Bernie Ecclestone had done for Formula 1. And he said it in the middle of Las Vegas. There was desert. Then they built Vegas.

    The analogy is too strong. I'm not going to let it go to waste.

    They built Vegas around gambling and it was a very, very good business. Then someone worked out you could get people to come for a show. You could bring the kids. You could spend a week in a city built on gambling without putting a chip on a table. And if you'd been coming for years to play blackjack, your table was still there.

    Bernie had the grands prix. What he built around them, as Stefano put it, was "a community that was passionate about technology, passionate about racing, passionate about travelling around the world". Then came the qualification: "But it was a community that was just focused on motorsport, full stop."

    Building beyond that is the Liberty era in short. Give people who might never have watched a motor race a reason to come. And try to make sure it's also still worth the trip for the people who've been coming for years.

    So how do you do the right thing when you've got both audiences in front of you? That's a problem Liberty faces with every single decision they make. I only had to deal with it for half an hour at our Axios x The Race's Beyond the Race: Investments in Innovation and Leadership event in Vegas, and I quickly realised I was going to disappoint someone.

    It was a pretty exclusive room of more than 100 guests, mainly American, mainly C-suite. They happily told me they were there because of Drive to Survive. One dad was particularly proud to tell me his 14-year-old daughter had persuaded him to watch F1. Now he reckons he's a bigger fan than she is.

    My dad got me into racing. His daughter got him into it. Explain that to Bernie.

    Also in the room were our own Scott Mitchell-Malm, Edd Straw and Glenn Freeman. Professional F1 journalists of the highest order and about as hardcore as hardcore F1 fans get.

    So do I push Stefano on the new engines or ask what the relationship between Toto Wolff and Christian Horner is really like? Get into the detail of James Vowles's proposed changes to the cost cap or ask about the next F1 movie?

    At one point I actually said to him, "I'm now feeling the pressure you probably feel". I got as far as mentioning Edd and the powertrains before Stefano was calling across the room to remind Edd he'd predicted this engine change a long time ago. The bloke charged with turning F1 into an entertainment brand is also quite happy talking engines with Edd. We've got a whole podcast to prove it.

    It's quite difficult, isn't it? This balancing stuff.

    The day before, I'd asked Derek Chang, Liberty Media's president and CEO, which had done more for F1 under Liberty: Drive to Survive or the cost cap?

    You can see a lot more of what he said on a wide variety of F1 and MotoGP topics in our full exclusive video interview here on The Race soon.

    So when Stefano gave me the same answer, I had another go.

    "Which one's more important?" Still both.

    I tried a third time.

    If he could go back and keep just one, which would he keep? He started with the budget cap. For a second there I thought I'd got him. Then he got back to Netflix. Both again.

    Derek understood why the teams had taken some persuading on both fronts. If you're a team principal who's spent years building up a budget that gives you an advantage on track, how 'unfair' does that advantage look from your seat? And how keen are you to let a Netflix crew into your garage? Ferrari and Mercedes both sat out the first series. Both signed up for the second.

    Liberty got those calls right. But don't underestimate how tough it was for Chase Carey to persuade people before there were billion-dollar offers for the smallest teams and a CEO dad sitting in Vegas being turned into an F1 fan by his daughter via a TV show.

    Now, having fought that hard to get the cap agreed, Liberty has Vowles asking to change it. Williams is trying to catch up with teams that built better facilities before the cap came in. Wolff wants changes to help bring younger people into the teams (and keep them). So even when two team principals agree with each other, they'd quite like Stefano to do two different things.

    I came away thinking Derek and Stefano are handling an impossible task about as well as anyone could. I'd spent half an hour trying to do right by people pulling me in different directions. They get to do it every day.

    If you worry about whether Liberty cares about F1, I think you can take some reassurance from how Derek judges whether they've got the balance right: "I'm not going to make 11 team principals happy. I'm not going to make every fan happy. And every day, can I keep them interested and at least have them thinking we're trying to do the right thing as we go? I think that's probably the objective."
