    汉密尔顿：勒克莱尔比对手车手对我更狠

    汉密尔顿认为法拉利需要进一步沟通以明确交战规则，并认同勒克莱尔比对手车队的车手对他拼得更凶的说法。

    刘易斯·汉密尔顿支持勒克莱尔比F1对手车手对他拼得更凶的说法

    墨西哥F1站与队友查尔斯·勒克莱尔发生冲突一周后，刘易斯·汉密尔顿坚持认为需要进一步沟通，以避免伤害法拉利

    刘易斯·汉密尔顿坚持认为法拉利需要进一步沟通以明确交战规则，并支持勒克莱尔比对手车队的车手对他拼得更凶的说法。

    上周末墨西哥站第一圈，汉密尔顿与勒克莱尔发生碰撞，两人在4号弯双双冲出赛道，这最终给了马克斯·维斯塔潘绝佳的夺冠机会，也让法拉利错失了可能的1-2名胜利，两人最终分列第二和第四。

    汉密尔顿对勒克莱尔在首圈如此激烈地缠斗感到愤怒，而这位摩纳哥车手却在4号弯切弯后永久保持领先。勒克莱尔仅因获得持久优势而被罚五秒，减轻处罚因素使这一惩罚从10秒降至5秒。

    他们在墨西哥的较量进一步印证了勒克莱尔似乎比任何人都更激烈地与队友竞争的说法，此前在新加坡大奖赛中，勒克莱尔尽管完全处于杆位得主马克斯·维斯塔潘的内侧，却在起步时退让了。然而，勒克莱尔在随后奥斯汀的美国大奖赛中确实与这位荷兰车手激烈缠斗，在COTA的S弯外侧超越了红牛车手。

    当被问及勒克莱尔比其他车手更激烈地与他竞争这一看法是否也是汉密尔顿所注意到的，这位七届世界冠军回答道：“是的。我看到的和你看到的一样。在新加坡，他完全处于1号弯的内侧，他已经赢得了领先，然后他退让了。这确实不同。

    “但我的意思是，如果你看看队友们，和塞巴斯蒂安（维特尔）也是一样。和卡洛斯（塞恩斯）也是一样。

    情绪高涨之际，汉密尔顿要求进行对话以明确车队的交战规则，并警告车队将面临“大问题”。Motorsport.com了解到，车队领队弗雷德里克·瓦塞尔在认为墨西哥站的事件显示出对车队2000名员工缺乏尊重后，已分别与两位车手进行了简短交谈。

    但由于瓦塞尔在比赛间隙已返回欧洲，而勒克莱尔在女儿出生后与家人共度时光，汉密尔顿表示仍需进一步对话。

    当被Motorsport.com问及在冷静下来后是否对墨西哥站事件有了不同看法时，他说：“我自然感到沮丧，现在仍然沮丧。所以，需要进行对话。我们会在时间允许时进行这些对话。”

    “我们曾有机会为车队赢得胜利。车队今年在带来升级方面做得非常出色，我认为他们完成了令人难以置信的工作。在过去三四场比赛中，我们获得的积分比任何其他车队都多。我们本可以轻松实现一二名完赛，所以我们绝对必须努力确保这种情况不再发生。”

    汉密尔顿也因公开游说让队友受到比维修区停留五秒更重的处罚而受到批评，因为在超车极为困难的赛道上，切弯给勒克莱尔带来的优势远大于五秒罚时。

    但这位41岁的车手表示他“不会被噤声”。“我真的不在乎大家对我所说的话有什么感受。我坦率直白，想到什么就说什么，”他说。

    “我确实看到有人说我说的话是经过算计的，我想用这样一个事实来反驳：当你在比赛中时，你的心率大约在170。你以每小时200英里的速度驾驶。你根本没有时间去算计自己说的话，也没时间去想媒体会怎么写，你不会想这些。

    “当然，有时候你说了一些话，事后可能会后悔，但我不会，我永远不会为做真实、真诚的自己而道歉。我不会被噤声。我从来、从来没有被噤声过。没有人（车队）要求我不要说任何话，事情就是这样。”

    Lewis Hamilton backs claims Charles Leclerc races him harder than F1 rivals

    One week on from his Mexico Formula 1 clash with team-mate Charles Leclerc, Lewis Hamilton maintains further talks are needed to avoid hurting Ferrari

    Lewis Hamilton maintains Ferrari needs further talks to iron out its rules of engagement, backing claims Charles Leclerc races him harder than drivers from rival teams.

    Hamilton and Leclerc came to blows on lap one of last weekend's race in Mexico with both drivers going off the track at Turn 4, which eventually handed Max Verstappen a golden opportunity to snatch away the win and costing Ferrari a possible 1-2 victory, instead the duo finishing second and fourth.

    Hamilton was incensed by how hard Leclerc raced on the opening lap, only for the Monegasque to permanently stay ahead as he cut the track at Turn 4. Leclerc only received a five-second penalty for gaining a lasting advantage, with mitigation bringing that punishment down from 10 seconds.

    Their Mexico fight further fed suggestions that Leclerc appears to race his teammate harder than anyone else, having come after a Singapore Grand Prix in which Leclerc backed off at the start despite being fully on the inside of polesitter Max Verstappen. Leclerc did race the Dutchman hard at the subsequent US Grand Prix in Austin, however, overtaking the Red Bull man around the outside of COTA's Esses.

    When asked if the idea of Leclerc racing him harder than other drivers is something Hamilton has also noticed, the seven-time world champion replied: "Yes. I see what you see. In Singapore, he was fully on the inside into Turn 1, he had won the lead, and he backed off. It was definitely different.

    "But I mean, if you look at the team-mates, it was the same with Sebastian [Vettel]. It was the same with Carlos [Sainz].

    As emotions ran high, Hamilton demanded talks to clarify the team's rules of engagement and warned of "big problems" for the squad. Motorsport.com understands team principal Fred Vasseur has spoken briefly to both drivers, albeit separately, after feeling the events of Mexico showed a lack of respect towards the team's 2000 people staff.

    But with Vasseur having travelled back to Europe in between races and Leclerc spending time with his family following the birth of his daughter, Hamilton says further talks are still needed.

    "Naturally I was frustrated, and I'm still frustrated," he said when asked by Motorsport.com if he views the events of Mexico in a different light now they have had time to cool off. "So, conversations need to be had. We will have those when the time permits.

    "We had an opportunity to win for the team. The team has done such an amazing job in bringing upgrades this year and I think they've done an incredible job. We've scored more points than any other team in the last three or four races. We could have easily had a one-two, so we definitely have to work towards making sure that doesn't happen again."

    Hamilton drew criticism, too, for openly lobbying for his team-mate to get a bigger penalty than the five seconds he was held at his pitstop, as cutting the track handed Leclerc a much bigger advantage than that on a circuit where overtaking proved extremely difficult.

    But the 41-year-old said he "will not be silenced". "I don't really care how everyone feels about what I've said. I wear my heart on my sleeve, and I say how I feel in the moment," he said.

    "I did see something that someone was saying that the things that I'm saying are calculated, and I just want to combat that with the fact that when you're in the race, your heart rate is like 170. You're driving 200 miles an hour. You don't have time to be calculated in what you're saying and thinking about what the media are going to write about, you don't think about that.

    "Of course, there's some times you say things and maybe you might regret it afterwards, but I don't, and I'm never going to apologise for being authentic and real. I'm not gonna be silenced. I've never ever been silenced. I have not been asked to not say anything [by the team], and that's just the way it is."
