    卡塔尔站轮胎问题加剧：15圈即达100%磨损

    倍耐力在卡塔尔大奖赛周五练习后评估是否需要采取更多预防措施，因为最受考验的左前胎在仅15圈后局部磨损已达100%。

    尽管已采用本赛季最高的前轮胎压并设定了最大使用圈数上限，倍耐力仍在评估卡塔尔大奖赛是否需要采取更多预防措施，因为周五练习带来了“一个又一个意外”。

    卢赛尔赛道本就是本赛季对轮胎最具挑战性的赛道，但首日赛道运行的数据显示，颗粒化、磨损和性能衰退的程度都高于赛前预期。

    据怀疑，这是由于赛道表面风化程度更高，以及2026款赛车下压力降低导致滑动。

    在这个每套轮胎设定27圈最大使用圈数、并被广泛讨论的周末，倍耐力已经看到证据：最受考验的左前胎在周五仅运行15圈后，局部区域的磨损就达到了100%。

    倍耐力首席工程师西蒙·贝拉表示：“这条赛道每次他们来到这里时给我们带来的惊喜都相当令人印象深刻。”

    三种配方轮胎都出现了颗粒化现象——其中在C2和C3配方上尤为严重。这加速了轮胎两个特定区域的磨损——靠近内侧胎肩处，以及最外侧胎肩处。

    而正是在这些特定区域，早期分析指出，在练习赛中仅经过15圈高油量运行后，一些磨损指标就已达到100%的水平。

    这些因素也导致卡塔尔出现了过去未曾见过的热衰减退化水平——每圈高达0.2秒——这将对策略产生影响，因为车手们将无法像过去那样在长距离阶段（stint）中持续施压。

    除此之外，倍耐力还在周五练习赛结束后收回的轮胎橡胶中发现了被碎石割伤的痕迹。

    贝拉表示：“我们看到其中一些割痕已经相当深地进入轮胎结构。也因此出现了一次红旗，在这条赛道上，这显然是一个额外的变量，可能在爆胎方面引起我们的关注。”

    去年周五练习赛后发现碎石割伤，促使倍耐力在周六运行前提高了胎压——目前也不排除会再次采取同样的应对措施。

    “我们正在评估所有变量，”他说，“如有必要，我们会提高胎压。”

    目前前轮胎压维持在周末前设定的29psi，这是有史以来进入一场赛事时的最高值，也与倍耐力去年在卡塔尔从周六起最终确定的水平相同。

    如果倍耐力选择进一步抬高胎压，那么这将成为F1现代时期以来的最高胎压。

    然而，贝拉很清楚，提高胎压很可能会进一步减小轮胎的接地面积，从而可能进一步加剧颗粒化。

    “这就是为什么如果可能的话，我不会提高胎压，”他说。“但如果出于完整性原因我们必须这么做，显然我们会进行评估。”

    倍耐力针对轮胎极端磨损调查结果正在考虑的另一项应对措施，是将目前的最长单次进站行驶圈数从27圈缩短。

    贝拉表示，分析将在倍耐力的实验室中对周五使用过的轮胎进行，以查看是否存在任何完整性方面的担忧。

    “我们会检查它们，切开它们，显然如果没有完整性问题，我们无论如何都会保留27圈，因为我们知道正赛中的颗粒化程度会降低，车队会做更多的管理来延长单次进站行驶圈数。”

    “这是一项正在我们实验室进行的分析，通过我们通常的规则审查，我们将了解是否继续保留27圈。”

    即使情况处于边缘状态，也未必需要缩短每段里程，因为在轮胎性能衰退起作用的情况下，倍耐力的分析是，车队跑最大距离根本没有意义，因为那会太慢。

    相反，由于比赛距离为57圈，车队将里程分成三段各19圈要合理得多。

    然而，车队可能会根据安全车出动的时机被迫调整安排。因此，即使他们进入比赛时并不打算将轮胎用到最大距离，如果安全车时机恰好如此，他们也可能被诱惑尝试勉强跑完最后27圈的一段。

    不过，贝拉认为，所看到的衰退水平意味着，从速度上看，跑27圈没有意义——而提前出动安全车更可能让比赛变成三次进站。

    英文原文

    Despite the highest front tyre pressures of the season and a maximum stint length already in place, Pirelli is evaluating if further precautions are needed for the Qatar Grand Prix after Friday practice served up “surprise after surprise.”

    The Lusail circuit is already the most challenging of the season for tyres, but the evidence from the first day of track running has shown graining, wear and degradation to be at higher levels than anticipated pre-event.

    It is suspected this has been caused by a more weathered track surface and the reduced downforce levels of the 2026 cars that are causing sliding.

    On a weekend where there is a much-talked-about 27-lap maximum stint length laid down for each set of tyres, Pirelli has already seen evidence of the most abused front-left sets hitting 100% wear in localised areas after just 15 laps of running on Friday.

    Pirelli’s chief engineer Simone Berra said: “It's quite impressive the surprises that this circuit gives to us every time they come here.”

    Graining has been encountered on all three compounds - with it particularly aggressive on the C2 and C3. This is accelerating the wear in two specific areas of the tyre - near the inside shoulder and then on the very outside shoulder.

    And it is in these specific areas where early analysis has pointed to some wear indicators having hit the 100% level after just 15 laps of high-fuel running in practice.

    These elements are also contributing to Qatar producing levels of thermal decay degradation not seen in the past - up to 0.2 seconds per lap - which is going to have an impact on strategies as drivers will not be able to keep pushing on with stints as they have in the past.

    On top of all this, Pirelli has also found evidence of gravel cuts in rubber that was returned to it after Friday practice.

    Berra said: “We have seen some of them quite deep into the construction. There was also a red flag due to this, and it's something that obviously in a circuit like this, it's an additional variable that can create some attention from our side in terms of punctures.”

    Gravel stone cuts discovered after Friday practice last year prompted Pirelli to increase tyre pressures ahead of Saturday running - and a repeat response has not been ruled out right now.

    “We are evaluating all the variables,” he said. “If necessary, we will increase the pressure.”

    Front pressures are currently at the 29psi set before the weekend, which is the highest they have ever been heading into an event and at the same level Pirelli settled on from Saturday onwards in Qatar last year.

    If Pirelli chooses to raise the pressures any more, then they will be the highest they have been in the modern era of F1.

    However, Berra is well aware that raising pressures will likely serve to reduce the contact patch of the tyres even more which could potentially increase graining even more.

    “That's why I wouldn't increase the pressure if possible,” he said. “But if we have to for integrity reasons, obviously we are going to evaluate it.”

    The other action Pirelli is considering in response to the findings about extreme wear of the tyres is reducing the maximum stint length from its current 27 laps.

    Berra said that analysis would take place in Pirelli’s laboratory on the tyres used on Friday to see if there are any concerns about their integrity.

    “We are going to check them, to section them, and obviously if there is no integrity issue, we will keep the 27 laps in any case because we know that graining level will reduce for the race, and the teams will do much more management to extend the stint length.

    “It's an analysis that is ongoing in our laboratory and, with our usual prescription review, we are going to understand if we continue with 27 or not.”

    Even if the situation is a marginal one, reducing the stint length may not necessarily be needed because, with degradation in play, Pirelli’s analysis is that it would make no sense for teams to run the maximum distance anyway as it would be too slow.

    Instead, with the race distance being 57 laps it is much more logical for teams to split their stints in three equal ones of 19 laps.

    However, teams could be forced to shuffle things around based on the timing of safety cars. So even if they headed into the race not intending to run tyres to the maximum distance, they could be tempted to trying to eke out a final 27-lap stint if safety car timing fell that way.

    Berra thinks however that the degradation levels seen mean that pace-wise it would not make sense to run for 27 laps - and early safety cars would more likely turn the race into a three-stopper.
