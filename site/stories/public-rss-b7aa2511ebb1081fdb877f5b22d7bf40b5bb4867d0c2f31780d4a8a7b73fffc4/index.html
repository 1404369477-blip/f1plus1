    “显然，你不能排除基米，”拉塞尔说。“他在蒙扎从第19位发车，现在他在第16位，所以这次他应该能提前几圈领跑。”

    “我想不起来他本赛季犯过多少错误，”拉塞尔说。“我们今天这边的表现很棒，但他整个赛季都无懈可击。”

    “我仍然继续相信、继续战斗，直到不再可能为止，但我没有感到任何压力。现在每次坐进车里我都感到兴奋，纯粹是因为我对自己更有信心。”

    “当这种信心开始建立时，就像是一个向上的循环。反过来，当你处于被动时，你开始失去信心，然后你开始更多地挑战极限，那正是失误发生的时候。”

    Kimi Antonelli 'very mad' about Azerbaijan GP qualifying crash as George Russell refuses to discount team-mate from battle for win

    Kimi Antonelli crashed in the first part of qualifying in Azerbaijan, leaving him 16th on the grid; George Russell exhibited Mercedes' strong pace by taking pole; watch the Azerbaijan GP on Saturday at 12pm with build-up from 10.30am, live on Sky Sports F1 and Main Event

    Kimi Antonelli hits the wall during qualifying at the Azerbaijan GP

    World championship leader Kimi Antonelli said he was "very mad" about making an "unnecessary" qualifying error that left him starting the Azerbaijan Grand Prix from 16th on the grid.

    The 81-point lead established by Antonelli at the top of the standings with nine races remaining has put him in a position where merely solid results will see the Italian to a maiden title.

    For that reason, along with the imperious form he has shown throughout the season so far, it came as a major surprise when Antonelli clipped the wall at the first corner during Q1 in Baku, especially given Mercedes were showing strong pace that should have enabled him to cruise through to Q3 at a canter.

    The Silver Arrows' strength at the circuit was evidenced in Antonelli's team-mate - and nearest title challenger - George Russell beating Ferrari's Charles Leclerc to pole by more than eight tenths of a second.

    Mercedes' Antonelli was left frustrated after an 'unnecessary' crash which ended his qualifying early at the Azerbaijan GP

    Asked whether he had been caught out by there being more grip than he expected at the first corner, Antonelli told Sky Sports F1: "Yeah, for sure. The grip was coming up very quickly lap by lap.

    "Suddenly I turned in and had a lot more front end compared to the lap before and I touched the wall.

    "So, mistake from my side. Very unnecessary. To be fair, I'm very mad about it and will try to do better tomorrow."

    'An incredible mistake' from 'the Kimi of old'

    Former world champion Jacques Villeneuve described the incident as "an incredible mistake".

    "It's not an inch, it's a lot," Sky Sports F1's Villeneuve said. "An incredible mistake and he does it on a weekend where his team-mate is in a league of his own.

    "Not the best of time for that, but with the current rules they facilitate getting back through the field."

    Highlights from qualifying for the 2026 Azerbaijan Grand Prix

    Three-time W series champion Jamie Chadwick said the crash reminded her of "Kimi of old", a reference to the 20-year-old's inconsistent debut campaign in F1, which raised major doubts over whether he could live up to his billing as a generational talent.

    Sky Sports F1's Chadwick said: "I say it's uncharacteristic, it's kind of the way we see Kimi drive a lot of the time is that there's one gear. It's all in. It's all everything.

    "Last year we saw that come with mistakes and crashes, this year we have not really seen that of him. It has felt a bit like Kimi of old in a way and it was like that one gear and he just didn't really need to do it.

    "That said, it is easy to make mistakes like that, we know that at a track like this. But you are in a Mercedes that was clearly in a league of his own at this track, first lap of Q1, you didn't need to take that much risk.

    "It just caught him out, the grip that he clearly had at Turn One. He turned in, you go into qualifying and the car is always a bit more alive I guess than it would have been for him in FP3 and it has caught him out."

    Russell 'can't discount' Antonelli from battle for win

    Saturday's race comes just three weeks on from Antonelli's stunning victory at the Italian Grand Prix, which saw him come from 19th on the grid to win his home race.

    Russell, who started second in Monza and led before being overhauled by Antonelli, is wary of the threat his team-mate could pose from towards the back of the grid in Baku.

    "Obviously, you can't discount Kimi," Russell said. "He started 19th in Monza and now he's in 16th, so he should be leading by a few laps earlier this time."

    Russell was asked whether he thought Antonelli was experiencing "jitters" as he nears a fist title, but pointed out that even in championship-winning campaigns, drivers tend to make mistakes.

    Sky Sports F1's Karun Chandhok is joined by George Russell to watch back his pole-setting lap for the Azerbaijan GP

    The Brit preferred to focus on his own strong performance so far in Baku, which had seen him appear to have an edge over Antonelli even before the Q1 crash.

    "I can't recall many mistakes he's made this season," Russell said. "The performance our side today was great, but he's been flawless this whole season.

    "When you look at every championship campaign, even for the winners, there are mistakes along the way.

    "I still keep on believing and keep on fighting until it isn't possible anymore, but I don't feel any pressure. I feel excitement every time I jump in the car now, purely because I feel more confident in myself.

    "When that confidence starts to build, it's just like this upward cycle. And on the flip side, when you're on the back foot, you start losing that confidence, and then you start pushing the limits more, and that's when mistakes happen.
