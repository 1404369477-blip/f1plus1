    威廉姆斯因公司重组超预算帽受罚

    威廉姆斯车队因2025赛季超出预算帽62.7万英镑（占总额0.48%）被罚，需向国际汽联缴纳罚款，并在未来12个月削减1.5%的风洞与CFD测试时间。领队沃尔斯表示，超支源于公司架构重组，车队原以为相关费用不计入预算帽。

    威廉姆斯解释称，拖延已久的公司重组触发了其F1预算帽处罚，因为车队所做的调整被其认为不计入预算帽。

    该车队被认定2025赛季超出预算帽62.7万英镑（占预算帽总额的0.48%），将须向国际汽联缴纳罚款，并在未来12个月损失1.5%的风洞与CFD测试时间。

    领队詹姆斯·沃尔斯在卡塔尔周五的新闻发布会上就此次超支面对了诸多提问。

    “背景是，威廉姆斯的公司架构自1977年以来基本未变，在现代环境下确实已不适配，”沃尔斯说。

    “我们本着开放、透明的态度与FIA合作，这一点我们自始至终都坚持，但我们认为这些成本，因为是公司重组，[将]不属于预算帽支出。”

    “是公司重组让我们超支的。我们没有从中获得任何财务利益，也没有因此获得任何体育利益，而且你可以看到，处罚也同时做了相应调整，与这一点保持一致。”

    “但我们的解释是，这些成本不在预算帽范围内。FIA的解释是它们属于预算帽范围，显然，经过大量讨论后，我们采纳了FIA的解释。”

    沃尔斯称这是一次“一次性公司重组”，因为之前的结构“不太符合目的”。

    沃夫斯鼓励“感兴趣的人”去英国公司注册处查看威廉姆斯车队的账目。

    根据英国公司注册处的记录，2025年期间，该车队“将其大部分业务和资产转移至一家新成立的子公司——威廉姆斯大奖赛工程服务有限责任合伙公司，该公司在其中持有99%的成员权益”。

    “此次公司架构的更新使组织适应未来发展，此前自成立以来近50年的运营中，架构相对未变，

    “这些变化是为了让我们的业务面向未来，实现更智能的工作方式，并使我们处于保持最高水平竞争力的有利位置。”

    “引入合作伙伴集团将有助于留住某些员工，并认可这些人员对实现商业计划的重要性。”

    “已向 威廉姆斯 Grand Prix Engineering Services LLP 的 1% 成员宣布成员分配。”

    沃尔斯将其描述为“从根本上说，它与之前的结构一样，是一个单一的垂直结构。”

    “从根本上说，这样做有很多弊端。你会看到围绕它已经创建了许多其他公司，这只是为了提高效率。”

    “其中涉及的成本相当可观。如果你想想作为一家企业必须做的事情，在我们这里，大概有接近2000份与各类供应商及其他方面的合同，因此你必须把这些合同从一家公司迁移到另一家公司。这是一项非常痛苦、耗时的任务。”

    “这还涉及一些法律事务。同时，根据英国法律，你还必须完成一些合规工作。”

    “最终，这个过程大概花了我们六个月的时间，我们大概是在六个月前启动的，这或许能让你了解它有多漫长。”

    “但在此期间，我们动用了财务和法律人员，为了度过那段时间，我们不得不加强这些人员配置。”

    威廉姆斯车队排名下滑，是因为他们对规则的理解与国际汽联不同。

    “显然，我们本应因此采取不同的做法，”沃尔斯承认道。

    “尽管规则并不清晰明确，存在解释空间，但国际汽联的立场非常明确，即：因为你们将合同从一个业务实体转移到另一个业务实体，这对他们来说实际上就是采购，而采购属于包含在内的活动。

    “对我们而言，我们将其定义为财务和法律活动，因为这实际上就是拿一份合同并在上面更改名称。

    “我能理解他们的解释，这也是我们接受违规的原因，但我希望这能帮助你们理解，在那种情况下，为什么真正关键的是细微差别，而非清晰明了。”

    这算不上什么重罚？

    鉴于空气动力学测试限制（ATR）体系下，风洞和计算流体力学（CFD）测试时间以5%为增量滑动调整，沃尔斯对风洞和CFD测试时间减少1.5%并不太担心。

    “我觉得可以这样看，发车格上一个名次对应5%的ATR额度，而我们只差1.5%。所以这还不到三分之一的名次，”沃尔斯说。

    “我非常希望我们明年能做得更好，让自己在发车格上回到更靠前的位置，那大约会是15%左右，差不多在这个区间。”

    “所以现实情况是，从大局来看，这只会是些小钱而已。”

    截至上一个空气动力学测试限制（ATR）周期，威廉姆斯在11支车队中排名第九，而且这一排名在2027年初不太可能改变。该排名表将在2027年中期第一个ATR周期结束后重置——届时按照沃尔斯估计的15%左右，威廉姆斯希望能排在锦标赛第六名附近。

    英文原文

    Williams has explained that overdue company restructuring triggered its Formula 1 cost cap penalty, as it made changes it thought were excluded from the cost cap.

    The team was found to have breached the 2025 cost cap by £627,000 (0.48% of the total cap) and will have to pay the FIA a financial penalty - as well as lose 1.5% of its windtunnel and CFD testing time for the next 12 months.

    Team boss James Vowles faced a number of questions on the breach during the Friday press conference in Qatar.

    “The background to it is that the company structure of Williams was largely unchanged from 1977 and wasn't really fit for purpose in a modern world,” Vowles said.

    “We went into it with every intention of being open, transparent with the FIA, which is what we held all the way throughout, but believed those costs, because it's company restructuring, [would] not be a cost cap expense.

    “It was the company restructuring that put us over. We’ve had no financial benefit from it, no sporting benefit as a result of it, and you can see that the penalty is pretty much adjusted to be in line with that as well at the same time.

    “But it was our interpretation of that, is that those costs are out of cap. The FIA’s interpretation of it are that they are in cap, and obviously we went with the FIA’s interpretation after much discussion.”

    Vowles called it a “one-off company restructuring” because the previous structure “wasn't quite fit for purpose”.

    Vowles encouraged “those interested” to look at Williams’s accounts on Companies House.

    According to Companies House, during 2025 the team “transferred much of its trade and assets to a newly formed subsidiary, Williams Grand Prix Engineering Services LLP, an entity in which the company is a 99% member.

    “This update to the corporate structure makes the organisation fit for the future, having been relatively unchanged in the nearly 50 years of operation since formation,

    “The changes are about future-proofing our business, enabling smarter ways of working and positioning us to stay competitive at the highest level.

    “The inclusion of a partner group will aid retention of certain staff and recognises the importance of those people to the delivery of the business plan.

    “Member distributions have been declared to the 1% member of Williams Grand Prix Engineering Services LLP.”

    Vowles described it as “Fundamentally, it's a single vertical structure as what was there previously.

    “There's many downsides to fundamentally having that. You'll see there's a number of other companies have been created around it, which is just more efficient.

    “The costs involved in it are fairly significant. If you think about what you have to do as a business, you probably have, in our case, close to 2,000 contracts with various suppliers and otherwise, that you have to migrate from one company to the next as a result of it. It's a very painful, lengthy task.

    “There's some legal that goes with it. There's some compliance you have to do in UK law as a result of it as well at the same time.

    “It was a process in the end that took, we initiated it probably around about six months, which probably gives you an idea as to how lengthy it is.

    “But it was using finance and legal personnel for that period of which we had to bolster up to get us through that period.”

    Williams fell down because it had a different interpretation of the regulations to the FIA.

    “Clearly, we should have done something differently as a result of it,” Vowles admitted.

    “Although the regulations aren't clear and black and white, it's interpretation, but the FIA is very clear on their perspective, which is the following: Because you've moved contracts from one business to the next, that effectively for them is procurement, which is an included activity.

    “For us, we defined it as a financial and legal activity because it's literally taking a contract and changing a name on it.

    “I can understand their interpretation, which is why we've accepted the breach, but I hope that helps you understand why it's subtleties, not clarity, really, in that circumstance.”

    Not much of a penalty?

    Vowles isn’t too worried about that 1.5% reduction in windtunnel and CFD testing time given that under the Aerodynamic Testing Restrictions system, there’s a sliding scale of windtunnel and CFD time in 5% increments.

    “I think to put things in perspective, one position on the grid is 5% of ATR, and we've got a 1.5% delta. So it's less than a third of a position,” Vowles said.

    “I'm rather hoping that we do a better job next year and put ourselves back up the grid, and that would be 15% something in that ballpark.

    “So the reality is it's going to be small beans in the grand scheme of things.”

    Williams is currently ninth out of 11 teams as of the last ATR period, and that’s unlikely to change for the start of 2027. That table will then reset after the first ATR period ends halfway through 2027 - when by Vowles’s 15% ballpark estimation, Williams will hope to be around sixth in the championship.
