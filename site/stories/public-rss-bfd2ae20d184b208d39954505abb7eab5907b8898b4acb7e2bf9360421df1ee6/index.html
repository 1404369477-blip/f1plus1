    伊斯坦布尔赛道改造以争取F1长期合约

    伊斯坦布尔赛道正对其F1赛道进行改造，以争取长期留在F1赛历中。

    伊斯坦布尔如何改造其F1赛道以锁定长期未来

    伊斯坦布尔公园赛道将于明年重返F1赛历。这条赛道深受车手喜爱，布局将保持不变，但在赛事本身方面，组织者希望吸取过去的教训，对其进行大幅改造。

    在现代一级方程式中，找到合适的平衡往往是一项挑战。这同样适用于技术规则：一方面要在满足车手和纯粹主义者之间取得平衡，另一方面要呈现F1希望为年轻车迷提供的观赏性，并不断带来超车动作——比如说，面向TikTok一代。

    但这种平衡术也适用于赛历和F1造访的举办地。许多近期新增的目的地是位于迈阿密、拉斯维加斯和马德里等迷人地点的临时赛道，但这些未必是让铁杆车迷兴奋的赛道。

    然而，明年赛历上将回归一场有潜力将两者优势结合起来的比赛。伊斯坦布尔是一座全球性城市——欧洲与亚洲之间的桥梁——因此正是F1希望纳入赛历的那种目的地。但这条位于博斯普鲁斯海峡亚洲一侧的赛道，也是一条能吸引车手和车迷的赛道，尤其是因为它著名的8号弯。

    在土耳其大奖赛回归之前，Motorsport.com 与土耳其汽车运动联合会（TOSFED）主席埃伦·乌奇莱尔托普拉吉进行了交谈。他同样抱有将两方面结合起来的雄心：一场符合现代F1标准的赛事，同时保留一条铁杆车迷喜爱的赛道。

    “是的，我也这么认为，”乌奇莱尔托普拉吉说。“从车迷的角度来看，我们总是更渴望观看老派赛道上的比赛，所以F1接受了我们的提议、我们重返赛历是件好事。我们都怀念过去的比赛，但归根结底，从商业角度来说，我们也需要像马德里这样的比赛。两者我们都需要，现代街道赛道，以及像伊斯坦布尔、蒙扎和银石这样的赛道。”

    话虽如此，所有永久性赛道都必须做出调整，以符合现代F1在设施和赛事整体呈现方面的标准。伊斯坦布尔的赛道布局将保持不变，但其周边将会有很大变化。

    “赛道将进行重大升级，”乌切尔托普拉吉解释道。“我们将把赛道现代化到一级方程式赛车目前达到的水平。我们当然也在基础设施方面开展工作。我们将尝试覆盖大部分看台，并尝试打造车迷区，尽一切必要努力为车迷提供最佳体验。”

    最后一点是这些计划的关键支柱。伊斯坦布尔公园赛道过去一直是车手们的最爱，但据乌切尔托普拉吉所说，未来五年——即新合同的期限——最大的挑战是让它同样成为车迷们的最爱；在这方面还有很多工作要做。

    “它一直是一条车手赛道，但现在我们正努力让它也成为一条车迷赛道，”他说。“这要从交通管制和人群管理开始。周边道路已经以新的方式建成，因此现在有进入道路和离开道路。交通流将得到改善，让进出赛道的体验更好。”

    后者尤为重要，因为此前几届土耳其大奖赛期间的交通相当混乱。这是乌切尔托普拉吉很清楚的一个问题。

    “我们知道该做什么。我们已经开始规划基础设施，同时也在扩建围场。容量将翻倍，基础设施将从头建设。除此之外，我们还将改善接待设施，包括利用我们著名的8号弯。”

    在这次赛事改革中，有一个方面特别值得注意：与以往几届相比，未来几年的土耳其大奖赛在组织方式上将大不相同。TOSFED不仅是土耳其赛车运动联合会，现在还是推广方——这一次，这应该意味着更直接的沟通渠道，以及更强的能力真正将雄心转化为行动。

    “赛道管理权已移交给TOSFED，因此作为推广方，在把事情办成方面形成了一种新的流程。一切都将由我掌控，并在国家的支持下，我们将尽力采取一切必要措施，”乌切尔托普拉吉说道。

    “最糟糕的部分是，在我们管理之前，赛道由一家私营公司管理，他们的优先事项不是这项运动，不是车迷，也不是一级方程式。所以不幸的是，我们在车迷体验方面遇到了问题，这就是为什么我们现在优先考虑车迷体验。”

    伊斯坦布尔公园赛道计划如何避免进一步的沥青问题

    除此之外，乌切尔托普拉吉知道基本要素仍需到位。赛道布局不会改变，沥青需要处于更好的状态。在F1最近几次造访期间，路面被证明存在问题，促使多位车手将其描述为“在冰上驾驶”——尤其是2020年下雨的那个周末，刘易斯·汉密尔顿正是在那里锁定了他的第七个世界冠军。

    “是的，当时沥青确实有问题，这一点我完全支持你，”乌切尔托普拉吉承认道。

    “我们并不太赞成在距离比赛仅剩很短时间的情况下重新铺设表层。部分问题在于赛道上没有举办过比赛，所以在F1之前没有比赛，这意味着我们遇到了路面问题。”

    不出所料，伊斯坦布尔的组织者迫切希望避免在2027年重蹈那些问题的覆辙。

    “目前，我们正与国际汽联（FIA）以及赛道专家密切合作，”他继续说道。“当然，我们需要更换路缘石等设施，但赛道布局将保持不变。而且我想这次我们不会更换沥青路面。”

    “它将保持不变，目前我们每年至少在伊斯坦布尔公园举办30周的‘体验活动’。在翻新和升级之后，我们将在F1之前重新开始举办国内和国际赛事。”

    这应能防止F1车手在抓地力水平方面再次遭遇令人不快的意外。

    土耳其大奖赛能否在2031年之后继续留在赛历上？

    所有这些最初都是基于五年合同来推进的，但乌切尔托普拉吉希望伊斯坦布尔在初始协议之后仍能留在赛历上，这意味着这些投资着眼于更长期的目标。

    “我们正在研究一个更长的期限。这就是为什么我们目前关于赛道的项目将超过五年。我们正在内部以及与州政府讨论如何扩大这个项目，而不会再次离开赛历，”他说。

    乌切尔托普拉吉非常清楚实现这一目标需要什么：兑现上述承诺。如果做到这一点，他相信其余的事情会自然随之而来。

    “当然，我们的承诺首先是五年，但如果我们能达到一级方程式（Formula 1）所要求的标准，那么对双方都有利。只要我们履行了所做出的承诺，并且未来五年能让车迷满意，那么我认为将伊斯坦布尔留在赛历上对双方都有利，”他总结道。

    How Istanbul is revamping its F1 circuit to clinch long-term future

    Istanbul Park will return to the F1 calendar next year. Beloved by drivers, the layout will remain intact, but when it comes to the event itself, the organisers want to learn from the past and give it a substantial overhaul

    Finding the right balance is often a challenge in modern Formula 1. That applies to the technical regulations: striking a balance between satisfying drivers and purists on the one hand, and delivering the spectacle F1 wants to offer younger fans on the other, with a constant stream of overtaking moves for, let’s say, the TikTok generation.

    But that balancing act also applies to the calendar and the venues F1 visits. Many of the recently added destinations are temporary circuits in glamorous locations such as Miami, Las Vegas and Madrid, but these are not necessarily the tracks that get diehard fans excited.

    Next year, however, a race will return to the calendar that has the potential to combine the best of both worlds. Istanbul is a global city – the bridge between Europe and Asia – and therefore the kind of destination F1 likes to have on its calendar. But the circuit, located on the Asian side of the Bosphorus, is also one that appeals to drivers and fans, particularly because of its famous Turn 8.

    Ahead of the Turkish Grand Prix's return, Motorsport.com sat down with Eren Uclertopragi, president of the Turkish Automobile Federation (TOSFED). He shares the ambition of bringing both aspects together: an event that meets the standards of modern F1, while retaining a circuit that diehard fans enjoy.

    “Yes, I think the same,” Uclertopragi said. “From a fans’ perspective, we are always more eager to watch the races in old-school tracks, so it's good that F1 accepted our proposal and that we are back on track. We all miss the old races, but at the end of the day, from the commercial side, we also need the races like Madrid. We need both, the modern street tracks and tracks like Istanbul, Monza and Silverstone.”

    That said, all permanent circuits have to adapt to meet the standards of modern F1, both in terms of facilities and the overall presentation of the event. The layout in Istanbul will remain intact, but a great deal will change around it.

    “There will be major upgrades to the circuit,” Uclertopragi explained. “We will modernize our circuit to the level F1 reaches right now. We are, of course, working on the infrastructural side. We will try to cover most of the grandstands and we will try to make fan zones to do whatever is necessary to give the best experience to the fans.”

    That last point is a crucial pillar of the plans. Istanbul Park has been a drivers’ favourite in the past, but according to Uclertopragi, the biggest challenge for the next five years – the duration of the new contract – is to make it a fan favourite as well; there is a lot of work to be done in that respect.

    “It was always a drivers’ circuit, but now we’re trying to make it a fan circuit as well,” he said. “That starts with the traffic control and crowd management. The surrounding roads have already been constructed in a new way, so that there are now incoming roads and outgoing roads. The traffic flow will be improved to make the experience of getting into the circuit and getting out of the circuit better.”

    The latter is particularly important, as traffic during previous editions of the Turkish Grand Prix was rather chaotic. It is an issue Uclertopragi is well aware of.

    “We know what we have to do. We have started already planning the infrastructure and we are expanding the paddock as well. The capacity will be doubled and the infrastructure will be done from scratch. Apart from that, we will improve our hospitality units, including using our famous Turn 8.”

    One aspect is particularly noteworthy in this overhaul of the event: the Turkish GP will be organised very differently in the coming years compared to previous editions. TOSFED is not only the Turkish motorsport federation, but now also the promoter – something that should mean more direct lines of communication and greater ability to actually turn ambitions into actions this time.

    “The management of the circuit has been handed out to TOSFED, so that is creating a new flow with regards to getting things done as a promoter. Everything will be in my hands and with the support of the state, so we will try to do whatever is necessary,” Uclertopragi said.

    “The worst part is that prior to our management, the circuit was managed by a private company and their prioritization was not the sport, not the fans, not Formula 1. So unfortunately we had problems with the fan experience and that is why we are prioritizing that fan experience right now.”

    How Istanbul Park plans to avoid further asphalt issues

    Aside from that, Uclertopragi knows that the fundamentals still need to be right. The layout will not change, and the asphalt needs to be in better condition. The surface proved problematic during F1's most recent visits, prompting several drivers to describe it as “driving on ice” – especially when it rained in 2020, the weekend when Lewis Hamilton secured his seventh world championship.

    “Yeah, there was a problem with the asphalt back then, I totally support you on that,” Uclertopragi admitted.

    “We were not a big fan of resurfacing the top layer with only a short period of time to go before the race. Part of the problem was that there were no races held at the circuit, so there was no race prior to F1 and that meant we had the surface problem.”

    Unsurprisingly, the organisers in Istanbul are keen to avoid a repeat of those problems in 2027.

    “Currently, we are in close cooperation with the FIA and experts from the circuit,” he continued. “Of course, we will need to change the kerbstones and so on, but the layout will be the same. And I guess we will not be changing the tarmac this time.

    “It will remain the same and currently we are hosting ‘experience events’ at Istanbul Park for at least 30 weeks a year. After the renovation and upgrades we will start holding national and international races again, prior to Formula 1.”

    This should prevent F1 drivers from once again being confronted with an unpleasant surprise when it comes to grip levels.

    Can the Turkish GP stay on the calendar beyond 2031?

    All of this is initially being done with the five-year contract in mind, but Uclertopragi wants Istanbul to remain on the calendar beyond the initial deal, meaning the investments are aimed at the longer term.

    “We are looking into a longer period. That's why the projects that we are having right now about the circuit will be going beyond five years. We are discussing internally and with the state how we can expand this project without leaving the calendar again,” he said.

    Uclertopragi knows all too well what is needed to achieve that: delivering on the aforementioned promises. If that happens, he believes the rest will follow naturally.

    “For sure, our commitment is for five years first of all, but if we deliver the standards that Formula 1 is looking for, then it will be beneficial for both parties. As long as we deliver the commitments we had and if we will have happy fans in the next five years, then I think it will be beneficial for both parties to keep Istanbul on the calendar,” he concluded.
