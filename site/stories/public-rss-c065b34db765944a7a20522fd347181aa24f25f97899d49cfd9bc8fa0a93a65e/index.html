    里卡多坦言F1回归令他身心俱疲

    里卡多在接受Beyond The Grid播客采访时回顾了职业生涯末期，表示在迈凯伦和重返小红牛的经历令他感到挫败，并对心理健康造成伤害。

    “我被打得遍体鳞伤”——里卡多如何在“令人羞辱”的F1回归中耗尽心力

    里卡多离开F1两年后，他敞开心扉谈论职业生涯的终结——这段经历不仅令人沮丧，也伤害了他的心理健康

    上个月，丹尼尔·里卡多做客Beyond The Grid播客时，听起来更加沉稳，甚至多了几分睿智。远离F1聚光灯的两年，给了这位澳大利亚人反思所需的时间，让他得以从新的角度审视自己在锦标赛中的最后几年。

    最后几年是一段挣扎。失去迈凯伦席位后，里卡多回到了他昔日的法恩扎车队，希望借此重返红牛——但未能如愿。他再也找不回久违的表现，而此前在沃金他已经历了一段备受打击的时光。他在意大利大奖赛上的惊人胜利，也难以掩盖一个事实：除此之外，他在最后70场比赛中再未登上领奖台。

    虽然他的F1之旅并未按照他自己的意愿结束——红牛决定让年轻车手利亚姆·劳森获得小红牛车队的第二个席位——但里卡多已经意识到，甚至说服自己，他还没有准备好退役，他已经不再处于适合比赛的心态之中。

    “到那个时候，我已经受够了被击倒；我想，我已经被打得遍体鳞伤，”他在谈到自己最后一场比赛——2024年新加坡大奖赛——时的心态说道。

    “我仍然充满渴望，想要证明些什么——主要是向我自己证明——但有些时刻我已经受够了，而且我认为自己也在否认，又回到了我们有时会竖起的那层保护壳：‘我能做到，我很坚强，我会挺过去，我足够好，等等等等’。确实有些时候我会想，‘唉，我不再热爱这个了，我真的不想待在这里’。睡一觉就好了，睡一觉就好了，你会到达那里，你会没事的，然后也许有一场好比赛或一个瞬间你会觉得，‘是的，我又找回了那团火’。

    “我当然仍然想要那个童话般的结局；我想要回到红牛车队，所有那些，我也很想再次成为马克斯的队友，再次尝试在前排竞争。但我内心深处知道，这一切让我越来越疲惫。我认为，由于很多因素，对我来说，再像以前那样表现和竞争已经不再那么容易了。你看到有些车手年纪比我大，仍然在高水平上做到这一点，所以每个人情况都不同。”

    “有太多的痛苦、过程，太多这些年正在发生和已经发生的事情，我觉得它们只是不断累积，而我很难把这一切都关在外面然后继续去做。”

    被情绪淹没的里卡多不确定自己能否做出那个可以说是健康的决定——自己主动叫停。

    “我当时的恐惧是，事后回想并体会到不再拥有那份合同让我多么满足，如果他们一直给我续约，我会不会一直做下去，我到底会不会关掉这个水龙头？我知道我有时是想关掉的，”他承认道。“我现在可以告诉你，2022年摩纳哥站赛后，我坐在公寓里，和我的团队交谈，我当时就说，‘我不想再这样下去了，我受够了’。这些年来我不止一次有过这样的对话——2015年银石是另一次。”

    里卡多在2022年摩纳哥大奖赛代表迈凯伦仅排位第14、正赛第13完赛，而这条赛道正是他四年前夺冠的地方。在2015年英国大奖赛上，他因超出赛道限制被取消第九快的时间后仅列第10位发车，起步时与罗曼·格罗斯让相撞，在后方车阵中挣扎，直到遭遇电气故障。

    “你身边的人会对你说，‘你很棒，你还能做到’，最终你也会相信很多这样的声音——我知道他们没有恶意，但你其实没有太多机会拥有自己的想法，我觉得这种情况可能还会继续下去，也许我现在还在比赛——这也是一个可怕的想法，”这位37岁的车手补充道。

    “我仍然不喜欢它结束的方式，但我内心也有一部分感激他们扣动了扳机，因为我不想现在还在做这件事。”

    尽管如此，里卡多仍在与自己所说的失败感和羞辱感作斗争，因为他的职业生涯戛然而止，他为自己的世界冠军梦想而哀悼。

    “你在这么小的年纪就开始比赛，多年来你建立起了一种，我想，冷酷——但那也是向内的，”他解释道。“你可能会对自己非常严厉，对自己期望很高。所以我认为，比如说，失败——那是我对它的看法，失败，没有达到我当时需要的水平，或者无法完成赛季，或者不管什么——那之后就是一种羞辱感。我失败了，我没有做到我曾经能做到的事。

    “在应对那一切、接下来的几个月里，我明白自己只是在逃避那份羞辱感。人们看到我在公共场合，我表现得‘哈哈哈，我玩得很开心’，喝点酒什么的，而我其实只是在逃避内心深处真实的感受，那是大量的痛苦和悲伤。‘一切都过去了。我曾经是那个渴望争夺世界冠军、赢得比赛的孩子；一切都过去了。’”

    于是里卡多退后一步，远离了F1围场和相关的媒体事务，优先留出他需要的独处时间。

    “就驾驶而言，我已经精疲力竭，更不用说情绪之类的了，我只是累了，”他说。“我只知道我需要远离一切——甚至远离人群。去年我做了很多独自旅行和出游，很多徒步之类的活动。我只知道我需要独自呼吸。”

    但这位八次大奖赛冠军得主去年五月确实出席了印第安纳波利斯500大赛，这重新点燃了他对赛车的热情。

    “重新做回车迷让我想起，我确实热爱赛车运动。”

    “I was getting beat up” – How Daniel Ricciardo burned out on ‘humiliating’ F1 return

    Two years on from Ricciardo’s F1 exit, he opens up about the end of his career – which was not only frustrating but damaging to his mental health too

    As Daniel Ricciardo joined the Beyond The Grid podcast last month, he sounded more grounded, almost wiser. Two years away from Formula 1’s limelight have provided the Australian with the time he required for introspection, allowing him to view his final years in the championship from a new angle.

    Those final years were a struggle. After losing his McLaren seat, Ricciardo returned to his old Faenza team with a view to make a Red Bull comeback – to no avail. He never found his long-lost performance again, having previously experienced a bruising time in Woking. His shock Italian Grand Prix victory hardly distracts from the fact that, other than that, he never finished on the podium in his final 70 races.

    While his F1 adventure did not wind up on his own terms – with Red Bull deciding that youngster Liam Lawson was the way to go for the second RB seat – Ricciardo has grown aware, even persuading himself that he wasn’t ready to retire, that he no longer was in the right state of mind for racing.

    “By that point, I was done getting knocked down; I was, I guess, getting beat up,” he said of his state of mind at his last race, the 2024 Singapore GP.

    “I was still hungry and wanting to try to prove something – mainly to myself – but there were moments where I was done, and I think also in denial, back to that protective shield that we may put up at times: ‘I can do this, I’m strong, I’ll get through this, I’m good enough, blah blah blah’. There were definitely times that I was like, ‘Ugh, I don’t love this anymore, I don’t really want to be here’. Sleep it off, sleep it off, you’ll get there and you’ll be fine and then maybe have one good race or one moment you’re like, ‘Yeah, I’ve got the fire back’.

    “I still wanted the fairy tale, of course; I wanted to get back with Red Bull Racing and all of that, and I would have loved to have been team-mates with Max again and tried to race at the front again. But I knew deep down I was getting more burned out by it all. I think, because of a lot of factors, it wasn’t as easy for me anymore to perform and compete at the level that I once did. And you see some guys that are still doing it at a high level, that are older than me, so it’s different for everyone.

    “There was just a lot of pain, process, a lot of things that just were happening and had happened over the years, that I think just built up, and it was just very hard for me to shut all that out and do it.”

    Overwhelmed by his emotions, Ricciardo isn’t certain he could have made the arguably healthy decision to pull the plug himself.

    “The fear I had is, in hindsight and learning how content I am not having that anymore, what if they kept extending contracts for me, would I keep doing it, was I ever going to turn the tap off? I know I wanted to at times,” he admitted. “I can tell you right now, Monaco 2022 after the race, I’m sitting in my apartment, talking to my team, and I was like, ‘I don’t want to do this anymore, I’m done’. I’ve had this conversation more than once over the years – Silverstone 2015 was another time.”

    Ricciardo qualified a lowly 14th and finished 13th in the 2022 Monaco GP with McLaren, at the track where he had won four years prior. In the 2015 British GP, he was just 10th on the grid after his ninth-fastest time was deleted for a track-limits infringement, collided with Romain Grosjean at the start, and struggled in the lower pack until he was struck by an electrical failure.

    “You have people around you that tell you, ‘You’re great, you can still do it’, and you end up also believing a lot of these voices – I know they don’t mean harm, but you don’t really get a chance to have your own thoughts much, and I think that would have probably just continued and maybe I would still be racing – and that’s also a scary thought,” the 37-year-old added.

    “I still don’t like how it ended, but there’s also a part of me which is grateful they pulled the trigger, because I wouldn’t want to be doing it now.”

    Still, Ricciardo struggled with what he described as a sense of failure and humiliation as his career ended abruptly and he mourned his dream of a world title.

    “You start racing at such a young age, and over the years you build, I guess, a ruthlessness – but that also is inward as well,” he explained. “You can be very harsh on yourself and expect a lot from yourself. So I think, let’s say, failing – that was my view of it, failing, not performing to the level I needed to at the time, or not being able to finish out the season, or whatever – it’s then a sense of humiliation. I failed, and I didn’t do what I once could.

    “Trying to deal with that, those next few months, I understood it as I was just hiding from the humiliation. People saw me out in public, I was, ‘Ha ha ha, I’m having a good time’, having a drink or whatever, and I was just hiding from what I really felt deep inside, which was a lot of pain and sadness. ‘It’s gone. I was once this kid that was hoping to fight for a world title and winning races; it’s gone’.”

    So Ricciardo took a step back, stayed away from the Formula 1 paddock and the associated media commitments, and prioritised the alone time he needed.

    “In terms of driving, I was burned out, let alone emotions and all that, I was just tired,” he said. “I just knew I needed time from everything – even people. I did a lot of solo travelling and trips last year, a lot of hiking and things. I just know that I needed to breathe on my own.”

    But the eight-time grand prix winner did attend the Indianapolis 500 last May, which reignited his passion for racing.

    “Being a fan again reminded me that I do love motorsports.”
