    悼念帕特里克·斯图尔特：2017年加拿大站与里卡多的领奖台时刻

    演员帕特里克·斯图尔特去世，享年86岁。2017年加拿大大奖赛领奖台上，他用丹尼尔·里卡多的赛车靴喝香槟，成为F1最令人难忘的领奖台瞬间之一。

    悼念帕特里克·斯图尔特爵士与丹尼尔·里卡多在F1领奖台上令人难忘的时刻

    《星际迷航》和《X战警》演员去世，享年86岁；他在2017年加拿大大奖赛上留下了F1最令人难忘的领奖台瞬间之一

    领奖台：帕特里克·斯图尔特用赛车靴喝香槟，丹尼尔·里卡多，红牛车队

    赛道之外的赛车文化。车手生活方式、时尚、旅行、设计，以及让赛车超越运动本身的故事。

    虽然帕特里克·斯图尔特爵士因《星际迷航》和《X战警》等系列作品而闻名，但F1车迷会记住他是一位令人难忘的赛车运动爱好者。2017年在蒙特利尔，他在领奖台上用丹尼尔·里卡多的赛车靴喝香槟，证明了他有多么热情。

    这位电影传奇人物在洛杉矶的家中去世，享年86岁，据称他与阿尔茨海默病和路易体痴呆症进行了短暂而私密的斗争。但在去世之前，他度过了不可思议的一生，不仅通过无数慈善事例奉献社会，还享受了生活中更美好的事物——其中之一就是2017年加拿大大奖赛领奖台上的香槟。

    蒙特利尔站冠军刘易斯·汉密尔顿与队友瓦尔特利·博塔斯站在一起，而里卡多为红牛车队获得第三名。斯图尔特主持了采访，这位演员以他著名的戏剧性表达开场：

    “恭喜你，刘易斯。你可能会说，没有什么比在蒙特利尔的周日下午沿着河边愉快而安静地驾驶更美好的了。但这次有点不同，不是吗？”他说。但真正登上全球头条的是他与里卡多的对话。

    这位澳大利亚车手已经把自己的“鞋饮”变成了标志性的领奖台庆祝方式——用自己比赛时穿的赛车靴喝香槟，而蒙特利尔的观众知道接下来会发生什么。

    “我觉得我听到了什么……我得做点什么，”里卡多说，随后他从靴子里一饮而尽香槟，然后把靴子递给了斯图尔特。

    当时76岁的斯图尔特毫不犹豫，从靴子里喝了一口，下方车迷顿时爆发出巨大欢呼。

    “这都快成仪式了！好年份！干杯！”斯图尔特说。

    汉密尔顿前一天刚刚追平了艾尔顿·塞纳的65个杆位纪录，对此印象深刻。

    “我为你做到这一点感到骄傲！这是我做不到的事。你看到他的脚有多湿了吗？”汉密尔顿说道。

    斯图尔特的回复迅速走红。

    “我不在乎——这是我第一次登上领奖台；谁的鞋我都愿意喝！”

    有趣的场面还在继续，斯图尔特问汉密尔顿从杆位领跑每一圈是什么感觉。“我马上告诉你，但你能读懂我的心思吗？”汉密尔顿问道。

    “能。你很高兴！”斯图尔特回答道，随后又带动人群高呼：“加拿大，你们听到了吗？蒙特利尔——听听他说的话！”

    这段视频在网上广泛传播。“首位在加拿大大奖赛上用鞋喝酒的骑士，”天空体育当时说道。

    2024年离开F1的里卡多在其职业生涯中又多次用鞋喝酒，但很少有哪次能比得上他与让-吕克·皮卡德舰长共享的那一次。

    Remembering Sir Patrick Stewart's unforgettable moment on the F1 podium with Daniel Ricciardo

    Star Trek and X-Men actor, who has died aged 86, delivered one of F1’s most memorable podium moments at the 2017 Canadian Grand Prix

    Podium: Patrick Stewart, does a Shoey, Daniel Ricciardo, Red Bull Racing

    Motorsport culture beyond the track. Driver lifestyle, fashion, travel, design and the stories that make racing more than a sport.

    While Sir Patrick Stewart made his name in franchises such as Star Trek and X-Men, he'll be remembered by Formula 1 fans as an unforgettable lover of motorsport. And in Montreal in 2017, he proved just how passionate he was by drinking champagne from Daniel Ricciardo's race boot on the podium.

    The film legend died aged 86 at his home in Los Angeles after what was described as a brief and private battle with both Alzheimer’s disease and Lewy body dementia. But before his passing, he had an incredible life where not only did he give through countless examples of philanthropy, but he also enjoyed the finer things life had to offer - one of them being champagne on the podium of the 2017 Canadian Grand Prix.

    Lewis Hamilton, winner of the Montreal race, stood alongside his team-mate Valtteri Bottas, while Ricciardo had finished third for Red Bull. Stewart led the interviews as the actor opened with his famous theatrical delivery:

    “Congratulations, Lewis. You might say that there’s nothing like a pleasant, quiet drive along the river on a Sunday afternoon in Montreal. But this was a little different, wasn’t it?” he said. But it was his conversation with Ricciardo that made headlines globally.

    The Australian had made the shoey his signature podium celebration, drinking champagne from his own race boot, and the crowd in Montreal knew what was coming.

    “I think I hear something… I have to do something,” Ricciardo said, before downing champagne from his boot and then handing it to Stewart.

    The then-76-year-old did not hesitate, drinking from the boot to huge cheers from the fans below.

    “This is becoming a ritual! Good vintage! Cheers!” Stewart said.

    Hamilton, who had just matched Ayrton Senna’s record of 65 pole positions the day before, was impressed.

    “I’m proud of you for doing that! It’s something I couldn’t do. Have you seen how sweaty his feet are?” said Hamilton.

    Stewart’s reply quickly went viral.

    “I don’t care – I’m on the podium for the first time; I’ll drink out of anybody’s shoe!”

    The fun continued when Stewart asked Hamilton how it felt to lead every lap from pole position. “I’ll tell you that in a second, but can you read my mind?” Hamilton asked.

    “Yes. You are happy!” Stewart replied, before rallying the crowd with: “Do you hear that, Canada? Montreal – what he said!”

    The clip was shared widely online. “The first knight of the realm to do a shoey at the Canadian GP," Sky Sports said at the time.

    Ricciardo, who left F1 in 2024, went on to do several more shoeys during his career, but few matched the one he shared with Captain Jean-Luc Picard.
