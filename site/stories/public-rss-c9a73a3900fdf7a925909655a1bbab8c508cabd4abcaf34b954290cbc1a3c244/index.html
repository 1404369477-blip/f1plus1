    F1 2027 calendar announced with Bahrain to host season-opener and increase in Sprint weekends in 24-race schedule

    Bahrain to host 2027 Formula 1 season-opener as Portugal and Turkey return to the schedule from next year, plus an increase in Sprint weekends; watch every session of the Azerbaijan Grand Prix from September 24-26 with a Saturday race, live on Sky Sports F1

    Craig Slater takes a look at the big talking points from the 2027 F1 season calendar, including a Sprint taking place in Monaco for the first time

    Formula 1 has announced its 2027 calendar and an increase in the number of Sprint weekends from six to 10, with the season starting in Bahrain.

    For a fourth consecutive year, there are 24 rounds scheduled with Portugal's Portimao and Turkey's Istanbul Park returning to the calendar.

    As expected, the Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort falls off the schedule while the Circuit de Barcelona-Catalunya moves into a rotational deal with Spa-Francorchamps that will see the Spanish event held in even numbered years from 2028 and the Belgian Grand Prix held in odd numbered years from 2027.

    F1 had delayed its calendar announcement for next year due to the ongoing conflict in the Middle East, which has created uncertainty over the end of the current season with Qatar and Abu Dhabi still currently scheduled to take place later this year.

    Bahrain will host a four-day pre-season test on February 24-27 before holding the 2027 season-opener on March 12-14 and it will be a Sprint weekend, with the Saudi Arabian Grand Prix a week later.

    Australia reverts to the third round of the season in early April and will also host a Sprint, as will the next race in Japan before China rounds off a triple-header.

    Lewis Hamilton and George Russell tussle for the lead of the 2026 Chinese GP Sprint

    Miami retains its slot in the first weekend of May before Sprint rounds in Canada and, for the first time, Monaco, with F1 highlighting fans are "set to enjoy two thrilling qualifying sessions" around the streets of Monte Carlo.

    The returning Portuguese Grand Prix slots into mid-June and is followed by a Sprint weekend at the British Grand Prix on July 2-4, with Austria moving to after Silverstone.

    Back-to-back weekends in Belgium and Hungary will take the F1 circus to the summer break.

    F1 2027 Calendar and Sprint announcement - key points

    Portuguese and Turkish Grands Prix return to calendar for first time since 2021

    Season-opener returns to Bahrain for first time since 2024 and it will be a Sprint weekend

    Number of Sprint weekends increased from six to 10

    Summer break is five weeks long, rather than four

    Season to conclude in Abu Dhabi on December 10-12

    A sprint round at Monza in early September will be the first event back after five weeks off, a week longer than usual, with the Spanish Grand Prix in Madrid a week later.

    Azerbaijan, Turkey and Singapore form part of a triple-header before the usual Americas triple-header in Austin, Mexico and Brazil, which is a Sprint weekend.

    Las Vegas will host the only Saturday race of 2027, although it will be Sunday morning in the UK, then two Sprint weekends in Qatar and Abu Dhabi on December 10-12 conclude the season.

    "The 2027 calendar brings together some of the most iconic circuits and vibrant cities in the world, creating an incredible stage for another unforgettable season of Formula 1," said F1 president Stefano Domenicali.

    "We are thrilled to welcome Portugal and Turkey to the championship and to expand Sprint racing to 10 events, giving our fans even more intensity, drama, and the wheel-to-wheel action that make our sport so special.

    "Formula 1 continues to go from strength-to-strength, attracting new audiences around the globe. Week after week, the world's greatest drivers will battle on some of the most challenging tracks in motorsport, supported by F1's outstanding teams, world-class manufacturers, and global partners. Together, they create a spectacle unlike anything else in sport. As we continue this remarkable journey of growth, I want to thank our fans around the world as their passion drives everything we do."

    "With 24 Grands Prix across five continents, the return of Portugal and Turkey, and the opportunity for an expanded Sprint programme, the calendar combines tradition, innovation, and fan engagement while maintaining the highest sporting standards."
