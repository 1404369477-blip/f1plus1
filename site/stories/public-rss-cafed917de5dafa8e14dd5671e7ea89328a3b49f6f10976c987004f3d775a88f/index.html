    2026年F1车队价值跃升47%，近100亿美元

    根据《福布斯》2026年报告，F1车队价值从2025年的36亿美元跃升至2026年的52亿美元，法拉利以95亿美元位居第一，梅赛德斯以92亿美元位居第二。

    根据《福布斯》2026年报告，F1车队价值从2025年的36亿美元跃升至2026年的52亿美元。

    报告显示，法拉利以95亿美元位居价值榜首位，梅赛德斯以92亿美元位居第二，这两家车队与NFL顶级球队、NBA最值钱的球队以及皇家马德里队价值相当。

    F1的11支车队因此平均价值为52亿美元，如果不包括缺席2025年排名的凯迪拉克，平均价值达到54亿美元，比去年增长53%。

    哈斯车队价值增长87%，从15亿美元增至28亿美元，而迈凯轮的价值增长了73%，达到76亿美元。

    福布斯将预估收入作为其计算的一部分：从2024年到2025年，预估收入增长了15%，每支车队达到5.3亿美元。

    报告称，迈凯轮和梅赛德斯-奔驰预计将在2026年首次突破10亿美元——只有NFL的达拉斯牛仔队以及足球队伍皇家马德里和巴塞罗那达到过这个水平。

    报告强调“倍数”是F1车队增长的动力：2019年，F1车队的估值是其上一赛季收入的2.3倍，而10支车队（不包括凯迪拉克）的平均估值是去年收入的10.3倍。

    这标志着过去七年F1车队商业模式的转变，意味着F1车队的价值与它们巨大的增长潜力相关，而不是实物资产和历史现金流。

    拥有一支一级方程式车队的股份因此被比作拥有一支有价值的美国体育俱乐部，因为一级方程式车队的收入倍数现在正接近NFL和NBA，并且远远超过NHL、MLB和欧洲足球。

    根据《福布斯》杂志，收入倍数的上升是2021年成本上限（2026年为2.15亿美元）的结果，这一上限限制了支出。

    成本上限加上康科德协议，使得法拉利、红牛、小红牛、梅赛德斯-奔驰、哈斯和阿尔派在2025年实现了盈利——预计麦克拉伦在2026年的利润将达到2亿美元。

    据估计，梅赛德斯-奔驰的利润最高，达到2.25亿美元。

    在赛道上只有11支车队的情况下，能够拥有一支F1车队的一部分是非常罕见的。体育新闻网站Sportico在11月报道，马克·沃尔特的凯迪拉克股份至少以30亿美元的价格出售。

    自由媒体CEO德里克·张最近表示，F1在美国取得了“巨大进展”，这是其持续关注的领域，而中国和印度“代表着F1和MotoGP的机会”。

    到2027年，梅赛德斯-奔驰和法拉利的价值预计将达到100亿美元。

    在这里拉斯维加斯实地考察，福布斯报道的F1车队平均价值47%的增长对我来说并不意外。

    我本周将与Liberty Media首席执行官Derek Chang进行一次采访，在《赛车商业》杂志上，听他谈论时，你能感受到管理这项赛事的人们对美国前景的乐观情绪。漫步在这座城市中，你就能理解为什么了。

    今年我参加了所有的三场美国大奖赛。在迈阿密和奥斯汀，确实感受到了一股商业热潮，但在这里的拉斯维加斯，感觉就像达到了高潮。就像拉斯维加斯的大部分事物一样。

    我与的团队商业总监们交谈时，他们几乎被这种兴趣所淹没。他们在四处寻找维修区通行证，或者试图把更多的人塞进Paddock Club。无论你去哪里，都能看到品牌活动。美国公司几乎是在往一级方程式上砸钱。

    看看F1自己在三天内宣布了什么。渣打银行升级了其合作伙伴关系。耐克签约了。Whoop也签约了。谁能想到，就在几年前，F1的赞助名单上会有可穿戴设备这一类别？这个系列正处于健康发展的状态。

    在六月的摩纳哥赛事中，阿斯顿·马丁的杰弗森·斯莱克问道：“我们的估值是收入六到七倍。为什么一支NBA球队的估值是收入十二倍？”《福布斯》现在将10支已建立的F1车队估值平均为收入10.3倍。从这个角度来看，杰夫看到的差距已经大部分缩小了。

    我们应该给这些个别数字多少权重？《福布斯》和《体育ico》相隔八天发表，仅法拉利一项就相差13亿美元。在赛季间歇期，我们将重新审视我们对车队所有权和估值的分析，并查看这两份报告背后的假设与人们实际达成的交易相比如何。

    或许争夺传球权是一个不错的衡量争夺所有权的指标。只有11支车队，但你通常还能找到一支更多的传球。如果传闻中的阿尔卑斯或凯迪拉克的股份出售在圣诞节前完成，我们可能会得到一个明确的数据点，了解现在永久进入的成本。

    英文原文

    The value of each Formula 1 team has jumped 47% from $3.6billion USD in 2025 to $5.2bn in 2026 according to Forbes' 2026 report.

    The report puts Ferrari highest at $9.5bn to Mercedes's $9.2bn, putting those two most valuable F1 teams on par with the top half of the NFL's teams, the three most valuable NBA teams, and Real Madrid.

    F1's 11 teams are therefore valued at an average of $5.2bn and, not including Cadillac - which was absent from the 2025 rankings - the average is up to $5.4bn, a gain of 53% from last year.

    Haas has made the largest increase in value - of 87%, from $1.5bn to $2.8bn - while McLaren's value has increased 73% to $7.6bn. Scroll down to view the full valuations table.

    Forbes uses estimated revenue as part of its calculations: from 2024 to 2025, estimated revenue rose 15% to $530m per team.

    Its report says that McLaren and Mercedes expect to surpass $1bn for the first time ever in 2026 - and only the NFL's Dallas Cowboys and football teams Real Madrid and Barcelona have reached that mark.

    The report highlights “multiples” as a catalyst for F1 teams' growth: in 2019, F1 teams were valued at 2.3 times their previous season's revenue and the 10 teams (excluding Cadillac) are now valued at 10.3 times last year's revenue on average.

    This signals a transformation of F1 teams' business models over the last seven years, implying that the value of an F1 team is tied to their huge potential for growth rather than physical assets and historic cashflow.

    Owning a stake of an F1 team is therefore likened to owning that of a valuable US sports franchise, with F1 teams' revenue multiples now closing in on the NFL and NBA - and well ahead of the NHL, MLB, and European football.

    The upswing in multiples is, according to Forbes, a consequence of the 2021 cost cap (at $215m for 2026) that limited spending.

    The cost cap, plus Concorde Agreements, have led Ferrari, Red Bull, Racing Bulls, Mercedes, Haas, and Alpine to be profitable in 2025 - with McLaren expected to hit a profit of $200m in 2026.

    Mercedes is estimated to have the highest profit of $225m.

    With just 11 teams on the grid, it's rare to be able to own a piece of an F1 team. Sportico reported in November that Mark Walter's stake in Cadillac is being offered for at least $3bn.

    Liberty Media CEO Derek Chang recently said that F1 has made “huge inroads” into the United States, a continued area of its focus, while China and India “represent opportunities” both for F1 and MotoGP.

    In 2027, the values of Mercedes and Ferrari are projected to hit $10bn.

    Being on the ground here in Las Vegas, it's no surprise to me that Forbes is reporting a 47% increase in the average value of a Formula 1 team.

    I've been talking to Liberty Media CEO Derek Chang for an upcoming interview in The Race Business this week and listening to him you get a sense of how buoyant the people running the championship feel about its prospects in America. Walk through the city and you can understand why.

    I've been to all three US grands prix this year. There was a real commercial buzz in Miami and Austin, but here in Vegas it feels like it's reaching fever pitch. As most things do in Vegas.

    The team commercial directors I've spoken to are almost overwhelmed by the interest. They're scrabbling around for paddock passes or trying to squeeze one more prospect into the Paddock Club. Everywhere you go there's a brand event. American companies are practically throwing money at Formula 1.

    Look at what F1 itself announced in three days. Standard Chartered upgraded its partnership. Nike signed up. So did Whoop. Who would have imagined, even a few years ago, that there'd be a wearables category on F1's sponsorship list? The series is in rude health.

    At our Monaco event in June, Aston Martin's Jefferson Slack asked “We're valued at six or seven times revenue. Why is an NBA team valued at 12 times revenue?” Forbes now puts the 10 established F1 teams at an average of 10.3 times revenue. On that measure, much of the gap Jeff saw has already closed.

    How much weight should we put on the individual numbers? Forbes and Sportico published eight days apart and disagreed by $1.3bn on Ferrari alone. Over the off season we'll revisit our work on team ownership and valuations and look at how the assumptions behind both reports stack up against the deals people have actually done.

    Perhaps the scrabble for passes is a decent KPI for the scrabble for ownership. There are only 11 teams and yet you can usually find one more pass. If either of the rumoured Alpine or Cadillac stake sales gets over the line before Christmas, we might get a firm data point of what it now costs to get in permanently.
