    Edd Straw发布2026年拉斯维加斯大奖赛车手排名

    Edd Straw在新的排名体系中评价了拉斯维加斯大奖赛中表现最佳和最差的车手，维斯塔潘在比赛中表现出色。

    谁在F1拉斯维加斯大奖赛的疯狂超车盛宴中表现最好（和最差）？

    这是Edd Straw在他的新等级排名驾驶员格式中的判断。

    通常，您可以通过在The Race Members' Club的这篇帖子下评论来询问Edd的排名 - 并尝试使用我们方便的视觉工具编制自己的巴西大奖赛等级列表！

    马克斯·维斯塔潘在排位赛中表现出色，尽管他只因为基米·安东内利的处罚而继承了杆位，但他最初的结果落后只是因为梅赛德斯车手的巨大牵引。

    但真正让维斯塔潘脱颖而出的是这场比赛：他领先超过90%的比赛距离，当其他人展示出事情如何轻易失控时，他从未看起来会失去比赛。

    这个级别包括了三位在卡洛斯·塞恩斯、皮埃尔·加斯利和兰多·诺里斯中发挥最好的车手。

    尽管奥斯卡·皮亚斯蒂的排位赛表现没有麦克拉伦队友诺里斯那么出色，但在比赛中仅落后他一步，完成了领奖台，而且在从第17位发车后需要从罚站中恢复过来，这帮助他进入了这个级别。

    威廉姆斯车队第八名完成者塞恩斯也克服了罚站，在第一圈就锁定了位置，并在整个比赛中在积分圈内徘徊，是唯一一位来自最快六支车队之外的车手得分。这与加斯利在保持在与更快车队同一组中取得第五名，为阿尔派赢得一个强劲的位置相似。

    弗朗哥·科拉平托与他的最佳大奖赛成绩持平，以第六名的成绩结束了一场精彩的比赛，但他在资格赛和比赛中缺乏他的阿尔卑斯车队队友的优势，不得不坐在加斯利的下面。

    奥利·贝尔曼从最后一排的处罚中恢复过来，以大约13名的成绩结束，足够高的成绩，足以让哈斯车队保持第七名的车队积分。

    对于刘易斯·汉密尔顿和伊萨克·哈迪亚尔来说，他们的周末因为一系列不幸和处罚而受到影响，这些处罚并不是明显的错误，但必须计入他们的账上。

    考虑到汉密尔顿在比赛中携带地板损坏，他的资格赛表现得很出色，在被法拉利车队的队友查尔斯·勒克莱尔推入旋转之前，他表现得很出色。然而，他之前因为离开赛道获得优势而被处以10秒处罚是可避免的，如果他只是合理地切角以防止碰撞，并归还位置的话。

    至于哈达尔，他本来有机会赢得胜利，但在安全车阶段他的后备策略表现不佳，这意味着即使他没有前翼问题并赢得了比赛——他至少能进入前三——他也会因为被罚10秒而失去这个结果。

    利亚姆·劳森在第八位顺利完成了资格赛，但比他的小红牛车队队友阿维德·林德布拉德落后两位，由于燃油泄漏导致他损失了一圈，他失去了在比赛中大放异彩的机会。即使安全车出动并帮他追回了一圈，他也没有时间重新加入车队，所以在那种情况下，从14位起步追回一些位置，虽然徒劳，但也是一种不错的表现。

    奥迪车手们都度过了一个稳固的周末，加布里埃尔·博尔特洛托的情况尤其糟糕，但如果有车手能匹配萨因斯的比赛驾驶质量，也许会有一些积分机会，这将为博尔特洛托和尼科·胡肯伯格赢得中等水平的成绩。

    至于费尔南多·阿隆索，那辆无望的阿斯顿·马丁给他提供的帮助很少，这使得很难公平地评价他，但考虑到他还有额外的动力单元问题，他至少有一个不错的周末。

    拉斐尔·卡马拉被纳入这个小组，因为他虽然落后于哈斯车队的队友贝尔曼的速度，在缺乏经验和被塞尔吉奥·佩雷斯撞墙的情况下，他仍然完成了专业的工作。

    兰斯·斯特罗尔和瓦尔特里·博塔斯都有足够的速度，但周末的表现并不突出，他们的速度不如队友快，但他们避免了犯下任何重大错误。这意味着他们因为不尽人意的但还算胜任的周末而在这个小组中。

    安东内利的案例中，他因为在一个关键时刻的失误而滑入这个小组。虽然这是对规则的挫败，但他需要意识到这一点，这让他失去了胜利的机会。

    他获得了杆位，但他承认，他的超级拖车更多是偶然而非有意策略，因为他在被迫进攻后，没有那个的话，他的排位赛速度并不特别出色。

    亚历克斯·阿隆索是这个小组中不幸的车手，因为在排位赛中缺少拖车，加上刹车问题，他没有任何机会开始比赛。

    这三个人在比赛中都犯了重大错误，这影响了他们的成绩，对于佩雷斯来说，他因此在下一次比赛中被处以五位的发车位置罚分。

    乔治·拉塞尔速度快，他在比赛中大部分时间都在和维斯塔潘交换位置，试图找出如何进行最后的冲刺，但他从未到达那里，因为他滑到了队友安东内利的后面，然后在5号弯道犯了错误，最终撞到了墙上。因此，他以第九名的成绩完成了本可以赢得的比赛。

    勒克莱尔在需要与法拉利队友合作时攻击汉密尔顿的愚蠢行为，加上尽管有下压力优势，他在排位赛中的表现似乎比汉密尔顿更差，这使得这个周末成为了一个难忘的周末。

    林德布拉德在拉斯维加斯的比赛中表现相当出色，但被安全车卷入并因此错失重大胜利，这显然是一个新手犯的错误，它抵消了他之前所有的出色表现。虽然他提到了换挡问题，但最终这必须归咎于他的失误。

    Who actually did the best (and worst) job in Formula 1’s wild overtaking-fest Las Vegas Grand Prix?

    Here’s Edd Straw’s judgement in his new tier list driver rankings format.

    As usual, you can ask your questions about Edd's rankings by commenting on this post in The Race Members' Club - and have a crack at compiling your own Brazilian GP tier list with our handy visual tool!

    Max Verstappen excelled in qualifying, as even though he only inherited pole position thanks to Kimi Antonelli’s penalty he was only behind in the initial results because of the Mercedes driver’s monster tow.

    But it was the race that really marked Verstappen out: leading over 90% of it by distance and never looking like losing it when others showed how easy it was for things to unravel.

    This tier includes a trio of drivers who got the most out of the race in Carlos Sainz, Pierre Gasly and Lando Norris.

    And while Oscar Piastri’s qualifying performance wasn’t as sharp as McLaren team-mate Norris’s, finishing just behind him in the race as they completed the podium, the added complication of having to recover from 17th on the grid after a grid penalty helps to elevate him into this tier.

    Williams’s eighth-place finisher Sainz also recovered from a penalty and after nailing the first lap, he spent the whole race in and around the points and was the only driver to score from outside the fastest half-dozen teams. That was similar to Gasly’s achievement in hanging in the group of quicker cars to bag a strong fifth place for Alpine.

    Franco Colapinto matched his best grand prix result with sixth, driving a good race to cap a strong weekend, but has to sit a tier below Gasly as he lacked his Alpine team-mate’s edge in qualifying and the race.

    Ollie Bearman recovered from a back-of-the-grid penalty to finish about as high as a Haas could have done in 13th, doing enough to trouble the Audi drivers to protect his team’s seventh place in the championship.

    In the cases of Lewis Hamilton and Isack Hadjar, both had strong weekends undone by a mix of misfortune and penalties that weren’t overt errors but have to count against them.

    Hamilton’s qualifying was well-executed given he carried floor damage and he raced well before being tipped into a spin by Ferrari team-mate Charles Leclerc, although the earlier 10-second penalty he was hit with for gaining an advantage by leaving the track was avoidable had he given the place back after justifiably cutting the corner to prevent a collision.

    As for Hadjar, he was in the mix for victory but played the backing-up game poorly under the safety car, meaning even if he had not had the front wing problem and won the race - and he’d surely have been in the top three at least - he’d have lost the result to the 10-second penalty he was hit with.

    Liam Lawson qualified decently in eighth, but that was two places behind Racing Bulls team-mate Arvid Lindblad, and he was denied the chance to make a big impact in the race by the fuel leak that cost him a lap. Even when the safety car was deployed and he got that lap back, he didn’t have time to rejoin the pack, so in that context making up some places to finish 14th represented a good, if fruitless, drive.

    The Audi drivers both had solid weekends, Gabriel Bortoleto’s particularly blighted by problems, but there was perhaps a minor points opportunity had the drivers matched Sainz’s quality of race drive, earning both Bortoleto and Nico Hulkenberg a middling tier.

    As for Fernando Alonso, the hopeless Aston Martin gave him little to work with and makes it difficult to evaluate him fairly, but he at least had a decent weekend given the additional power unit problems he had.

    Rafael Camara is included in the group as while he was off Haas team-mate Bearman’s pace, in the circumstances of his lack of experience and being booted into the wall by Sergio Perez, he did a professional job.

    Lance Stroll and Valtteri Bottas both had adequate but unremarkable weekends with their underlying pace not as sharp as their team-mates’, but they avoided making any significant errors. That means they are in this group for underwhelming, but competent, weekends.

    In Antonelli’s case, he slips into this group because of the misjudgement he made in lifting at a key moment. While that’s a frustrating negative of the regulations, it was something he needed to be aware of and cost him a shot at victory.

    He gets credit for pole position but he admitted that his monster tow was as much happenstance as a deliberate strategy after he was forced to attack, and without that his qualifying pace was nothing extraordinary.

    Alex Albon is the unfortunate driver in this group with the lack of a tow in qualifying and no chance to start the race thanks to brake problems.

    This trio all made significant errors in the race, which compromised their results and, in the case of Perez, earned him a five-place grid penalty for the next race.

    George Russell was quick and spent a good part of the race swapping places with Verstappen and trying to work out how to play the final shootout, but never made it there as he slipped behind his team-mate Antonelli then made the Turn 5 error that ended up with him in the wall. He finished ninth in a race he could have won for that reason.

    Leclerc’s foolishness in attacking Hamilton when co-operation with his Ferrari team-mate was needed, combined with the fact he seemed to struggle more in qualifying than Hamilton despite a downforce advantage, made this a weekend to forget.

    So much of what Lindblad did in Las Vegas was superb, but spinning under the safety car and throwing away a big result was a rookie error that undid all of the good work. While he referenced a bad shift, ultimately this has to go down as his blunder.
