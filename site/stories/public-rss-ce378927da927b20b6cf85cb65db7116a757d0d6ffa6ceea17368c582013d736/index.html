    阿隆索将重返达喀尔拉力赛

    阿隆索确认今年冬天重返达喀尔拉力赛，这将是他自2020年首次参赛以来的再次出战，他仍认为这一项目“几乎不可能”，但视其为迈向剩余目标的一步。

    阿隆索重返达喀尔：为何他的首次拉力赛经历是一次对系统的冲击

    阿隆索仍认为他的达喀尔项目“几乎不可能”，但将参加2027年赛事视为迈向自己剩余目标之一——赢得世界最艰难拉力赛——的又一步

    阿隆索在拉斯维加斯重申，在他看来，F1在2030年V8引擎回归之前已抛弃了16年的纯粹赛车，与此同时，这位西班牙人继续在赛车运动巅峰之外寻求那些纯粹的挑战。

    围场内此前已流传一段时间，阿隆索将在今年冬天重返达喀尔拉力赛，这将是他自2020年以来的首次，尽管这位两届F1世界冠军在正式宣布前仍与媒体打太极。

    当被问及他是否真的会去参加达喀尔拉力赛时，阿隆索回答说，他认为这“几乎不可能”，尤其是因为这项挑战与F1如此不同。

    如今已正式确认，阿隆索确实将驾驶一辆基于达契亚Sandrider打造、并采用阿斯顿·马丁涂装的Prodrive赛车参赛，这位西班牙人笑着说：“我没有改变我的看法（关于这几乎不可能）！我认为2027年的达喀尔将比2020年对我而言是更大的挑战。

    “当时我有六个月的准备时间。我去了不同的国家测试，包括卡塔尔、纳米比亚、摩洛哥和波兰。那是一个相当广泛的计划。此外，我在沙特参加了两次拉力赛。现在我在摩洛哥只有两天时间，然后就要出发了，”阿隆索在接受Motorsport.com采访时被问及挑战的规模时说道。

    “这有点短，但最终我们与Prodrive和阿斯顿一起决定，这对各方来说仍然是一次很好的经历，尤其是对我而言，因为我想赢得达喀尔的愿望不是什么秘密。这一次不可能实现，但我认为这是对未来的投资，可以积累更多里程，并更多地了解这项赛事。”

    重点主要在于积累经验，而且获胜将尤其困难，这一点也反映在阿隆索对即将到来的达喀尔拉力赛的态度上。

    “我认为这是我尝试过的最大挑战之一，因为缺乏准备，但我会尽量在第一周进行测试，并希望能在第二周参赛。”

    据阿隆索所说，这项赛事之所以成为如此重大的挑战，是因为它与F1的许多核心内容完全相反。他第一次体验拉力赛甚至让他感到震惊。

    “我记得在达喀尔测试的头几天，我晚上会头痛。我认为这来自于我不习惯的专注程度。眼睛，你四五个小时都不能眨，”他说。

    “在这里，我们会在方向盘上调整设置，通过无线电通话，查看屏幕并关注比赛进程，因为一圈又一圈你始终处于同一个狭小环境里。而在达喀尔，一切都是未知的，每一米都是全新的。”

    因此，阿隆索觉得自己能完全掌控赛车，但无法掌控环境条件，这与F1不同。

    “我无法完全掌控接近危险时的速度，尽管我有马克·科马这样出色的领航员。当你在茫茫荒野中全油门行驶10公里后，他在远处看到一点植被或绿色的东西，他总是告诉我，只要有绿色或植被，就意味着路面颠簸，”阿隆索解释道。

    “所以这类事情是我在驾驶过程中、借助他的经验学到的，但我想对其他车手来说，所有这些事情都是自然而然的，因为他们都经历过。我是在比赛进行中快速学习的过程，但作为一名车手，这对我来说非常着迷。”

    总有一天，这应该会带来一场达喀尔拉力赛的胜利，这是阿隆索很乐意加入他令人印象深刻的赛车运动履历中的一项成就，但他认为在今年冬季的比赛之前，他的对手们仍然拥有显著的优势。

    “我认为，以我的背景来看，挑战会比在那个环境中长大的人更大。我觉得我在2020年很有竞争力，但正如我所说，我做了充分的六个月准备。在那之前我已经积累了好几千公里的里程，而现在我没有这些。”

    “我的队友塞巴斯蒂安·勒布大概是历史上最伟大的拉力赛车手。他尝试了达喀尔九次或十次，却还没有取得胜利。我完全清楚其中的难度。”

    “这是一个非常巨大的挑战，但正因如此它才吸引人。正因如此它才有意思，也正因如此我才想在未来某一天努力赢得它。”

    Fernando Alonso returns to Dakar: Why his first rally-raid experience was a shock to the system

    Fernando Alonso still believes his Dakar project is "almost impossible", but sees his participation in 2027 as another step towards one of his remaining goals: winning the world's toughest rally

    While Fernando Alonso reiterated in Las Vegas that, in his view, Formula 1 has thrown away 16 years of pure racing before the return of V8 engines in 2030, the Spaniard continues to seek those pure challenges outside the pinnacle of motorsport as well.

    It had been known in the paddock for some time that Alonso would return to the Dakar Rally this winter for the first time since 2020, although the two-time F1 world champion still played games with the media ahead of the official announcement.

    Asked whether he would actually head to the Dakar, Alonso replied that he considered it “almost impossible”, particularly because the challenge is so different from F1.

    Now that it has been officially confirmed that Alonso will indeed compete in a Prodrive car based on the Dacia Sandrider and featuring Aston Martin colours, the Spaniard laughs: "I didn't change my opinion [about it being almost impossible]! I think the Dakar in 2027 will be a much bigger challenge than it was for me in 2020.

    "Back then I had six months of preparation. I went to different countries to test, including Qatar, Namibia, Morocco and Poland. It was quite an extensive program. Plus, I did two rallies in Saudi. Now I have two days in Morocco and off we go," Alonso said, when asked about the extent of the challenge by Motorsport.com.

    "That's a bit short, but at the end of the day we decided, together with the Prodrive and with Aston, that it's still a good experience for all parties, especially for me, because there's no secret that one day I want to win Dakar. It will not be possible this time, but I think it's an investment for the future in terms of accumulating more kilometres and knowing more about the series."

    The fact that the focus is primarily on gaining experience, and that winning will be particularly difficult, is also reflected in Alonso's approach to the upcoming Dakar.

    "I think this is one of the biggest challenges of the ones that I have attempted because of the lack of preparation, but I will try to test in the first week and hopefully race in the second week."

    According to Alonso, what makes it such a major challenge is that it is the complete opposite of much of what F1 is about. His very first experience of rally-raid racing even came as a shock to the system.

    "I remember the first days when I tested in Dakar, I had a headache in the night. I think it's coming from the level of focus that I'm not used to. The eyes, you cannot blink for four or five hours," he said.

    "While here, we change things on the steering wheel, we talk on the radio, we look at the screens and we follow the race, because you are in the same little environment lap after lap. In Dakar, it's into the unknown, every meter is new."

    As a result, Alonso feels that he has full control of the car, but not of the conditions, unlike in F1.

    "What I don't have is full control of the speed I approach dangers with, even though I have a great co-driver with Marc Coma. After you go full throttle for 10 kilometres in the middle of nothing, then in the distance he sees a little bit of vegetation or something green, and he always tells me that when there is something green or vegetation, it means a bumpy ride," Alonso explained.

    "So these kind of things I'm learning while driving, thanks to his experience, but I imagine that for the other drivers, all these things come automatically because they went through all these things. I'm in a fast-learning process while I'm in the middle of the race, but that's fascinating for me as a driver."

    One day, it should lead to a Dakar victory, a triumph Alonso would love to add to his impressive motorsport CV, but he believes his rivals still have a significant advantage heading into this winter's edition.

    "I think, coming from my background, the challenge is going to be bigger than if you grew up in that environment. I think I was competitive [in 2020], but as I said, I had a good six months of preparation. I had a good few thousands of kilometres before that, which I don't have now.

    "My team-mate Sebastien Loeb is probably the greatest rally driver in history. He tried Dakar nine or 10 times and he didn't achieve the win yet. I'm fully aware of the difficulty.

    "It's a very big challenge, but that's why it's appealing. That's why it's interesting and that's why I want to, in the future, try to win it one day."
