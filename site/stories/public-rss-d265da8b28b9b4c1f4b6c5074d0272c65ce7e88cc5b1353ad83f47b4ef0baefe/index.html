    诺里斯：卡塔尔站梅赛德斯“处于另一个水平”

    卡塔尔大奖赛周五两节练习赛均以安东内利和拉塞尔的梅赛德斯一二名收尾，其他车手无法匹配他们的速度，诺里斯称梅赛德斯“处于另一个水平”。

    诺里斯：卡塔尔站梅赛德斯“处于另一个水平”

    领跑者安东内利对迈凯伦和红牛车手组合握有接近半秒的优势，法拉利则深陷困境

    卡塔尔大奖赛周五的两节练习赛均以新科世界冠军安东内利与其梅赛德斯队友拉塞尔紧咬的一二名收尾，但在这条高速赛道上，其他人都无法匹配他们的惊人速度。

    在代表性较弱、于白天较热时段进行的第一节练习赛中，拉塞尔落后安东内利半秒，但在第二节练习赛中，他把与另一位梅赛德斯车手的差距缩小到约0.4秒。

    兰多·诺里斯和奥斯卡·皮亚斯特里驾驶的迈凯伦赛车，以及马克斯·维斯塔潘和伊萨克·哈贾尔驾驶的红牛赛车，在第一节练习赛中大幅缩小了此前巨大的差距，但第二节练习赛结束时仍比安东内利慢了约半秒。

    刘易斯·汉密尔顿和查尔斯·勒克莱尔驾驶的法拉利赛车也稍微接近了一些，但第二节练习赛结束时仍落后超过一秒。

    “我认为除了梅赛德斯之外，我们相对于其他所有人的表现看起来都还可以，”诺里斯赛后说道。“与其他所有人相比，梅赛德斯就是处在另一个水平。这总体上说明了我们落后了多少。

    “并不是说有什么东西在这里对他们特别有效。我的意思是，你可以清楚地看到，如果他们在这里很快，出于同样的原因，他们在很多其他地方也很快。所以我认为这更多是让我们认识到自己相对于他们所处的位置。”

    诺里斯不祥地预测，迈凯伦无法通过连夜调校工作来弥补差距。

    “我认为差距大概就是那样，”他说，“我得说每圈大约零点七秒。我们达不到那个水平。”

    维斯塔潘对于连夜在自己的赛车上解锁一些东西的前景略为乐观，他观察到——正如今年周五经常出现的情况——小红牛22的前后轴感觉“脱节”。更多的平衡或许还能带来零点几秒的提升，甚至可能达到半秒。

    “他们（梅赛德斯）在直道上可能很快，但平衡性或许也比我们目前的状态稍好一些，”他说，“我们自己都不满意，所以当你自己都不满意时，很难跑出同样的数字。”

    卢赛尔赛道只有一个慢弯，这或许能解释法拉利的重大差距。两辆梅赛德斯W17在赛道上最后一段高速连续右弯中显得特别快，在那里，平衡和下压力能让你在14号弯出口不冲出赛道边缘。

    勒克莱尔则表示，法拉利仍在琢磨这个差距有多少是因为SF-26意外地慢，又有多少是因为梅赛德斯超出了预期。

    “从今天上午的第一圈到FP2的最后一圈，我们都非常慢，很难理解异常点在哪里，”他说。

    “我认为不只是我们，对所有人来说都是如此，尤其是与梅赛德斯相比，他们似乎……他们总是拥有最好的赛车，但现在差距大到了我们未曾预料的地步。”

    法拉利体育总监迭戈·约韦尔诺表示认同：“今天梅赛德斯看起来特别快，不仅比我们快，也比其他车队快。”

    “原因可能有很多——他们在这里的套件表现比本赛季其余时间更好，或者他们在计划方面做出了不同的选择。不管是什么，动力、设定、车重、载油量，无论什么。他们很强。

    “他们显然比其他所有车队都领先一步。我们明天看吧，在周末剩余的时间里，他们的表现会如何。”

    Lando Norris: Mercedes “at a different level” in Qatar

    Pacesetter Kimi Antonelli had close to half a second in hand over the McLaren and Red Bull pairings, with Ferrari in real trouble

    Both practice sessions on Friday at the Formula 1 Qatar Grand Prix ended with new world champion Kimi Antonelli and his Mercedes team-mate George Russell in a closely matched 1-2, but nobody else could match their blistering pace around this high-speed circuit.

    Russell was half a second off Antonelli in the less representative first session, held during hotter daylight hours, but he found the best part of four tenths relative to the other Mercedes in FP2.

    The McLarens of Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri, along with the Red Bulls of Max Verstappen and Isack Hadjar, made some inroads into what had been a yawning gap in FP1, but still ended the second session in the order of half a second slower than Antonelli.

    The Ferraris of Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc also got a little closer but still ended FP2 over a second away.

    “I think we look okay relative to everyone, except Mercedes,” said Norris afterwards. “Mercedes are just at a different level comparing to everyone else. It shows just in general how far off we are.

    “It's not like something works better for them here. I mean, you can just clearly see if they're quick here, they're quick in a lot of other places for the same reason. So I think it's more a realisation of where we stand relative to them.”

    Ominously, Norris predicted that McLaren would not be able to make up the deficit through overnight set-up work.

    “I think the gap's probably right,” he said. “I'd say about seven tenths a lap. We're not getting close to that.”

    Verstappen was slightly more optimistic about the prospects of unlocking something in his car overnight, observing that – as has been so often the case on Fridays this year – the RB22’s front and rear axles felt “disconnected”. More balance could yet yield a couple of tenths, if not half a second.

    “They [Mercedes] are probably quick on the straight, but also maybe a bit nicer balance than what we have at the moment,” he said. “We are not happy ourselves, so when you're not happy yourself, it's very hard to get the same number.”

    The Lusail track has just one slow corner, which perhaps accounts for Ferrari’s major deficit. Both Mercedes W17s seemed particularly quick around the fast sequence of right-handers towards the end of the lap, where balance and downforce are rewarded by not skittering wide over the exit kerb of Turn 14.

    Leclerc, for his part, said Ferrari was still wondering how much of the deficit was the SF-26 being unexpectedly slow, or the Mercedes exceeding expectations.

    “We were just very slow from the first lap this morning to the last lap in FP2 and it's very difficult to understand what is the outlier,” he said.

    “I think not only for us but for everybody, particularly comparing to Mercedes, that seems to be... They always kind of have the best car, but now it's to an extent that we did not expect.”

    “Today Mercedes seems particularly quick, not only compared to us, but also compared to the others,” agreed Ferrari sporting director Diego Ioverno.

    “It can be whatever reason – that their package here is performing even better than in the remainder of the season, or maybe they've done different choices in terms of plan. Whatever it is, power, setting, weight of the car, fuel load, whatever. They are strong.

    “They are apparently a step in front of all the others. We'll see tomorrow, in the remainder of the weekend, how they will perform.”
