    勒克莱尔谈记者禁入：对措辞选择感到惊讶

    勒克莱尔表示自己并未参与禁止资深F1记者进入维修区，且不希望监管媒体，尽管报道有些个人化。

    查尔斯·勒克莱尔对记者禁令表示：“我对用词的选择感到惊讶”

    查尔斯·勒克莱尔就国际汽联对一名记者的禁令打破沉默，质疑了作者用词的选择

    对于法拉利车迷来说。最新的Scuderia 法拉利 F1新闻，车手更新，技术分析和来自马拉内洛及更远处的比赛反应。

    查尔斯·勒克莱尔并没有参与阻止一名资深的Formula 1记者进入维修区，他也不感兴趣于监管媒体——即使当头条变得有些个人化时。

    在2026年拉斯维加斯大奖赛之前面对媒体时，法拉利车手首次就国际汽联撤销《每日邮报》记者乔纳森·麦克埃voy赛季剩余三场比赛资格的决定发表了看法。

    "我看了那篇文章；我对用词的选择感到相当惊讶，但除此之外，我没有什么可说的。", 勒克莱尔说。"至于处罚本身，显然我与此事毫无关系，对此我也没什么可说的。"

    尽管他对专栏感到惊讶，勒克莱尔还是在常规媒体审查和麦克埃voy使用的特定措辞之间划了一条界限。

    "我只想说的是，作为一级方程式车手，我认为我们已经习惯了这种批评。不是这种批评，而是批评本身和对批评者的批评，勒克莱尔澄清说。""记者的工作当然是要提出一些尖锐的问题，有时还要做一些棘手的标题。但我对用词的选择感到惊讶。"

    争议始于麦克埃voy发表了一篇评论文章，回顾了墨西哥城大奖赛首圈勒克莱尔与法拉利车队队友刘易斯·汉密尔顿之间的接触，他在文中将勒克莱尔称为一个“狡猾的小杂种”。

    国际汽联总经理阿尔贝托·维拉雷尔随后通知麦克埃voy，他的资格认证被暂停，适用于拉斯维加斯、卡塔尔和阿布扎比，尽管他预计将在2027赛季回归。确认这一处罚时，国际汽联发言人引用了2026年一级方程式世界锦标赛指南，指出媒体通行证是由管理机构单独发放的，并需要“最高标准的公平和专业性”。

    在随后的专栏中宣布他的禁令时，麦克埃voy为自己的勒克莱尔言论辩护，称其为“轻松的”和“玩笑式的”，同时指责法拉利车队负责人弗雷德·瓦瑟尔“出卖”了他——将这位法国人描述为一个“胖乎乎的小人物”，他“不知所措”。没有证据将瓦瑟尔或法拉利与国际汽联的纪律处分联系起来。

    暂停引发了立即的僵局。《每日邮报》宣布全面抵制F1赛事和新闻发布会的报道，称禁令为“严厉的”和“对新闻自由的史无前例且令人愤慨的攻击”。体育部门负责人克里斯·迪恩要求立即恢复麦克埃voy的职位，前F1巨头伯尼·埃克莱斯顿也发表了评论，称国际汽联的行动“完全疯狂”。

    Charles Leclerc on journalist ban: “I was surprised by the choice of words”

    Charles Leclerc breaks his silence on the FIA ban of a journalist, questioning the writer's choice of words

    For Ferrari fans. The latest Scuderia Ferrari F1 news, driver updates, technical analysis and race reaction from Maranello and beyond.

    Charles Leclerc had no hand in getting a veteran Formula 1 reporter barred from the paddock, and he isn't interested in policing the press - even when the headlines get a little personal.

    Addressing the media ahead of the 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix, the Ferrari driver spoke for the first time about the FIA’s decision to revoke Daily Mail journalist Jonathan McEvoy’s accreditation for the remaining three rounds of the season.

    "I saw the article; I was quite surprised by the choice of words, but apart from that I don’t have much to say," Leclerc said. "Then on the sanction itself, obviously I’ve got absolutely nothing to do with it, and I don’t have much to say about it."

    Despite his surprise at the column, Leclerc drew a line between routine media scrutiny and the specific phrasing McEvoy used.

    "The only thing I’ll say is that as Formula 1 drivers, I think we are used to this kind of criticism. Not this kind of criticism, but to criticism in general and to critics," Leclerc clarified. "It’s definitely the job of journalists to kind of ask the bad questions and do some of the tough headlines sometimes. But I was surprised with the choice of words."

    The controversy began after McEvoy published an opinion piece reflecting on the opening-lap contact between Leclerc and Ferrari team-mate Lewis Hamilton at the Mexico City Grand Prix, in which he referred to Leclerc as a "sneaky little b*****d".

    FIA general manager Alberto Villarreal subsequently notified McEvoy that his accreditation was suspended for Las Vegas, Qatar, and Abu Dhabi, though he is expected to return for the 2027 season. Confirming the sanction, an FIA spokesperson cited the 2026 Formula 1 world championship guidelines, stating that media passes are issued at the governing body's sole discretion and require "the highest standards of fairness and professionalism".

    In a follow-up column announcing his ban, McEvoy defended his remark about Leclerc as "light-hearted" and "joshing", while alleging that Ferrari team principal Fred Vasseur had "grassed" on him - describing the Frenchman as a "tubby little nobody" who is "out of his depth". No evidence has linked Vasseur or Ferrari to the FIA’s disciplinary action.

    The suspension triggered an immediate standoff. The Daily Mail announced a full boycott of F1 race and press conference coverage for the final triple-header, calling the ban "draconian" and "an unprecedented and outrageous attack on the freedom of the press". Head of Sport Chris Dean demanded McEvoy’s immediate reinstatement, and former F1 supremo Bernie Ecclestone also weighed in, calling the FIA's move "completely mad".
