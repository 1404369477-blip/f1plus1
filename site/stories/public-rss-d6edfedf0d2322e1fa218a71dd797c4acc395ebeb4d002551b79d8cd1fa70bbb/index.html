    帕尔默：拉斯维加斯无人能复制维斯塔潘对拉塞尔的防守

    前F1车手乔利恩·帕尔默在F1 TV赛后节目中表示，拉斯维加斯大奖赛上维斯塔潘对拉塞尔的轮对轮防守，当前围场没有其他车手能做到。

    乔利恩·帕尔默：拉斯维加斯无人能复制维斯塔潘对拉塞尔的防守

    乔利恩·帕尔默表示，没有其他车手能完成维斯塔潘在拉斯维加斯对乔治·拉塞尔的那几次动作

    致红牛车迷。关注红牛车队，获取来自米尔顿凯恩斯的F1新闻、车手动态、技术解读与比赛分析。

    即便拉斯维加斯大奖赛全场出现352次超车和35次领先变化，马克斯·维斯塔潘这场胜利的关键时刻仍取决于轮对轮的胆识，而非电池能量分配表。

    前F1车手乔利恩·帕尔默在F1电视台赛后转播中盛赞维斯塔潘对乔治·拉塞尔的防守战，称这是当前发车格上任何对手都无法匹敌的表现——特别指出在梅赛德斯终于破解了红牛早期能量战术之后，维斯塔潘在比赛中段发起了一连串反击。

    这场头排对决在红灯熄灭之前就已背负着沉重的前情。维斯塔潘和拉塞尔在周六晚上抵达时都还带着火气，因为第三次练习赛中14号弯的碰撞引来了赛会的正式警告，拉塞尔痛斥这位荷兰人是个表现得“比别人都更大更好”的家伙。然而在50圈以高达300公里/小时速度进行的激烈较量中，两人在毫厘之间互有攻守，却始终没有发生接触。

    在开局阶段，维斯塔潘反复化解了拉塞尔和基米·安东内利的攻势，任由他们在直道上超越自己，随后在17号弯夺回第一。

    然而在第25圈安全车重启之后，拉塞尔调整了自己的能量部署——在第28圈和第31圈守住1号弯的领先位置，随后在第37圈将攻击点切换到17号弯。正是在那里，帕尔默看到了非同寻常的一幕。

    马克斯·维斯塔潘，红牛车队，乔治·拉塞尔，梅赛德斯

    “但对我来说，最神奇的是那三四次尝试，看起来乔治已经超过他了，”帕尔默说。“我不认为基米完全弄明白了安全车之后乔治到了哪里。然后他们在安全车下似乎想通了，有几次看起来已经板上钉钉了。

    “看起来乔治就要取得领先了。而我必须说，我不认为其他任何车手能像马克斯那样把它夺回来。

    “那种切入内线的坚韧，直接把车扔进去——我以为他要锁死轮胎，要打转，冷胎，真的很难，外面又脏又多尘，”帕尔默继续说道。“但他很有侵略性；他做得很精准。他设法在长长的左弯贴住内线，获得良好的牵引力，在3号弯从外侧绕过去。那就是神奇之处。”

    “令人屏息的”精准，紧张局势再度点燃

    帕尔默强调，虽然能量部署和尾流效应塑造了当晚的比赛，但“最终决定胜负的是赛车技巧”，并指出几乎任何其他车手都会让出弯角，等到下一圈在长直道上再反击。红牛车队领队洛朗·梅基斯在赛道边也表达了同样的看法。“他没有犯任何错误，”梅基斯对F1电视台说。“在这些圈里，他精确到最后一毫米、最后一公里每小时。看着令人屏息。”

    在维斯塔潘毫无瑕疵的同时，拉塞尔的攻势在第48圈瓦解，他在攻击安东内利时锁死轮胎，滑入5号弯的护栏，最终追回至第九名。赛后，维斯塔潘在新闻发布会上重提两人的恩怨，称拉塞尔“远低于我的水平”，并把那次迟来的让车称为“因果报应”。

    凭借六场比赛中的第四场胜利，维斯塔潘在争夺年度亚军的战斗中领先拉塞尔40分，证明他最有力的回击来自赛道。

    Jolyon Palmer: No rival could match Max Verstappen's fight against George Russell in Las Vegas

    Jolyon Palmer says no other driver could have pulled off Max Verstappen’s Las Vegas moves against George Russell

    For Red Bull fans. Keeping watch on Red Bull Racing, with F1 news, driver updates, technical insight and race analysis from Milton Keynes.

    Even in a Las Vegas Grand Prix packed with 352 overtakes and 35 lead changes, the defining moments of Max Verstappen’s victory came down to raw wheel-to-wheel nerve rather than battery spreadsheets.

    Speaking on F1 TV’s post-race broadcast, former Formula 1 driver Jolyon Palmer hailed Verstappen’s defensive fight against George Russell as a display no rival on the current grid could have matched - singling out a string of mid-race counterattacks after Mercedes finally cracked Red Bull’s early energy playbook.

    The front-row duel carried heavy baggage before the lights even went out. Verstappen and Russell arrived on Saturday night simmering after a Turn 14 clash in final practice that drew formal warnings from the stewards, with Russell slamming the Dutchman as someone who acts "bigger and better than everyone else". Yet across 50 intense laps at up to 300 km/h, the pair traded blows millimetres apart without making contact.

    Through the opening stint, Verstappen repeatedly neutralised Russell and Kimi Antonelli by letting them sweep past down the Strip before reclaiming first at Turn 17.

    Following the Lap 25 safety car restart, however, Russell adjusted his deployment - holding the lead into Turn 1 on Laps 28 and 31, and later switching his attack to Turn 17 on Lap 37. That was where Palmer saw something extraordinary.

    Max Verstappen, Red Bull Racing, George Russell, Mercedes

    "But the magic for me was the three or four attempts where it looked like George had done him," Palmer said. "I don't think Kimi ever quite cottoned on to where George got to after the safety car. Then they seemed to work it out under the safety car, and a few times it looked like it was a done deal.

    "It looked like that was going to be George into the race lead. And I have to say, I don't think any other driver would have been able to wrestle it back like Max did.

    "The tenacity to go inside, just throw a car in there - I thought he's going to lock up, he's going to spin, cold tyres, it's really tricky, it's dusty and dirty out there," Palmer continued. "But he was aggressive; he was precise with it. He managed to hug the inside through the long left, get some good traction to sweep round the outside at Turn 3. That was the magic."

    "Breathtaking" precision amid reignited tension

    Palmer stressed that while deployment and slipstreaming shaped the evening, "racecraft won it in the end," noting that almost any other driver would have ceded the corner and waited to fight back on the next lap down the Strip. Red Bull team principal Laurent Mekies echoed that verdict trackside. "He didn't put a foot wrong," Mekies told F1 TV. "He was to the last millimetre, to the last kph during these laps. It was breathtaking to watch."

    Where Verstappen stayed spotless, Russell’s charge unravelled on Lap 48 when he locked up attacking Antonelli and slid into the Turn 5 barrier, recovering to ninth. Afterwards, Verstappen revived their feud in the press conference, dismissing Russell as "so far below my level" and calling the late off "karma".

    With a fourth win in six races, Verstappen stretched 40 points clear of Russell in the fight for championship runner-up, proving his fiercest rebuttal came on the track.
