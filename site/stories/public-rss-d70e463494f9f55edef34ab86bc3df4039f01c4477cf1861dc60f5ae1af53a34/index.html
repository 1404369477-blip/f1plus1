    Kimi Antonelli crowned 2026 F1 world champion as Max Verstappen wins wild Mexico City Grand Prix from Lewis Hamilton

    Kimi Antonelli has won the 2026 F1 Drivers' Championship at 20 years and 68 days old, breaking Sebastian Vettel's record for youngest world champion by three years; Max Verstappen won a wild Mexico City Grand Prix from Lewis Hamilton amid drama between the Ferrari drivers

    Highlights from the 2026 Mexico City Grand Prix as Kimi Antonelli became the youngest world champion in F1 history

    Kimi Antonelli became the youngest Formula 1 world champion in history as Max Verstappen held off Lewis Hamilton to win a thrilling Mexico City Grand Prix.

    Antonelli finished third to secure the F1 Drivers' Championship with four rounds to spare, becoming the first Italian world champion since Alberto Ascari in 1953.

    The 20-year-old avoided all the chaos in one of the most action-packed races of the season and was hugely emotional after the chequered flag.

    Meanwhile, Verstappen took advantage of a big fight between the Ferraris of Hamilton and Charles Leclerc on the opening lap to claim his third win in four races.

    Pole-sitter Hamilton went side by side with Leclerc through the tight Turns 1 and 2 before Leclerc attacked again into Turn 4 around the outside.

    Lewis Hamilton overtook Ferrari team-mate Charles Leclerc before colliding with Max Verstappen as they battled for the lead in the race restart

    Both drivers went off the track but Leclerc went across the grass to take the lead, allowing Verstappen to capitalise by overtaking Hamilton.

    Leclerc was handed a five-second time penalty but a furious Hamilton said "the stewards are basically handling Charles this win" on the radio.

    Lewis Hamilton was left furious after Ferrari team-mate Charles Leclerc overtook him on the opening lap while leaving the track but was only given a five-second penalty for the infringement

    However, the race was stopped when Cadillac's Valtteri Bottas suffered a huge crash into the barriers as his suspension failed, leaving a standing-start restart with 26 laps remaining.

    Leclerc got a poor launch from second so Hamilton attacked Verstappen into Turn 1, with the pair making contact as the Red Bull driver came out on top.

    Even with a Safety Car for Racing Bulls' Arvid Lindblad crashing after tangling with McLaren's Oscar Piastri, Verstappen did just enough to keep Hamilton at bay with Antonelli just behind in third.
