    拉斯维加斯大奖赛排位赛：赢家与输家

    受雨水影响的练习赛让拉斯维加斯大奖赛排位赛更看重车手能否在第三节练习赛迅速进入状态。法拉利双车止步第二节排位赛，勒克莱尔在7号弯失误，汉密尔顿从第三节练习赛起表现不佳。

    受雨水影响的F1练习赛意味着，人们更加看重在第三节练习赛（FP3）中迅速进入状态，并把这种状态延续到拉斯维加斯大奖赛排位赛中。

    谁在关键时刻顶住了，谁又没能兑现此前的势头？

    对法拉利来说，这是跌宕起伏的一年，但在拉斯维加斯双车止步第二节排位赛（Q2）无疑是低谷之一——即便这条赛道的复杂特性让它有些特殊。

    夏尔·勒克莱尔在7号弯出现失误，未能完成第二个计时圈；而刘易斯·汉密尔顿从第三节练习赛起就显得缺乏说服力。

    勒克莱尔迅速指出，这个失误是“在大量挫败感之后发生的，随着我们在管理交通状况等方面犯下许多错误而不断累积”，并表示法拉利可能是第三次练习赛中出现的GPS问题的受害者。

    他补充说，法拉利必须全神贯注于提升轮胎温度，因为这是车队的“弱点”。

    汉密尔顿实事求是地谈到了他认为法拉利在哪里吃亏，他说：“在直道上损失很大一块。感觉我们没有很强的抓地力。我们一直很难让轮胎进入工作状态，我们就是有点找不到方向。”

    这很好地总结了情况。

    车队表示，他的最后一圈“非常非常好”，所以显然车队对汉密尔顿跑出的这一圈并不失望。

    而汉密尔顿认为，在第一节排位赛中丢失一块底板部件，单是下压力就让他损失了“零点三到零点四秒”，同时他还声称，一个部署问题是他第二节排位赛最后一圈的重大阻碍。——杰克·贝尼恩

    说到安东内利的第三节排位赛，很难不套用拉斯维加斯的赌博老梗。

    他做出了冒险决定，在5号弯减速让行，掉到车阵末尾。要是他晚几秒到达起跑线，他甚至根本无法开始自己的飞驰圈。

    相反，他刚好及时赶上，并发现自己处于完美的位置，在圈末借助多辆车的尾流直冲终点线，跑出最快圈速，巧妙地将他因更换引擎而受的五位发车格罚退的痛苦降到最低。

    尽管或许有一点幸运，但这很好地体现了安东内利今年全程处理事情有多么出色。他游走在灾难的边缘，只为碾压所有人。

    而且从发车格第六位起步，谁又敢赌他不会在正赛中迅速跻身维斯塔潘和拉塞尔之间呢？——乔什·萨蒂尔

    安东内利的发车格罚退将把拉塞尔提升到发车格第二位，但考虑到拉塞尔在第三节练习中领先九成（0.9秒）的强势表现，排位赛会让人觉得错失良机。

    事实上，拉塞尔在Q3的最终圈速比他在第三节练习中还要慢，他恳求的尾流最终也没有得到。

    他在排位赛后的新闻发布会上圆滑地说“我们会私下沟通，可以再谈”，但对于自己的请求被拒绝，他显然感到沮丧。

    尤其是因为这导致他被那位再次激怒他的车手击败，丢掉了（继承而来的）杆位。——JS

    尽管报告他的红牛赛车存在“一些奇怪的问题”，维斯塔潘的表现仍足以让他成为未受罚车手中的最快者。

    他不确定这些问题是否会在正赛距离中拖累他——就像在奥斯汀那样——但他已经做好了充分准备，争取在六场比赛中第四次获胜，并继续将自己确立为安东内利在2027年最大的威胁。- JS

    皮埃尔·加斯利在Q2中引人注目的第二名是否让他梦想着再次挑战杆位，就像在蒙扎那样？

    加斯利没有这么说，但接下来发生的事情无疑令人失望。即使期望挑战前列位置不现实，复制他Q2的圈速本可以让加斯利在Q3中排名第五，并且由于基米·安东内利的罚退，他在发车格上排名第四。

    然而，准备圈没有按计划进行，带着冷胎进入第一个弯角，在第一赛段损失了“两到三个十分之一秒”，圈速就此告吹，让他落后于两位小红牛车手，仅领先于队友弗兰科·科拉平托——后者幸运地只是擦到墙壁，没有更严重的后果。

    这种剧情转折恰好概括了 阿尔派 在下半赛季对小红牛的追赶。——杰克·科曾斯

    阿尔维德·林德布拉德已经拿下本赛季个人最佳排位成绩，并将因安东内利的发车位罚退而进一步受益，从第五位起步。

    林德布拉德今年跑出过许多非常强劲的排位圈——包括最近几场比赛，他说由于各种不同因素影响了他的成绩，这些圈速我们并未看到——但即便是他本人也不确定这一圈能否与自己的最佳表现相提并论。

    “当时太混乱了，轮胎不工作，所以我出现了很多转向不足，我在8号弯犯了一点小错误，所以这不是完美的一圈，”林德布拉德在被 The Race 问及这一圈是否感觉像是他本赛季较为特别的一圈时解释道。

    “但与此同时，最近几场比赛有很多事情没有按照我的意愿发展，那是我无法控制的。”

    利亚姆·劳森将后退两个位置发车，因为小红牛这对组合再次在排位赛中击败了两名阿尔派车手，他们正在为车队积分榜第五名而战。劳森在Q2给了林德布拉德一个尾流，林德布拉德总结说：“我觉得其实并不完全需要，但很感激。”——JB

    哈斯车队周五到来时希望让两辆车都进入Q2，并利用被罚退严重的奥利·贝尔曼为拉斐尔·卡马拉提供尾流，以获得更好的发车位。

    但首次使用干胎（光头胎）跑圈意味着那些期望很快被重新调整。

    贝尔曼表示：“不幸的是，我们的速度不够快。我们在第三节练习中已经证明了这一点。所以，是的，我没能完成一个计时圈，我只是在努力帮助拉法晋级。基本上，要让两辆车都进入第二节排位赛，那会太困难了。”

    事实证明，要让哪怕一辆车进入第二节排位赛都太困难了——尽管贝尔曼做出了“惊人的表现”，这是排位第17名的卡马拉所说的，他表示对队友“不能要求更多了”。

    再加上贝尔曼在第三节练习中于第一个弯道出现了一次诡异的打转——用他的话说，这与哈斯车队的“排位赛引擎映射以及后刹车基本上如何与MGU-K相互作用”有关——而卡马拉在第一节排位赛中实际上复制了同样的情况，这些加在一起，构成了哈斯车队一段时间以来在性能方面最艰难的一天。——JC

    英文原文

    The rain-affected Formula 1 practice sessions meant there was added emphasis on hitting the ground running in FP3 and carrying that through into Las Vegas Grand Prix qualifying.

    Who nailed it when it mattered most, and who failed to deliver on their earlier promise?

    It's been a rollercoaster year for Ferrari, but a double-Q2 exit in Las Vegas has to be one of the lowest moments - even if the intricacies of this track make it a bit of an outlier.

    Charles Leclerc made a mistake at Turn 7 and failed to get a second lap in, while Lewis Hamilton didn't look convincing from FP3 onwards.

    Leclerc was quick to point out the mistake was "following a lot of frustration, building up with a lot of mistakes with the way we managed" traffic, among other things, and said Ferrari might have been a victim of a GPS issue that hit during FP3.

    He added that Ferrari had to be fully focused on gaining tyre temperature because that is the team's "weakness".

    Giving a matter-of-fact take on where he thought Ferrari was losing out, Hamilton said: "It's a big chunk on the straights. It didn't feel like we had a tonne of grip. We were struggling to get the tyres working and we were just kind of nowhere."

    That sums it up well.

    His last lap was "very, very good", according to the team, so clearly it wasn't upset with the lap Hamilton put together.

    And Hamilton reckoned losing a piece of his floor in Q1 cost him "three or four tenths" in downforce alone, while he also claimed a deployment issue was a big hinderance on his final Q2 lap. - Jack Benyon

    It’s difficult to resist the Vegas gambling cliches when it comes to Antonelli's Q3.

    He made the risky decision to back off at Turn 5 and drop to the back of the pack. Had he arrived at the line a couple of seconds later, he wouldn't even have been able to start his lap.

    Instead, he made it just in time and found himself in the perfect place to pick up a monster, multi-car tow to the line at the end of the lap, setting the fastest time and minimising the pain of his five-place engine grid penalty brilliantly.

    Albeit perhaps a touch fortunate, it was a good example of how beautifully Antonelli has played things throughout this year. Walking right up to the edge of disaster in order to crush everyone.

    And from sixth on the grid, who would bet against him quickly getting himself amongst Verstappen and Russell in the race? - Josh Suttill

    Antonelli's grid penalty will bump Russell up to second on the starting grid, but qualifying will feel like a missed opportunity given how strong Russell was in FP3, which he topped by nine tenths.

    In fact, Russell actually ended up slower in Q3 than he'd been in FP3, left without the tow he pleaded for.

    He diplomatically said "we'll take it offline, and we can talk about it" in the post-qualifying press conference, but he was clearly frustrated to have had his request denied.

    Not least because it led to him being beaten to (an inherited) pole by the driver who has yet again infuriated him. - JS

    Despite reporting "some weird issues" with his Red Bull, Verstappen did enough to be the fastest of the unpenalised drivers.

    He wasn't sure if those issues will hamper him over a race distance - as they did at Austin - but he is well poised to fight for a fourth victory in six races and continue to set himself up as Antonelli's biggest 2027 threat. - JS

    Did Pierre Gasly's eye-catching second place in Q2 have him dreaming of another challenge for pole position, a la Monza?

    Gasly didn't say as much, but what came next was undoubtedly a disappointment. Even if it was unrealistic to expect a challenge for the top positions, replicating his Q2 lap would've put Gasly fifth in Q3, and fourth on the grid with Kimi Antonelli's penalty.

    Instead, the prep lap didn't go to plan and, with cold tyres into the first corner, "two, three tenths" were lost in the first sector and the lap was gone, leaving him behind both Racing Bulls drivers and ahead only of team-mate Franco Colapinto - who was lucky to escape brushing the wall without more severe consequences.

    It's the sort of turn of events that sums up Alpine's pursuit of Racing Bulls in the second half of the season. - Jack Cozens

    Arvid Lindblad already bagged his best qualifying of the year and will benefit further from Antonelli's grid penalty to start fifth.

    Lindblad's put in plenty of very strong qualifying laps this year - including in the last few races, which, he says, we haven't seen due to different things impacting his results - but even he wasn't sure this would be up there with his best.

    "It was such a mess, the tyres weren't working, so I had a lot of understeer, I made a bit of a mistake in Turn 8, so it wasn't a perfect lap," Lindblad explained when asked by The Race whether it felt like one of the more special laps of his season.

    "But also at the same time, there's a lot that's not gone my way the recent races, that's been out of my control."

    Liam Lawson will start two places further back, as the Racing Bulls duo again outqualified the two Alpine drivers they're fighting for fifth in the constructors' championship. Lawson gave Lindblad a tow in Q2 which "I don't think it was entirely needed, but grateful", Lindblad summarised. - JB

    Haas came into Friday hoping to get both cars into Q2 and using the heavily penalised Ollie Bearman to tow Rafael Camara to a better grid position.

    But its first taste of slick-tyre running meant those expectations were quickly readjusted.

    "Unfortunately, we weren't quick enough. We showed that in FP3," said Bearman. "So, yeah, I didn't get to set a lap, I was just trying to help Rafa to come through. It would have been too difficult basically to get both cars into Q2."

    It was too difficult, it turns out, to get even one car into Q2 - despite Bearman doing "an amazing job", per 17th-place qualifier Camara, who said he "couldn't ask for more" from his team-mate.

    Throw in a weird Bearman spin at the first corner in FP3 - in his words, to do with Haas's "quali engine map and how the rear brakes basically interact with the MGU-K" - that was effectively replicated by Camara in Q1, and this added up to Haas's most difficult day, performance-wise, for some time. - JC
