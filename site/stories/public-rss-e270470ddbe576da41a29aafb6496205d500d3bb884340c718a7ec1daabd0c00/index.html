    国际汽联用Kahoot问答游戏收集车手对2026赛季判罚意见

    在卡塔尔，国际汽联与F1车手的年度例会上首次使用互动问答游戏Kahoot!，就本赛季各项判罚征求车手意见。

    周四在卡塔尔，F1车手与国际汽联干事讨论比赛规则的年度例会以一种不同寻常的方式进行，会上玩起了Kahoot!游戏。

    这一聚会已成为固定安排，帮助车手与国际汽联赛事干事讨论今年以来做出的各项决定，并辩论未来是否需要对比赛准则或处罚的实施做出改进。

    不过今年的会议有个变化：国际汽联用车手参与的互动问答游戏Kahoot!来收集他们对本赛季所判罚的看法。

    车手们通过手机回答多选题，国际汽联逐一回顾了全年发生的多起事件，并征求他们的反馈，即各自认为本应采取什么行动——投票选项包括不同的罚时或不作任何处罚。

    据了解，部分被审查的事件在处罚决定上引发了车手和赛事干事的共识，而另一些事件则导致意见分歧较大。

    尽管以这种方式使用Kahoot!似乎有些奇怪，但其背后的想法是，这为车手们提供了一个机会，让他们能够比被迫公开表态时更诚实地发表意见，同时也突显出有时确实存在一些不那么明确的事件。

    这也有助于确保会议氛围更加轻松，大家可以开诚布公地讨论，因为每个人都感到自在，而不是过于正式。

    世界冠军安东内利对会议的进行方式表示欢迎。

    他说：“这对干事们来说很有好处，尤其是让他们看到我们如何看待某些场景，并让我们向他们表达自己的观点；而且好的一点是，房间里的我们大多数人对于我们看到的那个事件看法都一致。”

    “这对干事们来说很有好处，同时也可以讨论指导原则，而这些原则他们其实并不需要改变。”

    “只是要看到、理解我们的观点，并理解在某些场景下，这本来就只应算作一次比赛事故，不应处罚。”

    “有些场景处于如此模糊的边界，很难确定是谁的过错，与其给出处罚，不如就当作一次比赛事故。”

    虽然并非所有车手都到场，费尔南多·阿隆索身体不适，兰斯·斯特罗尔等人选择不出席，但像刘易斯·汉密尔顿这样此前不愿参加的车手这次也到场了。

    在会前谈到自己的理由时，汉密尔顿说：“去年我没去，但今晚我会去，是的。归根结底只是因为我想帮助他们……帮助比赛变得更好，我相信我们能够改进裁决体系。”

    马克斯·维斯塔潘认为，这次会议对车手和赛事干事双方都有益，能让大家达成共识。

    “这样做很好，”他说，“对他们来说也是如此，能听到我们的声音，了解我们认为什么应该判罚、什么不应该判罚。”

    "当然，然后他们会下定决心，决定明年要做什么，例如。但我们能开这些会很好。"

    虽然过去国际汽联与车手的会议有时会以重大意见分歧告终，但据了解，这次他们比以往任何时候都更加一致。

    对于明年的任何事情，没有重大的行动呼吁——尽管在处理车手通过切弯获得优势的情况时可能会有所动作。

    讨论的一个解决方案是，是否可以设立一种新的处罚，让车手在比赛结束时失去一个保证的位置，而不是被给予时间处罚。

    安东内利还表示，各方曾讨论过国际汽联是否应恢复一种机制，即在裁判认为车手以不公平方式获得位置时，告知车手需要交还该位置。

    他说：“我们谈了很多关于在尝试超车时切弯的问题，我们说过，例如最有效的做法就是直接告诉车手立刻让出位置。”

    “即使你吃到时间处罚，有时如果你手头有速度优势，你只要把差距拉开就没问题了，而如果直接交还位置，也会更公平。”

    英文原文

    Formula 1's annual get-together between drivers and the FIA stewards to discuss racing rules played out in an unusual way in Qatar on Thursday, as a game of Kahoot! was rolled out.

    The gathering has become an established fixture to help drivers and FIA race stewards discuss decisions made over the year to date and debate whether improvements need to be made to either racing guidelines or the imposition of sanctions in the future.

    This year's meeting had a twist to it though, as the FIA used interactive quiz game Kahoot! with the drivers to gather their views on penalties handed out during this season.

    With drivers teed up to give answers on multiple choice questions via their phones, the FIA ran through a host of incidents that had taken place over the year and got feedback from them on what action they each felt should have been taken - with votes cost for different time penalties or no sanction at all.

    It is understood that some of the incidents looked at triggered unanimous agreement from drivers and stewards over the punishment handed out, while others ended with opinion being more divided.

    Although using Kahoot! in this manner seems strange, the idea behind doing it was that it offered an opportunity for drivers to give a more honest opinion than if they'd had to openly speak out, as well as highlighting that sometimes there are incidents that are not so clear cut.

    It also helped ensure a more light-hearted mood to the meeting where things could be discussed openly because everyone was comfortable, rather than it being overly formal.

    World champion Kimi Antonelli welcomed the way that the meeting had played out.

    "It was good for, especially the stewards, to see how we see certain scenarios and to give them our point of view, and also the good thing is that most of us in the room were all aligned on the episode that we saw," he said.

    "It was good for the stewards to see, and as well to talk about guidelines, which they don't really need to change.

    "It's just seeing, understanding our point of view and understanding that in some scenarios it would just need to be a racing incident and no penalty.

    "Some scenarios are so borderline that it's so hard to establish who is at fault, and instead of giving a penalty it should just be a racing incident."

    While not all drivers were present, with Fernando Alonso unwell and some like Lance Stroll choosing not to attend, some like Lewis Hamilton who have been reluctant to join the meeting previously did attend this time around.

    Speaking about his reasoning before the get together, Hamilton said: "I didn't go last year but I'm going to go tonight, yes. Ultimately just because I want to help them…help the racing be better and the ruling system I'm sure we can improve."

    Max Verstappen reckoned that the meeting was beneficial for both drivers and stewards in terms of getting everyone on the same page.

    "It's good to do that," he said. "Also for them to hear us and our voices of what we think a penalty should be or not.

    "And then of course they make their minds up and they decide what they're going to do next year, for example. But it's good that we have those meetings."

    While the FIA and drivers' meetings in the past have sometimes ended with big differences of opinion, it is understood that they were more aligned this time around than ever before.

    There was no major call for action to be taken on anything for next year – although some movement may be made when it comes to dealing with occasions where drivers have gained advantage by cutting the track.

    One solution discussed was whether a new sanction could be created for drivers to lose a guaranteed position at the end of a race rather than be given a time penalty.

    Antonelli also suggested that there were discussions about the FIA returning to a system where drivers are informed about the need to hand back any positions gained unfairly in the opinion of the stewards.

    "We talked a lot about cutting a corner while trying an overtake, and we said that for example the most efficient thing would be just tell the driver to drop the position straight away," he said.

    "Even if you get a time penalty, sometimes if you have a pace in hand you can just build that gap and then it's fine, whilst if you give straight the position back is more fair as well."
