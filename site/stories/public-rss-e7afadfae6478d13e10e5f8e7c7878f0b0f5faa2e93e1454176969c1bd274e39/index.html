    车手对2030年F1超车辅助方案意见分歧

    拉斯维加斯大奖赛出现352次超车，维斯塔潘认为其中尾流效应这一要素应延续到2030年新车上，但车手们对超车辅助机制的具体运作方式看法不一。

    拉斯维加斯大奖赛的场面或许没能平息外界对F1现行规则中人为元素的批评，但其中有一个要素，就连立场坚定的批评者马克斯·维斯塔潘也认为应延续到2030年的新车上。

    尽管维斯塔潘在拉斯维加斯执行得恰到好处，帮助他拿下一场令人难忘的胜利，但他对比赛的进程并不满意，并把这场比赛比作《马力欧卡丁车》。

    然而，拉斯维加斯有一个潜在因素对其352次超车贡献很大，维斯塔潘建议在F1开始整合2030年愿景时对此加以考虑。

    那就是拉斯维加斯的场面并不只关乎电池电量差——正是电池电量差引发了我们在澳大利亚和中国等站看到的赛季初拉锯战。

    拉斯维加斯比赛的动态是由尾流如此强大所造就的：领先者无法拉开差距，后方车群压缩成一列火车。

    即使某辆赛车掉出了那个神奇的一秒窗口、会失去超车模式，它们只需沿着拉斯维加斯大道借助超强牵引力就能重新获得那个窗口甚至更多。

    这还得益于赛车在长直道末端耗尽电池能量——因此功率与阻力之比发生了剧烈变化。

    小红牛车手阿尔维德·林德布拉德表示：“这对比赛其实很有利。你半圈只有500马力，却带着F1的阻力。那就像把F3引擎装进F1赛车。你想找到尽可能大的尾流，因为阻力超大，但你却没有动力。”

    阿尔派车队的皮埃尔·加斯利补充道：“从第二名到垫底，这场比赛是确定的，因为你必须在能量管理方式、超车方式、驾驶方式以及保持在那段差距内以受益于尾流的方式上都很聪明。”

    “对于领跑者来说，无论你的速度多快、驾驶得多好，马克斯[维斯塔潘]或乔治[拉塞尔]都会因为破风并处于车阵前端而损失大量时间，所以根本没有办法[拉开差距]。”

    正是这种超强尾流现象，维斯塔潘认为应当成为2030年新一代赛车概念的核心——因为这将有助于改善比赛并减少对DRS等装置的依赖。

    当被The Race问及他对2030年起什么能造就最纯粹的赛车比赛的愿景时，维斯塔潘表示，组合式赛车概念和更简单的引擎将是完美的。

    维斯塔潘说：“从我们过去开发的所有东西来看，如果你看从上世纪80年代到2010年，当然你有统治性的赛车、统治性的车队。”

    “它们有时快一秒甚至两秒多。现在我不认为你还会看到那种情况。但你仍然可以设计出一种规则，让赛车在直道上阻力超大，这样你就不需要那么依赖DRS。”

    “对我来说，一键超车系统没问题。但是的，它需要是一台容易得多的引擎。比如每次我做表演圈，开小红牛7之类的，开起来就是很舒服，因为油门响应，一切都轻松。”

    “它起步顺畅，赛车不抖，没有迟滞之类的。开起来比我们现在的车舒服多了。”

    “所以只要我们能回到接近那种状态的引擎——我知道它需要某种混合动力元素，可能甚至需要涡轮增压器，因为否则在某些赛道上你甚至可能不被允许驾驶，因为太吵了，这没问题。”

    “只要它不凌驾于自然的驾驶元素之上，而目前当然正在发生这种情况。”

    2026年赛车常常反直觉的驾驶要求，持续让维修区里的许多人感到恼火，但人们对超车性质的变化同样不屑。

    2030年需要做出的重大决定之一，是使用哪种类型的额外超车辅助手段来配合新的涡轮V8引擎。

    目前摆在桌面上的选项包括：保留现有的超车模式概念、采用类似印地赛车那样的按键加速系统，或者回归 DRS。

    虽然 DRS 有时也因人为痕迹过重而受到批评，但阿斯顿·马丁车队的兰斯·斯特罗尔认为，相比现在的比赛方式，DRS 带来的是一种更自然的竞速形态。

    “我认为 DRS 时代的比赛比现在更好，”他说。“它们完美吗？不完美。在理想世界里，你希望能毫无干扰地跟住一辆车，然后不必依赖 DRS 或任何类似的东西。”

    斯特罗尔承认 DRS 有其缺陷，有时作用过强，有时又常常不够好用，但他觉得它的优点之一是能够创造超车机会。

    他说：“在那些DRS设计得当、直道长度合理并且有良好超车区域的地方，我们总能看到非常精彩的DRS比赛。但有些赛道你根本没法超车。所以那样也不行。我觉得这很棘手。”

    “要让所有条件都完美配合是很棘手的。但我们现在的做法绝对不是前进的方向，因为你并不是在刹车区完成超车。你是在直道中途超车，那并不激动人心。”

    “有了DRS，情况并不完美，远非如此。但你希望看到的是赛车足够接近，让你能在进入弯道时发起攻击，而不是仅仅因为你的速度快了50公里/小时就在直道上超过去。”

    这种超车的艺术——即超车是车手必须做出的动作，而不是不费力就发生的事情——是卡洛斯·塞恩斯认为至关重要的东西。

    他说：“我认为速度差应该更多出现在直道起点、也就是你试图保持尾流的时候，而不是在直道末端、超车和速度都太高的时候。”

    “我认为超车模式、一键加速、DRS，不管你怎么称呼它，它的动力应该出现在出弯时。也就是说，你获得额外的动力提升，比如说，用来弥补你在弯道中损失的时间，但这一提升不应该随后帮助你完成超车。”

    “我认为这一提升应该帮助你设法重新进入非常接近的尾流，然后利用气流和剩余的刹车点来完成你自己的超越动作。”

    “这就是我心目中一键加速、动能回收系统、能量释放，不管你怎么称呼它，应该起作用的方式。”

    “我觉得那次奇怪的超车也和DRS有关，当你在直道上速度差达到30公里/小时，你冲过来得太快，即使你处在前面，你也不敢防守、不敢切线挡住他，因为速度差太大了。”

    英文原文

    The Las Vegas Grand Prix spectacle might not have silenced critics over the artificial elements of Formula 1’s current rules, but there was one element to it that even staunch critic Max Verstappen thinks should be carried forward to the new 2030 cars.

    Despite his perfectly judged execution in Las Vegas that helped pull off a memorable triumph, Verstappen was not impressed with how things played out in a race he likened to Mario Kart.

    Yet there was one underlying aspect to Las Vegas that contributed a lot to its 352 passes, and is something that Verstappen suggests should be pondered as F1 begins pulling together its vision for 2030.

    It is that the Las Vegas show was not just about battery offsets, which were the trigger for the early season yo-yo battles that we saw in Australia and China for example.

    The dynamics of the Las Vegas race, with the leader unable to break away and a pack of cars compressed in a train behind, were set up by the slipstream being so powerful.

    Even if a car fell outside the magic one-second window where they would lose overtake mode, they would regain that and more long the Las Vegas Strip simply through a super powerful tow.

    This had been helped by cars running out of battery energy at the end of the long straight - so the power to drag ratio shifted dramatically.

    Racing Bulls driver Arvid Lindblad said: "It actually worked well for racing. You did half the lap with 500 horsepower, but with the drag of an F1. So then it's like putting an F3 engine in an F1 car. You want as big a tow as you can find because it's super draggy, but you have no power."

    Alpine's Pierre Gasly added: "From P2 to last, it was a certain race in terms of you had to be smart in the way you manage your energy, in the way you make the moves, in the way that you drive and stay in that gap to benefit from the tow.

    "For the leader, regardless of your speed and how well you drove, Max [Verstappen] or George [Russell] were losing so much time from punching in the air and being at the front end of that pack that there was just no way [to pull clear]."

    It was this super powerful tow phenomenon that Verstappen thinks should be central to the concept of the new generation cars in 2030 – as it would help improve the racing and reduce the need for things like DRS.

    Asked by The Race about his vision of what would make the purest car for racing from 2030, Verstappen said a combination car concept and simpler engines would be perfect.

    "With all the things that we have developed in the past, if you see from like the 1980s to 2010, of course you had dominant cars, dominant teams," Verstappen said.

    "They were sometimes over a second or two seconds a lot faster. Now I don't think you will have that anymore. But you can still come up with a formula where maybe a car is just super draggy in a straight line, so you don't need to rely so much on DRS.

    "A push-to-pass system for me is fine. But yeah, it needs to be a much easier engine. Like every time I do a show run, for example in an RB7 or whatever, it's just so nice to drive because the throttle response, like everything, is just easy.

    "It drives off nice, the car is not shaking about, it has no lag, stuff like that. It's just a lot nicer to drive than what we have currently.

    "So as long as we can get back to an engine close to that - I know it needs to be some kind of hybrid element, probably even a turbocharger because otherwise you're probably not even allowed to drive at some tracks anymore because it's too loud, which is fine.

    "As long as it's not overruling the natural driving element, which of course at the moment it's happening."

    The often counterintuitive driving requirements of the 2026 cars are something that continues to annoy many in the pitlane, but there is equal disdain about how the nature of overtaking has changed.

    One of the big decisions that needs to be made for 2030 is what type of extra overtaking aid will be used to accompany the new turbo V8s.

    Options on the table include keeping the current overtake mode concept, a push-to-pass system like IndyCar has, or going back to DRS.

    While DRS also had its critics at times for being artificial, Aston Martin’s Lance Stroll is one who thinks it delivered a more natural form of racing than what happens right now.

    "I think the DRS races are better than we have now," he said. "Were they perfect? No, in an ideal world, you'd want to kind of be able to follow a car with absolutely no disturbance and then not have to rely on DRS or anything like that."

    Stroll acknowledges that DRS had its flaws in terms of being too powerful at times, or often not working well enough, but he felt that one of its strengths was how it opened up overtaking opportunities.

    "Where the DRS was thought out well, and where the straights were reasonable and then there's a good overtaking zone, we always saw really good races with DRS," he said. "But there's some tracks you just can't overtake at all. So that doesn't work either. I think it's tricky.

    "It's tricky to get all the stars aligned. But what we have now is definitely not the way forward because you're not overtaking into a braking zone. You're overtaking halfway down the straight and that's not exciting.

    "With DRS, it wasn't perfect, far from it. But you want to just see the car getting close enough where you can make a move going into the corner and not just overtake down the straight because you are 50km/h quicker."

    This art of the overtake – about it being a move a driver has to make rather than something that happens without effort – is something Carlos Sainz thinks is essential.

    "I think the delta on speed should come more at the beginning of the straight when you are trying to keep the slipstream rather than at the end of the straight when the overtaking and the speed is too high," he said.

    "I think the power of the overtake mode, push to pass, DRS, whatever you want to call it, should happen exiting the corner. That you have an extra power boost, let's say, to catch up what you're losing in the corner, but the boost shouldn't then help you to finish the overtake.

    "I think the boost should help you manage to get back into the very close slipstream and then use the draft and the rest of the braking point to make your own move.

    "That is my ideal way of how a push to pass, KERS, deployment, call it whatever you want, should work.

    "I think the weird overtaking that happened also a bit with DRS is when you get delta top speeds of 30km/h and you arrive so quick that even when you're in front, you're too scared to defend and cut him off because the delta speed [speed difference] is so high."
