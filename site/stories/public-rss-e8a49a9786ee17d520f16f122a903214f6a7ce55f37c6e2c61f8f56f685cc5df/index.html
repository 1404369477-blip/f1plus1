    布克斯顿：F1在美国靠文化影响力立足

    评论员威尔·布克斯顿表示，尽管F1在美国的受欢迎程度不及纳斯卡和印地赛车，但其“文化影响力”使其在美国体育版图中占据独特位置。

    布克斯顿曾在2018至2024年期间在F1TV频道担任主持人，后于2025年加入福克斯体育担任印地赛车首席评论员。

    在拉斯维加斯举行的The Race x Axios“超越比赛：创新与领导力投资”活动上，布克斯顿被问及F1转投Apple TV在美国播出是否正确。

    他对Axios记者萨拉·菲舍尔表示，F1“不是美国最受欢迎的赛车运动”，排在纳斯卡和印地赛车之后。

    但他补充道：“一级方程式赛车拥有这两者都不具备的东西，那就是文化相关性和文化声望。”

    2025年，在Apple TV于美国转播F1之前，ESPN记录到每场F1大奖赛平均有130万观众，创下历史新高——与印地赛车持平——而纳斯卡赛车每场比赛的电视平均观众为245万。

    “纳斯卡赛车没有这种待遇。你走进7-Eleven，或者走进任何一家便利店……去买一罐红牛。上面印的是康纳·齐利施吗？是肖恩·范吉斯伯根吗？不，是马克斯·维斯塔潘。因为一级方程式赛车很酷。”

    “一级方程式赛车具有文化相关性。这有点像《与卡戴珊一家同行》，对吧？

    “所以，《与卡戴珊一家同行》的观众规模已经不如从前了。可是，维多利亚的秘密选了谁来穿那件价值5000万美元的胸衣？是凯莉·詹娜。因为她和她一家在文化上至关重要，而且至今仍留在美国民众的心里。

    “而我认为，这正是F1已经做到、并且将会被研究的事情。”

    巴克斯顿补充说，收视率并不是Apple TV和F1在美国所瞄准的唯一投资回报，这与自由媒体集团首席执行官德里克·张此前的观点相呼应。

    现在这已经超越了那一点。它不再只是关于体育。不再只是关于人们在周日下午收看比赛。

    这关乎认可度、可见度和那种文化影响力。这就是一级方程式赛车所做到的，尤其是在美国这里。这太不可思议了。

    “伯尼只对下架内容感兴趣”

    巴克斯顿见证了一级方程式赛车从2017年之前伯尼·埃克莱斯顿时代到随后自由媒体时代的演变，在此期间他曾为GP2系列赛、福克斯体育和NBC体育担任解说。

    作为Netflix《极速求生》的年度固定节目，他表示这档常被提及的电视节目并非F1迅猛增长的唯一原因。

    “事情没那么简单，因为正如斯特凡诺[多梅尼卡利，F1首席执行官]所暗示的，《极速求生》的成功与其推出时的环境背景关系密切，节目本身的作用同样如此。

    “但也不能简单地说《极速求生》就是一切，因为自由媒体集团在数字领域进行了大规模推进。

    “他们创建了一个此前从未存在过的YouTube频道，以及此前从未有过的Facebook页面和Twitter账号。”

    巴克斯顿指出，F1对数字媒体的新做法与前F1掌门人埃克莱斯顿的做法形成了鲜明对比。

    他说：“在伯尼·埃克莱斯顿执掌时期，F1对网络内容唯一的兴趣就是将其下架，而不是上传。这是事实。”

    “F1曾有一个完整的部门，其唯一职责就是寻找人们发布在网上的视频和内容。”

    他补充道：“但我认为《极速求生》非常重要，因为我们不再从技术角度讲述F1的故事，而是开始从个人、情感的角度来讲述。”

    我认为，这种叙事方式的转变，正是这项运动能够以不同于十年前的方式打动新车迷和新生代的核心原因。

    我认为，自由媒体集团接手后一级方程式赛车所做的一切，将会被后世反复研究，研究如何让一项运动不断演进，并将其从单纯的体育资产转变为一种文化现象。

    英文原文

    Even though Formula 1 is less popular in the United States than NASCAR and IndyCar, its "cultural relevance" gives it a unique spot in the country's sports landscape, according to broadcaster Will Buxton.

    Buxton presented on the in-house F1TV channel from 2018-24 before joining Fox Sports as lead IndyCar commentator in 2025.

    During The Race x Axios's Beyond the Race: Investments in Innovation and Leadership event in Las Vegas, Buxton was asked if F1's move to Apple TV in the US was the correct one.

    He replied to Axios Correspondent Sara Fischer that F1 is "not the most popular form of motorsport in America" as it sits behind NASCAR and IndyCar.

    But he added: "What Formula 1 has is something that neither of those has, which is cultural relevance and cultural cachet."

    In 2025, before Apple TV broadcasted F1 in the US, ESPN recorded an all-time-high of 1.3m viewers per F1 grand prix - on par with IndyCar - while NASCAR averaged 2.45m viewers per race on TV.

    Buxton reflected on F1's presence on advertising screens in New York City's Times Square in 2026.

    "NASCAR doesn't have that. You go into a 7-Eleven, or you go into any convenience store...to buy a can of Red Bull. Is Connor Zilisch on it? Is Shane van Gisbergen on it? No, it's Max Verstappen. Because Formula 1 is cool.

    "Formula 1 has cultural relevance. It’s a bit like Keeping Up with the Kardashians, right?

    "So, Keeping Up with the Kardashians does not have the audience that it once did. And yet, who did Victoria's Secret pick to wear the $50million bra? It was Kylie [Jenner]. Because she and her family are culturally vital and still are in the American population's mindset.

    "And I think that's what Formula 1 has done that will be studied."

    Buxton added that viewership is not the only return on investment that Apple TV and F1 will be targeting in the US, reflecting a previous sentiment of Liberty Media CEO Derek Chang.

    "It's bigger than that now. It's not just about sport. It's not just about people tuning in on a Sunday afternoon to the race.

    "It's about recognition and visibility and that cultural cachet. That is what Formula 1 has done, particularly here in America. That is so incredible."

    'Bernie was only interested in taking content down'

    Buxton has witnessed F1's evolution from the pre-2017 Bernie Ecclestone era to the following Liberty Media one, having commentated for the GP2 Series, Fox Sports, and NBC Sports over that change.

    As an annual fixture of Netflix's Drive to Survive, he said that the oft-cited TV show is not the only reason for F1's momentous growth.

    "It's not that simple because as Stefano [Domenicali, F1 CEO] intimated, the success of Drive to Survive had as much to do with the circumstances around when it launched as it did the programme itself.

    "But it's also not as simple as to say Drive to Survive was everything because Liberty had made a massive push into the digital domain.

    "They created a YouTube channel where one never existed and a Facebook page and a Twitter account where those things never existed."

    Buxton noted a stark contrast between F1's new approach to digital media and that of ex-F1 supremo Ecclestone.

    "The only interest that Formula 1 under Bernie Ecclestone had with online content was taking it down, not putting it up," he said. "That's true.

    "There was a whole department in Formula 1 whose sole job it was to find videos and content that people had put online."

    He added: "But Drive to Survive I think was really important because we stopped telling the story of Formula 1 from a technical perspective and started telling it from a personal, emotional perspective.

    "And that shift in storytelling I think has laid at the heart of why the sport speaks to new fans and a new generation in a way that it perhaps didn't do 10 years ago.

    "I think what Formula 1 has done since Liberty Media took over, I think it will be studied for generations to come as to how you evolve a sport and turn it from being simply a sporting property into being a cultural phenomenon."
