    凯迪拉克从阿尔派手中挖来斯金纳

    前红牛首席设计师克雷格·斯金纳在凯迪拉克的招募推动下放弃加盟阿尔派，于拉斯维加斯大奖赛周末前正式出任凯迪拉克首席开发工程师。

    凯迪拉克从红牛挖来的克雷格·斯金纳终于开始为车队工作，此时距离凯迪拉克在夏天最后一刻阻止他加盟竞争对手阿尔派已过去三个月。

    斯金纳曾在红牛工作20年，最终担任首席设计师，他于周三在拉斯维加斯大奖赛周末前正式加入凯迪拉克，出任首席开发工程师。

    他今年2月离开红牛并进入园艺假，之后原定于8月加盟阿尔派，甚至已在内部被宣布为新员工。

    但在凯迪拉克更换车队管理层、由马尔钦·布德科夫斯基接替格雷姆·洛登担任车队领队后，新一轮招募工作包括说服斯金纳转而与红牛前车手塞尔吉奥·佩雷斯在凯迪拉克重聚。

    不过，凯迪拉克不得不等待他们想要的人，因为阿尔派用斯金纳自己合同中的离职条款留住了他，这意味着尽管他从未正式加入该车队，仍需经历三个月的离职期。

    凯迪拉克多数股东TWG Motorsport的负责人丹·托里斯表示，佩雷斯一直在“利用他的人脉”为车队吸引人员的兴趣，并在寻找人才方面“发挥了关键作用”。

    据了解，斯金纳就是一个明显的例子，因为佩雷斯非常希望凯迪拉克在招募方面采取积极进取的策略，并且据信早在几个月前就已将他确定为目标。

    当《The Race》问及佩雷斯对凯迪拉克展现出能够如此行事、以这种方式从另一支车队挖走人才有多满意时，他只是微笑着说“我们合作得很好”。

    斯金纳不会是凯迪拉克最高级别的技术负责人，尼克·切斯特才是首席技术官，但在宣布斯金纳加盟时，凯迪拉克表示，他的任务是在整个开发流程中提供技术方向，支持整车概念、空气动力学设计、分析与交付之间的衔接。

    他入职之际恰逢另一位重要新援——新任空气动力学主管埃里克·布兰丁——首次在拉斯维加斯赛道现场亮相。

    曾担任梅赛德斯和阿斯顿·马丁空气动力学主管的布兰丁已加入凯迪拉克，出任空气动力学主管；而曾效力于迈凯伦、红牛和法拉利的蒂姆·利恩将于2027年1月入职担任首席工程师，负责空气动力学建模与仿真，并向布兰丁汇报。

    佩雷斯曾对凯迪拉克在他认为本应更早加强的领域缺乏招聘感到担忧；他相信，像这样的签约将迫使凯迪拉克提高标准。

    当被《The Race》问及他预计这些人会对车队产生多大影响时，他说：“影响很大，因为那些人来了，他们在F1已经做了一切。”

    “他们来这里只是因为想在一个不同的项目上证明自己。”

    “我们都知道他们有多出色，但这是一个不同的项目，我们需要基本上开始把这种文化落实到位，把这些人安排到位，我认为他们会给当前的组织带来很多东西。”

    “而且我认为，如果我们都足够聪明，我认为我们应该利用他们所有的经验，确保我们从他们身上榨取一切。”

    英文原文

    Cadillac’s ex-Red Bull recruit Craig Skinner has finally started working for the team three months after it stopped him from joining rival Alpine at the last minute in the summer.

    Skinner, who had a 20-year stint at Red Bull culminating in the role of chief designer, officially joined Cadillac as chief development engineer on Wednesday ahead of the Las Vegas Grand Prix weekend.

    He left Red Bull in February and went on gardening leave, after which he was due to join Alpine in August and was even announced internally as a new hire.

    But after Cadillac’s change of team management, with Marcin Budkowski replacing Graeme Lowdon as team principal, a new recruitment push included persuading Skinner to reunite with ex-Red Bull driver Sergio Perez at Cadillac instead.

    Cadillac had to wait to get its man, though, as Alpine held Skinner to the exit terms of his own contract, which meant a three-month period of leave despite never starting with the team.

    Dan Towriss, who heads up Cadillac’s majority owner TWG Motorsport, said Perez had been “using his network” to generate interest in the team from personnel and “been an instrumental part” in locating talent.

    Skinner is understood to be a clear example of that, as Perez was very keen for Cadillac to be aggressive with its recruitment and is understood to have identified him as a target months earlier.

    Perez only said “we all work well together” with a smile when asked by The Race how pleased he was that Cadillac had shown it was able to act as it did and poach someone from another team in such a manner.

    Skinner will not be the most senior technical figure at Cadillac, where Nick Chester is chief technical officer, but when he was announced, Cadillac said he will be tasked with providing technical direction across the development process, supporting the connection between car concept, aerodynamic design, analysis and delivery.

    His employment starting coincides with another major recruit - new aerodynamics head Eric Blandin - being trackside for the first time in Las Vegas.

    Ex-Mercedes and Aston Martin aero chief Blandin has joined Cadillac as head of aerodynamics while Tim Lean - formerly of McLaren, Red Bull and Ferrari - will be chief engineer when he joins in January 2027, in charge of aerodynamics modelling and simulation, reporting to Blandin.

    Perez has had concerns about Cadillac’s lack of recruitment in areas he felt should have been strengthened sooner; he believes signings like these will force Cadillac’s standards higher.

    “A lot,” he said when asked by The Race how he expected them to impact the team, “because those guys are coming in, they've done everything in Formula 1.

    “They're only here because they want to prove themselves on a different project.

    “We all know how good they are, but this is a different project where we need to basically start putting this culture in place, these people in place, and I think they will be bringing a lot to the current organisation.

    “And I think if we are all clever enough, I think we should use all their experience to make sure that we take everything out of them.”
