    帕尔默批评法拉利车手无视车队

    乔利恩·帕尔默批评法拉利车手在拉斯维加斯大奖赛第38圈的内斗中“完全无视车队”，并称弗雷德·瓦塞尔在法拉利已无计可施。

    乔利恩·帕尔默称弗雷德·瓦塞尔在法拉利已无计可施

    乔利恩·帕尔默批评法拉利车手在拉斯维加斯大奖赛第38圈的冲突中“完全无视车队”

    致法拉利车迷。来自马拉内罗及更广范围的最新法拉利F1新闻、车手动态、技术分析与赛后反应。

    在2026年拉斯维加斯大奖赛上，22名车手完成了创纪录的352次超车，且大多干净利落，但F1最激烈的队内冲突仍属于法拉利。

    第38圈，夏尔·勒克莱尔在7号弯内线插入刘易斯·汉密尔顿，两人正争夺第六名——这次碰撞导致他的队友打转，勒克莱尔因此被罚10秒，并引发F1 TV评论员乔利恩·帕尔默在赛后直言不讳的评判。

    当被问及法拉利搭档为何再次发生冲突时，帕尔默毫不掩饰自己的看法。

    “车手的自尊心，”帕尔默说。“我的意思是，夏尔显然是挑衅的一方；弗雷德（瓦塞尔）非常坚持责任在谁。我在第10圈左右就说过，当时所有赛车都在拼命争夺——那是什么，352次超车——我们还在说车手们能把比赛保持干净有多好。

    “然后我就想，‘你知道谁不会保持干净吗？不知怎么的，唯一一次接触就会发生在两辆法拉利之间。’果不其然，确实如此。为什么？因为总是这样。他们彼此之间的竞争比跟其他任何人都更激烈。”

    刘易斯·汉密尔顿，法拉利；夏尔·勒克莱尔，法拉利

    在复盘7号弯的回放时，帕尔默指出，虽然汉密尔顿在内线留出的空间很紧，但勒克莱尔在刚刚收到车队直接指令后几秒钟就去冒险，完全没有道理。

    “考虑到他几个弯之前刚刚被法拉利警告过，在那个弯道这么做是不必要的风险，”帕尔默说。

    “而且他们完全无视车队。我有点为弗雷德感到难过，因为你就能看到他；他已经被管理这两位车手搞得精疲力竭。我不知道接下来会怎样。你知道，你还能再多说什么呢？”

    帕尔默的同情是在法拉利车队领队弗雷德·瓦塞尔于赛道边接受了一次明显恼怒的采访之后表达的。三周前在墨西哥城，勒克莱尔因在第一圈切过汉密尔顿而被罚时五秒，两人此前已经进行过严肃的澄清谈话，而瓦塞尔这次没有拐弯抹角。

    “老实说，我的立场是，这又一次超出了可接受的范围，”瓦塞尔对F1电视台说。“我在墨西哥之后说了完全一样的话，但这是缺乏尊重——是对车队、对赞助商缺乏尊重，但主要是对在家里工作的人们缺乏尊重。我们在五个弯之前就把消息传递给了夏尔，对我来说这是一个职业性的错误。”

    到周二，瓦塞尔的沮丧进一步加深。在卡塔尔站前谈及此事的影响时，他承认内部压力“现在比18个月前大得多”，并补充说：“最近，他们之间的状况已经变得难以管理。”

    法拉利的夏尔·勒克莱尔，法拉利的刘易斯·汉密尔顿

    赛道上的代价令人痛心。排位赛仅列第10和第11后，两辆法拉利在第二阶段（stint）凭借足以登上领奖台的速度追至距领先者三秒以内——结果第38圈的纠缠让汉密尔顿（此前还因避让兰多·诺里斯被罚10秒）落到第七，勒克莱尔落到第十，仅收获可怜的七个积分。汉密尔顿拒绝公开加码指责，称争议“交由车队处理”，并希望“他们最终能解决”。

    法拉利在车队积分榜上仍以517分对440分、领先红牛77分稳居第二，剩余卡塔尔和阿布扎比两站；车手榜上汉密尔顿以267分对250分领先勒克莱尔17分，位列第三。

    Fred Vasseur is running out of answers at Ferrari, says Jolyon Palmer

    Jolyon Palmer criticises Ferrari's drivers for a "complete disregard for the team" after their Lap 38 Las Vegas GP clash

    For Ferrari fans. The latest Scuderia Ferrari F1 news, driver updates, technical analysis and race reaction from Maranello and beyond.

    In a 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix where 22 drivers traded a record 352 overtakes largely cleanly, Formula 1’s fiercest intra-team blowup still belonged to Ferrari.

    On Lap 38, Charles Leclerc dived down the inside of Lewis Hamilton at Turn 7 while battling over sixth place - tipping his team-mate into a spin, drawing a 10-second time penalty, and sparking a blunt post-race verdict from F1 TV analyst Jolyon Palmer.

    Asked why Ferrari’s pairing had come to blows yet again, Palmer did not hold back with his verdict.

    "Egos of drivers," Palmer said. "I mean, Charles clearly the aggressor there; Fred is pretty adamant where the blame lies. I said it on about Lap 10, when all the cars were fighting tooth and nail - what was it, 352 passes - and we were saying how good are the drivers being able to keep it clean.

    "And I just thought, 'You know who's not going to keep it clean? Somehow the one bit of contact will be between the two Ferraris.' And lo and behold, it does. Why? Because it always is. They race each other harder than they race anyone else."

    Lewis Hamilton, Ferrari, Charles Leclerc, Ferrari

    Reviewing the Turn 7 replay, Palmer noted that while Hamilton left tight room on the inside, Leclerc had no business gambling moments after receiving a direct team instruction.

    "Given he's just been warned by Ferrari a few corners before, it's an unnecessary risk to do that in that corner," Palmer said.

    "And they've got a complete disregard for the team. I feel a bit sorry for Fred because you just see him; he's worn out by trying to manage these two drivers. And I don't know what's next. You know, what more do you have to say?"

    Palmer’s sympathy followed a visibly exasperated trackside interview from Ferrari team principal Fred Vasseur. Having already held stern clear-the-air talks after Leclerc took a five-second penalty for cutting across Hamilton on Lap 1 in Mexico City three weeks earlier, Vasseur did not mince words.

    "Honestly, my position is that once again, it's more than unacceptable," Vasseur told F1 TV. "I had exactly the same word after Mexico, but it's a lack of respect - it's a lack of respect for the team, for the sponsors, but mainly for the people working at home. We passed the message to Charles five corners before, and for me it's a professional mistake."

    By Tuesday, Vasseur’s frustration had hardened further. Addressing the fallout ahead of Qatar, he admitted the internal pressure is "much worse now than it was 18 months ago", adding: "Lately, the situation between them has become unmanageable."

    Charles Leclerc, Ferrari, Lewis Hamilton, Ferrari

    The on-track cost stung deeply. After qualifying 10th and 11th, both Ferraris climbed within three seconds of the race lead during the second stint with podium-worthy pace - only for the Lap 38 tangle to leave Hamilton seventh (having also served an earlier 10-second penalty avoiding Lando Norris) and Leclerc 10th for a meagre seven-point haul. Hamilton declined to pile on publicly, saying the dispute was "for the team to deal with" and hoping "they'll eventually figure it out".

    Ferrari still holds a comfortable 77-point buffer over Red Bull for second in the constructors’ standings (517 to 440) with Qatar and Abu Dhabi remaining, while Hamilton leads Leclerc by 17 points (267 to 250) in third overall.
