    安东内利解释拉斯维加斯失速原因

    安东内利在拉斯维加斯大奖赛末段与维斯塔潘争夺胜利时短暂失去动力，他解释原因是自己尝试留在红牛车手身后时抬了油门。

    “我的错误是抬了油门”——安东内利在拉斯维加斯大奖赛胜利争夺中为何失去动力

    安东内利在与维斯塔潘争夺胜利时，末段突然短暂失去动力——因为他试图留在红牛车手身后。

    安东内利解释了这个古怪的“错误”，它让他错失了在拉斯维加斯大奖赛争夺F1胜利的机会。

    安东内利是排位赛中最快的车手，但因更换引擎被罚退五位，正赛从第六位起步；他是前十名起步车手中唯一使用中性胎而非软胎的车手，这导致他在第一圈又掉了两个位置。

    这位新科世界冠军在第17圈时追至第四位，并在比赛后半段向杆位得主马克斯·维斯塔潘和梅赛德斯队友乔治·拉塞尔发起挑战，在比赛后期成为红牛的主要对手。

    安东内利和维斯塔潘多次交替领先，'拉锯式'的较量再度上演，这位意大利车手在第43圈（共50圈）冲线时领先0.001秒，这令他的对手极为担忧。

    为了在最后一圈占据超越红牛的最佳位置，安东内利在倒数第二圈末尾没有对维斯塔潘发起进攻，但最终被两辆迈凯伦赛车超越，仅获得第四名。

    “基本上我在14号弯前收油，以保留超车（模式）并保持在马克斯身后，为自己争取最好的夺冠机会，”这位梅赛德斯车手解释道。“然而，这次收油触发了系统里的某个机制，导致我出弯时电量归零。所以我失去了一切。”

    当Motorsport.com就此事向他追问时，他补充道：“实际上我的错误是抬了油门。那触发了这个问题，所以我无法充入任何能量。所以到直道末端时能量就已经相当低了。因为我没有回收到任何东西，然后我看到能量显示为零。

    “基本上系统没有为此做好准备，而我的错误是松了油门，又稍微补了一点油门以保持一点速度，结果触发了系统，这意味着我在直道上损失了将近两秒。”

    安德烈亚·基米·安东内利，梅赛德斯，兰多·诺里斯，迈凯伦

    当有人向他提出梅赛德斯在直道上输给红牛时——尽管W17在比赛中的最高速度为320.6公里/小时，而小红牛22为314.3公里/小时——安东内利澄清道：“不，不，我们能够跟得上。

    “显然，这个周末我们的下压力水平较低，在弯道里有点更困难，但绝对在直道上帮到了我们。很清楚红牛在哪里部署得更多，但我们在最后一圈还有所保留，只是我甚至没能尝试。”

    至于这种“拉锯”式的比赛风格，与一些同行不同，安东内利很欣赏它。

    “我的意思是，有时确实有点压力，因为不可能拉开差距，而且各种直道上有那么多不同的速度，所以你必须非常有策略，”这位意大利人承认。“但除此之外，我认为这是一场有趣的比赛，很遗憾因为一些我无法控制的事情而没能一直战斗到最后。”

    “My mistake was to lift” – Why Kimi Antonelli lost power in Las Vegas F1 victory battle

    Antonelli was battling Verstappen for victory when he suddenly, briefly lost power late on – because he tried to stay behind the Red Bull

    Kimi Antonelli has explained the quirky “mistake” that cost him a shot at Formula 1 victory in the Las Vegas Grand Prix.

    The fastest driver in qualifying, Antonelli was sixth on the grid owing to a five-place drop for an engine change and was the only top-10 starter on mediums rather than softs, which contributed to a further two-position drop on lap one.

    The new world champion recovered to fourth by lap 17 and went on to challenge polesitter Max Verstappen and Mercedes team-mate George Russell in the second half of the race, emerging as the Red Bull’s main rival late on.

    Antonelli and Verstappen traded the lead repeatedly as ‘yo-yo’ racing returned, with the Italian 0.001s ahead on the finish line on lap 43 of 50, which was a major concern for his rival.

    Attempting to be in the optimal position to overtake the Red Bull on the final lap, Antonelli refrained from attacking Verstappen at the end of the penultimate tour, but ended up losing out to both McLarens and finishing just fourth.

    “Basically I lifted off before Turn 14 to keep overtake [mode] and stay behind Max to give myself the best shot for the win,” the Mercedes driver explained. “However, the lift triggered something in the system so I exited the corner at zero battery. So I lost it all.”

    Pressed on the issue by Motorsport.com, he added: “My mistake was to lift, actually. That triggered the issue, so I couldn't charge any energy. So already by the end of the straight you're quite low on energy. So because I couldn't harvest anything, then I see that I'm zero.

    “Basically the system wasn't ready for that and my mistake was to lift off, reapply a bit of throttle to keep a little bit of speed, and that triggered the system, and that meant that in the straight I lost almost two seconds.”

    Andrea Kimi Antonelli, Mercedes, Lando Norris, McLaren

    When it was suggested to him that Mercedes was losing out to Red Bull on straights – despite the W17’s top speed in the race being 320.6km/h, compared to the RB22’s 314.3km/h – Antonelli clarified: “No no, we were able to keep up.

    “Obviously, we had a lower downforce level this weekend and it was a bit more difficult in the corners, but definitely it was helping us in all the straights. It was clear where Red Bull was deploying more, but we had something in the pocket for that last lap, but I couldn't even try.”

    As for the ‘yo-yo’ style of racing, unlike some of his peers, Antonelli appreciated it.

    “I mean, it was a bit stressful at times because it was impossible to pull away and there were so many different speeds in all kinds of straights so you had to be very strategic,” the Italian admitted. “But other than that, I think it was a fun race, and it was a shame not to be able to fight all the way to the end [because of] something out of my control.”
