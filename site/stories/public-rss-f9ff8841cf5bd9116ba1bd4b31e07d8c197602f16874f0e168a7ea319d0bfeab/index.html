    拉斯维加斯大奖赛五大看点

    拉斯维加斯迎来期待中的群雄逐鹿，F1在娱乐之都呈现了一场最富娱乐性的赛事。2026赛季初期的 yo-yo 赛车风格再次回归，由于拉斯维加斯长直道上的电力供应不足，电池驱动的超车再次成为焦点。

    马克斯·维斯塔潘，红牛车队，乔治·拉塞尔，梅赛德斯

    在美国的人工娱乐之都，一级方程式赛车将最人工化和娱乐化的表演呈现出来。真是恰到好处。

    2026赛季早期那种像悠悠球一样的比赛又全面回归了，由于赛道上一英里长的直道上缺乏电力，这使得电池驱动的超车再次出现。一时间，我们仿佛在看印第安纳波利斯500英里赛，顶尖的赛车在无法摆脱其他赛车的情况下争夺领先。

    这导致了无数的位置变化和大量的车轮对车轮的比赛，让满座的观众和赛事官员们非常高兴。

    当然不是每个人都感兴趣，但你不能否认确实有值得一看的东西。而且这绝对比马德里赛道的西班牙大奖赛好看得多。

    法拉利陷入混乱，汉密尔顿和勒克莱尔再次发生碰撞

    法拉利来到拉斯维加斯有两个明确的目标：尝试赢得比赛（当然是）并尝试锁定F1世界车队锦标赛的第二名。鉴于斯库德里亚在长直道的拉斯维加斯赛道上引擎动力不足，不比红牛多丢超过10分以获得第二名的目标完全是现实的。

    但然后未解决的目标再次浮现。

    刘易斯·汉密尔顿和查尔斯·勒克莱尔似乎无法作为队友相互竞争。这一点在墨西哥已经很明显，因为他们互相让对方失去了胜利，但巴西的澄清谈话本应解决所有问题。两周后，情况比以前更糟。

    查尔斯·勒克莱尔，法拉利，刘易斯·汉密尔顿，法拉利

    汉密尔顿在拉斯维加斯大奖赛的开场阶段对勒克莱尔在车队中的争斗感到愤怒，在这次滑流、电池加速的‘比赛’中，当勒克莱尔冲出赛道并领先兰多·诺里斯时，他还在现场，并因此获得10秒的罚分。

    在安全车阶段开始后的一圈中服完罚分，为了避免阻碍在他身后的勒克莱尔，两人重新开始了赛道上的争执，这次轮到勒克莱尔犯错。但这一次，他撞上了队友，导致汉密尔顿旋转，并为自己赢得了10秒的罚分。显然，在巴西所说的所有话都只进了一耳朵就出去了。

    拉斯维加斯本周末，一场旧有的竞争在FP3练习赛中再次燃起，而FP3通常是大奖赛周末中引起最少关注的环节。维斯塔潘和拉塞尔在赛道上激烈竞争，仿佛在为赛车中的位置而战，英国人在赛后接受采访时毫不保留，声称他的对手“认为他自己比所有人都重要”，并且“有人需要让他知道自己的位置”

    在比赛中，两位车手之间既有竞争也有心理战术，他们还在车手锦标赛中争夺第二名。然而，在心理战中击败维斯塔潘几乎和在赛道上击败他一样困难，荷兰人几乎对拉塞尔的评论没有做出任何反应。

    维斯塔潘、红牛车队、乔治·拉塞尔、梅赛德斯

    从第一和第二的位置起步，他们在比赛开始时没有发生任何戏剧性的事件，但在一系列的超车中，他们多次交换领先位置。最后，维斯塔潘似乎总是能够掌控局势：每次拉塞尔设法超过他，荷兰人都会反击，显示出前一天口头攻击对他没有任何影响。

    最终，拉塞尔未能进入领奖台，而维斯塔潘赢得了比赛，并越来越接近在锦标赛中锁定第二名的位置。

    当关于摩纳哥大奖赛的消息在赛道中传播开来，称国际汽联决定红牛在2026年的F1中拥有一个基准动力单元时，许多人认为这只是一个糟糕的玩笑。红牛在赛季初甚至无法挑战胜利，这个裁决听起来几乎令人捧腹。

    然而，也许发动机并不是真正阻碍米尔顿凯恩斯车队的原因，而是赛车本身。现在这辆车已经基本解决了问题，马克斯·维斯塔潘一直处于领先位置。而在拉斯维加斯大奖赛中，维斯塔潘和伊萨克·哈德贾与梅赛德斯车队旗鼓相当，也许证实了国际汽联的测量一开始并不离谱。

    皮埃尔·加斯利自2023年拉斯维加斯大奖赛加入赛历以来，在拉斯维加斯取得了一些成绩。他两次在第二排发车，甚至在去年，通常被安排在最后一排的阿尔卑斯车队中，他也进入了Q3。然而，直到这个周末，他从未在那里得分。

    他的速度再次得到了体现。加斯利在FP2的湿滑赛道上排名第二，并在Q2中再次给人留下深刻印象，尽管在Q3中排名第九让他觉得错过了机会。

    这场比赛终于为他围绕赛道的高速带来了应有的回报。加斯利在比赛中早早取得进展，并在整个大奖赛的大部分时间里都在争夺领奖台。但在比赛还剩15圈时，这一挑战逐渐消失，但第五名是一个应得的回归。

    在之前的六场大奖赛中，阿尔派只获得了1分，加斯利的成绩，以及弗朗哥·科拉平托的第六名，正是车队所需要的。小红牛的双车退赛让阿尔派在争夺第五名的战斗中挽回了一些分数，但要在最后两轮中超越对手仍然是一项艰巨的任务。

    阿尔派的大部分速度可能归功于赛道的特性。它与9月份在蒙扎取得的意外杆位有一些相似之处，但卡塔尔也可能适合A526，因为车队希望以更强劲的姿态结束本赛季。

    英文原文

    Max Verstappen, Red Bull Racing, George Russell, Mercedes

    In America's artificial entertainment capital, F1 put the most artificial and entertaining spectacle. How fitting.

    The yo-yo racing from the early days of the 2026 season was back on full display, with the lack of electric energy available on the Strip's mile long straight bring back battery driven overtaking. For a while it felt like we were watching the Indy 500, with the top cars trading the lead without being able to break away from the rest of the pack.

    It led to numerous position changes and plenty of wheel-to-wheel races, much to the delight of the soldout crowd and event officials.

    It's certainly not for everyone, but you can't deny there was something to watch. And it was certainly a much better watch than the Madring's Spanish Grand Prix.

    Ferrari in meltdown as Hamilton and Leclerc clash again

    Ferrari came into Las Vegas with two clear objectives: Try to win (naturally) and try to lockdown second place in the F1 world constructors’ championship. While the first target was going to be a tall order given the Scuderia’s lack of engine punch on the long Las Vegas straights, not losing more than 10 points to Red Bull was entirely realistic to second P2.

    But then the unsolved objectives reared up once again.

    Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc seem unable to race each other as team-mates. That much was evident in Mexico as they cost each other the win, but clear-the-air talks were meant to have solved all that in Brazil. Two weeks later, it is worse than ever.

    Charles Leclerc, Ferrari, Lewis Hamilton, Ferrari

    Hamilton vented his fury at Leclerc for fighting him in the pack during the opening act of the Las Vegas GP slipstreaming, battery-boosting ‘race’, with the Monegasque on the scene when he ran off the road and kept ahead of Lando Norris to pick up a 10-second penalty.

    After having to serve his penalty a lap into the safety car period, in order not to hold up Leclerc who was behind him, the pair re-engaged their on-track disagreements and it was Leclerc’s turn to make the error. But this time he clattered into his team-mate, spinning Hamilton around, and earning himself a 10s penalty. Whatever was said in Brazil clearly went in one ear and out of the other.

    An old rivalry was reignited in Las Vegas this weekend following an incident during FP3, the session that usually generates the fewest headlines over a Grand Prix weekend. Verstappen and Russell went wheel-to-wheel as if they were fighting for position in a race, and the Briton didn't hold back when speaking to the media afterwards, claiming that his rival "thinks he is more important than everyone else" and that "someone needs to put him in his place".

    There was a mixture of rivalry and psychological gamesmanship at play, with the two drivers also fighting for second place in the drivers' championship. However, beating Verstappen at mind games is almost as difficult as beating him on track, and the Dutchman barely reacted to Russell's comments.

    Max Verstappen, Red Bull Racing, George Russell, Mercedes

    Starting first and second, there was no drama between them at the start of the race, but the pair went on to exchange the lead several times in a series of overtakes. In the end, Verstappen always seemed to have the situation under control: every time Russell managed to get past him, the Dutchman hit back, showing that the verbal attacks from the previous day hadn't affected him in the slightest.

    Ultimately, Russell lost out and finished well outside the podium places, while Verstappen went on to win the race and move ever closer to securing second place in the championship.

    When word started spreading through the paddock around the Monaco Grand Prix that the FIA had decided Red Bull had a benchmark power unit in F1 in 2026, many thought it was just a bad joke. Red Bull wasn't even close to challenging for wins at the start of the championship, and the verdict sounded almost laughable.

    Yet perhaps the engine wasn't really what was holding the Milton Keynes team back, but the car itself. That car is now largely sorted, and Max Verstappen is consistently fighting at the front. And the Las Vegas Grand Prix, with Verstappen and Isack Hadjar battling Mercedes on equal terms, perhaps confirms that the FIA's measurements weren't too far off in the first place.

    Pierre Gasly has made something of a speciality of Las Vegas since its arrival on the calendar in 2023. Twice he has qualified on the second row, and even last year, in an Alpine usually consigned to the back of the field, he reached Q3. Yet until this weekend, he had never scored a point there.

    His pace was evident again this time. Gasly was second in the wet in FP2 and impressed again in Q2, although ninth in Q3 left him feeling he had missed an opportunity.

    The race finally brought the reward his speed around the Strip had long promised. Gasly made early progress and spent much of the grand prix in contention for a podium. That challenge faded with 15 laps to go, but fifth place was a deserved return.

    With Alpine having scored just one point across the previous six grands prix, Gasly's result, backed up by sixth for Franco Colapinto, was exactly what the team needed. The double retirement for Racing Bulls allowed Alpine to claw back some ground in the fight for fifth, although catching its rival over the final two rounds still looks a tall order.

    Much of Alpine's pace was probably down to the circuit's characteristics. There are some parallels with Monza, where Gasly claimed a surprise pole in September, but Qatar could also suit the A526 as the team bids to finish the season on a stronger note.
