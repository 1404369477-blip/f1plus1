    佩雷兹：有人只满足于待在F1，我要推动凯迪拉克前进

    佩雷兹在结束红牛生涯并休赛一年后加盟凯迪拉克，他表示自己渴望推动这支新车队向前，并称有些人只是满足于待在F1。

    塞尔吉奥·佩雷斯对推动凯迪拉克在F1中前进充满信心：“有些人只是满足于来到这里”

    今年早些时候，塞尔吉奥·佩雷斯毫不客气地描述了他所认为的凯迪拉克一级方程式车队明显缺乏紧迫感的情况

    在F1中有一个共同的敌人，那就是时间。对于处于职业生涯暮年的老将来说，这不仅是秒表上的时间，也是在没有竞争力的情况下流逝的岁月。

    在经历了红牛任期的惨淡收场后，塞尔吉奥·佩雷斯在休整一年后加入了初创的凯迪拉克车队，迎接新的挑战。但最近在跨过300场大奖赛起步的门槛后，无论是新车队还是其他什么，仅仅完赛和凑数的这种新鲜感很快就让他厌倦了。

    车队仍然深陷运营问题、制造质量缺陷和可靠性小毛病之中，其中许多问题本应被追究，但佩雷斯认为有些问题早就该解决了。

    在这位如今36岁的车手签下他自认为最后一份F1合同（将他带到2029年）之前，佩雷斯是车队内部认为缺乏紧迫感、不值得让他长期留下的声音之一。

    持这种看法的并非佩雷斯一人。首席执行官丹·托里斯主导了赛季中期的全面改革，从创始车队领队格雷姆·劳登开始，但远不止于此，还有一连串人员离职。

    在拉斯维加斯回顾这段经历时，佩雷斯对他认为需要改变的地方直言不讳。

    他说：“对我来说，从第一天起就非常清楚，我回到一级方程式并不是仅仅为了待在F1里。我们是一支新车队，感觉很多人只是为能来到这里、享受美好时光而高兴。这不是我想在车队里看到的情况。”

    “我想确保我们是一支在工厂、在赛道、在运营层面都百分之百投入的车队，因为这是我们唯一能够前进的方式。竞争非常激烈，我认为这支车队里没有空间留给任何只是为来到这里而高兴的人。他们需要离开，因为这是一支我们将在未来几年全力推动、以确保我们能够取得进步的车队。”

    如今领导这项工作的责任落在了前雷诺人马钦·布德科夫斯基（Marcin Budkowski）身上。当被Motorsport.com问及他是否从洛登的继任者身上感受到同样的紧迫感时，佩雷斯回答说：“显然这更难，对吧？因为他处于不同的位置，而且他显然看事情的方式不同，因为他每天都能获得所有信息。”

    “但显然存在很大的紧迫感，因为F1里的事情变化非常快。明年对凯迪拉克来说是关键的一年，要确保我们能够展示我们的进步。因为否则我们都知道这个行业是如何运作的，压力会极快地攀升。”

    佩雷兹在幕后也一直在带头推动引入新鲜血液，他在引进前红牛盟友克雷格·斯金纳一事中发挥了作用，后者本周开始在车队担任首席开发工程师。前梅赛德斯和阿斯顿·马丁成员埃里克·布兰丁也已加入车队，并已在拉斯维加斯现场办公。

    “这些人在一级方程式里什么都做过，”他说。“他们来这里只是因为他们想在一个不同的项目上证明自己。我们都知道他们有多出色，但这是一个不同的项目，我们需要基本上开始把这种文化建立起来，把这些人安排到位，我认为他们将为当前的组织带来很多贡献。”

    考虑到车队面前还有多少工作要做，而且在2027年很可能又是艰难的建设之年之前，佩雷兹也乐于看到队友瓦尔特利·博塔斯留在车队，而不是提拔新秀科尔顿·赫塔。

    “他给车队带来了很多速度、很多经验，我认为在这个阶段这至关重要，”这位墨西哥人坚持说道。“我一直说，我认为车队目前有比车手更重要的事情要关注，所以在这方面我很满意。

    “现在我们可以排除那个干扰因素，确保我们在操作上能够顺利完成进站，并且做好各项工作，因为最近几场比赛在操作层面一直比较薄弱。”

    Sergio Perez bullish on pushing Cadillac forward in F1: "Some people were just happy to be here"

    Sergio Perez did not mince his words to describe what he felt was a distinct lack of urgency at the Cadillac Formula 1 team earlier this year

    There is one common enemy in Formula 1, and that is time. For veterans in the autumn of their career, that's not just the time on the stopwatch, but also the years going by without competing at the sharp end.

    Having recovered from a bruising end to his Red Bull tenure, Sergio Perez welcomed a new challenge after one year out with start-up Cadillac. But having recently crossed the threshold of 300 grand prix starts, the novelty of simply finishing races and making up the numbers soon wore off on him, new team or otherwise.

    The team remained mired in operational issues, build quality faults and reliability gremlins, many of which were to be accounted for, but some of which Perez felt should have long been solved by now.

    And before the now 36-year-old penned what he believes is his final F1 contract that takes him to 2029, Perez was one voice within the team who perceived a lack of urgency to make an extended stay worth his while.

    Perez was not alone with that view. CEO Dan Towriss led a mid-season overhaul, starting at the top with founding team boss Graeme Lowdon but going down much further than that with a raft of departures.

    Reflecting on the episode in Las Vegas, Perez was punchy on what he felt needed to change.

    "For me it was very clear from day one that I wasn't coming back to Formula 1 just for the sake of being here in F1," he said. "With us being a new team, it kind of felt like a lot of people were just really happy just to be here and have a good time. This is not something I want to see in the team.

    "I want to make sure that we are a team that is pushing 100% in the factory, at track, operationally, because it's the only way we will be able to move forward. The competition is super high and I think there is no room for anyone in this team that is just happy to be here. They need to be moving out because this is a team that we are going to be pushing super hard for the coming years to make sure we are able to progress."

    Leading that charge is now the job of former Renault man Marcin Budkowski. When asked by Motorsport.com if he felt the same urgency from Lowdon's successor, Perez replied: "Obviously it's harder, right? Because he's in a different position and he obviously sees things differently because he gets all the information on a daily basis.

    "But obviously there is a lot of urgency because things in Formula 1 can change very quickly. Next year is a crucial year for Cadillac to make sure that we are able to show our progress. Because otherwise we all know how this business works and the pressure can ramp up extremely fast."

    Perez had been leading the charge behind the scenes to bring in fresh blood, too, having played a role in bringing in former Red Bull ally Craig Skinner, who started working at the team this week as Chief Development Engineer. Former Mercedes and Aston Martin man Eric Blandin has also joined the team and is on the ground in Las Vegas.

    "Those guys have done everything in Formula 1," he said. "They're only here because they want to prove themselves on a different project. We all know how good they are, but this is a different project where we need to basically start putting this culture in place, these people in place, and I think they will be bringing a lot to the current organisation."

    With how much work lies ahead for the team, ahead of what will likely be another tough building year in 2027, Perez was also keen to see team-mate Valtteri Bottas retained at the team instead of promoting a rookie in Colton Herta.

    "He brings a lot of speed, a lot of experience to the team and I think at this stage it's crucial," the Mexican insisted. "I've always said I think the team has much more important things to focus on than drivers for now, so I'm happy in that regard.

    "Now we can take that distraction out of our way and make sure that we operationally can make good stops, and can make good things because the last few races operationally have been weak."
