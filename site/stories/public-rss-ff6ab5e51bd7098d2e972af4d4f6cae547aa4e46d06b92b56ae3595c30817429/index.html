    霍纳承认与沃尔夫的F1竞争变成“戏剧”

    克里斯蒂安·霍纳承认，他与托托·沃尔夫之间长期的F1竞争变得“更像戏剧”，但仍坚称那是“真实的”。

    克里斯蒂安·霍纳承认与托托·沃尔夫的F1竞争变成“戏剧”

    这两位车队负责人在各自车队年复一年争夺F1冠军期间展开了激烈的竞争

    托托·沃尔夫，梅赛德斯AMG车队负责人兼首席执行官，克里斯蒂安·霍纳，红牛车队负责人，在发车格上为奖杯掰手腕

    克里斯蒂安·霍纳承认，他与F1同行托托·沃尔夫之间长期的竞争变得“更像戏剧”——但仍坚称那是“真实的”。

    2010年至2023年间的每一个F1世界冠军，要么由沃尔夫执掌的梅赛德斯车队赢得，要么由霍纳领导的红牛车队夺得，这自然导致双方势同水火。

    这一幕常常被Netflix热门纪录片《极速求生》记录下来，其中尤为典型的一幕是2022年地面效应新规实施之初，两人就海豚跳问题展开的激烈争论。

    霍纳喜欢扮演“哑剧反派”的角色，但自从他在2025年7月被解雇后，这一切都平息了下来，因为接替他的洛朗·梅基斯是一个圆滑得多的人。

    “摄像机刚出现时，一切都很真实，”霍纳告诉《赛车运动》。“然后你能感觉到，随着赛季推进，出现了相当多男性形象管理，变得更有戏剧性了一些。”

    “但我始终觉得自己很真实，当然，这些人在拍一档电视节目，所以他们拍摄了成千上万小时的素材，关键在于你怎么剪辑。

    “你可以把某人剪成反派或英雄，或者反过来。那档节目之所以极其成功，是因为它突然揭开了那些竞争、个性、车手的个性、一些人物性格的面纱。”

    霍纳与沃尔夫之间的竞争在2021年达到顶峰，当时红牛车队的马克斯·维斯塔潘与梅赛德斯车队的刘易斯·汉密尔顿为车手总冠军展开了激烈争夺。

    在维斯塔潘追逐他的首个总冠军、汉密尔顿寻求创纪录的第八个总冠军的过程中，两人多次发生碰撞，而两支车队也在为车队总冠军展开较量。

    这一切在充满争议的阿布扎比收官战中结束，维斯塔潘击败汉密尔顿，为红牛赢得了自2013年以来的首个车手总冠军，而梅赛德斯则连续第八次夺得车队总冠军。

    “托托是一位强大的竞争对手，梅赛德斯是一支庞大的车队，”霍纳补充道。“他们曾经取得并享有的成功，正是这一点让2021年对我们更加重要。

    “但他情绪化、好胜、有动力，我们享受这种竞争。我对他的所作所为和成就怀有极大的尊重，但我认为在所有体育运动中，你都需要有竞争。

    “你们不可能一直只是最好的朋友，这不符合现实。当然，那是激励车队的一种方式——梅赛德斯就是对手。

    “作为一家能量饮料公司的子公司，我们必须努力与他们抗衡，向他们发起挑战。”

    2021年阿布扎比领奖台上的汉密尔顿和霍纳

    霍纳坚持认为，F1需要像过去那些年一样，在领先集团中形成激烈的竞争对抗，无论是阿兰·普罗斯特对埃尔顿·塞纳，还是詹姆斯·亨特对尼基·劳达。

    竞争对抗最终有助于售票和抢占头条，足球或板球同样如此，而在霍纳看来，这是车队追逐成功的自然结果。

    “有竞争是好事，只要不越界变成针对个人，”他说，“这在体育中是健康的，无论你是拥有佩普·瓜迪奥拉和何塞·穆里尼奥，还是弗格森和温格。

    “它能激发体育的活力，而且我认为在一支团队内部也是如此，如果你想赢，你就必须心中有团火。

    “一级方程式是世界上最激烈、最艰难的运动之一，你要求人们去做非凡的事情，去达到会影响他们家庭生活和在家时间等等的极限，因为这相当缺乏社交。

    “所以你在激励你的团队，这就是竞争，如果你能找到弱点，那么你就能看到它会渗透开来。”

    Christian Horner admits that F1 rivalry with Toto Wolff became "theatre"

    The two team bosses had a heated rivalry while their squads fought for the F1 title year on year

    Toto Wolff, Team Principal and CEO, Mercedes AMG, Christian Horner, Team Principal, Red Bull Racing, arm wrestle over the trophy on the grid

    Christian Horner has accepted that his long-standing rivalry with Formula 1 counterpart Toto Wolff became ‘more like theatre’ - but still assured it was “authentic”.

    Every F1 title between 2010 and 2023 was won by either Wolff’s Mercedes team or Horner’s Red Bull squad, which naturally caused them to be at loggerheads with each other.

    This was often captured on Netflix hit Drive to Survive with one particular moment being their heated argument about porpoising at the start of the ground-effect regulations in 2022.

    Horner liked to play the role of ‘pantomime villain’ but it’s all died down since his firing in July 2025, as replacement Laurent Mekies is a much more diplomatic individual.

    “When the cameras first turned up it was very authentic,” Horner told Autosport. “Then you could feel as the seasons went on that there was considerably more male grooming and it became a little more theatre.

    “But I always felt authentic to myself and, of course, these guys are making a TV show so they take hours and hours and hours of footage and it's all about how you edit something.

    “You can either make somebody look like the villain or the hero or vice versa. That show has been incredibly successful because it suddenly lifted the lid on some of the rivalries, the personalities, the driver's personalities, some of the characters.”

    The rivalry between Horner and Wolff reached its peak in 2021 when Red Bull’s Max Verstappen bitterly fought Mercedes’ Lewis Hamilton for the drivers’ title.

    They collided on several occasions as Verstappen chased his maiden crown with Hamilton hunting for a record-breaking eighth, while the two teams also battled for the constructors’ title.

    It ended in the contentious Abu Dhabi finale, where Verstappen dethroned Hamilton to give Red Bull its first drivers’ championship since 2013, while Mercedes took an eighth consecutive teams’ crown.

    “Toto is a formidable competitor and Mercedes are a massive team,” added Horner. “The success that they've enjoyed and had, that's what made ‘21 even more important to us.

    “But he's emotional, he's competitive, he's driven and we enjoyed a rivalry. I have huge respect for what he's done and achieved but I think in all sports you need to have rivalry.

    “You can't all just be best friends all of the time, it's not true to the reality. Certainly that was a way of galvanising the team that Mercedes are the opposition.

    “We have to try, as a subsidiary of an energy drinks company, to take them on and take the fight to them.”

    Hamilton and Horner on the Abu Dhabi podium in 2021

    Horner was insistent on F1 needing heated rivalries at the front as had happened in years gone by, whether it was Alain Prost versus Ayrton Senna or James Hunt versus Niki Lauda.

    Rivalries ultimately play a part in selling tickets and grabbing headlines, likewise in football or cricket, and to Horner it is a natural consequence of teams chasing success.

    “It's good to have rivalry, so long as it doesn't spill over into personal,” he said. “It's healthy in sport, whether you have Pep Guardiola and Jose Mourinho or Ferguson and Wenger.

    “It galvanises sport and I think as well within a team, you've got to have a fire in your belly if you want to win.

    “Formula 1 is one of the most competitive, hardest sports in the world and you're asking people to do extraordinary things and go to limits that have impact on their family lives and time at home and so on, because it is pretty unsociable.

    “So you're galvanising your group that that is the competition and if you can find a weakness, then you can see that it permeates.”
